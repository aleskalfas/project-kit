*A cross-process connection may **opt in** to an evaluable **hand-off contract** — "every upstream subject at its trigger state has a downstream subject picking it up" — and a new **report-only `health` operation** walks the declared contracts and reports every **missed hand-off**. This ships the first family of the cross-subject always-check slot [COR-035](COR-035-process-invariants.md) deferred, in the weakest honest posture: the check reports and sets an exit code, never gates a move. Connections without a contract keep their full inert status, and the runtime position engine still never reads `depends_on`. One accepted ruling is modified rather than sidestepped: [COR-038](COR-038-process-connections.md) said `depends_on` is never evaluated at all, and the health surface is a new, evaluating reader — this record re-scopes that claim explicitly.*

## Context

[COR-038](COR-038-process-connections.md) made cross-process wiring visible: a process declares its connections as inert `depends_on` metadata the engine never evaluates, and a render can draw the whole configured topology. [COR-035](COR-035-process-invariants.md) shipped single-subject always-checks and explicitly deferred their cross-subject sibling — "a cross-subject always-check ships where the engine first gains the right to read across subjects" — and COR-038's `constrained-with` relation points its enforcement at that same deferred slot.

A binding now demands the slot (the [COR-007](COR-007-pattern-extraction.md) recurrence trigger). An adopter running a design process coupled to a delivery process hit the gap in live use: every upstream subject had reached the state that hands work downstream, and the downstream process had picked up none of them — with nothing in the tooling able to say so. The topology was declared and drawable; whether the declared hand-off *actually happened* was invisible, a fact a human had to eyeball across two processes. The adopter's request names the need precisely: **detection before remediation** — the process should *show* a missed hand-off, read-only and systematically, before anyone builds machinery to fix one.

Nothing shipped answers that question. The render draws edges but evaluates nothing. `validate` is deliberately single-subject (COR-035). The cascade fold ([COR-037](COR-037-process-cascade.md)) reads across a crowd, but only child→parent, feeding a gate — a different machine with a different direction and a different consumer. A check that reads *across two connected processes' crowds* and reports the gaps is new, and because it evaluates a declared connection, it collides head-on with COR-038's ruling that `depends_on` is never evaluated. This record settles both: the check, and the honest re-scope of that ruling.

## Decision

**In plain terms:** a connection that wants its hand-off *checked* says so, by adding a small evaluable contract to its `depends_on` entry — the upstream trigger state, plus how to find the downstream counterpart. A new `health` operation, outside the runtime engine, walks every declared contract: for each upstream subject at its trigger state it asks the binding "which downstream subject picks this up?", and reports every one whose answer is *none*. The report never blocks anything; its exit code lets automation notice. Connections that don't declare a contract are exactly as inert as COR-038 left them.

### 1. The hand-off contract — an opt-in, evaluable sub-block on a `depends_on` entry

A `depends_on` entry **may** carry a hand-off contract: the upstream **trigger state** (the position at which work is ready to hand off) and the **two resolution-seam predicates** the binding supplies (point 2). The contract is:

- **Opt-in.** Entries without it retain COR-038's full inert contract — never evaluated by anything, malformed-is-lint-only, unable to affect any subject's movement. Nothing reinterprets existing declarations; the field is additive, and every existing definition validates byte-unchanged.
- **Orthogonal to `relation` and `mode`.** Evaluability is a property of the *contract* ("this hand-off is checkable"), not of the edge's mechanism. A `triggered-by` edge mediated by an external connector (`mode: push`) can carry a checkable contract — the check reads reality, not the mediation — and no adopter is forced to relabel an edge `constrained-with` to get checking. `constrained-with` remains the *name* for cross-subject constraints; the contract is how any relation's hand-off becomes checkable.
- **Shape-validated like the rest of the entry.** A malformed contract is a definition-authoring lint error, never a runtime failure — unchanged from COR-038's static/dynamic split. Shape lint stays *local* well-formedness; whether the contract can actually be interpreted against the upstream definition is checked at health time, where it fails closed (point 2).

### 2. The missed-hand-off check — definition and semantics

For each declared contract, the check: reads the **upstream subjects currently at the trigger state** — from a **binding-supplied candidate source, confirmed one subject at a time** against the engine's per-subject position resolution; asks the binding's **`resolve`** seam, per upstream subject, for the corresponding **downstream subject id(s) or absence**; and reports a **missed hand-off** for every upstream subject at the trigger whose downstream is **absent**.

- **Missed = absent.** The check asks *existence* of the downstream counterpart, not its position or progress — downstream progress is that process's own concern, readable by its own operations. Existence is a **binding-determined fact over reality** — whatever the binding's `resolve` answers; the check never holds an engine-side notion of subject registration. That is also what lets a **singleton endpoint** participate cleanly: a singleton upstream's candidate source yields its one subject; a singleton downstream's `resolve` answers from that singleton's reality like any other.
- **"At the trigger state" is a live snapshot.** Consistent with live detection and the pull-only engine (COR-038 point 3), the check evaluates the subjects *currently at* the trigger, re-derived from reality at each run. The journal cannot substitute a historical "has reached the trigger": under detection-over-reality a subject can advance with no journaled move, so a journal-derived "reached" would be **silently incomplete** — worse than an honest snapshot. A consequence bindings must author for: a trigger should be a **stable** state — one the subject holds until the hand-off happens (or terminal) — because a subject that transits the trigger ephemerally leaves the report, picked up or not. Declaring an ephemeral trigger is an authoring smell the shape reference documents; a trigger declared as a state *set* (at-or-beyond) is a named-deferred extension for the ephemeral class (Implications).
- **A contract that cannot be interpreted is indeterminate, never clean.** An upstream address that does not resolve to a definition, or a trigger state that is not a state of that definition, makes the whole contract **indeterminate** — reported distinctly, exit code non-zero. Without this rule a typo'd trigger or a renamed upstream state would report *green* forever, the lying-report failure this record exists to prevent.
- **Determinate-empty is clean; broken-empty is not.** Zero upstream subjects at the trigger — the candidate source evaluated without error and confirmed none — is a clean, determinate answer. An erroring or unavailable candidate source, an erroring `resolve`, or an unreadable upstream position is **indeterminate**: reported distinctly and holding the exit code non-zero, never counted as "nothing missed" (the same determinate-empty vs broken-read split COR-037 fixed for the fold). A candidate source pointed at a wrong or moved root that *successfully* returns nothing is the residual hazard here — named as the same authoring smell as the ephemeral trigger.
- **One report line per (contract × upstream subject)**, de-duplicated within a contract; a subject legitimately appears under several contracts when it is upstream of several couplings.
- **Cardinality degrades cleanly.** `resolve` may return several downstream ids (fan-out) — existence of at least one satisfies the hand-off; several upstream subjects may resolve to the same downstream (fan-in) — each upstream is checked independently.

### 3. Report-only, on a new `health` surface outside the runtime engine

The check **never gates**: no move is blocked, no transition fails, nothing is remediated. Like COR-035's invariants it is evaluated-and-surfaced, never acted on — with one honest difference: invariant violations surface on every `status` read, while misses surface **only when `health` runs**, because the re-scope in point 4 itself forbids the runtime status path from reading `depends_on`. The report is deterministic, offline, and machine-readable; its **exit code** (non-zero on any miss or indeterminate) is the automation hook, and its snapshot semantics are part of that contract — a red produced by a race (downstream created a moment after the run) is an honest answer about the instant evaluated. No *engine* behaviour ever rides on the read.

It ships as a **new `health` operation**, not an extension of `validate`: `validate` has a crisp per-subject contract (one subject's invariants through the single-subject runner) and takes a subject; `health` takes none — it walks contracts across the configured wiring. The natural pairing is with the render: the graph *draws* the declared topology, `health` *evaluates* the evaluable part of it. `health` is deliberately a broad name for one narrow shipped check (name-broad / ship-narrow, [COR-016](COR-016-scripted-scenario-storyboards.md)): the further signal families it could host stay named-deferred (Implications).

### 4. The honest re-scope of COR-038's reader-set claim — and what is preserved

COR-038 ruled `depends_on` "the first layer the engine never *evaluates*," with exactly two readers (schema, render). The health surface is a **third reader, and an evaluating one** — so this record **re-scopes that ruling** rather than pretending to sit beside it untouched:

> The **runtime position operations** (`status`, `can-move`, `move`, `validate`-of-position) and **every gate** never read `depends_on`. The **health surface** — out-of-runtime, report-only — evaluates the **opt-in hand-off contracts** and nothing else; entries without a contract are never evaluated by anything.

On acceptance COR-038 carries an amendment note covering **every sentence the re-scope touches** — the reader-set ruling ("only two readers"; "never evaluates at all", including its summary's "only a rendering tool reads"), the `push` gloss ("records it solely for visibility" — a push edge carrying a contract is now also evaluated by health), and the two "constrained-with … enforcement stays deferred" sentences (the slot's first family has shipped *checking*, not *enforcing*, and orthogonally to `relation` — enforcement remains deferred at supersession weight, see Alternatives) — so neither record lies.

What COR-038's acceptance actually protected is **fully preserved**: the position engine stays **pull-only** (the check pulls on its own turn; no eventing anywhere); **nothing gates on a connection** (report-only); the **derive-don't-annotate** rule is untouched (the contract enriches an edge the runtime engine cannot see — enforced edges stay derived).

### 5. The second bounded cross-subject read — sanctioned here, by declaration

[COR-037](COR-037-process-cascade.md) crossed COR-032's *never-enumerate* line **once**, for the fold, and expressly scoped its sanction to folds. The health check is a **second, distinct cross-subject read machine** — walk-and-report across a connected process pair, not fold-and-gate child→parent — and it does **not** inherit COR-037's sanction; it is sanctioned **here**, bounded as follows:

- **The health surface is not the engine.** It is an out-of-runtime reader beside it. COR-037's "crossed once *by the engine*" ruling stands untouched: the engine still reads across a crowd in exactly one place (the fold), still exposes no listing API, still holds no tree, and still acts only on the one subject it is given.
- **The discipline holds in seam form.** Candidate sets originate in **binding-supplied sources**; each candidate is confirmed **one subject at a time** through the engine's existing per-subject position resolution; downstream resolution is **point-to-point** per supplied id. (This is the *shape* COR-037's seam established, not the same seam: cascade's per-candidate confirm is a binding-supplied membership test, health's is the engine's own at-state position read — a deliberate, narrower choice recorded with the seam mechanism.)
- **The scope is acknowledged, not minimised.** A contract's candidate set is *process-wide at a state* — broader than cascade's one-parent scoping — and that is safe **because nothing rides on the read**: no gate, no move-legality, no stored state.
- **Future health signal families route through this surface** rather than growing rival cross-process read paths — the design-once discipline COR-037 applied to folds, applied here to reports.

## Rationale

**Why this is fulfilment — partially — and where it honestly is not.** The substrate reserved this: COR-035 named cross-subject always-checks as the deferred slot, COR-038 pointed `constrained-with` at it, and the deferred-slot discipline (COR-033 P5, name-broad / ship-narrow per COR-016) un-defers a named slot when a real binding demands it — the pattern every shipped slot followed. One adopter with a live, quantified gap is the demand. Two honesty notes. First, this ships the slot's **first family only** — hand-off existence; a general cross-subject invariant (COR-035's own example: "no two subjects hold the same lock") still has nothing to declare against, and stays named-deferred. Second, COR-035 forecast the slot shipping "where the engine first gains the right to read across subjects"; health deliberately ships **beside** the engine instead — the safer placement, noted so the two sentences don't silently disagree. What cannot be framed as fulfilment at all is COR-038's reader-set ruling — a foundational, signed-off sentence — so this record modifies it explicitly and narrowly (point 4) instead of leaving two accepted records in silent tension.

**Why the read is safe where the enforcing gate was rejected.** COR-038 rejected an engine gate over upstream state because its safety rested on an unenforceable promise not to read a peer's flapping mid-flight position — *gating* on a flapping read silently corrupts move-legality. The health check reads possibly-mid-flight positions too, but no engine behaviour rides on the read: no move-legality, no gate, no stored state — a report-only snapshot that is stale a moment later is still an honest report of the moment, and its one consumer (the exit code) carries snapshot semantics as a declared part of its contract. Report-only is not a diluted compromise; it is precisely the posture that makes the cross-endpoint read safe.

**Why substrate-shipped, not capability-local.** Not because the machinery is generic — candidates, resolution, and what "existence" means are all binding code — but because the *surface* must be shared: one report every tool and adopter reads the same way, one exit-code contract, one indeterminacy discipline, rather than a rival cross-process read path re-invented per binding (the same shared-surface argument that put invariants on the shared `validate` path in COR-035 and routed folds through one declared mechanism in COR-037). The health surface owns the walk, the report, and the fail-closed rules; the binding owns every domain fact through the seams.

**Why opt-in rather than evaluating every coupling.** Evaluating all `depends_on` entries implicitly would convert COR-038's visibility layer into an obligation retroactively — every existing annotation would acquire operational meaning and a correctness burden its author never accepted, and a stale `informational` edge would start failing CI. Opt-in keeps COR-038's authoring contract intact for everyone who wants only visibility, and makes the evaluable contract a deliberate, reviewable act.

**Why existence, not downstream position.** The demanding binding needs pickup detection ("nothing builds this ready design"), not downstream progress tracking. Checking position across the pair is a larger machine with no demanding binding; existence is the ship-narrow cut.

### Alternatives considered

- **Extend `validate` instead of a new operation.** Rejected — `validate` takes one subject and runs its invariants; the hand-off check takes no subject and walks the wiring. Overloading blurs a crisp signature into two shapes, and changing `validate`'s exit conditions would silently repurpose an existing CI contract.
- **Surface misses on the upstream subject's `status` read** (the COR-035 posture in full). Rejected — it would require the runtime status path to read `depends_on` and run cross-process resolution on every read, violating the re-scope in point 4 and taxing the hot path; `health` is the audit verb, deliberately off the runtime.
- **An enforcing posture** (block the upstream's further moves, or gate the downstream). Rejected — re-opens exactly the hazard COR-038 closed (move-legality riding on a cross-process read), and no binding asks for it. If a binding ever does, that is its own record argued against COR-038's gate rejection at supersession weight — not an increment on this one.
- **Evaluate all couplings implicitly (no opt-in sub-block).** Rejected above — retroactive obligation on inert declarations; breaks COR-038's authoring contract.
- **Keep it capability-local.** Rejected — rival cross-process read paths per binding, off the shared surface; the COR-035/COR-037 placement argument applies unchanged.
- **A stored reverse index, or a naming convention, as *the* downstream-resolution mechanism.** Rejected as the general mechanism — a stored index is derived data that drifts into a lying report (the derive-don't-store discipline COR-038 itself invokes), and a naming convention couples two processes' id spaces and breaks on fan-in/fan-out. Both survive as *implementations* a binding may choose behind the resolution seam; the seam decision and its rejected alternatives are recorded in the engine's own architecture record (per [COR-025](COR-025-adr-decision-space.md)).

## Implications

- **Shape contract.** A `depends_on` entry gains an **optional hand-off contract sub-block** — the trigger state plus the two seam predicates (candidate source, `resolve`). Additive — existing definitions validate byte-unchanged, no migration owed ([COR-010](COR-010-resource-lifecycle.md)). The **hosting state of the entry has no semantic effect on the check** (the check reads upstream reality and downstream existence only) — stated so the shape reference can say it rather than trip over it. Exact field layout lands in the process-area shape reference and the process schema, per the COR-034..038 pattern; the reference also carries the two authoring smells (ephemeral trigger; a candidate source that can silently return nothing against a wrong root).
- **A new `health` operation + its CLI surface** — out-of-runtime, deterministic, offline; scannable output plus a machine-readable form; exit non-zero on any miss or indeterminate (whether miss and indeterminate get distinct exit codes is a CLI-reference decision, not fixed here). Implementation homes it **beside** the engine, consuming the engine's per-subject position resolution, so "the runtime position operations never read `depends_on`" stays structurally true in code — placement and its pinned test recorded in the engine's architecture record.
- **Same change-set on acceptance** (atomically with the status flip — any gap leaves two accepted records contradicting): COR-038's amendment note covering every sentence enumerated in point 4; COR-035's deferred-list line updated to "first family shipped (hand-off existence), general cross-subject invariants still deferred," pointing here; the process-area reference's `depends_on` section (including its own restatements of the two-reader claim and the constrained-with deferral) and its core-operations list gain the contract and the `health` operation, with the deferred marker for this slot flipped to first-family-shipped.
- **Named-deferred siblings** — each its own future decision on a real demand: **general cross-subject invariants** (the slot's remaining families — mutual-exclusion and friends); the **data-coupling drift/diff** signal family; the **orphan-artifact inverse** (a downstream subject with no upstream counterpart); a **trigger state-set / at-or-beyond form** for the ephemeral-trigger class; **auto-remediation**; any **enforcing coupling** (supersession weight, see Alternatives); richer report reducers/summaries.
- **The grounding binding carries the first contract** — the demanding adopter declares the evaluable sub-block on its design→delivery coupling in its own capability decision, so the slot lands exercised, never inert.
- **The companion architecture record is authored with this record**, not at implementation time — a stated deviation from the fold's precedent (its mechanism record waited for engine work), justified because the demanding request posed three concrete resolution mechanisms and the seam choice had to be settled at design time. Its acceptance follows this record's, never precedes it.
- **Surface change** — a new principle, a new operation, an additive schema field — bumps the affected component's version per the project's versioning policy. **Acceptance carries two-maintainer sign-off** on three rulings: (i) the COR-038 reader-set carve-out as stated in point 4; (ii) the report-only posture as the boundary — any future enforcing coupling is a separate, supersession-weight decision; (iii) the second bounded cross-subject read as sanctioned and scoped in point 5, leaving COR-037's crossed-once-by-the-engine ruling intact.
