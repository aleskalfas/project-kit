**In plain terms:** when an issue moves forward, the forward cascade brings each container above it level with it. Where it cannot tell where a container stands, as where the board would not answer for its card or the card has no Status, the move goes ahead all the same. That container is left where it stands, since moving it would act on a guess, and so is one the cascade could not write to. The cascade says which containers it left, why, and how to finish, and goes on to the containers above, each moved only on what its own reads find. Once the move is made, what the cascade left is a warning beside it, never an exit code.

## Context

Two accepted rules meet at the forward cascade:

- **No container is to stand behind the work under it.** When an issue moves forward, each ancestor that is behind is brought up to the moved issue's state, capped at In Progress ([project-management:DEC-006-state-machine-and-cascade], "Cascade rule").
- **No command acts on a position it could not read** ([project-management:DEC-051-axis-carriage-activation] point 6). Where the board carries the state, a read of an issue's card that does not place it ends in one of four outcomes: the board cannot be read, the issue has no card, its card has no Status, or its Status names no open state. None falls back to the issue's labels or its Milestone.

At an ancestor the cascade cannot place, the two pull apart. Bringing it level needs its position, and its position cannot be read. The record on a board-carried state leaves which rule gives way to a project-management record ([pkit:ADR-074], Boundaries). Its point 5 holds every command that reads the state to two limits: it never guesses, and it keeps a board that would not answer apart from a finding about the issue. Where a command's exit codes tell a broken tool from a refusal, a board that would not answer takes the broken tool's code.

The record on a board that did not answer ([pkit:ADR-096]) narrows that code once a command's act is made. A command's act, in its terms, is the change the command is run to make, here the issue's move, and what the act sets off on another issue, such as the forward cascade, is no part of it. Once the act is made, the command takes no code that reads as the act not made. That record leaves the cascade to this one, within two limits: no ancestor moves on a position not read, which DEC-051 point 6 already holds, and a move once made takes no code that reads as the move not made.

The question names two options ([project-kit#3204](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3204)): the move waits on such an ancestor, or it is made with the walk stopped and reported. This record takes the second, and lets the walk go on past the ancestor rather than stop there (point 4).

Terms:

- **The walk** is the cascade's pass from the moved issue's parent up its hierarchy, one ancestor at a time. It ends at the top, where it cannot tell the next ancestor up, at an issue that is no container, and at one it has passed already.
- **A read of where an ancestor stands** places it in a state of the lifecycle, places it in none, or gets no answer.
  - **It places the ancestor in none** where what comes back is a finding about the ancestor. Where the board carries the state, that is one of DEC-051 point 6's three findings: the issue has no card, its card has no Status, or its Status names no open state. Wherever the state lives, it is also the finding that whatever carries the state names a value that is no state of the lifecycle, such as a label for a state the lifecycle does not have.
  - **It gets no answer** where what comes back says that the ancestor's position could not be evaluated, as where the board or the ancestor's issue could not be read, and where nothing comes back, as where whatever the read asks cannot be reached. A read that gets no answer gives no position, whichever read it is.
  - **An ancestor is level** where a read places it at or past the state the cascade brings it up to.
- **The first read** of an ancestor is made as the walk is planned: before the move, and before a person at a terminal confirms it.
- **A later read** is made after the move, just before the cascade would write to an ancestor, of each ancestor the first read did not place level.
- **An ancestor the cascade cannot place** is one whose later read places it in none or gets no answer.

## Decision

**A forward move is made whatever its cascade finds above it. The cascade writes to an ancestor only from a read made just before the write. An ancestor that read cannot place is not moved, one whose write is not made is moved no further, and each is reported. An ancestor left does not end the walk, and once the move is made, the cascade takes no exit code.**

### 1. The move does not wait on an ancestor

A forward move is made, or refused, on the moved issue's own reads and gates, never on an ancestor's position. No command refuses or holds a move because an ancestor its cascade will reach cannot be placed, whether the walk finds that before the move or after it.

Where the walk is planned before the move, what it found is shown before the move is confirmed. So a person at a terminal sees which ancestor the first read could not place, and may decline the move.

### 2. An ancestor the cascade cannot place is left where it stands

Nothing is written to it: no state, no Status, no journal entry and no comment. The cascade writes to an ancestor only from where its later read places it. The first read decides only which ancestors are read again, and what the preview shows. So an ancestor the first read could not place is brought level where its later read places it, and one whose later read gets no answer is left, whatever its first read found. Its position is never guessed: not from its labels or its Milestone where the board carries the state (DEC-051 point 6), and not from a read made before the move.

### 3. An ancestor whose write is not made is moved no further

A write is not made where it fails, or where what it needs cannot be read, such as the ancestor's issue read again for the write. The steps the ancestor took before that write stand, and so does the move.

### 4. An ancestor left does not end the walk

The walk finds an ancestor's parent from the command's own read of that ancestor's issue, not from the read of where it stands. Each ancestor is brought up to the moved issue's state, capped at In Progress, and never to the state of the ancestor below it, so an ancestor left bears on no move above it. So the walk goes on past it, and each ancestor above is brought level, or left alone, on where its own reads place it.

The walk ends where it reaches the top, or where it cannot tell the next ancestor up, as where the read of an ancestor's issue that it takes the parent from cannot be made: a command that writes on a parent writes nothing on one in doubt, or above it ([pkit:ADR-035] point 5). It ends too at an issue that is no container, which the cascade does not move, and at one it has passed already. A later read of an ancestor's issue, made only for the write to it, leaves the walk as it is, since the parent above was told already.

### 5. The report names each ancestor left, why, and how to finish

The cascade's closing report is a warning. It names each ancestor the cascade left, and the read or the write that left it, and, where the walk ended before the top, where and why. A read that got no answer is named as one, with what came back where anything did, and a board that would not answer as a board that would not answer. Neither is named as a finding about the ancestor, nor a finding as a read that got no answer ([pkit:ADR-074] point 5).

The closure cascade makes an ancestor eligible to close once its last open child closes, wherever the ancestor stands, while DEC-006's table starts that close only at In Progress, so an ancestor left behind can be offered for closing before it is level. The report says how to finish:

- **Where what carries the ancestor's state does not place it as meant,** such as a card with no Status, put that right. Where a read got no answer, wait until it can get one, as when the board answers again.
- **Then run the move again** where that finishes the cascade, which moves nothing twice. Where running it again does not finish it, as for an issue that reads Done without a close reported as completed, the next forward move under the ancestor brings it level.
- **Where no forward move is left to come under the ancestor,** as when the moved issue was the last open one under it, move the ancestor on its own once a read places it, before it is closed. That move is asked for directly, so its gates apply: a container in Todo needs scheduling of its own, a Milestone or an audited reason, though the scheduling given on the work under it covered it (DEC-006). The cost falls only where nothing under the container is left to bring it level.
- **Where the ancestor's state is set as a person meant it,** such as a container set outside the lifecycle on purpose, the container stays where it was put, and each forward move under it reports it again.

### 6. Once the move is made, the cascade takes no code

A run that makes the move, or finds the issue already at its target, exits as the move alone has it exit, a dry run of an issue already at its target included. An ancestor the cascade left, for any cause, is a warning beside it, never a code: not a refusal's, not the broken tool's, nor any other. That is within the limit [pkit:ADR-096] point 4 sets once a command's act is made: nothing the act sets off on another issue gives the command a code that reads as the act not made. So each command that composes the move reads the move as made.

A run that ends with the move not made, such as a dry run of a move not yet made or a run declined at the terminal, takes ADR-074 point 5's broken tool's code where the board would not answer for the card of an ancestor it previewed. An ancestor its first read places in none gives it no code, since that would not have held the move (point 1).

## Rationale

**Why the move does not wait.**

- **Waiting brings the ancestor no nearer level.** Where a read gets no answer, the ancestor's position is unknown, which is not the same as behind. Where a read places it in none, it is known not to be level, yet it stands nowhere the lifecycle moves from. Either way it cannot be moved without a guess, which DEC-051 point 6 forbids, so a hold on the work under it leaves it as it was.
- **Waiting would give a state set by hand a meaning no record gives it.** A container whose card sits in a column outside the lifecycle, such as Blocked, or whose label names such a state, or a container kept off the board, would hold every forward move beneath it. What a card a person places by hand means is a question DEC-006 leaves open, and a hold on the work would answer it by accident. The remedy lies on the ancestor, often someone else's, not on the work being moved.
- **Gates are per issue.** The cascade is not an authorisation and asks for none, and each gate governs a move on its own issue (DEC-006). A hold on another issue's state would be a gate nobody set on the move, stopping a move whose own gates passed.
- **Waiting could not hold for every move.** Where a merge or a completed close is made before the move to Done that follows it, nothing is left to hold. A walk planned whole can also be cut short after the move, by a later read that places an ancestor in none or gets no answer, or a write the board refuses. So a move made with an ancestor reported has to be handled whatever this record decides.

**Why DEC-006's Cascade rule is narrowed here, and not DEC-051's rule.** DEC-006 rejects leaving an ancestor behind and reporting it, where the cascade could move it, because a container behind a started child states something false. Here the cascade cannot move the ancestor without guessing, which DEC-051 point 6 forbids. Moving it on a guess would write a position nobody read, and refusing the move would hold the work on another issue's state. So the Cascade rule keeps its aim, that no container is to stand behind the work under it, and is narrowed to what the cascade can read and write: the container may stand behind the work under it until a later move brings it level. The narrowing accepts more than an unknown: where a read places the ancestor in none, the container is known not to be level, and stays so until whatever carries its state is set right or a record decides what such a state means. The rule is narrowed the same way at an ancestor whose write is not made: the move is made by then, and only a write made again can bring the ancestor level.

**Why the walk goes on.** The parent comes from the read of the ancestor's issue, which was made: only its position was not read. The state each ancestor is brought up to does not depend on the one below, so an ancestor above, brought level on its own reads, moves on a position that was read. A container ahead of the one below it is a state DEC-006 allows, since a container may stand ahead of the work under it. Stopping the walk would leave every container above behind, for a fault on one ancestor. An ancestor whose write failed is passed the same way, for the same reason. The walk ends where the next ancestor up cannot be told, since a write on a parent in doubt could move a container the moved issue is not under. It ends too at an issue that is no container, which the cascade never moves, and at one it has passed already, where going on would walk in a circle.

**Why the cascade writes only from a read made just before the write.** The first read is made as the walk is planned, before the move and before a person at a terminal confirms it, so it can be minutes old when the cascade writes. The cascade runs only once the move's own write has landed, so a later read that gets no answer most likely meets a passing fault, and a write from the first read in its place would most likely land. In between, another move under the same ancestor may have brought it level, as a start of work on a sibling does. A write sets where the ancestor goes without checking where it stands, so a write from the first read would then:

- where the board carries the state, set the ancestor's Status back behind the work under it, a move back DEC-006 holds for the user, which nobody authorised;
- where labels carry the state, take off a label the ancestor no longer carries and add the next, which can leave it two values on one axis, against the rule that an issue carries one value on each axis ([project-management:RS-PM-003]);
- on either, journal a step from a state the ancestor no longer held, which no later move takes back.

A read made just before the write leaves only the window every write leaves between its read and its landing. So the cascade writes from that read alone, and leaves the ancestor wherever it gets no answer, whatever left it without one: the board, the ancestor's issue, or whatever answers the read not reached at all. The first read is as old in each case. The cost is an ancestor a passing fault left unread, though the first read placed it, until the move made again, or the next forward move under it, brings it level.

**Why a read that gets no answer is kept apart from one that places the ancestor in none.** Either leaves the ancestor where it stands, and neither gives the cascade a position to write from. They differ in what they say, and in what finishes them. A read that places the ancestor in none says where it stands: outside the lifecycle, until whatever carries its state is set right. A read that gets no answer says nothing of the ancestor, and the remedy is to wait until it can be answered. So the report names them apart, as ADR-074 point 5 asks, and before the move only a board that would not answer takes the broken tool's code. A read that cannot reach whatever answers it is no answer for the same reason a board that would not answer is none: nothing was learnt of where the ancestor stands.

**Why the later read is made of an ancestor the first could not place.** A board that would not answer for an ancestor's card when the walk was planned may answer by the write, and a card set right in between places the ancestor. Leaving the ancestor on the first read alone would leave one the cascade could bring level, and the read costs what the read before each write already costs. An ancestor the first read places level is not read again, so a move under ancestors already level costs one read of each.

**Why nothing is written to the ancestor.** A notice on it would repeat on every forward move under a container set outside the lifecycle on purpose, or need a key to post it once that a later change of its state would have to reset. The report reaches whoever runs the move, where the move's own outcome is read. The report repeats too: each forward move under such a container names it again, a warning that recurs until its state changes or a record decides what such a state means, and that readers may learn to pass over. That cost falls on the output of the moves under the container, and on no issue.

**Why no code once the move is made, and ADR-074's code before it.** Commands compose the move and hand on, or act on, its code: a start of work reads a non-zero code as a failed start, and a merge or a close as a step to run again. After the move, a code for the cascade would tell them that a move that was made was not, or need each of them to learn a new code for a state the report already says. Before the move, nothing is made that a code could misreport. The cascade reads each ancestor's state, so ADR-074 point 5 binds those reads, and gives a board that would not answer the broken tool's code until the act is made. A dry run makes no move, nor does a run declined at the terminal, so each takes that code where the board would not answer for an ancestor's card. A read that places an ancestor in none is no broken tool, and would not have held the move, so it takes none. This record takes the code only where ADR-074 point 5 asks for it: what else a preview may leave unread, such as an ancestor's issue, keeps the code the command gives it. A dry run of an issue already at its target previews a move an earlier run made, so it takes no code for the cascade, as the run would not. The costs: a caller reading only the exit code of a move made learns nothing of an ancestor left behind, and where the board would not answer for an ancestor's card, a dry run exits with a code its run would not take. The warning is in the command's output, as it is for a cascade cut short by a failed write.

### Alternatives considered

- **The move waits: it refuses, writing nothing, where an ancestor its walk will reach cannot be placed.** The case: no move it lets through leaves a container behind the work under it, and the walk can be planned before the move, so the refusal costs no read. Rejected: it holds the work under a container on that container's state, for a position that may not be behind, and it cannot hold where a merge or a close is made before the move.
- **Wait only where the board cannot be read, and go on where a read places the ancestor in none.** The case: a board that would not answer is a broken tool, and a broken tool stops what rests on it, while an outage is likely brief. Rejected: the move rests on its own reads, which the board answered, and nothing the move needs rests on the ancestor's read.
- **Stop the walk at the ancestor, and report where it stopped.** The case: one line names where the walk ended, and a fault that left one ancestor unread likely reaches those above it too. Rejected: the containers above are left behind though each could be read and brought level, and going on costs at most a few reads, bounded by the hierarchy's depth.
- **Stop the walk only where the board cannot be read, and go on past a finding about one ancestor.** The case: an outage likely reaches every card above, and going on sends more calls to a board already failing, with one line in the report for each. Rejected: the calls are bounded by the hierarchy's depth, and a board that failed on one card may answer for the next.
- **Where a later read gets no answer, write from the first read where it placed the ancestor.** The case: what came back says nothing of where the ancestor stands, and the first read did place it, so the cascade could finish in one run through a passing fault. Rejected: the first read is made before the move, and before a person at a terminal confirms it, while a write from it most likely lands, since the move's own write landed moments before. Where another move brought the ancestor level in between, that write moves it back: a Status set behind the work under it with nobody's authorisation, or two state labels on one axis, and on either a journal entry for a step from a state it no longer held (see "Why the cascade writes only from a read made just before the write"). Leaving the ancestor costs a run made again.
- **Write from the first read only where no prompt stood between it and the move,** as where the move is confirmed up front or no person is at a terminal. The case: the first read is then seconds old, not minutes, so a false move back is rarer. Rejected: it keys what the cascade writes to how the command was run, a move under the same ancestor in those seconds makes the same false move, and what it gains is what the move made again gives.
- **Where nothing answers the later read at all, write from the first read,** as where whatever answers it cannot be reached. The case: where that is missing, the cascade would otherwise bring no ancestor level until it is restored. Rejected: the first read is as old whatever left the later read without an answer, so the write carries the same hazard as one made after a board that would not answer, and one rule serves every read that gets no answer.
- **Read again before the write only an ancestor the first read placed behind, and leave one it could not place on that read.** The case: an ancestor the preview names as unplaced is then left, so the preview and the report agree on it. Rejected: an ancestor a brief failure left unplaced at the plan would be left though the cascade could place it by the write (see "Why the later read is made of an ancestor the first could not place").
- **Retry, within the run, a read of an ancestor that got no answer.** The case: a brief outage would pass, and the cascade would finish in one run. Rejected here: a retry belongs in the board's read seam, which every read of the board goes through ([pkit:ADR-053]) and where it would serve every command alike. The later read reads again, just before the write, each ancestor the first read could not place, and running the move again, which the report asks for once the read can be answered, is the retry.
- **Move the ancestor on its labels or its Milestone where the board does not place it.** Rejected by DEC-051 point 6: the cascade would act on a position it guessed.
- **Write In Progress, at the cap, to an ancestor whose card has no Status or that has no card.** The case: no open container stands past In Progress, so the write could never move it backward, and a guess is weakest here, since the value written does not depend on where the ancestor stood. Rejected: the write would follow no transition the schema declares, from a state no read gave, so a journal would record a step from nowhere, and an ancestor that stood in Todo would read as started without having been scheduled, which DEC-006 rejects for containers. A missing card is also the board's membership to repair ([project-management:DEC-019-mandatory-issue-state]), which the cascade does not write. The remedy costs little: set the Status, and the move made again brings the ancestor level.
- **Post a notice on the ancestor, or on the moved issue, so whoever can repair its state learns of it.** The case: the report reaches the mover, often an agent, while the ancestor is often someone else's, and no check finds the container later. Rejected: see "Why nothing is written to the ancestor".
- **Record in the moved issue's journal that its cascade was cut short.** The case: an entry is no comment, so it repeats nowhere, and it outlasts the run's output. Rejected: the journal holds an issue's governed moves ([project-management:DEC-049-audit-journal-model]), and a cascade cut short is no move of the moved issue. Nothing reads such an entry to act on it, it goes stale once a later move brings the ancestor level, and a project that keeps no journal would record nothing.
- **A check, run apart from any move, that lists each container behind the work under it.** The case: it writes nothing and needs no key, so the objections to a notice do not reach it. It finds a container left behind whoever ran the move and whatever became of the report, and it finds containers left behind for other causes too, such as a walk that ended at a parent in doubt. Not taken here, and left to [project-kit#3246](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3246): it checks the whole hierarchy rather than act at one move, and where it runs, what it reads and how it reports are choices of their own.
- **No run takes a code for the cascade, not a dry run nor a run declined at the terminal.** The case: one code for every run whatever the cascade found, and a decline reports the person's choice, not a read. Rejected: neither run makes a move, so ADR-074 point 5 gives a board that would not answer for an ancestor's card the broken tool's code, and taking none would need that point narrowed again, for runs where nothing is made that a code could misreport.
- **A code of its own for a move made with its cascade cut short.** The case: a caller reading only the code would learn of the ancestor left, and ADR-096 point 4 lets a code that asks for a run again stand where a run again makes nothing twice. Rejected: every composing command would have to learn it, or would read it as the move not made, for a state the report says.

## Implications

- **DEC-006 is refined in place on this record's acceptance,** by the edits below, each carrying the pointer "(refinement per [project-management:DEC-070-cascade-unread-ancestor])". The first two narrow its Cascade rule to the ancestors the cascade can read and write. The other three restate as an aim DEC-006's claim that no container stands behind the work under it, where the move back rests on it, and name where a move back can leave a container behind:
  - **In its Cascade rule,** two changes:
    - In the forward-cascade bullet, the sentence "A container is never behind the work under it: when an issue moves forward, … capped at In Progress." reads instead: "No container is to stand behind the work under it: when an issue moves forward, the agent walks its ancestors — its parent, that parent's parent, and so on up — and brings each one it reaches that is behind up to the moved issue's state, capped at In Progress, wherever it can read where that ancestor stands and write to it."
    - After the rule's three bullets, before "Review is a Task's state alone", a paragraph is added: "**Where the forward cascade cannot bring an ancestor level.** The forward cascade moves an ancestor only from where it reads that ancestor to stand just before it writes to it, never from an earlier read. An ancestor it cannot read then, or reads in no state of the lifecycle, is left where it stands, and one whose write is not made is moved no further. The cascade names each ancestor it leaves behind, and the move stands all the same. An ancestor left does not end the walk, which ends at the top, where it cannot tell the next ancestor up, at an issue that is no container, and at one it has passed already. The cascade names any end short of the top."
  - **In its Alternatives considered,** the bullet "Refuse to start a child whose ancestor is unscheduled, or leave the ancestor behind and report it" gains: "Leaving behind an ancestor the walk reaches is rejected wherever the cascade can read where it stands and write to it. One the cascade cannot read just before its write, or reads then in no state of the lifecycle, or whose write is not made, is left and named, and the child's move is never refused for it."
  - **In "Moving back from In Progress", its first paragraph,** "since a container is never behind the work under it" reads instead "since no container is to stand behind the work under it". The landing rule that sentence gives stands.
  - **In "Moving back from In Progress", its last paragraph,** "The move back moves no other issue: a container may stand ahead of the work under it, never behind it." reads instead: "The move back moves no other issue: a container may stand ahead of the work under it, and is not to stand behind it. A move back checks a container's children alone, so where the forward cascade left a child container behind, moving the container back can leave it behind work further down."
  - **In its Rationale, the paragraph on a container moved back over a child,** "Refusing that move keeps the cascade's guarantee" reads instead "Refusing that move keeps to the cascade's aim".
  - **Checked to agree, and standing as written:**

    - **The Implications' open question on a card a person drags by hand,** and whether a cascade reconciles such a move on its own. This record decides only what the cascade does at an ancestor it cannot place, which DEC-051 point 6 forces. A card dragged to an open state is placed, and that question stays open.
- **The narrowing costs a move back something.** A move back keeps a container level with all the work under it only where each child container is level with the work under that child. Where the forward cascade left a child container behind, the container's move back can leave it behind work further down. Where the board carries the state, no move back is made, so the cost does not arise there. It lasts until the child is brought level (point 5). Whether a move back checks every issue under the container is left to [project-kit#3247](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3247).
- **ADR-074 needs no edit.** Its Boundaries leave the question to a project-management record, and this record keeps its point 5, as ADR-096 refines it.
- **This record rests on ADR-096 for one thing:** its narrowing of ADR-074 point 5 once a command's act is made, on which point 6's first paragraph rests.
