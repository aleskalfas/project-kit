title: Each act has one actor, the account its credential acts as, and no comment names another

**In plain terms:** each act the commands record, such as a move or a merge by bypass, has one actor: the account the backbone's reading says the credential acts as ([pkit:ADR-094]). The engine judges that actor on a move, and the journal records it. No comment the commands post names an authoriser beside the account that posts it, or carries an email. Where the reading names no account, the act has no actor, and the commands refuse nothing for want of a name. They make the move, hand it to the engine with no actor, and report each move the engine does not record as a gap in the journal. Where labels carry the state, an audited move made again posts its own comment whether or not the journal recorded the first. The capability runs only on an engine that takes no actor of its own ([pkit:ADR-098]). This holds in every project, under every credential.

## Context

**The terms.**

- **The account the credential acts as** is the one the backbone's reading of the acting account names, a user's or a bot's, in the backbone's one spelling of accounts ([pkit:ADR-094] points 1 to 3).
- **The commands know no acting account** where that reading names no account (ADR-094 point 2): where the service refuses the request for the account as an integration's, answers with an account of a kind unknown, or gives no answer about who the credential is.

**What the records say.**

- **The authoriser a comment names.** A comment names who authorised an act only where they differ from the account that posted it, and the actor is taken for the authoriser ([project-management:DEC-049-audit-journal-model] point 4). [project-management:DEC-052-comment-house-style] keeps the authoriser where another account than the authoriser posts the comment, and its boundary rule keeps a comment from existing to restate its author and post time.
- **The template.** The audit-comment template names the authoriser by name and email, `Bypassed by <name> <<email>>: <reason>` ([project-management:DEC-014-validation-severity-model]), and the comment of an override of a reviewer records whoever overrode by name and email ([project-management:DEC-050-per-reviewer-override] point 4).
- **The audit record.** Every audit record has an actor, "login + display name; email where the source has it", its hidden stamp encodes the record's fields, the default render shows an `actor · timestamp` metadata line, and a project may override the render. The record leaves the default's exact aesthetic unfrozen ([project-management:DEC-044-audit-log-facility] points 1 to 4).
- **Comments that record a person's word.** The override of a refusal by `--force` leaves a comment naming who gave it ([project-management:DEC-055-pr-lists-answers] point 3), and a head a person names at a terminal leaves one naming the commit, the command and who named it ([project-management:DEC-067-heads-that-start-programs] point 4).
- **The actor under a user's credential.** Where the service answers that a credential acts as a user's account, that account is every reader's identity, the actor a move hands the engine among them ([project-management:DEC-021-team-membership-gate], "Why every reader of the invoker's identity takes that login"). Where it does not, DEC-021 leaves the other readers open.
- **The engine's gate.** A gate's authorisation comes from another authority than the actor it gates ([pkit:COR-033] P4). The engine's cross-authority gate compares the actor a command hands it with the account this capability's predicate reports as having merged the pull request.
- **The journal.** `move-issue` and its wrappers delegate the journal to the engine ([project-management:DEC-033-rebind-issue-lifecycle-onto-process-substrate] D5). Where a project keeps one, it holds one entry for each governed move, with its actor, and a move that fails to journal is surfaced, not dropped (DEC-049 point 1). The drift check flags a state change with no entry where labels carry the state, and is skipped where the board or a derivation carries it (DEC-049 point 3).

**What happens today.**

- **The comments.** The commands fill the template's name with the identity the membership gate checks, and its email with git's configured email. Under a user's credential, the name is the posting account's own login, and the email is whatever git's configuration on the machine names.
- **The engine is told after the move.** A command makes its change on the tracker and then hands the move to the engine, whose refusal never fails the move: it leaves a gap in the journal, which the command reports as a move the engine did not record. A move the command does not hand the engine, such as a close the workflow declares no transition for, is reported the same way. The transition from Review to Done has a gate on an authorisation artifact, the merge the landing command makes itself.
- **Where the commands resolve no login,** they hand the engine no actor. The engine asks the service itself, and where the service refuses, as it refuses an installation's token, takes a placeholder name of its own. Its gate passes wherever the account that merged does not bear that name, so an agent's own merge passes it, and the journal records the placeholder as the move's actor.
- **The key of an audit comment.** A retry of an audited move finds the comment it already posted by a key, and the same move made again later posts its own. Where a journal is kept, the key tells the two apart by the journal's count of entries, and where none is, by the issue's state-label events on its timeline where labels carry the state. Where the board or a derivation carries the state and no journal is kept, the key is empty. The hook that fires after a move ([project-management:DEC-024-lifecycle-hooks]) names its occurrence by the same count, so its comment posts again for the same move made again.
- **What leaves a move out of the journal.** The engine refuses a move's entry where a gate of the move's transition refuses, and journals nothing where it cannot be reached. The shipped lifecycle has one gate that confirms another authority, on the move to Done, which no bypass and no move back makes. A project's override of the lifecycle may gate an audited transition so.

## Decision

**Under every credential, each act the commands record has one actor, the account its credential acts as, and no comment names an authoriser beside its poster; where the commands know no acting account, an act has none, and its move reaches the engine with no actor.**

### 1. Each act's actor is the account the credential acts as

- **One actor.** Each act the commands record, a move or another such as a merge by bypass, a refusal overridden by `--force`, an override of a reviewer or a head named at a terminal, has one actor: the account the backbone's reading names the credential acts as.
- **Nothing beside it is the actor:** not `PM_INVOKER_LOGIN`, git's configured email or the person the agent works for, wherever they stand at the membership gate.
- **For a move,** the commands hand that account to the engine's cross-authority gate as the actor, which the gate compares with the account this capability's predicate reports as having merged. The journal records it, where the project keeps one.

Under a user's credential, DEC-021 takes that account for every reader of the invoker's identity, so this point adds there only what point 2 does.

### 2. No comment names an authoriser beside its poster, or carries an email

- **No authoriser beside the poster.** An audit comment's authoriser is its act's actor, kept only where the poster is someone else (DEC-049 point 4). The account the credential acts as posts the comment, so no comment names an authoriser beside it.
- **Who gave a word.** The comment of an override by `--force` names who gave it by its poster: `--force` is given by whoever runs the command, the account that posts the comment, which the service shows beside it (DEC-055 point 3). The comment of a head named at a terminal names no person as having named the head (DEC-067 point 4): the account that posts it, which the service shows beside it, is the act's actor.
- **No email,** in a comment's visible text or in its hidden stamp. An audit record's actor is the account, in the one spelling, with no display name, since the reading gives none (ADR-094 point 1).
- **A reason the caller gives** may report a person's word, and stays the caller's report.
- **The default render** shows no actor on its metadata line, since the account that posts a comment is its actor, and the service shows that account beside the comment. A project's own template stays its own (DEC-044 point 3).

### 3. Where the commands know no acting account, an act names no actor

- **An empty actor.** The audit record of such an act holds no login or email, and its comment names no authoriser.
- **The move, with no actor.** The command hands the engine the move with no actor. The engine takes only the actor its caller names, and none by default ([pkit:ADR-098] point 1), so it records the move, or refuses its entry, as it judges a move no actor is named for. The capability's floor on the backbone is the release that brings that engine, so no engine the capability runs on takes an actor of its own.
- **Each move the engine does not record** the command reports, as it reports one the engine refused. Where the project keeps a journal, each is a gap there. Where labels carry the state, the drift check shows it (DEC-049 point 3). Where the board or a derivation carries the state, the check is skipped, and the command's report is the only trace.
- **Nothing refused for want of an actor.** A merge by bypass, an authorisation to merge given up front, the move to Done after a merge, and the record of a move to Done after a merge already made each pass as they do elsewhere, and each move among them reaches the engine as this point says.

### 4. One comment for each audited move, journaled or not

Under every credential, an audited move made again posts its own comment wherever the tracker gives the commands a count of the moves made, whether or not the journal recorded the first.

- **Where labels carry the state,** the key by which a retry finds its own audit comment counts the moves made, journaled or not, from the state-label events on the issue's timeline. So a move made again after one the journal missed posts its own comment (DEC-049 point 4). The journal misses an audited move where the engine refuses its entry, as under a project's override that gates an audited transition by another authority, or where the engine cannot be reached (Context).
- **The hook's occurrence** counts by the same key, so the comment of the hook that fires after a move posts again for a move made again after one the journal missed.
- **Where the board or a derivation carries the state,** the tracker gives the commands no such count. There the key and the hook's occurrence count the journal's entries where a journal is kept, and are empty where none is. So the same audited move made again posts its own comment, and the hook its own, only where the journal recorded a move between the two. That is a limit which stands today, and this record leaves it, until a count of the moves made is found there.

## Rationale

**Why the actor is the account the credential acts as.** COR-033 P4 asks that an authorisation come from another authority than the actor. The predicate reports who merged the pull request as an account, so the comparison means something only against an account, and the account that makes the move is the one its credential acts as. A name in its place, from the variable or git's email, makes an agent's own merge read as another authority's. Naming the account keeps the journal in step with the service's own record of who wrote each change, and gives the journal and the comments one actor, as DEC-049 has the comments project the journal (points 1 and 2).

**Why no authoriser beside the poster, under every credential.** Under a user's credential, the template's name is the posting account's own login, as DEC-021 takes it: a restatement, which DEC-049 point 4 and DEC-052's boundary rule already leave off a comment. The email is a setting of the machine, made by its user or its setup, which may name anyone (DEC-021, "Why a user's login the hosting service answers stands alone"), so beside the posting account it may name another person, the mixed stamp DEC-021 names as a cost. Under a credential that names no user's account, any name beside the poster comes from the caller's word, and reads as the authoriser of an act the service records as the poster's. That holds for a person at a terminal too: a name beside the posting account would be what the session says of them, never what the service records. Whoever gives `--force` runs the command, so the posting account is who gave it, and DEC-055 point 3's record of them holds through the poster. Dropping both loses nothing the commands can vouch for, and keeps whether a comment names someone beside its poster from turning on which credential a setup holds. An email kept only in the hidden stamp is posted all the same, and whoever reads the comment's source reads it. The display name goes with them, since the reading gives none, and a name looked up beside the account would be a second reading of who acts.

**Why the move reaches the engine with no actor.** An engine that takes an actor of its own enters a name the commands never knew, which reads as an account, passes its gate on any merge, the agent's own among them, and stands in the journal as the move's actor (Context). An engine that takes only the actor its caller names judges the move as one nobody named, so handing it the move loses nothing the commands can vouch for, and the journal misses only the moves that engine does not record. A floor on the backbone settles which engine the commands run on once, so no command reads which engine it has. A move left unrecorded leaves what the gate's refusal leaves on an agent's own merge, a gap in the journal, and the gap is surfaced, as DEC-049 point 1 asks of a move that fails to journal. Nothing is refused, so what passes on the caller's word, such as a bypass or an authorisation given up front, passes as it does elsewhere. A refusal would go further than the gate it stood in for, which refuses only the entry, never the move, and where the service gives no login, no second run would mend it.

**Why the key counts moves made, and why in a point of its own.** A key that counts the journal's entries stays put while a move goes unrecorded. The same audited move made again would then render the comment already posted, and post none, which breaks DEC-049 point 4's one comment per audited mutation. In the shipped lifecycle, no actor's want leaves an audited move out of the journal, since its one gate that confirms another authority is on a move no bypass reaches. What leaves one out is a project's override that gates an audited transition so, and an engine that refuses the entry or cannot be reached, under any credential. So the rule binds under every credential, not only where the commands know no acting account. The timeline gives the count where labels carry the state, and the board gives none the commands read today. The hook's occurrence counts by the same key, so its comment and the audit comment post again alike.

**What it costs.** The journal misses the moves the engine does not record, such as a move through a gate that must confirm another authority. In a project that keeps a journal, the tracker's timeline, with the service's record of who wrote each change, is then their only trail. Where the board or a derivation carries the state, nothing but the command's report shows the gap. Under a credential that is not the person's own, the comment of a head named at a terminal records nobody who named it, which DEC-067 point 4 had it record: the service records the posting account, and the reason the caller gives may report the person's word. And a project upgrades the backbone to the release whose engine takes no actor of its own before it takes this capability's release that builds point 3.

**Why an empty actor, and not a placeholder.** A placeholder reads as an account, can match one of that name, and records a name the commands never knew. An empty actor grants nothing, and the service records which account posted each comment.

### Alternatives considered

- **Each act's actor only under a credential that names no user's account,** leaving the name and the email the template gives a comment under a user's credential. The case: no comment changes in a project that runs no agent under such a credential. Rejected: the name there restates the poster, the email may name another person, and whether a comment names someone beside its poster would turn on the setup's credential.
- **Keep the email in the hidden stamp only.** The case: tooling keeps a field DEC-044 point 1 gives, and no reader of the rendered comment sees it. Rejected: the stamp is posted with the comment, and names the same setting of the machine.
- **Let the engine take its own actor where the commands know none.** The case: nothing for the commands to decide. Rejected: an engine's own choice reads as an account and passes its gate on any merge (Context).
- **Hand the engine no move where the commands know no acting account.** The case: one rule, which asks nothing of the engine. Rejected: an engine that takes only the actor its caller names records each move none of whose gates compares the actor, and the journal would miss all of them for no guard gained.
- **Key the rule to what the engine takes,** handing no move to an engine that would take an actor of its own. The case: the capability runs on either engine. Not taken: each command would read which engine it runs on, and a floor on the backbone settles that once.
- **A placeholder in place of an empty actor,** such as `unknown`. Rejected: it reads as an account (Rationale).
- **Refuse a move whose transition's gate needs an actor, where the commands know none,** such as the move to Done. The case: no move goes past the gate. Rejected: the gate refuses only the journal entry, never the move, so the refusal would go further than the gate, stop a bypass, an authorisation given up front and a merge a person approved, and no second run would mend it where the service gives no login.
- **Refuse every act that leaves an audit record where the commands know no acting account,** so that every record keeps an actor. Rejected: it would stop every bypass and every audited override under a credential whose login the service does not give, and wherever a lookup fails, for want of a name the commands never knew.
- **Keep who named a head beside the posting account,** in the comment of a head named at a terminal. The case: DEC-067 point 4 keeps its record of the person. Rejected: under a credential that is not the person's own, the name would be the session's word, read as the authoriser of an act the service records as another account's (Rationale).

## Implications

- **What changes in every project.**
  - Each audit comment the commands render loses the name and the email the template gives its authoriser, and carries no email in its stamp. An audit record's actor loses its display name and its email. The default render's metadata line shows no actor.
  - The comment of a head named at a terminal names no person as having named the head. Under a credential that is not the person's own, it records nobody who named it.
  - Where labels carry the state, the key of an audited move's comment, and the occurrence of the hook that fires after a move, count the moves made, journaled or not.
  - A run under a person's own credential that the service gives no answer for, where a command still acts, hands its moves to the engine as point 3 has it, and its audit records' actor is empty rather than taken from git's configured email.
  - Under a user's credential with `PM_INVOKER_LOGIN` set to another account, DEC-021 has the commands hand the engine, record and name the account the credential acts as (DEC-021, "Every reader of the invoker's identity"), so nothing else changes there.
