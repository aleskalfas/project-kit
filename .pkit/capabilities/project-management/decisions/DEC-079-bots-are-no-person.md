**In plain terms:** in every project, under every credential, a bot's account stands for no person: an app's account, and any other the backbone's one spelling of accounts reads as a bot's ([pkit:ADR-094]). No command makes it an assignee or hands it a Task, since an assignee names a person, and a command makes a bare login an assignee only where the hosting service answers that it names a user's account, refusing with the cause where the service gives no such answer. No claim on work a bot writes counts wherever the claim names who wrote it. In human mode, no approval counts from a bot's account or from an account the project registers as a reviewer agent's, nor as an approving review that carries a reviewer agent's verdict marker, and neither is asked for a review. A bot's request for changes still holds a merge until a person approves after it. Where that spelling cannot tell an account's kind, as for a bot whose login the service gives with no `[bot]` suffix, its claim is held, void only against a user of another login, and its approval counts for nothing. What agent mode counts beside a reviewer agent's verdict, this record leaves to an issue of its own.

## Context

**The terms.**

- **The backbone's spelling of accounts** is its one function that puts an account any surface names into one spelling that carries the account's kind, and compares accounts ([pkit:ADR-094] point 3).
- **A bot's account** is one that spelling reads as of the bot kind. An app's account is one.
- **An account of a kind unknown** is one that spelling reads as neither a user's nor a bot's, such as an account the service records as a bot's but gives with no `[bot]` suffix.
- **A reviewer agent's account** is one the project registers in the remote path's list ([project-management:DEC-028-agent-as-approver-paths]). It may be a user's account kept for an agent, which the service records as no bot.
- **The verdict marker** is the hidden line that stamps a reviewer agent's verdict for `done-work`'s gate, in each form DEC-028's "The verdict signal" gives it: bare, `<!-- pkit-verdict -->`, or naming the head the reviewer reviewed, with the base it reviewed it against where known, `<!-- pkit-verdict sha=<oid> base=<oid> -->`.

**What the records say.**

- **Assignment names a person.** Every issue has an assignee, someone responsible, in closed mode a member and in open mode any account ([project-management:DEC-019-mandatory-issue-state], "Mandatory assignment"). Its checks at filing are `hard-reject` findings of [project-management:DEC-014-validation-severity-model]'s, which the `--force` of [project-management:DEC-046-override-flag-convention] overrides.
- **A claim on an issue of work counts only from the assignee's account** ([project-management:DEC-043-ownership-substrate-selection] point 3), a filter the ownership fold applies in one place ([pkit:ADR-041] point 2). A claim on a state issue counts by its author's standing ([pkit:ADR-071] point 3), which this record leaves as it stands.
- **In human mode,** the gate passes on an approving review or an `Approved` comment from any non-author identity, and the members whose role matches, but the author, are assigned as reviewers ([project-management:DEC-027-review-modes], "Human path"). [project-management:DEC-026-work-ownership-lifecycle]'s tables count the comment from a commenter other than the author, and its Rationale ("Why the three-way OR") from the last commenter that is no bot.
- **In agent mode, the records disagree** on what counts beside a registered reviewer agent's fresh `APPROVED` verdict. DEC-028's Decision adds the verdict as a fourth path and keeps the other three open there: an approving review, an `Approved` comment "from non-author human", for which it gives no test, and a bypass. DEC-026's two `done-work` rows, DEC-027's "Two modes" table and DEC-028's own Implications give agent mode only the verdict and a bypass.
- **The native review.** `review-pr` delivers each verdict as a native review too, "with the same verdict body", and DEC-028 notes that the gate accepts a native approving review through the human path ("Native-only is deferred"). In human mode, it says, a verdict does not gate ("`review-pr` in `human` mode").

**What happens today.**

- **Approvals in human mode.** The commands count an `Approved` comment from any commenter but the author, a bot among them. Of the reviews, they read DEC-026's "latest APPROVED review" as the latest review that approves, requests changes or was dismissed, and pass where it approves.
- **Approvals in agent mode.** The commands count a registered reviewer agent's verdict and a bypass, as DEC-026, DEC-027 and DEC-028's Implications have it, and neither an approving review nor an `Approved` comment, which DEC-028's Decision keeps open there.
- **A verdict as a native review.** `review-pr` posts the native review under the credential it runs with, and its body is the verdict comment, the verdict marker among it. So on the local path in human mode, an agent's `APPROVED` reads as an approving review by whoever ran the command.
- **Assignees.** A command takes an assignee named in the call as it is given, and the service decides whether that account can be assigned.

**What was measured.** On a trial where an agent ran under an App installation's token ([project-kit#3164](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3164)), the comments the App wrote, read under a person's credential, gave its login with a `[bot]` suffix and the type `Bot`. gh's JSON gave the same account on its pull requests with an `app/` prefix, marked as a bot. The service also lets an app take its owner's own name, and lets some bots be assigned, as it lets its own coding agent be. Whether the field the service declares to hold an issue's assignees, as users' accounts, gives such a bot is not measured ([project-kit#3171](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3171)).

**What the service documents.** Looking an account up by its login works with an App installation's token and needs no permission. It answers the account, or that it found none ([GitHub, "REST API endpoints for users", "Get a user"](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/users/users#get-a-user)). A workflow's own token is an App installation's token ([GitHub, "GITHUB_TOKEN"](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/concepts/security/github_token)). The lookup answers that it found none, too, for an enterprise's managed user, or an app installed in an organization that uses them, where the credential has no access to that organization. The lookup is not measured under either token (#3171).

## Decision

**In every project, a bot's account stands for no person: no command makes it an assignee, no claim on work that names its author counts from it, and in human mode no approval counts from it or from a reviewer agent's account.**

Which account is a bot's, and whether two accounts are one, the commands read as the backbone's spelling of accounts has it (ADR-094 point 3), in each rule below.

### 1. An assignee is never a bot's account

- **No bot as an assignee.** No command makes a bot's account an assignee, at filing, on an assignment or by handing a Task to it, whether or not a member's entry names it, since assignment names a person (DEC-019). `handoff-issue` hands a Task from one person to another.
- **The form of an assignee.** An assignee a command is given, in the call, by `--me` or by default, is a value that stands for an account, read by its form (ADR-094 point 3). A bare login names a user's account, `<name>[bot]` and `app/<name>` a bot's, and a string with no spelling names none.
- **A bare login, on the service's word.** A command makes a bare login an assignee only where the service answers that it names a user's account. Where the login is the acting account the backbone's reading named as a user's, that reading is the answer. Otherwise the command looks the login up with the service, as `add-member` does (ADR-094 point 3), once for each assignee it names, in a command that already reaches the service. It refuses a bot's spelling, and a string with no spelling, without asking.
- **What the lookup answers.** The command refuses where the lookup answers an account of another kind, such as a bot's whose login carries no `[bot]` suffix or an organization's, or answers that no account has the login. It refuses too where the lookup gives no answer, such as for a rate limit, a server error, a refusal, a timeout or no network, and where a host other than the service's public one answers that it found none, since such a host may hide an account from the credential. Each refusal names the login and its cause. On the public host, that tells a login mistyped from a lookup that failed, which passes once the service answers and the command is run again. On another host, a login mistyped and an account hidden read alike, and a second run changes neither: a person checks the login, or runs the command under a credential that sees the account.
- **No override.** These refusals are preconditions of their commands, never a `hard-reject` finding of DEC-014's, so the `--force` DEC-046 gives such a finding does not reach them.
- **Where the lookup misreads.** Where the backbone's measurement (#3171) finds the lookup giving a bot the service lets be assigned a user's type, the lookup keeps that bot out of no assignment. Its claims still count or not by point 2, which reads the author's kind from the claim's own record. A record of its own then decides how a bare assignee is confirmed, as ADR-094 point 1 has it for `add-member`'s lookup.

### 2. A claim on work never counts from a bot's account

- **Never from a bot's.** A claim on an issue of work counts only from the assignee's account, and never from a bot's account, even one the service lets be assigned. The guard reads the author's kind, never whether the service lets that author be assigned.
- **A kind unknown.** A claim whose author is of a kind unknown is one the ownership fold cannot judge. It is held, neither counted nor freed, unless the assignee is a user's account of another login, once the `[bot]` suffix and gh's `app/` prefix are set aside, against which it is void (ADR-094 point 3). So a claim from an account the service records as a bot's but gives with no suffix is void on a person's Task of another login. It is held on a Task whose assignee bears its login, or is of a kind unknown too.
- **Labels.** A label names no author, so under the label substrate the guard has nothing to read, and a label counts as [project-management:DEC-035-instance-ownership] has it.

### 3. Only a person's approval counts as a person's

- **Where this holds:** in human mode, wherever the gate counts an approving review or an `Approved` comment as a person's approval. In agent mode this point decides nothing. Whether the gate counts a person's approval there beside a reviewer agent's verdict, as DEC-028's Decision says and DEC-026, DEC-027 and DEC-028's Implications deny (Context), is left to [project-kit#3395](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3395), filed for it.
- **Who approves.** An `Approved` comment or an approving review counts only from an account the spelling reads as a user's, that is another account than the pull request's author, and that the project does not register as a reviewer agent's. So an approval from a bot's account counts for nothing, nor does one from an account of a kind unknown, or from one the comparison cannot tell from the author.
- **No verdict as a person's approval.** An approving review whose body carries the verdict marker, in any of its forms, counts for nothing as a person's approval, whoever posted it. It is a reviewer agent's verdict, which `review-pr` delivers as a native review too, and in human mode a verdict does not gate (DEC-028, "`review-pr` in `human` mode").
- **The order of reviews.** The latest review that approves, requests changes or was dismissed decides, as the commands read DEC-026's "latest APPROVED review", and it passes only where it approves. An approval that does not count is left out of that order. So a bot's request for changes, or a reviewer agent's delivered as a native review, holds the merge until a person approves after it, as a person's request does, and a bot's approval clears nothing.
- **Who is asked.** Human mode assigns as a reviewer no member whose entry names a bot's account or a reviewer agent's account. It leaves out the author, and any member the comparison cannot tell from the author.
- **An entry with no spelling.** An entry of the remote path's list with no spelling, such as `@bot`, leaves the comparison unable to tell any account from it (ADR-094 point 3). So until a person mends it, no approval counts as a person's and human mode asks no member for a review. The refusal names the entry, and why it names no account.

## Rationale

**Why an assignee is never a bot's account.** An assignee says who is responsible for the work (DEC-019). A bot answers for nothing it does: the person who set it to work does. Assigned to a bot, a Task names nobody responsible, and the bot's claims on it count for nothing (point 2).

**Why the service confirms a bare login.** A bare login names a user's account by its form, but the service lets some bots be assigned by a login with no suffix, as its own coding agent. Read by its form alone, such a bot would be made an assignee through the commands. The lookup reads the kind the service records, at one request for each assignee named, in a command that already reaches the service. The service documents the lookup as open to an App installation's token, a workflow's among them, so a command under such a credential confirms an assignee as any other does.

**Why a lookup that fails refuses.** A failed lookup says nothing of the account, so assigning on it would make the service's coding agent an assignee whenever the lookup times out. Refused with its cause, the assignment waits for a second run, which passes once the service answers. What that costs:

- **Where a token is refused the lookup,** against the service's reference, no bare login but the acting account a reading named as a user's could be assigned under it. Under an app's credential, every filing that names a bare assignee would refuse, the default assignee of the person the agent works for among them. The measurement tells (#3171).
- **On an enterprise's own host** that hides accounts from the credential, each assignment of an account it hides refuses until a credential that sees the account runs the command.

**Why the guards read an author's kind.** A claim means something because only the assignee's own account can make it (DEC-043 point 3). A guard derived from the service refusing a bot as an assignee would fail once a bot can be assigned. Read from the author's kind, the guard holds for every bot, whatever the service lets it be, and alike on every machine, since an author's kind is the same wherever it is read.

**Why an account of a kind unknown is held, not counted as a bot's.** The backbone's spelling leaves a bot whose login carries no suffix of a kind unknown, since nothing measured says every surface gives it one login (ADR-094 point 3). This record follows that spelling, so such an account's claim is held, as the fold holds every claim it cannot judge, wherever the assignee could be that account. That hold no reader can judge, and the clash guard reads it as a claim that counts (DEC-035 point 6), until a person assigns the Task to another or removes the comment. A claim such an account writes on a person's Task is void, as any other account's is, since the person's login tells the two apart (ADR-094 point 3). Counted as a bot's instead, the claim would free a Task whose claim no reader can tell from its assignee's, on a reading no measurement backs.

**Why a bot approves nothing as a person.** A person's approval is what human mode asks for. DEC-026's Rationale already counts the comment only from a commenter that is no bot, and reading the approver's kind brings DEC-026's tables, DEC-027 and the commands to that rule, and applies it to reviews too. A request for changes stays in the order, since holding a merge back grants nothing, and a person's later approval clears it as it clears a person's request.

**Why agent mode is left out.** The accepted records disagree on whether agent mode counts a person's approval beside the verdict, and the commands take one side (Context). Settling it either edits DEC-028's Decision or builds paths the commands lack, a choice this record's reasons do not reach, so it stays with #3395. This record's tests read where every record agrees that a person's approval counts: human mode.

**Why a review that carries the verdict marker counts for nothing as a person's.** `review-pr` posts its native review under the credential that runs it, so on the local path an agent's approval would read as an approving review by that credential's account, which is neither registered as a reviewer agent's nor the author. The marker says the review is a verdict, which DEC-028 judges as a verdict, by its reviewer and its freshness, and which in human mode does not gate (DEC-028, "`review-pr` in `human` mode"). Counted as a person's, a verdict would gate there after all, and gone stale would still pass. The marker, in each of its forms, is a test that reads no account. A person who pastes a verdict, marker and all, into an approving review of their own counts for nothing until they approve without it.

**Why a reviewer agent's account approves nothing, and is not asked.** A reviewer agent's account gives no person's approval, whatever the service records of it, and the project already names those accounts (DEC-028). Asked for a review, it would be asked for an approval that cannot count. This record decides only its approvals and requests for review. Where the service records it as a user's account, it may be an assignee and its claims count, as any user's account's do (points 1 and 2), since those rules read the kind the service records, and registering it says only that its approval is an agent's. This assumes a project registers no person's own account as a reviewer agent's. A maintainer whose own account runs the remote reviewer, such as through their own token, loses their approvals in human mode, so such a project registers an account of the agent's own. An account kept for an agent that the project does not register is a user's account, so its approval counts in human mode: registering it is what tells it from a person's.

**Why an entry with no spelling stops every approval.** Such an entry is most likely a login mistyped, and the comparison cannot tell any account from it (ADR-094 point 3). Read as naming no account, it would let the reviewer agent it was meant to name approve as a person. Read as *cannot tell*, it grants nothing, and the refusal names it, so a person mends it. What that costs is every human-mode approval until then.

**What it costs a project whose approvals came from a bot.** A project that approves in human mode by a bot, such as a workflow that approves under its own token, stops passing `done-work`'s gate there until a person approves. A project that runs `review-pr` in human mode no longer passes by the native review it delivers.

**What it costs a project that assigns work to a bot,** such as the service's own coding agent. The commands refuse to assign it (point 1), so the project assigns it on the service's own surface. What the commands then read of the Task turns on whether the field of an issue's assignees gives that bot, which is not measured (Context):

- **Where it does not,** the commands read the Task as unassigned, `start-work` assigns whoever starts it, and the bot's claim on it is void, since that person's login tells the two apart.
- **Where it does,** that field gives a bot's account a user's kind. The backbone then reads no user's kind from it until a record decides how it is read (ADR-094 point 1, "Other kinds"), and that reaches every project's reads of assignees, people's among them: a claim by a person, set against an assignee of a kind unknown with the same login, is held rather than counted (point 2). That record weighs this record's costs again, beside ADR-094's reading of DEC-026's `start-work` row, which rests on the field (#3171).

### Alternatives considered

- **Assign a bot's account where the service lets it be assigned.** The case: a project hands work to the service's coding agent through the commands. Rejected: the assignee would name nobody responsible, and the bot's claims would count for nothing.
- **Read a bare assignee by its form alone,** with no lookup. The case: no request beyond those the command makes. Rejected: a bot whose login carries no suffix, such as the service's coding agent, would be made an assignee through the commands, in open mode, and in closed mode wherever a member's bare entry names it.
- **Assign a bare login where the lookup fails.** The case: a filing never waits on the service. Rejected: a failure says nothing of the account, and the service's coding agent would be assigned whenever the lookup fails.
- **Drop every review by a bot in human mode,** requests for changes included. The case: one rule for every bot's review. Rejected: a bot's request for changes would stop holding a merge, which loosens a guard for no gain.
- **`review-pr` posts no native approving review in human mode.** The case: no verdict reaches the gate as a review. Not taken: the review's body already carries the marker, so one test at the gate refuses it, whoever runs `review-pr` and whatever version, and the native review still shows the verdict on the pull request.
- **Treat only the accounts the service records as bots as no person.** The case: one source for an account's kind. Rejected: a reviewer agent's user account would then approve in human mode.
- **Count a bot's claim for its assignee,** loosening DEC-043 point 3. The case: a clone under a bot's credential could take part in instance ownership. Rejected: anyone holding the credential could claim work for any person.
- **Read an account the service records as a bot's but gives with no suffix as a bot's.** The case: its claim does not count, so it holds nothing. Not taken: the backbone's spelling leaves its kind unknown, its spelling across surfaces unmeasured, and the hold costs only where such an account writes a claim on a Task whose assignee could be that account.

And DEC-028 already has a verdict not gate in human mode ("`review-pr` in `human` mode"), so the marker brings the gate to what DEC-028 states, rather than taking away a path it gives. The changeset of the change that builds point 3 names both, so the release's notes reach a project at its upgrade.
