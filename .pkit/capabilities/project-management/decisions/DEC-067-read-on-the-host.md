**In plain terms:** a person who merges outside the landing command is owed what that command prints. That is the author's sentences, what the change leaves out of date, what a delegate decided, and what the merge would leave answered apart. The other merge commands print those lines, and printing them starts nothing the pull request's head holds. Where they cannot derive them, and at the host's button, the person reads them on the host.

The description holds the author's sentences and the delegate's statement. What the change leaves out of date is in the answer files of its diff, at every head. The change check's run on the host reads it out, where one stands. So no comment and no new list is written, and reading starts nothing on the reader's machine.

Three core records change, and a person must accept that. COR-050 says the person is told, and nothing tells a person who merges past every command. So COR-050 point 3 gains what telling is there. COR-062 and COR-064, which tell through it, each gain a sentence for what that cannot carry.

## Context

The landing command prints what the person who authorises the merge is told ([project-management:DEC-065-answers-read-from-check] point 4). The other merge commands and the host's button print nothing, so the person there runs the view. Its lines name no head.

The view starts no program of the pull request's head away from a clean checkout at it. So one anchor that needs such a program stops the view on every change. It then says only that a checkout reads the answers (DEC-065 point 2).

DEC-065 point 6 says the person there is told only the sentences, so that two tellings fall short. A third falls short with them, which it does not name:
- **What is left out of date.** The person who authorises the merge is told each sentence. They are told which artefacts and link targets are left out of date, until which follow-up ([COR-050](../../../decisions/core/COR-050-anchors-and-friction.md) point 3).
- **What a delegate decided.** The person learns the artefact, the outcome and the words, and that the delegate decided it. An answer it declined reaches them too ([COR-064](../../../decisions/core/COR-064-answer-delegate.md) point 5).
- **What the merge would leave answered apart.** The person is told of each live revalidation on the base's tip that the change's answer did not read ([COR-062](../../../decisions/core/COR-062-answers-in-own-files.md) point 7).

DEC-065 weighed the other merge commands printing these lines. It set that aside for the cost of a run in a checkout of the head, and left the route to be weighed again. The view derives the same lines with no such checkout.

DEC-065 point 6 knows that the answer files can be read in the diff, and it still calls the telling short there. Three things are on the host already:
- **The description.** This capability has an author's sentence stand there, as COR-050 point 3 asks. Its delegate also says there each answer it decided and each it refused.
- **The answer files.** A change's answers are files it adds, so each deferral's words are in its diff (COR-062 point 1).
- **The change check's run.** The check reports each answer the change wrote, with its words, and each deferral it kept (COR-050 point 12). Many projects run it on their pull requests.

DEC-065 took out of the description a list of every answer. The list outgrew what the host keeps and brought stops of its own, and the reviewer never worked from it. Most of its entries said only "still accurate". So the question here is how little must be added to what is already there.

## Decision

**The other merge commands print what the landing command prints, derived with no checkout of the head, and printing it starts nothing the head holds. Where no command tells the person, they read it on the host. The host holds it in the description, the answer files of the diff, and the change check's run where one stands at the head.**

**A printout names the head it was derived at, and says where to read when it holds no answer. No comment and no new list is written. COR-050 point 3 says what telling is where no command tells, and COR-062 and COR-064 each gain a sentence to match.**

### 1. What a person is owed outside the landing command

- **The author's sentences** on what changed in each updated document.
- **What the change leaves out of date:** each document and link target, with the words that name its follow-up.
- **What a delegate decided,** with its outcome and words, and that the delegate decided it.
- **What a delegate declined,** with why.
- **What the merge would leave answered apart:** each revalidation on the base's tip that the change's answer did not read.

The landing command's other lines are a friction setting, a change to the fixers' grant list and the unread count. They come with a printout, and no core record owes them. A change to the delegate's name is another question, which [project-kit#2122](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2122) tracks. Whatever line it adds belongs in the printout, so the other merge commands carry it too.

### 2. The other merge commands print the lines

- **What they print.** They print what the landing command prints (DEC-065 point 4). They derive it at the head with no checkout of it, as the view does.
- **What they start.** Printing the lines starts nothing the head holds, wherever they run. The view starts a head's programs at a clean checkout at that head, and a merge command does not. There too it has the change check refuse an anchor that needs such a program. A command's own files are its checkout's, as for any command, and this record changes nothing there.
- **When.** They print before they ask, and before they refuse a merge nobody can authorise. A merge authorised up front prints as it merges.
- **Where they cannot derive.** They say why, and where to read (point 4). The merge goes on, since neither result stops it.
- **What stays.** Their gates stay as they are, and they write nothing. A merge the landing command hands on prints once.

### 3. Where no command tells, the person reads on the host

No command stands at the host's button, and a head whose anchors need its programs prints no lines. There the person reads. Each place is on the host, so a reader needs no clone and starts nothing.

- **The description** holds the sentences and the delegate's statement. A delegate says with the change each answer it decided and each it declined (COR-064 point 5), and on this capability's projects that is here. Neither is derived at a head, so neither names one.
- **The answer files in the diff** hold what is left out of date, on every project and at every head the host shows. Each deferral is there in its own words, kept ones and link targets' included.
- **The change check's run,** where one stands at this head, reads those lines out. It also flags each answer not asked for, and reports what the merge would leave answered apart. The host shows a run against the commit it ran for. Where a run stands at no head, such as one a fork's approval holds back, the reader uses the diff.

An agent that merges for a person points them to these places, and says that it read nothing. It does not read the answer files out, since that would be its own account, the case DEC-055's Context feared.

### 4. A printout names its head and says where to read

- **The head.** The lines of a merge command, and of the view, open with the head they were derived at.
- **No answer.** Where either prints no answer, for any reason, it says where to read with nothing started. That is the description, the diff, and the check's run where the project has one. The view keeps what it says of a checkout at the head.

### 5. What this asks of the core records

Three core records change, and a person accepts that with this record. Only COR-050's edit changes what telling is.

- **COR-050.** What telling is lives in its point 3, so the edit goes there, where every project reads it. The point gains what telling is where no component tells, and a component that may show the list shows it where it can. Without this, a merge at the host's button fails that point's letter unless something shows the lines unasked. That something would be a comment, so this edit is the price of refusing one.
- **COR-062.** The answered-apart report has no place that every project holds, since the diff cannot show it. So point 7 says where it is first read when nothing with the change holds it.
- **COR-064.** Its point 5 tells through COR-050 point 3, so it takes the new words. They cannot carry who decided, which no check knows, or a declined answer, which no file holds. So point 5 gains the delegate's own duty to say both with the change. Any agent a person names then owes it, not only this capability's.
- **Each component's duty** in those records' Implications gains "where it can", so a merge command that cannot derive breaks none of them.

### 6. What this gives up

- **A person at the host's button who does not look** learns nothing, and nothing shows whether they looked. A comment above the button would put the lines in their way, and this record gives that up to write nothing.
- **A project with no component that carries out merges** has every merge read this way, so telling there is reading alone.
- **A merge authorised up front** prints as it merges, so a person who authorised first reads afterwards. An agent tells them what the view prints before it asks, as the pm skill has it. The landing command's two holds on such a merge do not reach these commands.
- **Each merge through the two commands** pays for one pass of the check.
- **The diff reports more than is live.** An answer a later round replaced, a file put back and a moved answer all show as added. That error is the safe one.
- **The diff reports less than is stale.** A document left stale with no answer, in warning mode, is in no answer file. So "left out of date" means what the change declares, and the gap is widest on a project with no run.
- **A deferral in the diff** sits among every other answer, in a file written for the check.
- **What the merge would leave answered apart** reaches nobody before the merge where there is no printout and no run. The whole-repository check reports it as debt afterwards.
- **A run** can be long, and it lives in the host's log, which the host may not keep for ever. It holds every answer, not only what is owed.
- **Whether an answer was asked for** cannot be read in the diff. A run flags it, and a reviewer of the answers may name each such answer in its verdict.
- **A delegate's statement** is the delegate's own. The reviewer checks each answer it decided, and nothing checks that the statement names them all. A later head can also leave it behind. A reader of a run can compare it with the answers flagged not asked for.

## Rationale

**Why the commands print.** They are this capability's own, and COR-050 point 3 asks that the person be told. The lines cost them one pass of the check, with no write and no new place. Against that stands a core record narrowed for a path where a command can tell. A core record should give way only for what no command can do.

**Why that is not the removed list.** The list was a copy kept in the description, repeating every answer. A printout keeps nothing and holds only what is owed, and the landing command already builds it. An agent that merges off a terminal reads the same lines, and relays them.

**Why nothing new is written.** The list showed what a second copy of the answers comes to. The answer files and the run exist for those who keep the documents consistent, and the sentences for the reviewer. A person reads them beside those agents, at no further write. The delegate's statement stands already, and it names only what no file says: who decided.

**Why the diff is the floor.** It is the one place on every project and at every head. It gives the answer's own words, with no agent between them and the reader. It holds kept deferrals too, so they need no ruling. A comment or a verdict would be a second account of it, and a second account can fall behind.

**Why reading counts as being told where no command tells.** The honest trade is a comment in the way against no write at all. A comment would sit above the button and notify, which the diff does not. But it needs a write on each head, for lines no agent reads, and a head with no comment would read as nothing owed. A person who looks finds the words.

**Why the core records change.** What telling means is COR-050's to say, so the edit is made there and not left in a capability's record. COR-062 and COR-064 tell through that point, and what they tell of is partly out of its reach. So each says what it does there, and neither is left promising what nothing delivers.

**Why COR-066's fallback does not fit.** COR-066 has the change's author show a list where no component does. That list is a check's output, which the change does not hold. A deferral's words are in the change already, so an author's copy would add nothing and could go stale. The answered-apart report is a check's output too, but it is read against a base's tip that moves after any author writes.

**Why the delegate's telling is not the check's.** The check cannot tell who decided an answer, and COR-064 rejects a field that would. So no line derived from the check can say "the delegate decided this". That statement never passed through the view, so the view's limit never touched it.

**Why the lines start nothing, even at the head.** A reader who goes to a clean checkout at the head has made that choice, as DEC-065 has it. A merge is no such choice, and an agent may merge with nobody watching. So the check refuses for a merge command there too, and every place in point 3 is read on the host. This record therefore rests on nothing about which heads a command may start programs from, the question [project-kit#2697](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2697) takes.

### Alternatives considered

- **No merge command printing, so every merge outside the landing command reads.** Not taken, though it is the least that could be built. It narrows a core record on a path where a command can tell, to save one pass of the check. Choosing it drops point 2 and the first of COR-050's new sentences, and the rest stands.
- **The merge commands printing only where to read.** Not taken. It would print on every merge and say nothing of this one.
- **The merge commands refusing where the answers cannot be derived, as the landing command does.** Not taken. On a project whose anchors need the head's programs, it would refuse every merge. A project with no checks has no other way to land.
- **The merge commands deriving exactly as the view does.** Not taken. From a clean checkout at the head, that would start the head's programs on a merge.
- **The merge commands deriving nothing at a clean checkout at the head.** Not taken. That is an ordinary place to merge from, and a project with no such anchor would lose the lines for nothing.
- **A comment from the move to review, with only the lines owed.** Not taken, though it is the route if "told" must mean shown unasked. No move is asked for after review, so a fix round's deferral goes unposted, and no comment reads as nothing owed. On a delegate's batch it would list each document still accurate, which is the list again. It also needs a comment kind, a guard on the head's words and a limit on length.
- **A comment from CI.** Not taken. The run already holds the words at that head, so a comment would repeat them in a second place. A job that posts also needs a write token, which a fork's run lacks. A project may lift the lines into its run's own summary, which needs none.
- **The reviewer's verdict naming each deferral.** Not taken. Its words are an agent's, so nothing would show a line was missing, and a person alone would read them. The diff already holds what it would repeat, at later heads too.
- **The view reading the answers as data.** Not taken. It would ask the backbone for a run that skips resolvers, to print what the run and the diff hold already. It needs a clone. Whether an answer was asked for would stay unknown on an artefact with such an anchor, as DEC-065 found.
- **A commit status at the head.** Not taken. It holds too little to carry the words, and it joins the checks a project's merge gate reads.
- **The author saying each deferral in the description.** Rejected. It gives every author a new duty and the move a new refusal, for a line a person can read already. It is the shorter list DEC-065 rejected, and a later head can leave it wrong.
- **Leaving the core records as they stand, and keeping "falls short" for a person who does not look.** Not taken. Accepted records would go on saying what nothing delivers.

## Implications

The edits to accepted records below are made in this record's change, once a person accepts it (COR-066 point 5). Each artefact anchored on what they alter is asked for an answer then, and that change declares them for the backbone. What builds on them may follow in a later change.

- **COR-050's Implications** read "shows point 3's list there where it can" in place of "may show point 3's list there".

- **COR-064 point 5** gains a sentence under "Where". It reads: "The delegate says with the change which answers it decided and which it declined, since no check can tell who decided."
- **COR-064's Implications** read "tells the person what the delegate decided where it can".
- **No pointer to this record** goes into a core record, since the words stand in core terms alone.
- **[project-management:DEC-065-answers-read-from-check]** is refined in place (refinement per this record):
  - **Its summary** says the other merge commands print what the landing command prints where they can derive it, beside the view. It says a person who merges elsewhere reads it on the host, and that this is what they are owed.

**What builds on them:**

- **The other merge commands** print as point 2 has it.
- **The change check** gains a way to be told to start nothing. It then refuses an anchor's resolver at a clean checkout at the head, as it does away from one.
- **The view** names its head and says where to read, as point 4 has it. If it names the folder of answer files, it takes that from the backbone's output.
- **The definition of this capability's delegate** has the statement name each answer it decided by its artefact and outcome. The words stay in the answer file. It says in its own words why it declined each answer it declined. It quotes no answer's reason, since a quoted reason can read as closing the follow-up it names.
- **The documentation.** The capability README and the pm skill say what the merge commands print and where a person reads. This project's analysis says the same of the actor who authorises a merge.
- **Tests** cover a head whose anchors need a resolver, from a clean checkout at it and from elsewhere. In both, a merge command prints no answer, starts no resolver, names where to read and merges. They also cover a head that derives: its lines name it, come before the question, and print once under the landing command.
- **No migration.** No file of an adopter's moves.
- **Surface changes.** For this capability, what the two commands print and the view's lines. For the backbone, the check's new way of refusing. The change that builds each declares it.
- **DEC-055 and DEC-056** do not change. What DEC-056 point 5 leans on stays: the reviewer's check, and a delegate's telling that COR-064 point 5 now carries with the change.
