title: A forward move never waits on an ancestor, and its cascade writes only from reads made after it
status: proposed

**In plain terms:** when an issue moves forward, the forward cascade finds each container above the issue that stands behind it, and brings that container up to the issue's state, as far as In Progress. It writes to a container only from reads made after the move, never from a read made before it, such as the one a preview shows. Where the cascade cannot tell where a container stands, as where the board would not answer for its card or the card has no Status, the move goes ahead all the same. That container is left where it stands, since moving it would act on a guess. A container whose write fails is left too, moved no further. The cascade goes on to the containers above, each moved only on what reads made after the move find. Its report is a warning wherever the cascade did not finish, whatever kept it from finishing, and it says why, and how to finish. So it warns of more than a container it left, such as an open container at Review or Done, where no move of the lifecycle puts one. Once the move is made, a warning stands beside it, never an exit code.

## Context

Two accepted rules meet at the forward cascade:

- **No container is to stand behind the work under it.** When an issue moves forward, each ancestor that is behind is brought up to the moved issue's state, capped at In Progress ([project-management:DEC-006-state-machine-and-cascade], "Cascade rule").
- **No command acts on a position it could not read** ([project-management:DEC-051-axis-carriage-activation] point 6). Where the board carries the state, a read of an issue's card that does not place it ends in one of four outcomes: the board cannot be read, the issue has no card, its card has no Status, or its Status names no open state. None falls back to the issue's labels or its Milestone.

At an ancestor the cascade cannot place, the two pull apart. Bringing it level needs its position, and its position cannot be read. The record on a board-carried state leaves which rule gives way to a project-management record ([pkit:ADR-074], Boundaries). Its point 5 holds every command that reads the state to two limits: it never guesses, and it keeps a board that would not answer apart from a finding about the issue. Where a command's exit codes tell a broken tool from a refusal, a board that would not answer takes the broken tool's code.

The record on a board that did not answer ([pkit:ADR-096]) narrows that code once a command's act is made. A command's act, in its terms, is the change the command is run to make, here the issue's move, and what the act sets off on another issue, such as the forward cascade, is no part of it. Once the act is made, the command takes no code that reads as the act not made. That record leaves the cascade to this one, within two limits: no ancestor moves on a position not read, which DEC-051 point 6 already holds, and a move once made takes no code that reads as the move not made.

The question is what the move does at such an ancestor ([project-kit#3204](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3204)): whether it waits on it, and, where it does not, what the walk does there and above it. This record has the move made, and the walk go on past the ancestor (points 1 and 4).

Terms:

- **The walk** is the cascade's pass from the moved issue's parent up its hierarchy, one ancestor at a time. Point 4 says where it ends.
- **An issue's parent records** are the two records that name its parent: its native sub-issue link, where the tracker supports one, and its body's first line ([pkit:ADR-035]).
- **A read of where an ancestor stands** places it in a state of the lifecycle, places it in none, or gets no answer.
  - **It places the ancestor in none** where what comes back is a finding about the ancestor, which is open: that what carries its state places it in no state of the lifecycle. Where the board carries the state, that is one of DEC-051 point 6's three findings: the issue has no card, its card has no Status, or its Status names no open state, such as Done on an open issue. Where labels carry it, that is a label for a state the lifecycle does not have.
  - **It gets no answer** where the ancestor's issue, or, where the board carries the state, its card, could not be read: where the tracker or the board said so, could not be reached, or replied with anything but one well-formed answer. A read that gets no answer gives no position, whichever read it is.
  - **An ancestor is level** where a read places it at or past the state the cascade brings it up to.
- **The first read** of an ancestor is made as the walk is planned: before the move, where the run makes one, and before a person at a terminal confirms it. It reads the ancestor's issue and, where the board carries the state and the ancestor is open, its card.
- **A later read** of an ancestor is made after the move, just before the cascade would write to it. Point 2 says which ancestors get one, and what it reads.
- **The parent check** reads an issue's parent records alone, once, after the move, to find whether the issue still names the parent it had when the walk was planned. Point 2 says which issues get one.
- **An ancestor the cascade cannot place** is one that a read of it, the first or the later, places in none or gets no answer for. Where it matters which, this record says so: an ancestor the first read could not place, or one its later read cannot place.
- **An ancestor left** is one whose later read cannot place it, or whose write of what carries its state is not made (points 2 and 3). A container that stands behind the work under it for another cause is not one, such as one for which the lifecycle declares no way from where it stands, one the first read placed level and that was moved back before the cascade would write to it, or one above where the walk ended.

## Decision

**A forward move is made whatever its cascade finds above it. The cascade writes to an ancestor only from reads made after the move: of the ancestor, and of the issues below it on the walk. An ancestor its later read cannot place is not moved, one whose write is not made is moved no further, and each is reported. Leaving an ancestor does not end the walk, and once the move is made, the cascade takes no exit code.**

### 1. The move does not wait on an ancestor

A forward move is made, or refused, on the moved issue's own reads and gates, never on an ancestor's position. No command refuses or holds a move because an ancestor its cascade will reach cannot be placed, whether the walk finds that before the move or after it.

Where the walk is planned before the move, what it found is shown before the move is confirmed. So a person at a terminal sees which ancestor the first read could not place, that the run reads it again where the walk reaches it, and where the walk as planned ends, and may decline the move.

### 2. The cascade writes to an ancestor only from reads made after the move

Each ancestor the first read did not place level gets a later read, once, just before the cascade would write to it. That read reads where the ancestor stands, and the ancestor's issue: its labels, whether it is open, its type, its parent records and where its card is found. Each write to the ancestor takes its inputs from that read, as the writes before it left them.

An ancestor its later read cannot place is left where it stands. Nothing is written to it: no state, no Status, no journal entry and no comment. The first read decides which ancestors the walk can reach, which of them get a later read, and what the preview shows. What the cascade writes rests on reads made after the move alone. So an ancestor the first read could not place is brought level where its later read places it, and one whose later read gets no answer is left, whatever its first read found. An ancestor's position is never guessed from its labels or its Milestone where the board carries the state (DEC-051 point 6), and nothing written to it rests on a read made before the move.

The later read asks the process engine where the ancestor stands, as the commands that move an issue do ([project-management:DEC-033-rebind-issue-lifecycle-onto-process-substrate] D5), and takes the position the engine gives, in a state or in none. The engine evaluates no position where it cannot be reached, replies with anything but one well-formed answer, or answers that it could not evaluate the position. Where the engine evaluates no position, the later read is made directly instead, once, from the ancestor's issue and, where the board carries the state, its card, as the first read is made. So a later read gets no answer only where the ancestor's issue or card could not be read. A direct read that gets no answer is not made again in the run, and the ancestor is left.

The cascade writes to an ancestor only where each issue below it, down to the moved issue, still names after the move the parent it had when the walk was planned. Each of them is read for that after the move, before the cascade's first write above it: by its later read where one is made, and otherwise by its parent check. Where one of them no longer names that parent, or cannot be read, the cascade writes nothing to that ancestor or above it, and the walk ends there (point 4). No other issue is read for a changed parent. So where the moved issue, or an ancestor that gets no later read, is given another parent after the walk was planned, and nothing above it is written, nothing finds the change. The new parent stays behind, with no warning, until a later forward move under it reaches it.

An ancestor the first read places level gets no later read: nothing reads again where it stands, and nothing is written to it. So one moved back after that read, as while a person at a terminal reads the preview, stays behind the work under it, with no warning, until a later forward move under it brings it level. Its parent check, where one is made, reads its parent records alone.

### 3. An ancestor whose write is not made is moved no further

A write of what carries the ancestor's state is not made where it fails, as where the tracker or the board refuses it. The ancestor is then moved no further. The steps it took before that write stand, and so does the move. What else the cascade writes once the ancestor's state has moved, such as a Status kept only as a mirror of its labels, a comment on it or its journal entry, is no such write: where it fails, the report says so, and the ancestor's steps stand.

### 4. Leaving an ancestor does not end the walk

The walk finds an ancestor's parent from its issue, not from where it stands, and a read can fail to place the ancestor, as where its card cannot be read or names no state, and still read its issue. Each ancestor is brought up to the moved issue's state, capped at In Progress, and never to the state of the ancestor below it, so an ancestor left bears on no move above it. So the walk goes on past it, and each ancestor above is brought level, or not moved, on where its own reads place it.

The walk ends where it reaches the top, or where it cannot tell the next ancestor up, as where the read of an ancestor's issue that it takes the parent from cannot be made: a command that writes on a parent writes nothing on one in doubt, or above it ([pkit:ADR-035] point 5). That holds of the first read, from which the walk is planned, and of the reads after the move, from which the cascade writes (point 2). After the move the walk ends too at an issue whose later read or parent check finds that it no longer names the parent it had when the walk was planned, since any parent it names by then was neither read as the walk was planned nor shown before the move. It ends at an issue that is no container, which the cascade does not move, and at one it has passed already.

The ancestor whose issue the first read could not read, and at which the walk ends, is no parent in doubt: the issue below it named it, and only the parent above it is in doubt. So it is an ancestor the first read could not place, given a later read and brought level where that read places it and finds it a container (point 2). No later read extends the walk, so the cascade writes on no ancestor the walk did not reach as it was planned.

### 5. When the report is a warning, and how to finish

The cascade's closing report is clean only where, by what the cascade read and wrote, the walk ended at the top, each ancestor it reached stands level, each step it took was journaled where a journal is kept, and no open container it reached stands at Review or Done (below). Any other outcome makes the report a warning, such as an ancestor left, an end short of the top that the cascade finds, a step it could not journal, or an ancestor for which the lifecycle declares no way from where it stands. It gives no warning of a container behind the work under it whose position it did not read after the move, as one moved back after the first read placed it level (point 2), though it warns of each end short of the top that it finds.

The report lists each ancestor the walk planned, each with what became of it, as for one the first read placed level, or one above where the walk ended. It names each ancestor left, and the read or the write that left it, and, where the walk ended short of the top, where and why. It names a read that got no answer as one, and, among such reads, a board that would not answer as such. With each, it passes on what the read said of why, such as an issue not found, so that a cause that lasts can be told from one that passes. It never names such a read as a finding about the ancestor, nor a finding as a read that got no answer ([pkit:ADR-074] point 5).

A read can place an open ancestor at Review, which DEC-006 keeps for a Task alone, or, where labels carry the state, at Done, which a container reaches only through its close (DEC-006). The cascade does not move such an ancestor, since it is level, and the report warns of it. Where the board carries the state, an open ancestor whose card names Done is placed in none (DEC-051 point 6), so it is left (point 2).

For each ancestor left, and each open container at Review or Done, the report says how to finish: what to put right, mend or wait on, and then which of the move made again, the next forward move under the ancestor, or a move of the ancestor on its own finishes the cascade. It says too that a container whose state is set as a person meant it, such as one set outside the lifecycle on purpose, stays where it was put, and that each forward move under it warns of it again.

### 6. Once the move is made, the cascade takes no code

A run that makes the move, or finds the issue already at its target, exits as the move alone has it exit, a dry run of an issue already at its target included. Whatever makes the cascade's report a warning (point 5) is a warning beside it, never a code: not a refusal's, not the broken tool's, nor any other. That is within the limit [pkit:ADR-096] point 4 sets once a command's act is made: nothing the act sets off on another issue gives the command a code that reads as the act not made. So each command that composes the move reads the move as made.

A run that ends with the move not made, such as a dry run of a move not yet made or a run declined at the terminal, takes ADR-074 point 5's broken tool's code where the board would not answer for the card of an ancestor it previewed. An ancestor its first read places in none gives it no code, since that would not have held the move (point 1).

## Rationale

**Why the move does not wait.**

- **Waiting brings the ancestor no nearer level.** Where a read gets no answer, the ancestor's position is unknown, which is not the same as behind. Where a read places it in none, it is known not to be level, yet it stands nowhere the lifecycle moves from. Either way it cannot be moved without a guess, which DEC-051 point 6 forbids, so a hold on the work under it leaves it as it was.
- **Waiting would give a state set by hand a meaning no record gives it.** A container whose card sits in a column outside the lifecycle, such as Blocked, or whose label names such a state, or a container kept off the board, would hold every forward move beneath it. What a card a person places by hand means is a question DEC-006 leaves open, and a hold on the work would answer it by accident. The remedy lies on the ancestor, often someone else's, not on the work being moved.
- **Gates are per issue.** The cascade is not an authorisation and asks for none, and each gate governs a move on its own issue (DEC-006). A hold on another issue's state would be a gate nobody set on the move, stopping a move whose own gates passed.
- **Waiting could not hold for every move.** Where a merge or a completed close is made before the move to Done that follows it, nothing is left to hold. Even a walk planned whole can leave an ancestor after the move, where a later read places it in none or gets no answer, or the board refuses a write. So a move made with an ancestor reported has to be handled whatever this record decides.

**Why DEC-006's Cascade rule is narrowed here, and not DEC-051's rule.** DEC-006 rejects leaving an ancestor behind and reporting it, where the cascade could move it, because a container behind a started child states something false. Here the cascade cannot move the ancestor without guessing its position, which DEC-051 point 6 forbids, or taking it from a read made before the move, which can move the ancestor back (see "Why the cascade writes only from reads made after the move"). Moving it so would write a position nobody read just before the write, and refusing the move would hold the work on another issue's state. So the Cascade rule keeps its aim, that no container is to stand behind the work under it, and is narrowed to what the cascade can read and write: the container may stand behind the work under it until a later move brings it level. The narrowing accepts more than an unknown: where a read places the ancestor in none, the container is known not to be level, and stays so until whatever carries its state is set right or a record decides what such a state means. The rule is narrowed the same way in three more places. One is an ancestor whose write is not made, since the move is made by then and only a write made again can bring the ancestor level. Another is one the first read places level that is moved back before the cascade would write to it, which gets no later read (see "Why the later read is made of each ancestor the first read did not place level, and of no other"). The last is a parent the moved issue or an ancestor was given after the first read, which stays behind until a later forward move under it reaches it.

**Why the walk goes on.** The parent comes from the read of the ancestor's issue, which was made where only its position was not read. The state each ancestor is brought up to does not depend on the one below, so an ancestor above, brought level on its own reads, moves on a position that was read. A container ahead of the one below it is a state DEC-006 allows, since a container may stand ahead of the work under it. Stopping the walk would keep every container above behind the work under it, for a fault on one ancestor. An ancestor whose write failed is passed the same way, for the same reason. The walk ends where the next ancestor up cannot be told, since a write on a parent in doubt could move a container the moved issue is not under. That holds after the move as before it: an issue that cannot be read then, or that no longer names the parent it had when the walk was planned, gives the cascade no write above it. The ancestor whose issue the first read could not read, and at which the walk ends, is no such parent: the issue below it named it, and only its own parent is in doubt. So it gets a later read and is brought level as any ancestor the first read could not place. The walk goes on from no later read, since the ancestors above that one were never read as the walk was planned, nor shown before the move. It ends too at an issue that is no container, which the cascade never moves, and at one it has passed already, where going on would walk in a circle.

**Why the cascade writes only from reads made after the move.** The first read is made as the walk is planned, before the move and before a person at a terminal confirms it, so it can be minutes old when the cascade writes. In between, another move under the same ancestor may have brought it level, as a start of work on a sibling does. The cascade runs only once the move's own write has landed, through the tracker and, where the board carries the state, the board, which the later read reads too. So a later read that gets no answer from them most likely meets a passing fault, and a write from the first read in its place would most likely land. A write sets where the ancestor goes without checking where it stands, so a write from the first read would then:

- where the board carries the state, set the ancestor's Status back behind the work under it, a move back DEC-006 holds for the user, which nobody authorised;
- where labels carry the state, take off a label the ancestor no longer carries and add the next, which can leave it two values on one axis, against the rule that an issue carries one value on each axis ([project-management:RS-PM-003]);
- on either, journal a step from a state the ancestor no longer held, which no later move takes back.

A read made just before the cascade would write leaves only the window every write leaves between its read and its landing. So the cascade writes from that read alone, and leaves the ancestor wherever it gets no answer, whether the board or the ancestor's issue could not be read: the first read is as old in each case. The cost is an ancestor a passing fault left unread, though the first read placed it, until the move made again, or the next forward move under it, brings it level. Where the fault lasts until someone mends it, as where an issue body's first line names a parent that does not exist, the cost is larger: each forward move under the ancestor leaves it and warns of it again, and the move made again changes nothing until the fault is mended.

Which issues are the ancestors is read before the move too. Where the moved issue or an ancestor is given another parent in between, a write along the walk as planned moves a container the moved issue is no longer under. A container in Todo is then taken through Backlog to In Progress on a scheduling commitment given on work no longer beneath it, and journaled as caused by that move, while the new parent goes unread under a clean report. That is the harm a write from the first read does, so the cascade reads each issue below an ancestor after the move, before it writes to that ancestor, and writes nothing where the walk changed. An ancestor with a later read is read so by it. The cost is a parent check of the moved issue, and of each ancestor the first read placed level that lies below a written one, bounded by the hierarchy's depth. A move that writes to no ancestor costs none, and the move made again walks from the parent the issue names by then. The cost is larger where such a read gets no answer, though the fault passes. A parent check of the moved issue that gets none ends the walk below its first ancestor, so the cascade writes nothing at all, where without the check only an ancestor whose read failed would have been left. The report names where the walk ended, and the move made again finishes the cascade once the read is answered. The check guards the writes, so it reads no issue with no write above it: a parent given to such an issue, where it gets no later read of its own, goes unread, and stays behind with no warning until a later forward move under it reaches it. No write lands on a container the moved issue is no longer under, which is the harm the check is for, and finding every such change would need a read of each issue the walk passes on every forward move (see "Parent-check every issue the walk passes" under Alternatives).

**Why the later read asks the process engine, and is made directly, once, where the engine evaluates no position.** DEC-033 D5 hands a move's mechanics, its position, validation and journal, to the engine, and keeps the cascade among the domain side-effects project-management retains. This record reads the two clauses together: which ancestors the cascade moves, and how far, is the side-effect project-management keeps, while each step it writes is a move whose position is mechanics D5 hands on. A step's origin is that position, the one the engine validates the step against and journals it from. So the later read, from which each step's origin comes, asks the engine first, and takes the position it gives, in a state or in none. The cost is a second read: where the ancestor stands comes from the engine, and each write's inputs from a read of its issue, with a moment between the two that one read would not leave. The first read sets no step's origin: it decides only which ancestors get a later read and what the preview shows, and writes nothing, so it reads the ancestor directly. A direct read and the engine place an ancestor alike: the engine's position and a command's own reading are one inference over the same inputs ([pkit:ADR-074] point 4), and DEC-033 holds the two to the same result (its Implications, behaviour parity).

The engine places an ancestor through the lifecycle's classifier, the predicate that reads the issue and its card and passes both to that inference (ADR-074 point 4). Where the engine cannot be reached, replies with anything but one well-formed answer, or answers that it could not evaluate the ancestor's position, it gives the cascade no position, and nothing is learnt of where the ancestor stands. Its answer cannot tell a read of the ancestor that failed from a classifier that failed before it read, as where the classifier's own code would not load. A read made directly then, of the ancestor's issue and, where the board carries the state, its card, is as fresh as the engine's would have been, and places the ancestor by the same inference, so it is no second account of position. The direct read is made once: where it too gets no answer, the ancestor is left (see "Retry, within the run" under Alternatives). So the cascade brings ancestors level where the engine is missing or cannot evaluate a position, and no write rests on a read made before the move.

Where a journal is kept, a step written from a direct read goes unjournaled wherever the engine still cannot place the ancestor when the cascade journals the step. The engine journals a step from the origin it is given only while its own reading places the ancestor at that origin or at the step's target (the process README, "Why `--from`"). The report warns of each such step, as the run reports any move it could not journal ([project-management:DEC-049-audit-journal-model]), and no later move adds the entry. Where the fault lies with the engine itself, as where it cannot be reached or its classifier cannot load, the move that caused the step, made while the fault lasted, goes unjournaled too. Where the fault lies with the ancestor alone, the journal holds that move and lacks the step: the cost of bringing the ancestor level on a read the engine could not make.

**Why a read that gets no answer is kept apart from one that places the ancestor in none.** Either leaves the ancestor where it stands, and neither gives the cascade a position to write from. They differ in what they say, and in what finishes them. A read that places the ancestor in none says where it stands: outside the lifecycle, until whatever carries its state is set right. A read that gets no answer says nothing of the ancestor, and the remedy is to mend what kept it from an answer, or to wait where the fault passes. So the report names them apart, as ADR-074 point 5 asks, and before the move only a board that would not answer takes the broken tool's code. A read that gets nothing back that is one answer is no answer for the same reason a board that would not answer is none: nothing was learnt of where the ancestor stands.

**Why an open container at Review or Done is read there, and warned of.** DEC-006 keeps Review for a Task alone, and has a container reach Done only through its close. Where the board carries the state, an open issue whose card names Done is placed in none (DEC-051 point 6), and one whose card names Review is at Review. Where labels carry it, an open issue labelled Review or Done is at that state. The process engine and every command read these alike, as one inference over the same inputs (ADR-074 point 4), so the cascade reads such an ancestor where it stands: past In Progress, so level, and nothing is written to it. Yet an open container at Review or Done stands where no move the lifecycle declares puts a container, so its state was set outside the lifecycle and states something false. The cascade's report is the one signal of it that reaches whoever moves the work under it. So the report warns of it on either substrate: as a finding where the board carries the state and the card names Done, and as an open container in a state DEC-006 rules out for it otherwise. The cost is a warning on the output of the moves under such a container, until its state is set right or it is closed, and one falsehood the two substrates report under two names. A closed container is not warned of: it reached Done through a close, a move the lifecycle declares (see "Warn of a closed container above the moved issue too" under Alternatives).

**Why the report is clean only where the cascade finished.** A warning keyed to a list of causes misses each cause the list leaves out, such as a step the engine could not journal, and an ancestor for which the lifecycle declares no way from where it stands. A report clean only where the walk ended at the top, each ancestor it reached stands level, each step was journaled where a journal is kept and no open container stands at Review or Done warns of both. It warns too of any cause found later, with no list to keep up, so whoever reads only its first line learns whether anything the cascade reached needs putting right.

**Why the report says how to finish.** An ancestor left can be offered for closing before it is level: the closure cascade makes an ancestor eligible to close once its last open child closes, wherever it stands, while DEC-006's table starts that close only at In Progress. Where no forward move is left to come under it, as when the moved issue was the last open one under it, only a move of the ancestor on its own brings it level, once a read places it and before it is closed. That move is asked for directly, so its gates apply: a container in Todo needs scheduling of its own, a Milestone or an audited reason, though the scheduling given on the work under it covered it (DEC-006). The cost falls only where nothing under the container is left to bring it level.

**Why the later read is made of each ancestor the first read did not place level, and of no other.** A board that would not answer for an ancestor's card when the walk was planned may answer by the write, and a card set right in between places the ancestor. Leaving the ancestor on the first read alone would leave one the cascade could bring level, and the read costs what the later read of an ancestor placed behind already costs. An ancestor the first read places level gets no later read, so a move under ancestors already level, as a start of work under a started container is, costs one read of each, the first. The first read can be as old for it as for an ancestor the first read places behind, but what an old read costs differs. A write from it can move a container back with nobody's authorisation, leave it two state labels, or journal a step from a state it no longer held. Writing nothing lets a container moved back after the first read stay behind the work under it, with no warning, until the next forward move under it brings it level. A move back already bears a cost of that kind, where a child container stands behind the work under it (Implications).

**Why nothing is written to the ancestor.** A notice on it would repeat on every forward move under a container set outside the lifecycle on purpose, or need a key to post it once that a later change of its state would have to reset. The report reaches whoever runs the move, where the move's own outcome is read. The report repeats too: each forward move under such a container warns of it again, a warning that recurs until its state changes or a record decides what such a state means, and that readers may learn to pass over. That cost falls on the output of the moves under the container, and on no issue.

**Why no code once the move is made, and ADR-074's code before it.** Commands compose the move and hand on, or act on, its code: a start of work reads a non-zero code as a failed start, and a merge or a close as a step to run again. After the move, a code for the cascade would tell them that a move that was made was not, or need each of them to learn a new code for a state the report already says. Before the move, nothing is made that a code could misreport. The cascade reads each ancestor's state, so ADR-074 point 5 binds those reads, and gives a board that would not answer the broken tool's code until the act is made. A dry run makes no move, nor does a run declined at the terminal, so each takes that code where the board would not answer for an ancestor's card. A read that places an ancestor in none is no broken tool, and would not have held the move, so it takes none. This record takes the code only where ADR-074 point 5 asks for it: what else a preview may leave unread, such as an ancestor's issue, keeps the code the command gives it. A dry run of an issue already at its target previews a move an earlier run made, so it takes no code for the cascade, as the run would not. The costs: a caller reading only the exit code of a move made learns nothing of an ancestor left, and where the board would not answer for an ancestor's card, a dry run exits with a code its run would not take. The warning is in the command's output, as it is for a cascade left incomplete by a failed write.

### Alternatives considered

- **Hold the move, or the walk, on the ancestor** (see "Why the move does not wait" and "Why the walk goes on").
  - **The move waits: it refuses, writing nothing, where an ancestor its walk will reach cannot be placed.** The case: after no move it lets through does a container stand behind the work under it, and the walk can be planned before the move, so the refusal costs no read. Rejected: it holds the work under a container on that container's state, for a position that may not be behind, and it cannot hold where a merge or a close is made before the move.
  - **Wait only where the board cannot be read, and go on where a read places the ancestor in none.** The case: a broken tool stops what rests on it, and an outage is likely brief. Rejected: the move rests on its own reads, which the board answered, and nothing the move needs rests on the ancestor's read.
  - **Stop the walk at the ancestor, or only where the board cannot be read, and report where it stopped.** The case: one line names where the walk ended, and a fault that left one ancestor unread likely reaches those above it too, as an outage reaches every card above, so going on sends more calls to a board already failing. Rejected: the containers above stay behind though each could be read and brought level, the calls are bounded by the hierarchy's depth, and a board that failed on one card may answer for the next.
- **Write from the first read.** Each variant carries the hazard of a write from a read made before the move (see "Why the cascade writes only from reads made after the move").
  - **Where a later read gets no answer, write from the first read where it placed the ancestor.** The case: what came back says nothing of where the ancestor stands, and the first read did place it, so the cascade could finish in one run through a passing fault. Rejected: such a write most likely lands, since the move's own write landed moments before, and leaving the ancestor costs a run made again.
  - **Only where no prompt stood between the first read and the move,** as where the move is confirmed up front or no person is at a terminal. The case: the first read is then seconds old, not minutes, so a false move back is rarer. Rejected: it keys what the cascade writes to how the command was run, a move under the same ancestor in those seconds makes the same false move, and what it gains is what the move made again gives.
  - **Where the process engine evaluates no position,** as where it cannot be reached. The case: the cascade still brings ancestors level where the engine is missing. Rejected: a read made directly then is as fresh as the engine's would have been, while the first read is as old as ever, so the write carries the hazard for nothing the fresh read does not give.
  - **Write along the walk as planned, and accept the gap where the moved issue or an ancestor no longer names the parent it had when the walk was planned,** as the gap for an ancestor moved back after the first read is accepted in DEC-006. The case: no read is added, and a parent changed in the minutes of a prompt is rare. Rejected: a write along a walk that changed moves a container the moved issue is no longer under, the harm the parent check is for, while the gap for an ancestor moved back writes nothing.
- **Leave, or read otherwise, an ancestor the process engine evaluates no position for** (see "Why the later read asks the process engine, and is made directly, once, where the engine evaluates no position").
  - **Leave every such ancestor, as one whose later read gets no answer is left.** The case: every later read has one reader, the engine the commands that move an issue ask (DEC-033 D5), and the cascade writes no step the engine cannot place, so a kept journal misses none of its steps. Rejected: a read made directly then places the ancestor by the inference the engine runs (ADR-074 point 4), so this leaves containers behind the work under them for a fault in the engine, not in any read of them, for as long as that fault lasts, which the move made again does not mend. It keeps the journal whole only where the fault lies with the ancestor alone: where the fault lies with the engine itself, the move that caused the step goes unjournaled as well.
  - **Leave such an ancestor only where a journal is kept, and read it directly where none is.** The case: a kept journal is load-bearing (DEC-049), and the engine will not journal a step from a position it cannot evaluate, while a project that keeps no journal loses nothing by the write. Rejected: what the cascade moves would turn on a project's journal setting, and a project that keeps one would keep containers behind the work under them for a fault in the engine. Where that fault lies with the engine itself, the entry it saves is one the same fault withholds from the move that caused it.
  - **Read directly only where the engine cannot be reached or replies with no well-formed answer, and leave the ancestor where it answers that it could not evaluate the position.** The case: an engine that answers so has made its own read, and a direct read then asks again of a board or a tracker that may just have failed. Rejected: the engine's answer cannot tell a read that failed from a classifier that failed before it read, so this would leave every such ancestor for as long as the classifier is broken, the cost "Leave every such ancestor" is rejected for. One direct read costs at most one more read of each such ancestor, bounded by the hierarchy's depth, though not in time: where the classifier was stopped for not answering in time, the direct read asks the same tracker or board again, and waits as long as that read does.
  - **Make every later read directly, and never ask the engine where the ancestor stands.** The case: a direct read places the ancestor by the inference the engine runs (ADR-074 point 4), as the first read does. Where the ancestor stands and each write's inputs would then come from one read, with no window between them, each ancestor written would cost one call to the engine and one read of its issue less, and no direct read would wait first on a classifier stopped for not answering in time. It journals as the read through the engine does, since the engine accepts a stated origin only while its own reading places the ancestor there (the process README, "Why `--from`"). Rejected: it sets aside D5's hand-off of each step's position to the engine, as this record reads D5, even where the engine gives a position. The engine checks each step under either reading, so what this record's later read keeps over this is that hand-off, not the check.
- **Read other issues again after the move, or fewer** (see "Why the later read is made of each ancestor the first read did not place level, and of no other", and the paragraph on which issues are the ancestors in "Why the cascade writes only from reads made after the move").
  - **Read again only an ancestor the first read placed behind, and leave one it could not place on that read.** The case: an ancestor the preview names as unplaced is then left, so the preview and the report agree on it. Rejected: an ancestor a brief failure left unplaced at the first read would be left though the cascade could place it by the write.
  - **Read again every ancestor, those the first read placed level included.** The case: a container moved back after the first read, as while a person at a terminal reads the preview, is then brought level by the move, rather than staying behind under a clean report. Rejected: it adds a read of where each level ancestor stands, through the process engine, on every forward move, though most forward moves are made under containers already level. For a level ancestor below one the cascade writes, whose issue its parent check reads already, the read of where it stands is what is added, and for every other, as on every move that writes to none, the whole later read. A container so moved back is brought level by the next forward move under it.
  - **Read a level ancestor again only where a prompt stood between the first read and the move.** The case: the first read is then minutes old, and a person at a terminal waits on the run anyway, so the read costs little there. Rejected: it keys what the cascade reads to how the command was run, as the write from the first read where no prompt stood keys what it writes, and a container moved back in the seconds a run with no prompt takes stays behind the same way.
  - **Place a level ancestor again where its parent check is made, and bring it level where it is then found behind.** The case: its issue is read already, so a container moved back after the first read is brought level wherever it lies below one the cascade writes, and a move that writes to no ancestor adds no read. Rejected: placing it asks the process engine where it stands, as every later read does, an engine call for each such ancestor on every move that writes, and reading its issue already saves only the read of its issue.
  - **Parent-check every issue the walk passes after the move, whether or not a write lies above it.** The case: every parent changed after the walk was planned then ends the walk and is warned of, the new parent included, though it is still not moved. Rejected: it adds a read of the parent records of each ancestor the first read places level on every forward move, the common move that writes to no ancestor included, though it makes no engine call, and a check where nothing above an issue is written guards no write. The new parent is brought level by the next forward move under it.
- **Warn of other states, or of fewer** (see "Why an open container at Review or Done is read there, and warned of" and "Why the report is clean only where the cascade finished").
  - **Place an open ancestor labelled Done in none where labels carry the state, as an open issue whose card names Done is placed where the board carries it.** The case: the two substrates report one falsehood under one name, and DEC-006's edits name no state that is open on one substrate only. Rejected: the cascade would place such an ancestor apart from the inference the process engine and every other command run (ADR-074 point 4), and it would read such an ancestor again on every forward move. The report warns of it either way.
  - **Pass over an open container at Review, or at Done where labels carry the state, as level, with no warning.** The case: the cascade reads its state as every other command does, and would not move such an ancestor either way. Rejected: one falsehood would be a warning where the board carries Done, and pass with no warning where labels carry it or where the state is Review.
  - **Pass over an open ancestor whose label names a state the lifecycle does not have, as level, with no warning.** The case: such a label may be a project's own, set on purpose, and the cascade would not move the ancestor either way. Rejected: no read placed the ancestor level, so the report would read clean over a container whose state no read gave, and nothing else signals the label to whoever moves the work under it. Placed in none, it is warned of, and the report says how to set it right or keep it (point 5).
  - **Warn of a closed container above the moved issue too.** The case: DEC-006 has a container reach Done only once every child is done, so a closed one above work moving forward states something false as well, and is passed over as level. Rejected: it reached Done through a close, which a person authorises and the lifecycle declares, while an open container at Review or Done stands where no declared move puts it. Whether work opened under a closed container calls for reopening it is a question of that close, not of the forward cascade.
  - **Make the report a warning for a list of causes:** an ancestor left, an end short of the top, and an open container at Review or Done. The case: each cause on the list is one this record decides, and the report warns of nothing it does not name. Rejected: the list leaves out a step the engine could not journal, and an ancestor for which the lifecycle declares no way from where it stands, and each cause found later would need it extended.
- **Bring the ancestor level some other way.**
  - **Retry, within the run, a read of an ancestor that got no answer, until it answers or a limit is reached.** The case: a brief outage would pass, and the cascade would finish in one run. Rejected here: a retry loop, with its waits and its limit, would serve every command that reads the board or the tracker, not the cascade alone. For the board it belongs in the read seam every read of the board goes through ([pkit:ADR-053]), and no record places one for a read of the tracker. The direct read made where the process engine evaluates no position is one read, not a loop. Running the move again, which the report asks for once the read can be answered, is the retry.
  - **Move the ancestor on its labels or its Milestone where the board does not place it.** Rejected by DEC-051 point 6: the cascade would act on a position it guessed.
  - **Write In Progress, at the cap, to an ancestor whose card has no Status or that has no card.** The case: no open container stands past In Progress, so the write could never move it backward, and a guess is weakest here, since the value written does not depend on where the ancestor stood. Rejected: the write would follow no transition the schema declares, from a state no read gave, so a journal would record a step from nowhere, and an ancestor that stood in Todo would read as started without having been scheduled, which DEC-006 rejects for containers. A missing card is also the board's membership to repair ([project-management:DEC-019-mandatory-issue-state]), which the cascade does not write. The remedy costs little: set the Status, and the move made again brings the ancestor level.
- **Signal an ancestor left elsewhere than in the report.**
  - **Post a notice on the ancestor, or on the moved issue, so whoever can repair its state learns of it.** The case: the report reaches the mover, often an agent, while the ancestor is often someone else's, and no check finds the container later. Rejected: see "Why nothing is written to the ancestor".
  - **Record in the moved issue's journal that its cascade was left incomplete.** The case: an entry is no comment, so it repeats nowhere, and it outlasts the run's output. Rejected: the journal holds an issue's governed moves ([project-management:DEC-049-audit-journal-model]), and a cascade left incomplete is no move of the moved issue. Nothing reads such an entry to act on it, it goes stale once a later move brings the ancestor level, and a project that keeps no journal would record nothing.
  - **A check, run apart from any move, that lists each container behind the work under it.** The case: it writes nothing and needs no key, so the objections to a notice do not reach it. It finds a container that stands behind the work under it whoever ran the move, whatever became of the report and whatever the cause, such as a walk that ended at a parent in doubt or a container moved back after the first read. Not taken here, and left to [project-kit#3246](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3246): it checks the whole hierarchy rather than act at one move, and where it runs, what it reads and how it reports are choices of their own.
- **Give the cascade other exit codes** (see "Why no code once the move is made, and ADR-074's code before it").
  - **No run takes a code for the cascade, not a dry run nor a run declined at the terminal.** The case: one code for every run whatever the cascade found, and a decline reports the person's choice, not a read. Rejected: neither run makes a move, so ADR-074 point 5 gives a board that would not answer for an ancestor's card the broken tool's code, and taking none would need that point narrowed again, for runs where nothing is made that a code could misreport.
  - **A code of its own for a move made with its cascade left incomplete.** The case: a caller reading only the code would learn of the ancestor left, and ADR-096 point 4 lets a code that asks for a run again stand where a run again makes nothing twice. Rejected: every composing command would have to learn it, or would read it as the move not made.

## Implications

- **DEC-006 is refined in place on this record's acceptance,** by the edits below, each carrying the pointer "(refinement per [project-management:DEC-075-cascade-unread-ancestor])". The edits to its Cascade rule and to its Alternatives considered narrow the Cascade rule to the ancestors the cascade can read and write. The other three restate as an aim DEC-006's claim that no container stands behind the work under it, where the move back rests on it, and name where a move back can set a container behind the work under it:
  - **In its Cascade rule,** two changes:
    - In the forward-cascade bullet, the sentence "A container is never behind the work under it: when an issue moves forward, … capped at In Progress." reads instead: "No container is to stand behind the work under it: when an issue moves forward, the agent walks its ancestors — its parent, that parent's parent, and so on up — and brings each one its walk reaches and finds behind up to the moved issue's state, capped at In Progress, wherever it can read where that ancestor stands and write to it."
    - After the rule's three bullets, before "Review is a Task's state alone", a paragraph is added: "**The forward cascade writes only from reads made after the move.** It writes to an ancestor only from a read of it made just before the write, and only where each issue below it, down to the moved issue, still names after the move the parent it had when the walk was planned. An ancestor it cannot read then, or then places in no state of the lifecycle, is left where it stands, and one whose write is not made is moved no further. Each is warned of, and the move stands. Being left ends no walk, so the walk goes on past such an ancestor, though it still ends where it cannot tell the next ancestor up, as where the issue it takes that parent from cannot be read, before the move or after it, and nothing above that issue is written. A container also stays behind the work under it, with no warning, until a later forward move under it brings it level, where it was read level as the walk was planned and moved back after that read, or was given as a parent after the walk was planned to an issue that gets no later read and has nothing written above it. DEC-075 points 2 and 4 say which reads the cascade makes and where its walk ends, and point 5 what its report warns of, an end of the walk short of the top among them."
  - **In its Alternatives considered,** the bullet "Refuse to start a child whose ancestor is unscheduled, or leave the ancestor behind and report it" gains: "Leaving an ancestor the walk finds behind is rejected wherever the cascade can read where it stands and write to it. One the cascade cannot read just before it would write to it, or then places in no state of the lifecycle, is left where it stands, and one whose write is not made is moved no further. Each is warned of, and the child's move is never refused for it."
  - **In "Moving back from In Progress", its first paragraph,** "since a container is never behind the work under it" reads instead "since no container is to stand behind the work under it". The landing rule that sentence gives stands.
  - **In "Moving back from In Progress", its last paragraph,** "The move back moves no other issue: a container may stand ahead of the work under it, never behind it." reads instead: "The move back moves no other issue: a container may stand ahead of the work under it, and is not to stand behind it. A move back checks a container's children alone, so where a child container stands behind the work under it, moving the container back can set it behind work further down."
  - **In its Rationale, the paragraph on a container moved back over a child,** "Refusing that move keeps the cascade's guarantee" reads instead "Refusing that move keeps to the cascade's aim".
- **The narrowing costs a move back something.** A move back keeps a container level with all the work under it only where each child container is level with the work under that child. Where a child container stands behind the work under it, which the narrowed rule allows, the container's move back can set it behind work further down. Where the board carries the state, no move back is made, so the cost does not arise there. It lasts until the child is brought level (point 5). Whether a move back checks every issue under the container is left to [project-kit#3247](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3247).
- **ADR-074 needs no edit.** Its Boundaries leave the question to a project-management record, and this record keeps its point 5, as ADR-096 refines it.
- **DEC-033 needs no edit.** Its D5 hands a move's mechanics, its position among them, to the process engine, and keeps the cascade among the side-effects project-management retains. This record reads each step the cascade writes as such a move, and which ancestors it moves, and how far, as that side-effect (see "Why the later read asks the process engine, and is made directly, once, where the engine evaluates no position"), so D5 stands as written.
- **This record rests on ADR-096 for one thing:** its narrowing of ADR-074 point 5 once a command's act is made, on which point 6's first paragraph rests.
- **The command that moves an issue changes with it, and its tests hold each case the points decide,** in a change that rests on this record once it is accepted.
- **Documents follow, each passage by what it says,** not by the document it sits in:
  - **A passage whose subject is the forward cascade, and which says which ancestors it moves,** carries this record's limit, or sits beside a passage that does. A passage sits beside another in the same section, or as a use case's variant sits beside the step it names.
  - **Such a passage that the capability ships with its commands** changes in the change that builds this record, with the code it ships beside, so that what ships says what the shipped commands do. The workflow schema's account of the forward cascade is such a passage, though DEC-006 names the schema as its rule's encoding, so that encoding changes with the commands rather than with DEC-006's edits.
  - **Any other such passage states the lifecycle's rule,** and changes in this record's change, with the DEC-006 edits, marking what the build has yet to make where its document marks such gaps.
  - **A passage that names the cascade by the effect it is for stands,** such as a view's overview, or a diagram's node, relation or description, though it says that the issues above are brought forward. It says what the cascade is for, and leaves which ancestors it moves to DEC-006 and to the passage that tells how the cascade works. So does a passage whose subject is something else, such as a close or one run given as an example, wherever it sits.
- **No check finds later a container that stands behind the work under it.** The report is the signal for each ancestor left, and the next forward move under the container, or the move made again, brings it level once a read places it. A container that stays behind with no warning, as one moved back after the first read, or a parent given after the walk was planned that nothing read, has no signal until then. A check that lists the containers behind the work under them is left for later (Alternatives).
- **No migration is owed.** Nothing an adopter holds is renamed, removed or restructured. What the build changes, such as the walk going on past an ancestor the cascade cannot place, and the codes of a dry run and of a declined run, is a surface change of the capability, declared by a changeset in the change that builds it.
