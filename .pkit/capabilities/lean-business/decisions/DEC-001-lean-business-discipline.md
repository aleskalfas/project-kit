**In plain terms:** the capability guides a person, or a team, through the lean method of validating a business model. They sketch a canvas for each way the business could work, judge it at the desk, rank what is riskiest in it, declare an experiment before it runs, read its result against what was declared, and end each cycle, 90 days by default and one at a time for an idea, in persevere, pivot or pause for the variant that did best. Being away costs nothing: nothing expires, and a person who comes back finds, from the files alone, where they left off and what waits on them. The capability carries the method's work toward product/market fit, and the method's growth stage joins it later. The method comes from *Running Lean* (third edition, Ash Maurya), credited and told in the capability's own words. The capability ships no passage of the book and no wording of the canvas template, and nothing it ships is under the template's licence. It needs no other capability, and meets three others only at seams it names.

## Context

A person with a business idea needs to learn, cheaply and early, whether it can work, before building it. *Running Lean* (third edition, Ash Maurya, 2022) gives a method for that. It models the business on a one-page canvas, filters the model at the desk, ranks the riskiest assumptions, and tests them in time-boxed experiments inside 90-day cycles. Progress is *traction*, evidence that people outside the team care, not features built (introduction, A Traction-First Approach Is the New Way Forward). The third edition puts the canvas and its desk checks before the loop of hypothesis and test, since that loop only helps once a reasonable idea goes in (introduction, Don't Start with an MVP).

The method resists capture in places:
- **Most of its quality rules are judgements,** such as whether a problem is framed against a real alternative. Data can hold the links and the tags, and a person judges the content.
- **Its order is free in one place and fixed in another.** The first sketch has no order, and the desirability review has a strict one.
- **Its numbers are loose.** Several figures disagree with the text (for one each, ch. 3, Steve estimates how many active customers he'll need; ch. 4, Steve Charts His Traction Roadmap, figure 4-4), so its guidelines warn and do not refuse.
- **Its canvas template is published under Creative Commons BY-SA 3.0** (Part I, figure I-1), and adapts the Business Model Canvas, published under the same licence.

The core layer supplies the capability itself, an opt-in installable discipline (COR-017) shipped from an origin (COR-031), and the test of what every project needs (COR-014). It supplies the dependencies a capability declares (COR-030), and connection points addressed by role (COR-053). The records of the capability's data and process name what else they receive.

This record decides what the capability carries and leaves out, who decides in it, its name, how it credits its sources and stays clear of their licence, its words, its seams with other capabilities, and how it meets a person who comes back after time away. The record of its data (DEC-002) and the record of its process (DEC-003) decide the rest.

## Decision

**The lean-business capability carries the lean method's loop, from a canvas to product/market fit and, later, growth. It tells the method in its own words, credits its sources, ships nothing under their licence, keeps every judgement the person's, orients a person who comes back from the files alone, and works with no other capability installed.**

### 1. What the capability carries

The capability carries the lean method of validating a business model, as *Running Lean* (third edition) teaches it: model the business, find what is riskiest in the model, test it, and repeat each cycle until the evidence decides.
- **The loop it guides:**
  - the idea's goal, which every variant's viability is judged against
  - a canvas for each variant of the idea, with the desk verdicts of desirability, viability and feasibility
  - the assumptions drawn from a canvas and its viability estimate, ranked by risk
  - an experiment on the riskiest, declared before it runs, with an expectation it could fail and a time box
  - its result, held against what was declared, and the next action it leads to
  - the cycle the experiments run in, one at a time for an idea and exploring one or more of its shortlisted variants, and its review, which names the variant that did best. Its window is sized when it is planned, 90 days by default. Life may suspend it, and nothing extends it.
- **Its stages.** It carries the work of the method's first stages, from the first canvas toward product/market fit. The gates that mark a stage reached, customer/problem fit, problem/solution fit and product/market fit, come later with the parts that judge them (point 2), and so does how the capability records a stage reached. Growth, the method's work once paying customers arrive, extends this capability when it comes, and never becomes a capability of its own, so one capability holds the whole method.
- **What belongs here.** A part of the method belongs in the capability when it helps a person decide, on evidence, whether a business model works. A tool for running the business once it works, such as invoicing or a customer database, does not.

### 2. What it leaves for later

Some of the method's parts wait until a real use needs them (COR-007), each joining by a record of its own:
- **the team's rituals:** campaign proposals written alone and voted on, sprints, and the six meetings of a cycle
- **problem discovery's tools:** the interview script, the customer forces canvas for each interview, and the segments grouped from them
- **the offer** and its gates
- **the stage gates:** customer/problem fit, problem/solution fit and product/market fit, and where an idea's stage is recorded
- **planning and pitching** beyond the first stage's target: the rollout plan and the pitch
- **growth,** as point 1 says

Until then a person runs these outside the capability, and records what they found as an experiment's result. Problem discovery in particular is generative experiments (DEC-002 point 9), so the capability holds their declarations and results, though not their interviews. The book gives it up to two sprints (ch. 7, Running a Mafia Offer Campaign), so it runs as two experiments of two weeks at most, a broad one and then a narrow one.

### 3. The person judges, the skill guides, the commands hold what data can

- **The person decides** each question the method leaves to judgement: each desk verdict, which variants go on and which a cycle explores, how the assumptions rank, each experiment's declaration and next action, whether and since when life stopped a cycle and when it resumes, and each cycle's decision. The record of its process says which of these are moves of a process, and how the commands hold each to the person (DEC-003).
- **The skill carries the judgements.** A composite skill, paired with the commands, asks the method's questions in the method's order where it fixes one, such as the desirability review's, and leaves the order free where it is free, such as the first sketch. It explains, proposes and drafts.
- **The commands hold what data can.** They make and change the records, the content around each judgement as well as the judgement: each entry of a canvas and its links, each input of the estimate and the traction target, each assumption, and each field of a draft experiment has a command that writes it, gives it the ids it needs, and refuses at once what the data shows is wrong. They warn where the method gives only a guideline (DEC-002 point 14).
- **Four ways in:**
  - a conversation with an agent through the skill, the main way in: the capability's lean coach, or any agent that follows the skill, the skill drafting through the commands (refinement per [lean-business:DEC-004-lean-business-coach])
  - the commands, at a terminal
  - the data files, readable in the repository, where an edit by hand is held by the check as a command's write is (DEC-002 point 14)
  - a view built from the data, later (point 8)

### 4. The name

The capability is named `lean-business`, and its commands also answer to the short alias `lean`.
- **It names the discipline, not the book.** The book's own notice names "Running Lean" a trademark of its publisher, and "Lean Canvas" and LEANSTACK may be marks too. So the capability refers to them and is named after none.
- **It names the whole method.** Growth extends this capability (point 1), so a name for one stage, such as experiments alone, would fit it for one release.
- **Its meaning is said once.** The capability's README says that it means validating a business model by the lean method, not running a business lean.

### 5. Its sources, credited, and the book's text kept out

- **The credit.** The capability's README credits *Running Lean* (third edition, Ash Maurya, 2022) as the source of its method, and the Lean Canvas, Ash Maurya's adaptation of Alex Osterwalder's Business Model Canvas, as the source of its canvas.
- **In its own words.** Every description, hint, question, message and example the capability ships is written in its own words. The capability rests on the reading that copyright protects how the book expresses the method, not the method itself, so a capability that tells the method in its own words needs no permission. That is the reading this record rests on, not legal advice.
- **No passage of the book shipped.** The capability ships no passage of the book: not in its records, schemas, skill, tests or messages. Its skill writes no passage of the book into a project's files.

### 6. The licence: nothing ships under the canvas template's licence

Every file the capability ships is under the methodology's own licence, and none is under the template's Creative Commons BY-SA 3.0.
- **The fields.** The canvas's schema uses the template's box names as its field names, since a name says what a box holds. Each field's description is in the capability's own words.
- **Nothing of the template's wording.** No prompt, question, hint or example of the template is copied into a schema, a skill or a message.
- **The grid.** The terminal draws a canvas as a grid arranged as in the book:
  - five columns of the canvas's boxes, in the book's order, with the README giving which box lies where
  - along the bottom: cost structure and revenue streams
  - under the grid, a line that credits the Lean Canvas and the Business Model Canvas it adapts, and names the licence they are published under, as the template carries such a line
- **The premise.** The box names and their arrangement are taken as no protected expression of the template, so neither the schema nor the grid is an adaptation of it, and the share-alike terms do not reach them. The line credits where the arrangement comes from and tells a reader where the template and its licence are. It claims no licence for the grid.
- **A file under that licence is a decision of its own.** A template, a schema or a page that carried the template's wording would put a file under another licence among the methodology's files. It needs a record before it ships, and this record makes none.

### 7. Its words

The terms the capability's records use:
- **An idea:** one business a person explores, with one goal. It holds one or more variants.
- **A variant:** one story of how the idea makes a business, told on one canvas, of one archetype: *direct*, where the customer pays; *multisided*, where one side pays for another's use; or *marketplace*, where the business brings buyers and sellers together.
- **A canvas:** the one-page model of a variant, in the boxes of the Lean Canvas (point 6), each box holding entries.
- **A side:** one group of customers that a multisided or marketplace model serves, each with segments of its own. A *segment* is one kind of customer the canvas names in its customer-segments box.
- **A verdict:** a person's yes or no on one desk step of a variant. It is a desk note, a cheap filter before any evidence, and never a result.
- **The estimate:** what the idea's goal asks of a variant, worked out through the customer factory from the canvas's inputs: how many customers it needs, and how fast it must win them as others leave.
- **An assumption:** something the model takes for granted that no evidence supports yet, the book's *leap of faith* (ch. 6, Seven Habits for Highly Effective Experiments, habit 5): an entry of the canvas, or an input of its estimate.
- **An experiment:** one test of one assumption, declared before it runs and judged against what it declared.
- **A cycle:** the window, sized when it is planned and 90 days by default, in which experiments on one or more of an idea's shortlisted variants run toward its goal, ending in a review that names the *winning variant*, the one that did best. An idea runs one cycle at a time.
- **A suspension:** a stretch in which the person's life, not the work, stopped a running cycle. Its days count toward no window, and toward no time box of an experiment that had not started by its first day. An experiment already running keeps its dates, since the world went on answering it.
- **The customer factory:** the steps a customer passes through, acquisition, activation, retention, revenue and referral, in the method's model of a business.

**Words it does not use.** For a group of customers it says *side* or *segment*, never *actor*, and it names nothing a *journey*. Those words are the software-analysis capability's, for the roles that use a system and their paths through it ([software-analysis:DEC-001-software-analysis-discipline] point 1). A canvas models a business, not a system.

### 8. Independent, with three seams

The capability works with no other capability installed, and declares no dependency (COR-030).
- **Work tracking.** Where a work tracker is installed, the capability meets it only through the work-tracking role, never by a capability's name (COR-053). By default an experiment and a cycle link to no work item. Whether one is tracked as a work item, or beside one, is decided by a later record, once a real use asks for it.
- **Evidence.** An experiment's result says where its evidence lies, in words or by a link. A later record may let a result cite the evidence capability's records, in that capability's own form ([evidence:DEC-001-citation-discipline]).
- **A view beyond the terminal.** The terminal is the capability's view. A page, drawn by a capability that draws pages or by a command of this one, is a later record's choice, and reads the status's structured form, whose version keeps such a reader from breaking (DEC-002 point 15).

### 9. Shipped with the methodology, and a project's ideas stay the project's

- **Shipped by the methodology** (COR-031), and installed by a project only when it asks for it (COR-017).
- **A project's ideas are the project's.** Its ideas live in its own files, outside the capability's folder (DEC-002 point 1), so no project's idea ships with the capability, and uninstalling it removes none of them.
- **The recorded location goes with the capability.** It lies in the capability's own configuration, inside the capability's folder, which uninstalling removes (COR-017). So a project that recorded a folder other than the default records it again after installing the capability anew, before any command writes, or the next command that writes records the default.

### 10. A person who comes back finds where they left off

A person may leave an idea for days or months, and the capability is built for it.
- **Being away costs nothing.** Nothing expires. An experiment past its end, or a cycle past its window, waits for the person, and nothing is decided while they are away. When life interrupts a running cycle, the person suspends it, then or on coming back, and its days count toward no window, and toward no time box not yet started. An experiment already running keeps its dates and comes due on them, and its result waits for the person (DEC-002 point 12).
- **The files alone orient them.** The status of an idea is a briefing, worked out from the files each time it is shown and kept nowhere, so it rests on no one's memory, the person's or an agent's. It gives:
  - after a long silence, first, an offer to record the gap as a suspension, where the cycle can take one under the rules of DEC-002 point 12, from the day after their last recorded work on it, with what it would do to the cycle's end. It is an offer, never an act: only the person suspends a cycle (DEC-003 point 5). It comes first, since a suspension covers no day on which work on the cycle was recorded, so the gap is best recorded before the person works again.
  - their last act, and the day of it
  - what was planned: the goal, and the cycle's objective, key results and constraint, with how far it has come
  - what runs and is unfinished, with each experiment's declared expectation beside the result it waits for
  - what waits on them, in the order it is best taken, what others wait on first, each with the command that takes it
- **Across ideas,** the status gives one line for each idea.
- **One briefing for the person and the agent.** An agent in a session reads the same briefing before it proposes anything, so the two start from one account of where things stand.
- **What the build sets:** how long a silence must be before the briefing makes its offer, and how the briefing is laid out.

## Rationale

**Why this method, and this edition.** The method gives one loop with a clear finish, a decision on evidence, and fits a person working alone as well as a team. The third edition puts the desk work before the experiments, so a person stops a weak idea before testing it, and it runs the loop inside a fixed cycle, which gives the capability its subjects: a variant, an experiment and a cycle.

**Why one capability for the whole method.** Growth runs the same cycle over new artefacts, on the same canvas and goal. A capability of its own would split one model across two and make it depend on this one.

**Why the person judges.** The method's own rules are mostly judgements about the person's business. A wrong verdict there costs the person months, and no check of files can tell a sound one from a hopeful one. So the agent explains and proposes, and the person decides (DEC-003 point 5).

**Why a command for every write.** The methodology the capability ships in writes its own records through commands, and the capability follows it. A person at a terminal and an agent through the skill then take one path, ids are given rather than typed, and a broken link or a wrong id shows when it is written, not at the next check. The files stay plain data, so an edit by hand stays possible, and the check holds it as it holds a command's write.

**Why its own words, and the book's text kept out.** On the reading this record rests on, the method is free to carry and its expression is the author's. Quotation would rest on an exception that keeps the extent to what a purpose needs, and a public repository of passages that walks the whole method is the weakest case for it. Words of its own also let the capability's text follow the capability as it changes.

**Why nothing under the template's licence.** A file under that licence would put the methodology's files under two licences, and would bind each copy and adaptation of it to the same terms, apart from every other file. Box names name what a box holds, which a schema needs, and descriptions in the capability's words carry none of the template's wording. The grid keeps the arrangement a reader of the book recognises at a glance, and its line credits where the arrangement comes from. The premise of point 6 is a reading this record rests on, as point 5's is, and a record of its own revisits it if it is found not to hold.

**Why `lean-business`.** It names the whole method and its object, a business, and is short enough to type, with `lean` shorter still. It names neither the book nor a mark.

**Why independent.** A person exploring an idea may have no work tracker and no documentation, and a repository may exist only to explore ideas. The seams let a later record connect the capability where it helps, without either side naming the other.

**Why the files orient a person who comes back.** The method is meant for people who work on an idea in the hours a job leaves too, as the book's main character does (introduction, Catch-22), so a long silence is a common case. A person back after weeks remembers little of what they expected, and an agent in a new session remembers nothing. A briefing worked out from the files serves both, and cannot drift from them. Setting the declared expectation beside the result it waits for keeps the expectation the one fixed before the run, not the one memory supplies after it. Nothing expires, since a deadline that closed a subject in the person's absence would decide for them (point 3).

**Why the gap is offered, never recorded.** Whether a silence was life interrupting, or work the files do not show, only the person knows. Recording it as a suspension gives the cycle back its days, so it is the person's to say, and the agent's part is to offer it with what it would change. The threshold of a long silence is left to the build, since only use shows what "long" means for a person working on an idea part-time.

### Alternatives considered

- **`lean-experiments`.** Rejected. It covers the experiments alone, while most of the first release is the canvas and its desk stage.
- **`business-model-validation`,** with growth as a capability of its own. Not taken. The name is long, and growth would split one model across two capabilities.
- **A name after the book or the canvas,** such as `running-lean` or `lean-canvas`. Rejected. Either may be a mark, and names a source rather than the discipline.
- **Shipping the canvas template, credited, under its own licence.** Not taken. It would put a file under another licence among the methodology's, and needs a record of its own. Box names and descriptions in the capability's words serve the schema without it.
- **Quoting the book where it says a thing best.** Rejected. Quotations would accumulate across files into a walk of the whole method.
- **A briefing kept as a file, written as the person acts.** Rejected. It would be a second home for facts the files already hold, and would drift from them.
- **A gap recorded as a suspension without the person, after a long silence.** Rejected. A silence may be work the files do not show, and only the person knows which.
- **Deadlines that close a subject while the person is away,** such as an experiment closed or a cycle paused once its date is long past. Rejected. They would decide in the person's absence what only the person decides.
- **A page as the view from the start.** Not taken. A first loop holds one canvas, a ranked list and one experiment, which a terminal shows well. A page earns its place once a cycle runs for weeks with many experiments, and building it now builds ahead of the need, which the method itself warns against.

## Implications

- **The capability is stamped** through the capability-author skill, and its package declares the alias `lean` and no dependency.
- **Its README** credits *Running Lean* (third edition), the Lean Canvas and the Business Model Canvas, says what the name means, and gives the layout of an idea's files, how one loop runs, the briefing's layout, and how long a silence must be before the briefing offers to record it (point 10).
- **DEC-002 and DEC-003** decide the data and the process. The parts point 2 leaves for later each need a record of their own before they are built.
- **The terminal grid** carries the credit and licence line of point 6, in the capability's own words beside the names it credits.
- **No accepted record is edited.** This record decides nothing against a record the project receives from elsewhere.
