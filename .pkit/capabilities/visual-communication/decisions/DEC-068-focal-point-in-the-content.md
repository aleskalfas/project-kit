**In plain terms:** the rule on one focal point, `RS-GRAPHIC-001`, judges what the eye meets first among the parts of a page that show its point, and never takes the page's title for it. A page's title and its lead state the point. They rank above the rest of the page, and nothing outranks them. The focal point is what shows the point: it is seen first after them, and the rest ranks below it. A group of equal parts may be the focal point as one. Where the content ranks its parts, no part is the focal point only because it comes first in the order of reading. On a page with no lead, such as a cover, the title is judged with the rest, and the page's frame is left out everywhere. The rule fits every kind of piece its set judges: on a page with no single point, what matters most is what the page's job needs, a poster or a web page has a lead only where its content gives a text under its title that states its point, and a figure's caption is judged with the rest of the figure. This record refines the two points of the record of the principles that read the rule the other way.

## Context

The record of the principles names visual hierarchy, and the designer's squint test, as the sources of `RS-GRAPHIC-001` ([visual-communication:DEC-028-principles-of-visual-communication] point 8). It says that `COMM` asks whether a page says its point, and this rule whether the eye finds it first. Its point 4 gives "whether the eye finds the point first" as an example of what only the drawn page shows.

A page's title and its lead state its point. The title names what the page is about, and the lead is the sentence under the title that states the point, such as what fills a deck's `lead` slot. A figure's caption counts as its lead (`RS-COMM-001`). A lead is the text a page's content gives to state its point, and whether a page has one is read from its content, since no field declares it ([visual-communication:DEC-029-piece-brief] point 2).

Read so, the eye finds the point first on any page whose title is seen first, and a title at the top of a page is seen first on nearly every page that has one. The review's first calibration graded this rule more leniently than its outside judges, and most of those grades named the title as what is seen first ([project-kit#3230](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3230)). On 17 November 2026, asked whether the focal point is a page's title, which states the point and is seen first, or lies in the content below the title and the lead, the maintainer chose the content ([project-kit#3396](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3396)).

The rule on running text, `RS-GRAPHIC-008`, leaves which text ranks first to `RS-GRAPHIC-001` ([visual-communication:DEC-046-faults-lower-grades] point 4). So the rule still judges that the title and the lead rank first.

The words it uses:
- **A page's frame** is what its layout draws alike on every page, such as its title bar and its footer. `RS-GRAPHIC-007` leaves it out, since it is the same on every page (DEC-028, Rationale, "Why the form rule's wording").
- **What shows the point** is the part of a page, or the group of parts, that bears out what its title and its lead state, such as a slide's main visual.
- **A page with no single point** is a page of a piece that does not explain, such as a map of a system, a board people fill in or a mock-up of a screen (DEC-028 point 3).

## Decision

**`RS-GRAPHIC-001`'s focal point lies in a page's content. On a page with a title and a lead, the two state the point, rank above the rest and are outranked by nothing, and the focal point is what shows the point, seen first after them. The rule's words say so, reach every kind of piece `GRAPHIC` judges, and anchor the points they rest on.**

### 1. The focal point is what shows the point, seen first after the title and the lead

- **The title and the lead rank first.** On a page with a title and a lead, the two state the page's point. They rank above the rest of the page, and nothing outranks them. So the rule judges which text ranks first, as `RS-GRAPHIC-008` leaves it to.
- **The focal point lies in the rest of the page.** It is what shows the point. It is seen first after the title and the lead, and size, weight, colour and position rank the rest below it. A part that outranks it breaks the rule, as a part that outranks the title and the lead does.
- **A page with no lead.** On a page whose layout gives it no lead, such as a cover, or whose content leaves its lead empty, nothing beside the title states the point. Its title is judged with the rest, and may be the focal point itself.
- **The frame is left out,** as `RS-GRAPHIC-007` leaves it. The title and the lead are no part of it, wherever the layout sets them.
- **Its sources.** Visual hierarchy, which DEC-028 point 8 names, as Robin Williams teaches it: hierarchy is made by contrast, so two parts that are not the same are made very different, by their size, weight, colour, value or the space around them (*The Non-Designer's Design Book*; the design knowledge's topic on hierarchy). So the rule asks a page to set what shows its point apart from the rest by such contrast, once its title and its lead have stated the point. Michael Alley's assertion–evidence structure supports the reading, and is no new source of the rule: a slide's headline asserts its claim, and its body is the visual evidence for it (*The Craft of Scientific Presentations*). DEC-028 names him as a source of `RS-COMM-001` alone (point 6).

### 2. A group may be the focal point, and the order of reading alone makes none

- **A group of equal parts.** Where the content gives parts as equals, such as three equal options, small multiples or the steps of a process, what shows the point is the group, and it may be the focal point as one. The eye takes parts that lie close, look alike or share a region as one group (Max Wertheimer's laws of grouping; Stephen Palmer's common region; the design knowledge's topic on hierarchy). To rank one of them above the others would add a difference the content lacks, which `RS-GRAPHIC-007` asks a page not to draw.
- **Not by the order of reading alone.** Where the content ranks its parts, no part is the focal point only because it comes first in the order of reading. Without other cues the eye enters a page where its writing starts (Edmund Arnold's Gutenberg diagram; the design knowledge's topic on where reading starts). So a part met first only so is met first whatever its rank, and the page has ranked nothing.
- **Position still ranks.** A part's place on the page ranks it, beside its size, weight and colour, where the place sets it apart, as Rudolf Arnheim counts a part's isolation and its place in the frame among what gives it weight (the same topic). So this clause and the rule's "Size, weight, colour and position" say one thing.

### 3. The rule reaches every kind of piece its set judges

`GRAPHIC` judges every piece, whatever its purpose (DEC-028 point 3), and the rule's words reach each kind:
- **A page with no single point** has no point to show. What matters most on it is what its job needs most, as `RS-GRAPHIC-007` reads emphasis on such a page. It has no lead, so its title is judged with the rest.
- **A page with no lead in a deck's sense,** such as a poster or a web page, has a lead where its content gives one: a text under its title that states the page's point, read from its content (DEC-029 point 2). A poster's line under its headline and a web page's sentence under its heading are such texts where they state the point. The page's title and its lead then rank first, and its focal point lies in the rest. Where its content gives none, its title is judged with the rest, as on a cover. A web page's frame is what its site draws alike on every page, such as a shared header and a footer.
- **A figure's caption,** which `RS-COMM-001` counts as its lead, is judged with the rest of the figure. It lies by the picture it describes, and is met after it: where one part dominates a page, the eye enters there, as most readers entered a printed page through its dominant picture (Mario Garcia and Pegie Stark's EyeTrack; the topic on where reading starts). A rule that asked a caption to outrank its picture would fail every figure drawn as figures are.

### 4. The shape of the rule's words

- **The shape DEC-028 gives.** The rule judges every piece (DEC-028 point 3). It says apart what the text and the drawing show (DEC-028 point 4): nothing from the text, since what is seen first shows only once the page is drawn, and the whole rule on the drawn page. It binds on nothing until a piece's content is an artefact (DEC-028 point 5).
- **Its origin is this record,** and it anchors the points it rests on: DEC-028 points 3, 4 and 8, the lead's definition in DEC-029 point 2 and in `RS-COMM-001`, and this record's points 1 to 3. So a change to how a lead is defined flags the rule to be read again, as [visual-communication:DEC-046-faults-lower-grades] point 7 has a rule anchor the points it rests on.
- **It names no grade.** A rule's text names a grade only where a break of it blocks a bar ([visual-communication:DEC-042-grades-bar-and-loop] points 1 and 2), and no source calls a break of this rule unworkable.
- **Its statement is its set's.** This record gives the reason for each clause, and `GRAPHIC` holds the words, as DEC-028 has it for every rule ("Why the reasons live in a record"). A rewording after the change that lands them is made in the set, by the set's own path.

## Rationale

**Why the content, and not the title.** A title at the top of a page is seen first on nearly every page that has one. A rule that took it for the focal point would be met by every such page, and would judge nothing the layout does not already give. What a page's design decides is whether the part that bears out its title and its lead is seen next, before what matters less, as Alley's structure has a slide's body be the evidence of its headline. DEC-028's "the eye finds the point first" reads the title, which states the point, as what the eye should find first. This record reverses that reading, so it is a record and not an edit in place.

**Why the title and the lead still rank first.** Left out with no clause of their own, they would leave a page whose body outranks its headline unjudged, and `RS-GRAPHIC-008`'s hand-over of which text ranks first would land on no rule. Read so, the rule judges both orders a page gives: its title and its lead before the rest, and what shows the point before what matters less.

**Why a group may be the focal point.** Three equal options, or the panels of small multiples, show a point together. A rule that asked one of them to stand out would fail every such page, or have its designer draw a difference the content lacks.

**Why the order of reading alone makes no focal point.** The direction of writing is where the eye falls back when nothing stands out, so it ranks every page alike. A rule met by whatever part comes first in the order of reading would pass a page that ranks nothing. Position stays among the means that rank, where it sets a part apart, so the rule keeps what its own words name.

**Why the frame is left out.** It is the same on every page, as the form rule's reason has it, so it is no part of what a page's own point needs first. A fix to it belongs to the layout of every page.

**Why a page's job stands for its point where it has none.** DEC-028 has `RS-GRAPHIC-003` speak of a page's job, not its message, so that it fits a piece that does not explain (point 9), and `RS-GRAPHIC-007`'s emphasis follows a page's job on a page with no single point (Rationale, "Why the form rule's wording"). The focal point follows the same line, so the set reads one way for every piece.

**Why the rule anchors the lead's definition.** Its first clause rests on what a lead is. A change to that definition, in DEC-029 point 2 or in `RS-COMM-001`, could move which text ranks first on every page, and the anchor has the rule read again then.

### Alternatives considered

- **The title as the focal point,** as DEC-028 point 8 reads. Rejected: every page whose title is seen first would meet the rule.
- **The title and the lead left out, and not ranked.** Rejected: a page whose body outranks its title and its lead would pass, and `RS-GRAPHIC-008`'s hand-over would land on no rule.
- **The order of reading ranks nothing.** Rejected: the rule's own words name position among what ranks, so the clause would take back what they say.
- **One part only, never a group.** Rejected: a page of equal options would fail, or gain a difference its content lacks.
- **A figure's caption ranked with a title.** Rejected: it would fail every figure whose picture is seen before its caption.
- **An edit to DEC-028 in place, with no record of its own.** Rejected: it reverses what DEC-028 point 8 says the rule asks, and an edit in place only refines what a record decides.
