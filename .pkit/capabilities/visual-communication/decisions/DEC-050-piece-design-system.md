**In plain terms:** before any page of a piece is designed, the designer decides the piece's *design system*: its grid, its type scale, its spacing, what each colour means, one look for each kind of container, and the lines and signs that draw relations. It chooses them from what the piece's libraries offer, and writes them in the brief, each with its reason. Every page then takes its type, colour and space from the system. A change to the system sends every page through its stage again.

## Context

The designer's method orders the choices on one page: the structure, then the form, then the hierarchy, and last type, colour and space ([visual-communication:DEC-030-design-agents] point 3). Nothing decides those last three for the piece as a whole. So each page was designed and mended alone, and the pages did not read as one piece. A colour meant one thing on one page and another on the next.

A brief has a part for the whole piece and a part for each page ([visual-communication:DEC-029-piece-brief] point 3). The brief's part for the whole piece holds goals, relations and reasons, and no choice the pages share.

The design process makes the system a stage of its own, before the pages ([visual-communication:DEC-047-design-process] point 2). This record says what that stage decides and where it is written.

## Decision

**A piece's design system is the set of choices its pages share. It is decided before the pages, from what the libraries offer, and the brief's part for the whole piece holds it.**

### 1. What a design system is

A piece's *design system* is the choice, among what the piece's libraries offer, of:
- its grid;
- its type scale;
- its spacing;
- its colour roles, with what each colour means on every page;
- one look for each kind of container;
- the lines and signs that draw relations, such as a flow or a level.

It names roles of the libraries, and never a value. A value stays the brand's ([visual-communication:DEC-001-visual-communication-discipline] point 5).

### 2. Decided before the pages, from what the libraries offer

- **Before any page.** The system stage ends before the pages stage begins (DEC-047 point 2).
- **From the libraries.** The designer chooses among the roles the brand, the notation and the component libraries under the piece's lock offer.
- **Where they offer too little,** the designer takes one of the three ways of DEC-030 point 4: it extends a library through its owner, makes a local component, or stops. A stop there stops the process (DEC-047 point 5).

### 3. Written in the brief, each choice with its reason

- **Where.** The brief's part for the whole piece holds the system.
- **What each choice says.** It names the role of the libraries that carries it, and its reason, as any reason of a brief names the principle it rests on (DEC-029 point 2).
- **A piece of one page** has a system too, since what it draws must still agree.

### 4. Every page takes from the system

- **Type, colour and space.** A page takes them from the system, never apart from it.
- **The check reports a page that does not.** A page that uses a role the system does not name asks for attention. It does not fail, since a true one-off may stay in a piece (DEC-001 point 4).

### 5. A changed system reaches every page

Each page's evidence names the system it was designed under ([visual-communication:DEC-048-stage-evidence-and-refusals] point 3). So a change to the system sends every page through its stage again.

## Rationale

**Why before the pages.** A professional decides the system once and then applies it. Pages that each choose their own type and colour can each be sound and still not read as one piece.

**Why in the brief.** The brief already holds why a piece is designed as it is. The system is the first of those reasons, and the reviewer reads it there against the drawing.

**Why roles and not values.** A value in the brief would be a second brand. Naming roles keeps the look in the libraries, where a fix reaches every piece.

**Why attention and not a failure.** Whether a page may step outside the system is a judgement. The check can see that it did, and a reviewer weighs whether it should.

### Alternatives considered

- **The system left to the designer's method, unwritten.** Rejected: that is what gave pages mended alone.
- **The system as a file of its own.** Not taken: the brief is where a piece's reasons live, and a second file would need its own schema and reader.
- **The system held as values.** Rejected: it would copy the brand into the piece.
- **A page outside the system fails.** Not taken: a one-off is sometimes right.

- **No migration.** The system is a new part of the brief, and a brief without it reads as before (COR-010).
