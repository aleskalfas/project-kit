**In plain terms:** a *calibration* is the test that shows whether the capability's reviewer judges design rightly. Until one is accepted, a change to a piece runs its stages once, one review is made, and its findings go to the person who answers for the piece, each with its picture. No agent takes those findings, or any other critic's, and changes the pages again by itself. After a review report, or any other reading of a piece, no change to that piece starts without the person's word. Nor does a change to a library or a brand it draws with. No reading of one piece chooses the change to another piece either. The word counts once the person was shown that report or reading, and the pages. The commands refuse what they can see, which is a change after a review report, and the session's rule alone holds the rest. This record also says when the reviewer counts as calibrated, how a calibration lapses or is withdrawn, where the designs a calibration grades stand, and when a calibration may run. About the designer's loop after a calibration it decides nothing: the landing change that accepts one decides how that loop runs from then on.

**What it overturns.** For every project, and only until the reviewer is calibrated, part of the record of the design agents: a designer that another session dispatched starts no review, and the designer makes no round by itself, where that record has it mend for up to two rounds on a finding. Design is never frozen. It goes one run at a time, with the person between the runs.

## Context

The design process orders a piece's own stages ([visual-communication:DEC-047-design-process]). Its review stage has the reviewer read the pages, and the designer mend what it found, round after round ([visual-communication:DEC-030-design-agents] point 2).

A redesign was once started that way before any calibration had tested the reviewer. The first calibration found no design good enough, the professional ones included, and its verdicts on the same image split from one reading to the next ([visual-communication:DEC-042-grades-bar-and-loop], Context). Rounds steered by such findings do not move toward good work. They move toward what the reviewer happens to say. Every stage of the piece itself was in order, and nothing could have refused the redesign.

The designer mends what it judges right, and stops after two rounds on a finding (DEC-030 point 2). The record of the review's grades hands that on for the time before a calibration (DEC-042 point 9). Two rounds on each finding are rounds that an untested reviewer chose. The rules on words were where the first calibration did worst, and cuts of words were the main difference among the designs it graded (DEC-042, Context), so a loop of word changes is no safer than a loop of layouts.

Whoever maintains the capability decided that the calibration comes first, since otherwise an untested reviewer can push the design the wrong way, and that the process must not allow the same mistake again.

A calibration gives its evidence rule by rule (DEC-042 point 10). So a calibrated reviewer is calibrated for some rules and not for others. A calibration's evidence is on grades, and none of it is on a fault as such, a brief finding or a note of taste.

## Decision

**Until the reviewer is calibrated, a reading of a piece's design goes to the person and chooses no next change, to that piece or to any other, or to a library or a brand either draws with. After a review report or a reading of a piece, no change to that piece starts without the person's word, nor one to a library or a brand it draws with. The word is given once the person was shown that report or reading, and the pages. One word reaches ahead: a named series of rounds on one piece, asked by a person who was told what an uncalibrated reviewer means. A command says whether the reviewer is calibrated, and the stage commands, the pass and the merge check refuse what they can see. About the designer's loop after a calibration this record decides nothing.**

Words this record takes from the record of the review's grades, each as that record defines it:
- **A calibration** is a test of the reviewer on a *ladder*, a series of designs of one piece that an outside judge ranked first (DEC-042, Context and point 9). A *rung* is one design of a ladder.
- **A project's bar** is the grade the project asks its pages to reach, on the rules it names (DEC-042 point 3).
- **The hold** is the switch by which the reviewer's verdict ends the designer's loop at that bar (DEC-042, Context and point 9).
- **A round** is one mend by the designer, then a render, a look and a review of every page (DEC-042 point 6).

Words this record defines, each used in one sense:
- **A reading** is a judgement of a piece's design by any agent or critic other than whoever builds a change to that piece that the person asked for. It may judge the drawn pages, the design system written in the brief, or the rendered markup. The reviewer's is one, and so is any other critic's, and that of the session that runs the process where it dispatched whoever builds the change ([visual-communication:DEC-047-design-process] point 5).
- **Who designs.** What the person asked for starts a change. Whoever builds that change designs by its own judgement, its judgement of the piece's existing pages included. That is the designer, or the session that runs the process where it designs the piece itself.
- **Four things are no reading.**
  - The judgement of the person who answers for the piece is no reading, and where it asks for a change it is a request (point 3).
  - The look of whoever builds the change at pages it just built is its own work (point 2). It starts nothing and waits for nothing, so planned work goes on between such looks.
  - A session that made the change gives no reading of it, after its context was compacted too. Nor does a designer, fresh or not, that builds a change the person asked for, of the pages it was set to change, whoever made them.
  - A judgement of text that states no piece's design, such as a decision record, is outside this record (point 5).
- **Defined here alone.** The other records of the set use the word as defined here.
- **The person** is whoever answers for the piece ([visual-communication:DEC-047-design-process] point 6).
- **A change** to a piece is everything done to its files between two review reports of it, with the stages run on those files. It begins with the first edit after one review report, or with the piece. The next review report closes its edits, and the critique of the files that report read is its last stage (DEC-047 point 2). However many edits or commits it holds, it is one change, with one cause (point 4).
- **Which change a stage belongs to.** A stage run on the files the piece's last review report read belongs to the change that report closed, and starts no new one. So the critique that follows a review needs no cause of its own (point 4). *A change* is defined here alone, and the other records of the set point here.
- **A landing change** is another thing: what a project lets through to its default branch as one unit, which may hold changes to several pieces ([visual-communication:DEC-048-stage-evidence-and-refusals] point 8). This record names a landing change in full every time.
- **A run** is one passage of a change through the stages, from where it enters to the critique (DEC-047 point 4). Three other things are named in full every time. A *reviewer's run* is one start of the reviewer, for a review or for the critique, and its report names its model and effort. A *calibration's run* is one carrying out of a calibration (DEC-042 point 9). A *named series of rounds* is what the person may ask for (point 3).
- **An automatic round** is a round that a reading's findings started, with no word from the person. A round of a named series rests on the person's word, and is not automatic.
- **The person's word** on a piece is one of five things (point 3). Four *start a change*: a named mend, a request in the person's own words, a work item approved after the review report, and a named series of rounds. Each is given after the review report it follows, once the person was shown that report and its pages. The fifth, an acceptance, lets the piece through as it stands and starts none. A command records each. Until that command ships, the session's rule alone asks for the person's word ([visual-communication:DEC-047-design-process] point 7), and no named series runs (point 3).
- **A keep** is the person's saying, at a showing, that the pages shown are the ones later pages are compared with (point 2). An acceptance is a keep, and so is keeping a series' result. Keeping, by itself, starts no change and lets nothing through.

### 1. When the reviewer is calibrated

A calibration is *in force* while three things are true:
- **A calibration report is accepted** (DEC-042 point 9).
- **It was not withdrawn** (point 7).
- **Nothing it measured has changed.** Each item on DEC-042 point 9's list is the same as the accepted calibration recorded: the major of each covered rule set, the covered rules' text, the reviewer's definition, the design knowledge, and the model and effort the review ran on. A command compares each by its digest.

Three things make that comparison exact:
- **The files as the capability ships them.** The digests are of the reviewer's definition, the rules and the design knowledge as the capability ships them. A project's deployed copy of the reviewer, with the project's own paths filled in, is not compared.
- **A project's own additions.** A rule of a project's own set is never covered. Where a project rewords a covered rule in a set of its own, or hands the reviewer design knowledge of its own, the reviewer is not calibrated in that project. No command detects either, since the digests are of shipped files, which a project's own additions leave as they are. Only the session's rule holds this (point 5).
- **The model and the effort, from the report of a reviewer's run.** Each review report, and each report of the critique's own reviewer's run, names both ([visual-communication:DEC-048-stage-evidence-and-refusals] point 3). No command sets the model. Where a command starts the reviewer through the backbone's runner, it sets the effort, and the runner's request has no key for a model (the adapters README, "The agent runner"; DEC-048 point 6). Where a harness lets only a session start the reviewer, a command sets neither. So on every path a command learns the model only from the report. The reviewer is *calibrated* in one of two forms, and both are needed on every path until the runner can be told a model:
  - **Of a reviewer's run.** The reviewer was calibrated for a reviewer's run where a calibration was in force when the run was made, and that run's own report names a model and an effort that calibration covered. The report's entry names that calibration (DEC-048 point 3). A review report of any other reviewer's run is an *uncalibrated review report*, and the evidence says so. For example, a calibration covered one model at high effort. A review made on that model at that effort is a calibrated one. A review of the same pages made on another model is not, though nothing else differs.
  - **Of a project, before a reviewer's run.** A command must also answer before the reviewer starts, when no report yet names a model. The reviewer is calibrated in a project where a calibration is in force, and the last review or critique report recorded in the project came from a reviewer's run made under that same calibration, which the reviewer was calibrated for. A run made under an earlier calibration, withdrawn or lapsed since, does not count, whatever model it ran on. For example, a project's last review ran on the covered model under the calibration now in force, so the reviewer counts as calibrated there when the next change starts. A project with no such report is not there yet. Its next review is made as point 2 says, and where that report names a covered model and effort, the reviewer is calibrated in the project from then on.
  - **Which report is the last.** The reports of every piece in the project are ordered by the time each one's entry records, read from the files as they are. So two clones, and the two sides of a merge, give one answer.
  - **An uncovered model or effort ends it.** A report that names a model or an effort the calibration did not cover makes the reviewer not calibrated in that project, until a report there names a covered one. In the example, the project's next review runs on another model. From then on the reviewer is not calibrated in that project, until a review there runs on the covered model again.
  - **What that costs.** After each calibration, and after each lapse or withdrawal, a project's first review is made the slower way, as point 2 says. That follows from the definition: no report in the project was made under the calibration now in force.
  - **What the bare words mean.** Across the records of this set, and in each passage they write into an accepted record, "until the reviewer is calibrated", "once it is calibrated" and "while it is calibrated", said with nothing more, mean the second form: calibrated in the project. The first form is always said in full, as "calibrated for that reviewer's run". Point 4's condition is narrower than either, and each place that needs it states it whole: the piece's last review report is an uncalibrated one, and the reviewer is not calibrated in the project.

A rule is *covered* where it has its own evidence in the accepted calibration report (DEC-042 point 10). A *covered finding* is an unmarked grade below the level the accepted calibration's bar asked (DEC-042 points 1 and 4), on a covered rule, in a report of a reviewer's run that it was calibrated for. So a finding counts at the level the calibration tested, and at no level nobody tested. Every other finding is *uncovered*: a fault, a brief finding, a note, and a grade on any other rule. The critique and the library parts use these two words ([visual-communication:DEC-049-critique-step] point 5; [visual-communication:DEC-052-library-parts-open-findings] point 3).

*Calibrated in the project* is what the hold needs first. The hold is on for a project where the reviewer is calibrated in it and the project's bar covers only rules the calibration gave evidence for (DEC-042 point 10).

### 2. Until then: the stages once, one review, and the findings go to the person

- **The stages once.** A change runs its stages once, from where it enters (DEC-047 point 4).
- **One review, started by a command or by the session that runs the process.** A designer that another session dispatched never starts it. Where a command can start an agent, it starts the reviewer through the backbone's runner, and records the review report before any agent reads it.
- **Where no command can start an agent,** as where a harness lets only a session start one, the command writes the review's instructions. The session that runs the process starts the reviewer with them, receives the review report as the agent's result, and records it whole through the command before it does anything else. The evidence says that a session started the review.
- **Where the session designs the piece itself,** there is no other session. It starts the reviewer itself, with the command's instructions, and records the review report whole before anything else.
- **What only the rule holds there.** A session that started the review has seen the review report before any command has. That it records the report whole, and chooses no change by it, is kept by the session's rule and by nothing else (point 5).
- **The findings go to the person,** every one, of whatever kind, as the review wrote them. The person is shown the drawn pages, and each finding with its image, before being asked anything about them. Beside them stand the same pages as they stood when the person last kept them or, where no keep is recorded, as the default branch has them (DEC-047 points 6 and 7).
- **No automatic round.** The designer mends nothing from that review report by itself, and no second review follows.
- **No other critic either.** No agent asks another critic to read the pages and then changes them by what it said. After any reading, as after a review report, the next change waits for the person's word (point 4). So a session does not go on "under the plan" after a reading it asked for, or after its own look at pages that a designer it dispatched made.
- **Every kind of change.** This covers a redesign and a change of words alike, and a change to a library or a brand the piece draws with.
- **Every piece.** No reading of one piece chooses the next change to another piece, or to a library or a brand that one draws with. The other piece does not wait (point 4).
- **The designer's own look is the designer's.** While it builds, the designer mends what it sees on its own pages, and what the capability's check fails (DEC-030 point 2; DEC-042 point 6). That look is no reading. Once a review report is recorded, whatever that report names is the person's to name, a fault the designer could have seen for itself included. What the designer then sees for itself, and the report does not name, is a *slip*, such as a picture it left out. It goes to the person with the findings, and its mend waits for the person's word too (point 4). The line is drawn at the review report, since no command can tell afterwards what the designer saw from what the report told it.
- **The piece has its pass all the same.** What a pass needs is DEC-048 point 4's list, which asks nothing of what the findings say, and the verdict informs (DEC-049 point 4). The critique that reads that report belongs to the change the report closed, and needs no cause (point 4). So the piece may land as it is, and be published, with its findings unmended and with no sign-off. Nothing waits for the person until the piece is next changed (point 4).

### 3. What the person may ask for

Four of the person's words start a change. Each is given after the review report it follows, once the person was shown that report and its pages:
- **A named mend.** The person names findings of a review report to mend. The designer mends those, one review follows, and its new findings go to the person again.
- **A request in the person's own words.** The person asks for a change that no review report chose, such as new words, a new page or a newer version of a library. "Carry on with the plan", said of work the person approved earlier, is such a request. It is recorded as the person said it.
- **A work item approved after the review report.** The person approves a work item whose words ask for the change, once shown the piece's last review report and its pages. That approval is the person's word, whoever wrote the item's text. It is recorded with the item it names.
- **A named series of rounds.** The person may ask for rounds all the same. A series is the person's word only where all of these hold:
  - the person asked for it on one named piece, for a number of rounds the person gave;
  - the person had first been told what an uncalibrated reviewer means: its verdicts on the same pages split from one reading to the next, and rounds it chose made pages worse;
  - the capability's command recorded the request before the first round, and keeps the state the piece's files were in then.

  A named series of rounds runs only once the capability's command records it and keeps its starting state. Before that command ships, no series runs. So every series has the starting state its end is compared with.

  Within the series the review's findings choose each mend, because the person asked for that. So inside it the untested reviewer chooses each fix, which is the first mistake again, made with the person's consent. Its rounds rest on the person's word, so they are not automatic rounds. The evidence says they ran under a reviewer that was not calibrated.
- **A series ends with before and after.** The command that records the series notes the state the piece's files were in when it started, and the stage commands keep that state ([visual-communication:DEC-048-stage-evidence-and-refusals] point 3). At its end the person is shown its first pages and its last, side by side, and says whether to keep the result or go back to where the series started. Keeping it is a keep, and going back is a request of the person's.

The fifth word starts no change:
- **An acceptance** lets a piece through as it stands, for the files as they are (DEC-048 point 4). It names no next change, and is the cause of none (point 4).

About the four that start a change:
- **One review report at a time.** A named mend names findings that exist. Any other word given ahead starts nothing. "Mend whatever comes back" names no finding, and a work item approved before a review report has not weighed that report (point 4). A named series is the one word that reaches ahead, and it names its piece and its number.
- **Named mends are not counted out.** The person may name findings of the next review report, and so on. Nothing ends that chain but the person, and each named mend is shown at the merge (DEC-048 point 8).
- **Recorded on the person's word.** A command writes what the person asked for. An agent runs it only once the person has said so, as it records an acceptance (DEC-048 point 4).
- **Refused where the pages were not shown.** The command that records any of the four, or an acceptance, refuses where the command that shows the person that report's pages and findings was not run for it (DEC-047 point 7). A request in the person's own words is guarded like the rest. Otherwise "shall I go on improving the piece?" and "yes" would be recorded as a request, with no page brought up. The refusal catches a showing left out in haste. It cannot tell that the person looked.

Two things are no word of the person's, and start nothing after a review report:
- **A work item approved before the review report.** The person approved it before the review said anything, so the approval has not weighed the report. Once shown the report and its pages, the person says "carry on", and that request is the word.
- **The designer's own statement.** A slip the designer sees for itself is told to the person with the findings (point 2). The designer's statement that it mends one is no cause.

### 4. After an uncalibrated review report, a change starts only on the person's word

This point applies to a piece while its last review report is an uncalibrated one, and the reviewer is not calibrated in the project.
- **By the review report, on every branch.** The evidence is committed with the piece (DEC-048 point 5). So the piece's last review report is the same on the branch that made it and on every later one. A new branch does not start anything again.
- **A cause first.** A stage command runs for that piece, on files changed since that report, only once the change's *cause* is recorded. A cause is one of the four words of the person's that start a change (point 3), given after that review report. A named series serves as the cause of each of its rounds, up to its number.
- **The critique of the reported files needs none.** The critique that follows a review runs on the files its report read. It is the last stage of the change that report closed, and of no new one (definitions). So the refusal never catches it, and before a calibration every piece can get its pass, as point 2 says.
- **Nothing else is a cause.** A work item approved before the report is none. Nor is the designer's statement that it mends a slip, and nor is an acceptance.
- **A slip's mend waits like any other.** The designer tells the person of the slip with the review's findings, and the person asks for its mend, or leaves it. What another agent notices on the pages is a reading, and no slip.
- **One word, one change.** A named mend, a request or an approved work item is the cause of one change, which the next review report ends. The change after that needs a word given after that report.
- **Nothing is counted.** Each change after a review report rests on a word given after it. So no command keeps a count of changes made without one, and only a series counts, its own rounds.
- **Whenever the next change comes.** While this point applies to a piece, its next change waits for the person's word, whatever it is and however much later: a corrected word, a translation or a library's patch among them.
- **What the session's rule asks beyond the commands.** The rule's wait is wider in four ways, and no command sees any of them (DEC-047 point 7).
  - **Any reading starts it.** A reading of a piece starts the wait as a review report does, and the look of a session at pages that a designer it dispatched made is one. The person's word then follows that reading, once the person was shown it and the pages. The look of whoever builds the change at pages it just built starts none.
  - **A library and a brand wait too.** After a review or a reading of a piece, a change to a library or a brand that it draws with waits like a change to the piece. A command sees such a change only on the library's own pages, or once it reaches the piece through its lock (DEC-047 point 1).
  - **How a session knows of a review, before the commands record one.** A review report counts where it is kept for the piece in the clone's local folder, or where the session knows of it. The loop keeps each round's report in that folder (DEC-042, Implications). A session that finds none and knows of none is asked nothing (point 5).
  - **While the reviewer is not calibrated in the project.** The rule waits then even where the piece's own last review report was a calibrated one, which this point's condition leaves out. That is stricter and harms nothing while no calibration is accepted. The decision that comes before a calibration's acceptance revisits the rule's words (point 6).
- **A sister piece and a copy do not wait.** The wait covers the piece read, and a library or a brand it draws with. A review or a reading of one piece starts no wait for another piece, whose own changes start as before. What holds the other piece is the rule against a reading's choice (point 2): no reading of one piece chooses the change to another. So a session does not apply one deck's findings to a sister deck, or to a copy of the deck made as a new piece. Where the person who answers for the other piece is shown the findings and asks for them there, that is a request of the person's, and no reading chose it. The other piece has no review report of its own, so no command sees any of this (point 5).
- **Two branches.** Two branches can each start a change after the same review report, each on a word of the person's. Each is shown at the merge beside that report ([visual-communication:DEC-048-stage-evidence-and-refusals] point 8).
- **No automatic round follows.** One review follows each change, as after any change, and its findings go to the person.
- **A record, not a proof.** No command can tell that the person said what an agent recorded, or that a question put to the person did not carry a reading's choice, as "shall I mend what the review found?" does. The showing refusal catches only pages that were never brought up. Each cause is shown at the merge beside the review report it follows (DEC-048 point 8).

### 5. Where a change without a cause is refused

- **By the stage commands.** A stage command refuses to run, as its own precondition (DEC-048 point 6). It names the review report, and what it would run on.
- **By the pass.** DEC-048 point 4's list, which is the pass's whole definition, holds a condition for this. This record restates none of it.
- **By the merge check.** The capability's merge check refuses a landing change whose pieces have no pass, and so refuses this too, wherever a project runs it (DEC-048 point 8).
- **What nothing here prevents.**
  - **A loop by hand.** Looking is free (DEC-047 point 1), and a session may start the reviewer by hand ([visual-communication:DEC-015-viewing-step-and-reviewer] point 7). A loop run outside the stage commands leaves no evidence. It can end in one run of the stages, one review and a pass.
  - **Another critic.** No command sees who read a page. A loop steered by another critic leaves the same single run. Only the rule asks for the person's word after such a reading (point 4).
  - **A session that knows of no review.** Before the commands ship, a review report lies in the clone's local folder, which is never committed. A session in another clone, or one that came after that folder was cleared, finds no report. The rule then asks nothing of it.
  - **A word the person never gave.** A cause recorded on no word of the person's reads like any other.
  - **A review report read before it is recorded,** where a session started the review (point 2), or where the designer started it in its own loop and the report then names a model the calibration did not cover (point 1).
  - **A person who did not look.** The command that records any of the person's five words refuses where the showing command was not run for that review report (point 3). No command can tell that the person looked, or what an agent said beside the pages (DEC-047 point 7).
  - **A piece copied or made again.** A new piece made from an old one's files has no review report, so point 4's refusal does not apply to it. A piece removed whole and added again does the same. The merge shows a piece removed and a piece added (DEC-048 point 8), and no command refuses. The new piece does not wait. The rule's words only forbid a reading of the old piece to choose the change to the new one (point 4).
  - **The choice of model and effort.** No command sets the model a review or a critique runs on, and where a session starts the reviewer it chooses the effort too (point 1). On a model or an effort the calibration did not cover, the verdict informs and the piece has its pass ([visual-communication:DEC-049-critique-step] point 5). The pass notes that it was made under no calibration, and nothing refuses.
  - **A piece outside the process.** A piece from before the process has no review report, and nothing makes it enter the process but the merge check, where a project runs it (DEC-048 points 8 and 10).
  - **A helper that loads no rule.** A loaded rule binds only a session that loads its file, and a harness may start a helper for another agent that loads none (COR-070). A helper that runs a designer and a critic for a session is then bound by nothing here, the rule included.
  - **A project's own additions** that leave the reviewer not calibrated (point 1).
  - **Anything, until the commands ship.** Before the stage commands exist no command refuses. From the day these records ship until then, the rule's words hold all of this, in the sessions that load them (DEC-047 points 7 and 8).
  - **What binds there,** the helper apart, is the session's rule alone (DEC-047 point 7).
- **What this record leaves allowed.** A critic that judges a decision record, or other text that states no piece's design, gives no reading, and an author may revise by it. So the methodology's own order of critique and then revision stays as it is (COR-024). A plan that states the form of a piece's pages is a written design, and a judgement of it is a reading.

### 6. About the designer's loop after a calibration, this record decides nothing

- **The accepted records stand as they are.** Once the reviewer is calibrated, the designer's loop runs as DEC-030 point 2 has it. Where the hold is on, it runs as DEC-042 point 6 has it, to the bar and into polish, whether the bar asks *professional* or *excellent*. This record overturns no sentence of either for that time.
- **Decided with the calibration's acceptance.** A landing change that refines DEC-042 records a calibration's acceptance (DEC-042 point 9). That landing change, or a record accepted before it, decides how the designer's loop runs from then on: which findings may start a round or keep the loop going, whether any critic but the capability's reviewer may steer one, where the loop ends, and whether a project may keep automatic rounds off. It covers the designer's loop inside a critique round too, since each critique round passes through the review stage (DEC-049 point 5). Whoever decides it then has a calibrated reviewer's reports to look at.
- **No calibration is accepted before that is decided** (DEC-047 point 8). It may decide that the accepted records stand as they are.
- **What the set does decide for that time** is decided where it belongs. The critique's verdict decides, and its rounds run to their bound and their end (DEC-049 point 5). A covered finding counts on a library part (DEC-052 point 3). An uncovered model, a lapse or a withdrawal ends it (points 1 and 7).

### 7. A lapse, and a withdrawal

- **A lapse is a change.** It is found by a digest: something the calibration measured is not what it was. Nobody declares it.
- **A lapse undoes nothing.** The reviewer is then not calibrated, so points 2 to 5 apply from the next change on. A piece that got its pass while the reviewer was calibrated keeps it, since the pass says truly what it was made under.
- **A withdrawal says the calibration was wrong.** Whoever maintains the capability may withdraw an accepted calibration that proved wrong, though nothing it measured changed.
- **Where a withdrawal is written.** In the calibrated data the command reads, by a landing change that whoever maintains the capability accepts. The same landing change takes the withdrawn report's acceptance out of DEC-042 (point 9), so that record states only a calibration in force, and keeps no list of past ones.
- **When it reaches a project.** The calibrated data ships with the capability. A project reads a withdrawal, as it reads a new calibration, only once it next takes the capability's files. Until then it reads the calibration as in force, and its pieces are told nothing, as with a library's state (DEC-052 point 5).
- **What a withdrawal does.** The reviewer is then not calibrated. Every piece whose pass was made under that calibration asks for attention, and names it. So does every library part whose state was read from a review report made under it (DEC-052 point 2). Nothing fails.
- **What answers that attention.** The person's acceptance of the piece, or the library's owner's acceptance of the library part, as it stands, or a pass made under a calibration accepted later. A new reviewer's run, made while the reviewer is not calibrated, does not answer it. It would be one more review of the same files, and would show nothing.

### 8. The held-out ladder is outside this record

A calibration grades a held-out ladder (DEC-042 point 9), and someone must draw it before any reviewer is calibrated.
- **What a ladder is here.** A piece whose designs never become another piece's, a library's or a brand's.
- **A ladder mark, set by the command that starts it.** The ladder's piece carries a *ladder mark* in its own file, in the lines commands rewrite. The ladder mark comes with the piece. The merge check refuses a landing change that adds it to a piece the default branch already has, or that removes it.
- **What the ladder mark costs.** No command publishes a piece that carries it, makes it final or writes a readers' copy of it.
- **Why every project's commands carry it.** A project that adopts the capability calibrates nothing (DEC-042 point 10), so a ladder mark is set only where a calibration is run. The commands and the merge check are the same in every project. So the mark's refusals ship in all of them, and act only where a mark is set.
- **It draws on the libraries as they are.** A ladder's builder extends no library. What the libraries cannot draw, it makes as a local component of the ladder.
- **Who ends its rounds.** Its builder may work in rounds, and anyone may say when a round's design is done, except the reviewer under calibration. That reviewer reads no rung before the calibration's run.
- **Outside the loaded rule only under a rule of the repository's own.** The loaded rule puts a calibration's held-out designs outside it only where a rule of the repository's own names them and never lets them become another piece's design. Where such a rule stands, a ladder's rounds break no rule of the capability's. A project's rule never loosens a capability's (COR-070), and this one may switch the exemption on only because a person accepts that rule. Everywhere else a ladder's designs are under the rule like any piece's, the mark or no mark (DEC-047 point 7).
- **Who ranks it.** The outside judge ranks the ladder afterwards (DEC-042, Context), and the calibration reads that ranking alone.
- **At the merge.** A piece with a ladder mark needs no pass. The merge check shows it as a ladder, and checks only the ladder mark and that it reached no reader.
- **What the ladder mark does not stop.** Its files can be copied into another piece, which then needs one pass like any other. No command can tell a copied design. So the loaded rule's exception rests on a rule of the repository's own that forbids the copy, and a session's own statement that a piece is a ladder sets nothing apart (DEC-047 point 7).

### 9. When a calibration may run

A calibration may run only once four *preconditions of a calibration's run* are true. The second of them takes in the four things DEC-042 point 9 has a calibration fix before it runs. All four are apart from the four conditions of DEC-042's Context, which its report must then meet.
- **What it rests on has landed.** The rules it grades by, the reviewer's definition and the design knowledge are on the default branch of the repository the capability is authored in (DEC-042 point 9).
- **Those are fixed first.** Their digests are recorded before the calibration's run, with everything DEC-042 point 9 has a calibration fix first.
- **The ladder is ranked.** The outside judge ranked it, and whoever maintains the capability confirmed the ranking from the images (DEC-049 point 6).
- **The ladder has both ends.** The ranking places at least one design below the outside judge's pass line and one at or above it. A ladder with no professional design cannot show that the review finds one good enough.

Who holds a calibration to them:
- **Its command, once there is one.** It refuses to run until the four preconditions are true.
- **Whoever accepts its calibration report, always.** A calibration run before that command exists is held to the same four preconditions at its acceptance, and a calibration report that does not show them is not accepted.
- **A ladder drawn before the ladder mark exists** carries none. It takes the ladder mark in the landing change that ships the mark.

## Rationale

**Why the question sits on the round, and not on the stage.** The harm was never that a page was designed. It was that an untested reviewer chose what was designed next, again and again. A single run that ends at the person does no such harm, so nothing needs to be frozen. Refusing the design stages themselves would stop every project's design until a calibration, and again each time a model retires.

**Why any reading, and not only the review's.** Another critic is as untested as the reviewer, and a loop it steers does the same harm. Naming only the review would leave that loop allowed by the words of the rule.

**Why the person's own judgement is no reading.** The person answers for the piece, and may ask for any change to it. A rule that kept the person's view of the pages from choosing a change would forbid the one choice this record leaves in place.

**Why the refusal follows the review report, and not the branch.** A refusal counted for each branch lets a loop run one round to a branch, each round landing with a pass. Tied to the piece's last review report, it asks one thing of the change that follows, on whatever branch: the person's word, given after that report.

**Why a request has a way through, and a slip waits.** A person who asks for new words after a review is not mending its findings. Without a word for that, the request would be refused, or written down as a round. A slip is different. An agent that left a picture out could put it back with no harm, but no command can tell a slip from a mend the review chose. The person is shown every review report in any case, so the slip joins the list the person already reads, and its mend costs one sentence.

**Why a series needs the person told, a piece and a number.** A series is the one word that lets the untested reviewer choose each fix. "Shall I run rounds?" and "yes" would be the first mistake again, with one sentence of consent. "The reviewer is not calibrated" tells a person who is no designer nothing, so the person is told what was found: its verdicts split from one reading to the next, and rounds it chose made pages worse. A piece and a number keep the consent to what was asked. Before and after, side by side, let the person judge what the rounds did, which no count of rounds does. A series runs only once a command records it, so the state its end is compared with always exists.

**Why an acceptance is no cause.** An acceptance says that the files may land as they are. It says nothing of what is changed next. If it served as a cause, a change, a review and an acceptance could follow each other for ever, each change steered by the review before it, with the person saying only "accept".

**Why no change without the person's word, not even one.** There are two reasons. *Never* is a bright line: no session has to judge whether its change is the one allowed, and nobody has to ask afterwards which change that was. And a slip's mend cannot be told from a mend the review steered, so the one change a looser rule allowed could be chosen by the review, with every statement true. A rule can state "one change" as easily as "none". No rule can verify either, and across sessions the rule holds only where a session knows of the review (point 4). Whoever maintains the capability asked that nothing visual be decided before it is seen, and the person is shown every review report in any case.

**Why any reading starts the wait, and why it reaches a library and a brand.** A wait that began only at a review report, for the piece alone, would leave two doors open under one approval. A session could have another critic read the pages and go on "under the plan", or change the library's components after a review of the piece. Each is the loop this record answers, with every statement true. The designer's own look is left out, or planned work of several steps would stop after each look.

**Why another piece is held by the reading's rule, and not by the wait.** A third door is to apply one deck's findings to a sister deck, or to a copy. The rule against a reading's choice closes it: no reading of one piece chooses the change to another. A wait on every other piece would close it too, but it would hold an unrelated piece like a copy, after each review of any piece in the project. The cost of the narrower wait is that a session judges whether a reading of one piece chose its change to another, which nobody can check.

**Why a work item approved earlier starts nothing after a review.** The redesign that went wrong ran its rounds under one approval. An approval given before a review cannot have weighed what the review then said. An approval given after the review, by a person shown the report and its pages, has weighed it, and is the person's word. So planned work goes on with one sentence, "carry on", said with the pages in view.

**What this costs.** A sentence from the person for every change to a piece after an uncalibrated review report: a slip's mend, planned work, and a corrected word or a library's patch a month later. A reading of one piece may not choose the change to a sister piece or a copy, and that asks no sentence of the person where no reading chose the change. A session that cannot ask the person stops at every reviewed piece, since a work item approved before the review is no word. So no unattended run of several work items changes a reviewed piece without stopping. A project that always runs on an uncovered model stays this way. The cost is taken because each looser rule leaves a loop in which every statement is true.

**Why a dispatched designer does not start the review.** A designer that starts the reviewer reads the findings before any command has seen them, and the refusal would then wait on the designer to record them first. A review report recorded before the designer reads it is there to refuse on. Where a session must start the reviewer, the same weakness moves to that session. Where the designer is that session, it stays with the designer. The record says both.

**Why this covers words too.** The first calibration did worst on the rules on words, and the designer may reword, cut or move most of a piece's words alone (DEC-042 point 7). A loop of word changes under a reviewer that is not calibrated is the same mistake with other material.

**Why one review, and not two rounds.** Two rounds on each finding are rounds the untested reviewer chose. One review tells the person what it saw, and the person, who alone answers for the piece, chooses.

**Why a fault waits for the person too.** The designer could confirm a fault by looking, at no cost. But once the review report names it, no command can tell the designer's own look from the report's. A line drawn at "what the designer could have seen" would be a line nobody can check. The cost is a fault that waits for one sentence from the person.

**What the person between the runs is worth.** The person is often no designer, and cannot judge a finding of design. "Mend these" will often be said by habit. So the person's word does not test the steering. It makes each step seen, recorded and slower, and it leaves the choice with whoever answers for the piece. What can be promised before the reviewer is calibrated is a designed run and an honest list with pictures, not professional design.

**Why nothing is decided about the designer's loop after a calibration.** The harm this record answers lies before a calibration, and so does everything whoever maintains the capability asked of it. A rule on which findings may keep a loop going afterwards would be written with no calibrated reviewer to look at. One such rule, that only a covered finding starts a round, would overturn the polish rounds and the rounds to an *excellent* bar, and would leave no end to a loop on a finding the designer cannot mend. The landing change that accepts a calibration must refine the record of the grades in any case, and has the reports to judge by. The cost is that one harm is put off and not answered: under a calibration over a few rules, the designer's loop runs on notes of taste and on rules nobody tested, as the accepted records have it. No calibration is accepted until that has been decided, so the harm cannot arrive unseen. The critique's rounds are decided all the same, and that record says why they can be (DEC-049, Rationale).

**Why the commands refuse.** The redesign that went wrong was started in good faith by a session that coordinated others. Only a refusal by the command, with its reason, shows whoever starts a run what is missing.

**Why the model is read from the report of a reviewer's run, and why an uncovered one ends it for the project.** No command sets the model the reviewer runs on. The backbone's runner takes an effort and no model, and where only a session can start the reviewer a command sets neither. The review report names the model. Asked only before each reviewer's run, the question would be answered "calibrated, as far as can be told" in a project that always runs on a model the calibration never covered, and its designer would start every review for good. So the report that names an uncovered model settles it for the project until a report names a covered one. Once the runner can be told a model, a command can answer before the start, and the second form may go.

**Why the designer's own look is no reading.** The designer cannot build a page without looking at it, and what it sees there is its own work, not a judgement handed to it. A rule that counted that look as a reading would forbid the designer to put back, while it builds, a picture it left out. What this record guards against is a judgement from outside the building choosing the next change. So the line is drawn at who builds the change the person asked for. Where another agent builds it, the look of the session that dispatched that agent is a reading like any critic's. A designer that the person's request set to change a piece designs by its own judgement, of pages another made too. What the person asked for starts the change, and not who drew the pages before. Were such a designer's judgement a reading, no session but the one that first built a piece could design on it without dictation. A session that made a change did make it, and a compacted context changes what it remembers, not who made it.

**Why a reading covers the design, drawn or written.** A critic that judged the design system written in the brief, or the rendered markup, and steered rounds by it, would do the same harm by another door. A judgement of a decision record is no judgement of a piece's design, and the methodology asks for exactly that critique before a proposal is shown (COR-024). So the line is drawn at what is judged: a piece's design, in whatever form. A plan falls on either side by what it states: a redesign plan that gives each page's form is a written design.

**Why the showing is asked before any word of the person's is recorded.** Whoever maintains the capability asked that the pages be seen before anything about them is decided. A command cannot tell that the person looked. It can tell that the pages were never brought up, which is how a hurried session would skip them. So the refusal catches haste, as every refusal on recorded evidence does (DEC-048 point 9). A request in the person's own words is the cheapest word for a session in haste, so it is guarded with the rest.

**Why a lapse keeps a pass and a withdrawal does not.** A lapse says that something changed. What was made before the change was made under a calibrated reviewer, and that stays true. A withdrawal says that the calibration was wrong all along, so the passes made under it rest on an error. They ask for attention and fail nothing, since a project cannot mend them at once in its files (COR-055 point 1).

**Why a withdrawal is written in the data, and not kept in the record.** A record states the present. One that listed each calibration accepted and withdrawn would grow into a history. The data the command reads holds the state, and version control holds how it came about.

**Why a new reviewer's run does not answer a withdrawal.** After a withdrawal the reviewer is not calibrated. One more review by it of files nobody changed would clear the attention with nothing shown.

**Why the ladder is outside.** A calibration cannot wait for a reviewer that only a calibration tests. The ladder is safe while nothing reads its builder's helper, the reviewer under calibration never touches it, and its designs go nowhere. The last of these no command keeps, so the loaded rule's exception rests on a rule of the repository's own that keeps it (point 8).

**Why the ladder mark has a cost.** An exception declared by whoever starts the work is how the redesign that went wrong was started. A ladder mark that keeps a piece from every reader is not worth taking for a piece meant to be read. Before the mark exists, the exception rests on a rule a person accepted for that repository, and never on what a session says of its own work.

### Alternatives considered

- **No design stage runs until a calibration is accepted.** Not taken: a project on a model no calibration covered could never design. A single run with one review does the job without a freeze.
- **The freeze as a setting only the repository the capability is authored in turns on.** Not taken: the harm of rounds under a reviewer that is not calibrated is the same in every project.
- **Two rounds on each finding before a calibration.** Rejected: rounds chosen by an untested reviewer.
- **The refusal counted for each branch.** Rejected: one round to a branch passes it.
- **One change after a review on no new word of the person's: a slip's mend, or a change under a work item approved earlier.** Rejected: the one change it allows may be steered by the review, unseen by the person, and a slip's mend cannot be told from such a change. It also gives up the bright line that *never* keeps.
- **A count for each kind of change made without the person.** Rejected: a slip, a review and a slip pass it, and so does one work item named after each review.
- **An approved work item serves as often as an agent names it.** Rejected: rounds under one approval are the redesign that went wrong, and each statement would be true.
- **A slip's mend runs once without the person, and a work item approved before the review report never serves.** Rejected: a slip's mend cannot be told from a mend the review chose. Planned work waits for the person's sentence all the same, so it saves only the sentence for a slip.
- **The wait starts only at a review report, and only for the piece.** Rejected: another critic's reading, or a change to the library the piece draws with, would carry the loop on under one approval.
- **After a review or a reading of one piece, every other piece waits too.** Not taken: an unrelated piece would be held like a copy. The rule against a reading's choice holds a sister piece and a copy.
- **A fresh designer's judgement of pages another made is a reading.** Not taken: a session asked tomorrow to improve a piece could then design nothing on it by its own judgement.
- **The designer's own look starts the wait too.** Not taken: planned work of several steps would stop after each look, and the look is how a page is built.
- **A dispatched designer starts the review, and a command records it afterwards.** Rejected: the refusal would rest on the designer recording what it had already read.
- **The designer mends the reviewer's faults alone before a calibration.** Not taken: nobody can tell afterwards that the designer saw the fault for itself, and one sentence from the person mends them all.
- **Once the reviewer is calibrated, only a covered finding starts a round or keeps the loop going.** Not decided here: it overturns part of DEC-042 point 6, and it was drafted with no calibrated reviewer to look at. The landing change that accepts a calibration weighs it (point 6).
- **A setting by which a project keeps automatic rounds off.** Not decided here. Before a calibration there is no automatic round to switch off, so the setting would decide only for the time after one. It belongs to the landing change that decides the designer's loop after a calibration (point 6).
- **A count that ends a chain of named mends.** Not taken: any number would be a guess, and the person may always ask again. Each named mend is shown.
- **Whether a change counts as a redesign decides if it may run.** Rejected: the line cannot be drawn rightly from the files. A typo in a goal would count as a redesign, and a page's words cut by half would not.
- **Readiness declared by whoever starts the work.** Rejected: that is how the redesign that went wrong was started.
- **Calibrated found by the friction checks on the calibration report.** Rejected: one answer there says "unchanged, with a reason", which would let a calibration be confirmed without being run again (COR-050). The model is also no file a friction check can anchor on. So *calibrated* is a comparison of digests of its own.
- **The model asked only before each reviewer's run.** Rejected: a project that always runs on an uncovered model would count as calibrated between its reviews.
- **Nobody may withdraw a calibration.** Rejected: a reviewer found to be wrong, with nothing changed, would stay calibrated for ever.
- **A withdrawal fails every piece that got its pass under it.** Rejected: validation fails nothing a project cannot mend at once in its files.
- **Each withdrawal recorded as a new passage of DEC-042.** Not taken: the record would become a history of calibrations.
