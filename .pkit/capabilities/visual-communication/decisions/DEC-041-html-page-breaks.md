**In plain terms:** where a printed figure or site's page runs on across sheets, it breaks only between lines of text, so no line is cut, and a picture that fits a sheet stays whole on one. A heading stays on the sheet of what it heads, and no line of a paragraph stands alone on a sheet, unless what they keep is taller than a sheet. Texts, pictures or paragraphs side by side that leave no place to break are the exception: they may run past a sheet's end, leave a line alone, or set a heading apart from what it heads. In print, the page file lays the page out in the rows of one grid, so the browser breaks between rows, while the document keeps its reading order and a reader's tools get each text whole. On screen each text, the elements around it and each layer lie as today, and in the test the screen drew as today's form; beneath them the file adds only what print needs: a text's lines in parts that take no box on screen, a layer's parts the screen hides, and each shape drawn by reference to its one definition. A deck's slides print as they do today. For example, a section's heading near a sheet's foot moves to the next sheet with the first two lines of its paragraph.

## Context

[project-kit#3011](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3011) asks where a page that runs on breaks in print. [visual-communication:DEC-026-html-page-print] point 1 asks that it break between a text's lines, never through one, and leaves the break to the browser until a record of its own decides it. The renderer places each text, line and layer absolutely ([visual-communication:DEC-020-html-page-drawing] points 2 and 3), so the browser breaks where the sheet ends, whatever lies there. Firefox's faults in print ([project-kit#3012](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3012)) may be mended in the page's form, which this record decides. The runs behind this record are the evidence of its pull request.

Terms:
- A *place a break may fall* is a height, across a page's whole width, that crosses no line of text (point 1).
- A *strip* is a stretch of a page as wide as the page, between two places its file offers (point 3). It is no *band* ([visual-communication:DEC-022-web-site-and-parts] point 7), which the viewing step cuts from a page's image.
- A *keep* is a stretch of a page the file asks the browser not to break: a picture (point 1), or a heading with what it keeps with and a paragraph's first or last lines (point 2). It holds where it fits a sheet's area.
- A *portion* of a text, a layer or a run's grounds is the element that holds what of it lies in one strip.
- A line's *step band* is the stretch one line step tall that the engine sets it in: from its text's top, its index times the step down.
- A text's *paragraph* is its runs up to a line feed ([visual-communication:DEC-012-text-runs] point 1). The *element of a text marked as a paragraph* is the one [visual-communication:DEC-040-html-page-reader-side] point 1 gives it, which may hold several. A *heading*, an *instance*, an instance's *text* and a *reading order* are as [visual-communication:DEC-033-structure-for-readers] points 4 to 6 have them.
- A *sheet*, its *area* and a *fixed page* are as DEC-026 has them.

## Decision

**A printed page that is no fixed page breaks only between lines of text, and keeps each picture whole where it fits a sheet, at places its file offers, in print, as the rows of one grid that keeps the document in reading order and forces no break.**

### 1. A break never crosses a line of text, and a picture that fits a sheet stays whole

- **A line spans its font's height,** from the highest ascent to the lowest descent of its runs' faces around its baseline, as the engine sets lines, cut back to its step band where the band is shorter. No break crosses it.
- **A picture is kept whole where it fits a sheet,** a keep of its own. One its component marks as needing no text (DEC-033 point 5) is a shape. One whose text the content leaves empty is kept whole, as any picture is.
- **A box, a line, an ellipse or a path may cross a break,** and each sheet shows its part.
- **Side by side,** a break falls only where it crosses none of their lines.

### 2. A heading stays with what it heads, and no line of a paragraph stands alone

- **A heading keeps whole with the first text or picture of its instance after it in reading order that lies below it:** that text's first two lines, or the picture whole (DEC-033 points 4 and 6).
- **An instance with a text of its own** counts by the first text or picture it holds, since its own text is drawn nowhere (DEC-033 point 5).
- **A heading after which its instance holds nothing below it** keeps whole alone.
- **A paragraph keeps two lines on each side of a break,** so a paragraph of three lines or fewer keeps whole.
- **Nothing else keeps but a picture** (point 1), such as a card, a section or a diagram.

### 3. The file offers each place, and asks for each keep

- **Each gap between lines and pictures offers two places:** the highest and the lowest heights in it that cross the fewest shapes. Where those two lie within a px of each other, it offers one, in the middle of the widest stretch that crosses the fewest.
- **Where lines lie over or beside a picture,** each gap between those lines offers places by the same rule, inside the picture's keep.
- **A strip keeps whole on a sheet, and so does each keep where it fits,** so the browser breaks at the last place before the sheet's end that no fitting keep spans. Where keeps side by side leave no such place, such as paragraphs whose ends never share a gap, the browser breaks one where it picks.
- **A keep taller than a sheet's area yields** at a place inside it, never through a line, and each fitting keep it holds still holds.
- **No break is forced,** within a page or before one.
- **A strip taller than a sheet's area runs on,** as a page does (DEC-026 point 1), and the browser cuts it where the sheet ends: a picture taller than the area that no line lies over or beside, pictures side by side that overlap, or texts side by side each set closer than its font is tall, whose step bands' edges seldom meet.

### 4. In print the page is one grid, its strips the rows; on screen it lies as today

- **In print, one grid holds the page,** each element in the row of its strip, by the page file's print rules. On screen each text, the elements around it and each layer lie as today, as DEC-020 points 2 and 3 and DEC-040 points 1 and 2 have them. Beneath them the file changes only as print needs: a text holds its lines in portions, laid out on screen as no box; each layer's portions lie beside it, laid out on screen as nothing; and each shape is drawn by reference to its one definition. One document serves both, its order the reading order (DEC-020 point 3, as DEC-040 point 5 has it).
- **Each row's height is declared from the scene,** never taken from its items. A row's top is its strip's height in the scene, a share of the page's width (DEC-020 point 2) rounded to a quarter of a CSS px by CSS `round()`, and its height runs to the next row's top, so no sum of heights a browser rounded sets a row's place. The page reads its width from the element around it, a size container, since a grid cannot read its own. The print rules that round lie inside `@supports` on `round()`, and an unrounded set of them outside it, which a browser without `round()`, before Chrome 125, Firefox 118 and Safari 15.4, takes.
- **A text in portions.** A text keeps its one element, which holds a portion in each strip its lines lie in, and the portion holds the text's lines there, each still placed alone. On screen the element lies at its frame and each portion is laid out as no box, by `display: contents`, so each line lies as today. In print the element is laid out as no box, and each portion fills its strip's row at the text's left and width, each line at its height in the scene less the row's top.
- **A layer in portions, in print alone.** A run of shapes between two texts is one layer whole, laid out on screen alone, and in print one layer in each strip it reaches, showing what of each shape lies there, at the strip's height in the scene and as tall as the strip. The whole and its portions lie together at the layer's place in the document.
- **A run's grounds in portions** (DEC-039 point 1) are one element in each strip the run's lines reach, lying together at the grounds' place (DEC-040 point 5).
- **Each shape of a page in strips is defined once,** in an `svg` of no size at the page's top, which each medium lays out, and the whole layer and each portion draw it by reference, so each HTML id still lies once. Each reference carries the shape's trace id and its paint, so the file's rules on a layer's paint, its forced colours among them (DEC-023 point 7), reach it. A clipped shape's clip lies beside its definition, and each reference carries the clip (DEC-039 point 4).
- **The elements around texts** (DEC-040 point 1), such as a list, its entries and a figure, lie on screen as DEC-040 has them, and in print are laid out as no box too, so the portions they hold lie in the page's grid. A picture's element (DEC-040 point 7) lies at its picture's frame, in print spanning its picture's rows, and asks for no keep.
- **The draw order holds across a strip's edge.** Where the document's order differs from the draw order, each portion carries in print its whole's z-index, as DEC-040 point 5 has the whole carry it.
- **A keep is an empty box spanning its rows,** one for each keep, laid out in print alone.

### 5. A fixed page keeps its one element

- **A fixed page,** such as a deck's slide, lies as today, and where it runs on, it breaks where the browser picks (DEC-026 point 1).

### 6. A text in portions is still one text

- **The check for dropped text** matches a text's element, its portions' lines in order, as the scene's one text (DEC-020 point 6).
- **A reader's tools get it whole.** On screen a text's element and the elements around it keep their boxes, as DEC-040 points 1 and 4 have them, and its portions, laid out as no box, hold its lines in order, so a reader's tools get the roles DEC-040 gives, as far as the test reached them. The Task's tests hold the rest of DEC-040 point 1's elements.

## Rationale

**Point 1, lines and pictures.** A line cut, or moved over the next, cannot be read (DEC-026 point 1). The font's height is how the engine sets a line and sizes its ground under forced colours (DEC-039 point 1), the renderer reads it with no browser (DEC-020 point 1), and it holds the brand's accents, a háček on a capital among them. Cut back to its step band, a line of a text set closer than its font is tall, such as a heading at a step of 1.1 em, still leaves a place between it and the next, so no text alone is one strip that a sheet's end cuts through a line. Where a text's step is taller than its font, the font's height leaves a gap, so it meets places beside a text of another step. Two texts side by side, each set closer than its font is tall, meet a place only where their step bands' edges meet, so they mostly form one strip (point 3). A picture may hold words, so it is kept whole where it fits, as a line is. Only the component's mark says that a picture shows nothing a reader lacks, such as a flourish (DEC-033 point 5). The content's empty text says only that the words around the picture already say it to a reader's tools, and a seeing reader may still read words it holds, such as a screenshot its paragraph describes. A box or a line in two parts reads as one, as a ruled box does across a book's pages. The costs: a diagram may part between its labels; ink past a line's font height, such as a stacked accent, may be cut at a sheet's end; and in a text set closer than its font is tall, so may ink past its step band, such as a long descender at a step of 1.1 em, and the line's ground under forced colours, which the font's height sizes past the line's strip.

**Point 2, the keeps.** A heading at a sheet's foot heads nothing on that sheet, and a lone line at a sheet's foot or head reads as a stray. A heading heads its own instance (DEC-033 point 4), and what lies above or beside it is on its sheet already. Two lines are CSS's own default for a paragraph's first and last lines at a break. The heading's rule rests on DEC-033's marks and reading order, so it reaches a piece as the scene comes to hold them. A card or a section kept whole would leave a sheet's foot empty wherever one is taller than what remains, for no line saved.

**Point 3, offered, never forced.** The renderer cannot know where a sheet ends, since the sheet is the reader's (DEC-026 point 4), and a forced break would spend the paper DEC-026 point 1's compact print saves. A gap's highest and lowest places keep the stretch between them out of the strips of the lines and pictures around it, and only what a shape's edge inside the gap leaves past them joins a strip, so the picture of 0.95 of the area lay in a strip that fit the area, where one place in the gap's middle made the strip taller. Places between the lines over or beside a picture let a picture taller than a sheet break between lines, where its strip alone would be cut through one. A keep that could never yield would run on past the sheet's end, where Firefox 148 lost every line past it, so a keep yields where it does not fit. Each keep is a box of its own, so the fitting keeps inside one that yields still hold, which keeps merged into one box did not. The costs: where texts or pictures side by side offer no place, their strip runs past a sheet's end, where the browser cuts a picture that fits a sheet, and Firefox 157 loses the lines past the sheet's end and lines beyond it; where keeps side by side leave no place, a line may stand alone on a sheet; and either may set a heading apart from what it heads. Places that cross a line outside its ascender and descender would mend most of the first, at the cost of point 1's rule, as the Alternatives weigh: on the test's tight texts side by side, the decided form lost up to 260 of 287 lines in Firefox 157 and up to 4 in Firefox 148, cut up to 9 and set a heading apart on every print, where those places lost none, cut up to 5 and set none apart.

**Point 4, one grid in print.** CSS breaks only between boxes in the document's flow, and a grid's rows are such boxes, whatever order the document gives its items. So the reading order, which a reader's tools, the keyboard and a selection follow (DEC-040 point 5), stays the document's, where a stack of strips would order the document strip by strip. In the tree's order the grid paints as today, and z-index carries the draw order where the orders differ, so no later strip paints over ink that crosses a strip's edge. The grid lays the page out in print alone, since on screen it moved some lines' ink by a whole CSS px while their boxes stayed, for a cause not found, and the form of it that kept their ink lost lines in Firefox 157's print. So are the elements laid out as no box, since only the grid needs a text's portions as its items, and laid out so on screen, they lost the element of a text marked as a paragraph from Chrome's tree and the block of code's `pre` from both browsers'. A text a break may part is in portions, since one element across rows was cut, and its element stays one, laid out as no box in print, so the text and the elements around it keep their place in the document, where a subgrid let keeps break. Each line lies at its height in the scene less its row's top, so it lies inside its portion's box, which fills the row; it is the placement that printed clean, since a portion offset from its row by one length lost lines in Firefox 157's print. A keep is a box of its own, since Firefox ignored keeps set on a row's items. A row's height is declared from the scene, since a row sized by its items collapses where no shape spans its strip, and heights a browser rounds add up down a tall page. The rounding lies inside `@supports`, since a declaration that reads a custom property holding a function the browser lacks turns invalid as its value is computed, and takes no declaration before it in its place. Each layer lies whole on screen and in portions in print alone, since in portions on screen its pictures drew otherwise than today's, by up to 255 levels and in up to 67 of 180 measures a device px off, and under forced colours its portions drew otherwise than DEC-040's elements, where the whole layer drew as today's. Each shape is defined once and drawn by reference, by the whole and by each portion alike, so it lies once in the file however many strips it crosses, and its definition lies where both media lay it out, since the whole is laid out on screen alone and the portions in print alone. Each reference carries the shape's paint, since Chrome drew paint that lay on a definition past the file's forced-colours rule. The costs: two layouts of one document, so the screen's tests do not reach print's layout, which a layout check under print media reaches with no printer, though not where a sheet ends; Firefox 157 lost lines in forms whose strips all fit, for a cause not found, so a form that holds in Chrome may not hold there; and the file holds each layer twice, whole and in portions, though each shape's geometry once.

**Point 5, fixed pages.** A fixed page shrinks to the area's width and the room its keyline leaves (DEC-026 points 2 and 3), and a 16:9 slide fits A4 and Letter either way up (`outputs.md`, Printing), so a slide never runs on. Strips would part its texts for nothing in print. The cost: a fixed page that runs on breaks where the browser picks, such as a handout's A4 page printed on Letter, which at Letter's width is taller than its area.

**Point 6, one text.** The step matches the scene's texts to the document (DEC-020 point 6), and a text in portions is still one text of the scene, in one element. A reader's tools follow elements, and on screen a text's element and those around it are DEC-040's, each with its box, so a reader's tools get them as DEC-040 has them: Chrome's tree matched that of DEC-040's elements over today's form, and Firefox gave each element the same role. A portion laid out as no box, and a layer's portions laid out as nothing, add nothing a reader's tools name, and a focused link's raise stays on its text's element.

### Alternatives considered

- **The break left to the browser,** as today: Chrome printed lines across a sheet's edge and cut pictures, and Firefox lost lines and shapes or set text off its tints.
- **Rules of the file on today's elements,** such as `break-inside: avoid` on each text: they print as no rule does, since the elements lie outside the flow (DEC-026, Alternatives).
- **Strips that hold nothing, under today's elements:** each browser set the elements by the sheet's end, and text parted from its shapes.
- **A stack of strip boxes in the flow:** its document runs strip by strip, not in reading order, and a later strip paints over ink that crosses a strip's edge.
- **Strips that clip what overflows them,** on one axis or both: they cut a line's ink past its strip, against DEC-020 point 4, and the shrink they seemed to mend came from printing before the faces had loaded.
- **The grid on screen too,** one layout for both: it moved the ink of some lines on screen by a whole CSS px, rounding each row's top to a whole CSS px kept the move, and the form that kept every line's ink lost lines in Firefox 157's print and printed blank sheets in Firefox 148.
- **A text one element spanning its rows:** Chrome and Firefox 148 printed its lines across a sheet's edge, and Firefox set headings apart from their text.
- **The elements around a text's portions as subgrids:** each browser set a heading apart from its text or left a line alone.
