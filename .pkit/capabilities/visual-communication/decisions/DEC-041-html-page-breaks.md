**In plain terms:** where a printed figure or site's page runs on across sheets, it breaks only between lines of text, so no line is cut, and a picture that fits a sheet stays whole on one. A heading stays on the sheet of what it heads, and no line of a paragraph stands alone on a sheet, unless what they keep is taller than a sheet. Texts, pictures or paragraphs side by side that leave no place to break are the exception: they may run past a sheet's end, leave a line alone, or set a heading apart from what it heads. The page file offers its places for print alone, so the page on screen and its reading order do not change. A deck's slides are left as they are, and one that runs on breaks where the browser picks. For example, a section's heading near a sheet's foot moves to the next sheet with the first two lines of its paragraph.

## Context

[project-kit#3011](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3011) asks where a page that runs on breaks in print. [visual-communication:DEC-026-html-page-print] point 1 asks that it break between a text's lines, never through one, and leaves the break to the browser until a record of its own decides it. The renderer places each text, line and layer absolutely ([visual-communication:DEC-020-html-page-drawing] points 2 and 3), so the browser breaks where the sheet ends, whatever lies there. Firefox's faults in print ([project-kit#3012](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3012)) may be mended in the page's form, which this record decides.

Terms:
- A *place a break may fall* is a height, across a page's whole width, that crosses no line of text (point 1).
- A *strip* is a stretch of a page as wide as the page, between two places its file offers (point 3). It is no *band* ([visual-communication:DEC-022-web-site-and-parts] point 7), which the viewing step cuts from a page's image.
- A *keep* is a stretch of a page the file asks the browser not to break: a picture (point 1), or a heading with what it keeps with and a paragraph's first or last lines (point 2). It holds where it fits a sheet's area.
- A page's *plan* is where its places and keeps fall, worked out from the scene and the faces with no browser (points 1 to 3).
- A *portion* of a text, a layer or the grounds of a run of texts is the element that holds what of it lies in one strip.
- A line's *step band* is the stretch one line step tall that the engine sets it in: from its text's top, its index times the step down.
- A text's *paragraph* is its runs up to a line feed ([visual-communication:DEC-012-text-runs] point 1). The *element of a text marked as a paragraph* is the one [visual-communication:DEC-040-html-page-reader-side] point 1 gives it, which may hold several. A *heading*, an *instance*, an instance's *text* and a *reading order* are as [visual-communication:DEC-033-structure-for-readers] points 4 to 6 have them.
- A page's *form without strips* is the one DEC-020 points 2 and 3 lay it out in, with the elements around its texts that DEC-040 points 1 and 2 add: each text one element at its frame, and each layer one whole.
- A *sheet*, its *area* and a *fixed page* are as DEC-026 has them.

### What the test showed

The test printed a site's page the HTML renderer drew, rewritten by a script into each form below, in Chrome 157, Firefox 157 and Firefox 148, on A4, upright and turned, and on Letter. [The test's log](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3011#issuecomment-6867475640) holds its cases, how it judged a line cut, and every count.

- **Today's form.** Chrome printed lines across a sheet's edge, cut pictures that fit the area and set headings apart from their text. Firefox 157 lost lines and shapes past the first sheet, and Firefox 148 parted text from its shapes.
- **Rules on today's elements,** and **strips that hold nothing:** each browser printed as with no rule, or parted text from its shapes.
- **A stack of strip boxes in the flow:** the page held in all three browsers, but its document ran strip by strip, and on screen a later strip painted over a descender crossing a strip's edge.
- **One grid, its keeps as `break-after: avoid` on a row's items:** Chrome kept them, and both Firefoxes ignored them.
- **One grid, a text one element spanning its rows:** Chrome and Firefox 148 printed lines across a sheet's edge, and both Firefoxes set headings apart from their text.
- **One grid, one place in a gap's middle:** Chrome cut a picture that fit the area, whose strip the gaps made taller than the area.
- **The decided form,** points 1 to 4: in all three browsers, wherever keeps fit a sheet and left a place, no line was lost, printed twice or cut, every line lay on the shapes beneath it, every heading stayed with what it keeps, and no paragraph left a line alone. A picture taller than the area with lines over it, and a text set closer than its font is tall, broke between their lines, and a picture taller than the area with no line over it was cut where the sheet ended. Each portion of a text as an element of its own, the draw order carried by z-index, and each layer in portions on screen too, each printed as this form did.
- **Tight texts side by side** met no common place and formed one strip taller than the area: Chrome printed every line, Firefox 157 lost most of the narrow page's lines, both Firefoxes cut lines across a sheet's edge, accents among them, and a sheet's end set their heading apart on every print.
- **Paragraphs side by side whose ends never share a gap,** and **pictures side by side that overlap,** left no place outside a fitting keep, so each browser broke one where it picked, leaving lines alone, cutting pictures that fit and setting headings apart.
- **A line spanning its font's height uncut:** a text set closer than its font is tall formed one strip taller than every area, and each browser printed its lines across a sheet's edge.
- **Keeps merged where they overlap:** each browser left a line alone where the merged keep yielded.
- **Rows' heights from their items:** where no shape spanned a strip, its row collapsed and every later line rose, in Chrome, Firefox and WebKit.
- **A browser without `round()`,** as such a browser reads the file: guarded by `@supports`, each line lay at its place and the page printed as the decided form; unguarded, most lines moved.
- **Rows' tops unrounded:** down a wide page laid out narrower than its own width, each line drifted from its place in the scene, but with its shapes, so its prints judged alike.
- **The reader's elements,** as DEC-040 spells them: on screen, Chrome's tree and Firefox 148's roles matched those of DEC-040's elements over today's form; laid out as no box on screen too, they lost a paragraph's element from Chrome's tree and the block of code's `pre` from both, and as subgrids they let keeps break.
- **A shape a strip's edge parts,** defined once and drawn by reference: each HTML id lay once and the shape printed in each portion in all three browsers, but Firefox's print left a clip that holds a reference to its shape unapplied, and drew a lighter device row at each strip edge of the clipped shape.
- **Arrows and each portion's clip:** each head printed across a strip's edge. Chrome snaps a portion's clip to whole CSS px, which printed white hairlines where portions met until each clip reached half a CSS px past its box; where portions then overlap, a device row across a shape printed darker, and translucent paint printed twice in a device row at a strip's edge.
- **Boxes filled by a gradient and by a pattern,** paint the renderer does not yet draw: Chrome printed both across each strip's edge, and both Firefoxes lost the lines of the section beside them, as today's form did in Firefox 157 and on some of Firefox 148's prints, for a cause not found.
- **A page after another:** a short page in strips lay whole on one sheet, on the last sheet of the page before where it fit.
- **A tagged PDF,** as Chrome's print dialog saves one: it kept each heading, the list, the figure, its caption and each `code` in order, and each paragraph once its element carried `role="paragraph"`, but set the block of code's lines outside their group.
- **Forced colours:** in print, under their setting to override a page's colours, both Firefoxes applied the page's forced-colours rules, and Chrome's emulation kept the colours as written; on screen the decided form drew as DEC-040's elements over today's form, but for the test's clipped shape.
- **Firefox 157's shrink:** it shrank a few prints, of today's form and in strips alike, each of which printed at full size in another run; Firefox 148 shrank none.
- **The screen,** in Chrome, Firefox and WebKit: the decided form drew as today's, within each browser's own differences from itself, but over the test's clipped shape. Each layer in portions on screen too drew its pictures a device px off, and the grid on screen too moved some lines' ink by a whole CSS px.

**Not reached:** Safari, and WebKit's print, which printed nothing under the tool used, and WebKit's accessibility tree, since Safari's remote automation was off; Windows and Linux; a print dialog's own header and footer; a reader's scale in Chrome and Firefox 157; pages of two widths in one file; a fixed page that runs on; a reader's minimum font size; a dark mode in print, and a real forced palette, on screen or in print; a brand of another face, or whose body step is shorter than its face's ascent and descent; texts side by side set closer than their font is tall, each a column of several texts with a gap of at least a step between its paragraphs, which may meet common places there; a browser that lacks `round()`, for which the test renamed the function; the grounds of a run of texts in portions, which the renderer does not yet draw; why Firefox lost the lines beside a box filled by a gradient or a pattern; a link across a strip's edge, its focus and its raise; a selection, a copy and find across a text's portions; a screen reader; the roles of the elements DEC-040 point 1 gives a text that the test did not build; an instance with a text of its own, which changes only which text a heading keeps with, and so only the plan.

## Decision

**A printed page that is no fixed page breaks only at places its file offers, between lines of text, and keeps whole each picture, each heading with what it heads and two lines of a paragraph on each side of a break, where they fit a sheet; point 3 names where the browser breaks otherwise.**

### 1. No place a break may fall crosses a line of text, and a picture that fits a sheet stays whole

- **A line spans its font's height,** from the highest ascent to the lowest descent of its runs' faces around its baseline, as the engine sets lines, cut back to its step band where the band is shorter. No place crosses it.
- **A picture is kept whole where it fits a sheet,** a keep of its own. One its component marks as needing no text (DEC-033 point 5) is a shape. One whose text the content leaves empty is kept whole, as any picture is.
- **Every shape but a picture may cross a break,** and each sheet shows its part.
- **Side by side,** a place lies only where it crosses none of their lines.

### 2. A heading stays with what it heads, and no line of a paragraph stands alone

- **A heading keeps whole with the first text or picture of its instance after it in reading order that lies below it:** that text's first two lines, or the picture whole (DEC-033 points 4 and 6).
- **An instance with a text of its own** counts by the first text or picture it holds, since its own text is drawn nowhere (DEC-033 point 5).
- **A heading after which its instance holds nothing below it** keeps whole alone.
- **A paragraph keeps two lines on each side of a break,** so a paragraph of three lines or fewer keeps whole.
- **Nothing else keeps but a picture** (point 1), such as a card, a section or a diagram.

### 3. The file offers each place, and asks for each keep

- **Each gap between lines and pictures offers two places:** the highest and the lowest heights in it that cross the fewest shapes. Where those two lie within a px of each other, it offers one, in the middle of the widest stretch that crosses the fewest.
- **Where lines lie over or beside a picture,** one test, that a line's height and the picture's overlap, each gap between those lines offers places by the same rule, inside the picture's keep.
- **Among pictures side by side,** each picture's top and bottom offers a place where it crosses no line, inside the keep of each picture it crosses.
- **A strip keeps whole on a sheet, and so does each keep where it fits,** so the browser breaks at the last place before the sheet's end that no fitting keep spans. Where keeps side by side leave no such place, such as paragraphs whose ends never share a gap or pictures that overlap, the browser breaks one where it picks.
- **A keep taller than a sheet's area yields** at a place inside it, never through a line, and each fitting keep it holds still holds.
- **No break is forced,** within a page or before one.
- **A strip taller than a sheet's area runs on,** as a page does (DEC-026 point 1), and the browser cuts it where the sheet ends, through a line where one lies there: a picture taller than the area that no line lies over or beside, a stretch taller than the area where pictures side by side overlap, or texts side by side each set closer than its font is tall, whose step bands' edges seldom meet.

### 4. In print the page is one grid, its strips the rows; on screen it keeps its form without strips

- **In print, one grid holds the page,** each element in the row of its strip, asking not to be broken, by the page file's print rules.
- **On screen the page keeps its form without strips.** Beneath what shows, the file changes only as print needs: a text holds its lines in portions, laid out on screen as no box; each layer's portions lie beside it, laid out on screen as nothing; each shape is drawn by reference to its one definition; and a paragraph's element carries its role (point 6).
- **One document serves both,** its order the reading order (DEC-020 point 3, as DEC-040 point 5 words it).
- **Each row's height is declared from the scene,** never taken from its items. A row's top is its strip's height in the scene, a share of the page's width (DEC-020 point 2) rounded to a quarter of a CSS px by CSS `round()`, and its height runs to the next row's top, so no sum of heights a browser rounded sets a row's place. The page reads its width from the element around it, a size container, since a grid cannot read its own. The print rules that round lie inside `@supports` on `round()`, and an unrounded set of them outside it, which a browser without `round()`, before Chrome 125, Firefox 118 and Safari 15.4, takes.
- **A text in portions.** A text keeps its one element, which holds a portion in each strip its lines lie in, and the portion holds the text's lines there, each still placed alone. On screen the element lies at its frame and each portion is laid out as no box, by `display: contents`, so each line lies where the form without strips places it. In print the element is laid out as no box, and each portion fills its strip's row at the text's left and width, each line at its height in the scene less the row's top.
- **A layer in portions, in print alone.** A run of shapes between two texts is one layer whole, laid out on screen alone, and in print one layer in each strip it reaches, at the strip's height in the scene and as tall as the strip, showing what of each shape lies there and half a CSS px past its box on every side, so that portions a browser clips at whole px still meet. The whole and its portions lie together at the layer's place in the document.
- **The grounds of a run of texts in portions** (DEC-039 point 1) are one element in each strip the run's lines reach, lying together at the grounds' place (DEC-040 point 5).
- **Each shape of a page in strips is defined once,** in an `svg` of no size at the page's top, which each medium lays out, and the whole layer and each portion draw it by reference, so each HTML id still lies once. Each reference carries the shape's trace id and its paint, so the file's rules on a layer's paint, its forced colours among them (DEC-023 point 7), reach it. A clipped shape's clip lies beside its definition, and each reference carries the clip (DEC-039 point 4).
- **The elements around texts** (DEC-040 point 1), such as a list, its entries and a figure, lie on screen as DEC-040 has them, and in print are laid out as no box too, so the portions they hold lie in the page's grid. A picture's element (DEC-040 point 7) lies at its picture's frame, and in print spans its picture's rows and asks, as each item of the grid does, not to be broken, as the picture's own keep asks too.
- **The draw order holds across a strip's edge.** Where the document's order differs from the draw order, each portion carries in print its whole's z-index, as DEC-040 point 5 has the whole carry it.
- **A keep is an empty box spanning its rows,** one for each keep, laid out in print alone.

### 5. A fixed page keeps its one element

- **A fixed page,** such as a deck's slide, keeps its form without strips, and where it runs on, it breaks where the browser picks (DEC-026 point 1).

### 6. A text in portions is still one text

- **The check for dropped text** matches a text's element, its portions' lines in order, as the scene's one text (DEC-020 point 6).
- **A reader's tools get it whole.** On screen a text's element and the elements around it keep their boxes, as DEC-040 points 1 and 4 have them, and its portions, laid out as no box, hold its lines in order, so a reader's tools get the roles DEC-040 gives.
- **A paragraph keeps its role in print.** On a page in strips, each element of a text marked as a paragraph carries `role="paragraph"`, so a saved PDF's tags keep it where print lays it out as no box, as DEC-040 point 1 has a role spell a word where its element alone does not reach a reader's tools.

## Rationale

**Point 1, lines and pictures.** A line cut, or moved over the next, cannot be read (DEC-026 point 1). The font's height is how the engine sets a line and sizes its ground under forced colours (DEC-039 point 1), the renderer reads it with no browser (DEC-020 point 1), and it holds the brand's accents, a háček on a capital among them. Cut back to its step band, a line of a text set closer than its font is tall, such as a heading at a step of 1.1 em, still leaves a place between it and the next, so no text alone is one strip that a sheet's end cuts through a line. Where a text's step is taller than its font, the font's height leaves a gap, so it meets places beside a text of another step. Two texts side by side, each set closer than its font is tall, meet a place only where their step bands' edges meet, so they mostly form one strip (point 3). A picture may hold words, so it is kept whole where it fits, as a line is. Only the component's mark says that a picture shows nothing a reader lacks, such as a flourish (DEC-033 point 5). The content's empty text says only that the words around the picture already say it to a reader's tools, and a seeing reader may still read words it holds, such as a screenshot its paragraph describes. A box or a line in two parts reads as one, as a ruled box does across a book's pages. The costs: a diagram may part between its labels; ink past a line's font height, such as a stacked accent, may be cut at a sheet's end; in a text set closer than its font is tall, so may ink past its step band, such as a long descender at a step of 1.1 em, or, at a step closer still that the face decides, an accent on a capital, where no keep holds the place, as none does in a paragraph of four lines or more between its second line and its last but one, such as a pull quote's at a step of 1 em, while a heading's keep and a paragraph's hold the rest where they fit; and the line's ground under forced colours, which the font's height sizes past the line's strip.

**Point 2, the keeps.** A heading at a sheet's foot heads nothing on that sheet, and a lone line at a sheet's foot or head reads as a stray. A heading heads its own instance (DEC-033 point 4), and what lies above or beside it is on its sheet already. Two lines are CSS's own default for a paragraph's first and last lines at a break. The heading's rule rests on DEC-033's marks and reading order, so it reaches a piece as the scene comes to hold them. A card or a section kept whole would leave a sheet's foot empty wherever one is taller than what remains, for no line saved.

**Point 3, offered, never forced.** The renderer cannot know where a sheet ends, since the sheet is the reader's (DEC-026 point 4), and a forced break would spend the paper DEC-026 point 1's compact print saves.

**Two places in a gap.** A gap's highest and lowest places keep the stretch between them out of the strips of the lines and pictures around it, and only what a shape's edge inside the gap leaves past them joins a strip. So a picture nearly as tall as the area lay in a strip that fit the area, where one place in the gap's middle made its strip taller, and Chrome cut it.

**Places over a picture.** Places between the lines over or beside a picture let a picture taller than a sheet break between lines, where its strip alone would be cut through one.

**Places among pictures.** A picture's top and bottom among pictures side by side offer places, since a picture that fits is kept whole as a line is (point 1), and a picture cut loses what it shows where a heading set apart still reads, so a picture comes before a heading's keep (point 2). On the overlapping pictures the two came out nearly even: the browsers cut 12 pictures that fit, where with no such places they cut 13, and set 16 headings apart, where they had set 15.

**Keeps that yield.** A keep that could never yield would run on past the sheet's end, where Firefox 148 lost every line past it, so a keep yields where it does not fit. Each keep is a box of its own, so the fitting keeps inside one that yields still hold, which keeps merged into one box did not.

**The costs of point 3.** Where texts side by side offer no place, their strip runs past a sheet's end, where Firefox 157 loses lines, and Firefox 148 cuts lines and parts them from their shapes. Where keeps side by side leave no place, such as pictures that overlap or paragraphs whose ends never share a gap, the browser breaks one where it picks, cutting a picture that fits a sheet or leaving a line alone. Either may set a heading apart from what it heads.

**Places outside a line's ascender and descender,** kept a px from them, would mend most of the first cost, at the cost of point 1's rule. On the test's tight texts side by side, a case built against the decided form, they lost no line, cut far fewer and set no heading apart. Whether a brand sets its texts so close follows from its face and its body step, and a brand whose body step is shorter than its face's ascent and descent sets every body text so.

**But they reach further into a line.** The decided form's places cross a line's font height only in a text set closer than its font is tall, by half the font's height less the step, which grows as the step falls: 0.079 em at a step of 1.1 em. In the test's texts they crossed no glyph's ink. Those places reach into a line whatever its step, deeper than the decided form's at all but the tightest steps, and past an accent on a capital. On rows built against them they cut accents where the decided form cut none. There the decided form moved each row whole to the next sheet and so used more sheets, which point 2 declines for a card that saves no line, but which here keeps a line's ink whole.

**And they lie nearer ink,** to which browsers proved sensitive. Placed with no px kept, they lost lines in Firefox 157 though no strip outgrew the area, and kept a px, under a clip this record rejects, they lost lines in Chrome that the decided form's clip printed. This record keeps point 1's rule.

**Point 4, one grid in print.** CSS breaks only between boxes in the document's flow, and a grid's rows are such boxes, whatever order the document gives its items. So the reading order, which a reader's tools, the keyboard and a selection follow (DEC-040 point 5), stays the document's, where a stack of strips would order the document strip by strip. In the tree's order the grid paints as the form without strips does, and z-index carries the draw order where the orders differ, so no later strip paints over ink that crosses a strip's edge.

**The grid in print alone.** On screen the grid moved some lines' ink by a whole CSS px while their boxes stayed, for a cause not found, and the form of it that kept their ink lost lines in Firefox 157's print.

**No box in print alone.** Only the grid needs a text's portions as its items, and laid out as no box on screen, the elements lost the element of a text marked as a paragraph from Chrome's tree and the block of code's `pre` from both browsers'.

**A text in portions, its element one.** A text a break may part is in portions, since one element across rows was cut. Its element stays one, laid out as no box in print, so the text and the elements around it keep their place in the document, where a subgrid let keeps break.

**Each line at its height less its row's top** lies inside its portion's box, which fills the row. It is the placement that printed clean, since a portion offset from its row by one length lost lines in Firefox 157's print.

**A keep is a box of its own,** since Firefox ignored keeps set on a row's items.

**A row's height is declared from the scene,** since a row sized by its items collapses where no shape spans its strip.

**Its top is rounded** so that a layout check can hold each line at its place. Unrounded, rows drifted down a page laid out narrower than its own width, which harmed no print, since each line drifted with its shapes, but which a check could not tell from a fault.

**The rounding lies inside `@supports`,** since a declaration that reads a custom property holding a function the browser lacks turns invalid as its value is computed, and takes no declaration before it in its place.

**Each layer lies whole on screen and in portions in print alone,** since in portions on screen its pictures drew a device px off, and under forced colours its portions drew otherwise than DEC-040's elements, where the whole layer drew as the form without strips does.

**Each shape is defined once and drawn by reference,** by the whole and by each portion alike, so it lies once in the file however many strips it crosses. Its definition lies where both media lay it out, since the whole is laid out on screen alone and the portions in print alone.

**Each reference carries the shape's paint,** since Chrome drew paint that lay on a definition past the file's forced-colours rule.

**Each portion shows half a CSS px past its box,** since Chrome snaps a portion's clip to whole CSS px, which left a gap between portions where a strip's edge lay half a px off that grid, printed as a white hairline across shapes. Half a px past closes it wherever the edge lies, and the margin a clip takes reaches every side of the box alike.

**The costs of point 4.** The document has two layouts, so the screen's tests do not reach print's layout, which a layout check under print media reaches with no printer, though not where a sheet ends. A browser below the floor takes the unrounded set, whose rows drift down a wide page laid out at an area's width, harmless in print for the same reason. Firefox 157 lost lines in forms whose strips all fit, both Firefoxes beside a box filled by a gradient or a pattern, as in the form without strips, and Chrome under a clip and on plans this record rejects, for causes not found, so a form that holds in one browser, or under one clip, may not hold in another. A shape a break crosses shows the same half px of ink on each sheet; where two portions overlap, a device row across a shape prints darker, and in Firefox 148, at a reader's reduced scale, a column down a shape's side. Translucent paint prints twice in a device row at a strip's edge, as it does clipped at the strip too. A tagged PDF, as Chrome's print dialog saves one, keeps every line in order and each heading, paragraph, list, figure and caption, but loses the block of code's group, whose element lies as no box in print. And the file holds each layer twice, whole and in portions, though each shape's geometry once.

**Point 5, fixed pages.** A fixed page shrinks to the area's width and the room its keyline leaves (DEC-026 points 2 and 3), and a 16:9 slide fits A4 and Letter either way up (`outputs.md`, Printing), so a slide never runs on. Strips would part its texts for nothing in print. The cost: a fixed page that runs on breaks where the browser picks, such as a handout's A4 page printed on Letter, which at Letter's width is taller than its area.

**Point 6, one text.** The step matches the scene's texts to the document (DEC-020 point 6), and a text in portions is still one text of the scene, in one element. A reader's tools follow elements, and on screen a text's element and those around it are DEC-040's, each with its box, so a reader's tools get them as DEC-040 has them: Chrome's tree matched that of DEC-040's elements over the form without strips, and Firefox gave each element the same role. A portion laid out as no box, and a layer's portions laid out as nothing, add nothing a reader's tools name, and a focused link's raise stays on its text's element. A saved PDF is a main way to keep a print, and Chrome's tagged PDF left out each paragraph, whose element print lays out as no box, until the element carried its role; the role repeats on screen what the element's name says, which changed neither Chrome's tree nor Firefox's. It does not mend the block of code's group, which that PDF still loses.

### Alternatives considered

- **The break left to the browser,** as in the form without strips: Chrome printed lines across a sheet's edge and cut pictures, and Firefox lost lines and shapes or parted text from its shapes.
- **Rules of the file on the elements of the form without strips,** such as `break-inside: avoid` on each text: they print as no rule does, since the elements lie outside the flow (DEC-026, Alternatives).
- **Strips that hold nothing, under the elements of the form without strips:** each browser set the elements by the sheet's end, and text parted from its shapes.
- **A stack of strip boxes in the flow:** its document runs strip by strip, not in reading order, and a later strip paints over ink that crosses a strip's edge.
- **Strips that clip what overflows them,** on one axis or both: they cut a line's ink past its strip, against DEC-020 point 4, and the shrink they seemed to mend came from printing before the faces had loaded.
- **The grid on screen too,** one layout for both: it moved the ink of some lines on screen by a whole CSS px, rounding each row's top to a whole CSS px kept the move, and the form that kept every line's ink lost lines in Firefox 157's print and printed blank sheets in Firefox 148.
- **A text one element spanning its rows:** Chrome and Firefox 148 printed its lines across a sheet's edge, and Firefox set headings apart from their text.
- **The elements around a text's portions as subgrids:** each browser set a heading apart from its text or left a line alone.
- **The elements laid out as no box on screen too:** Chrome left the element of a text marked as a paragraph out of its tree until it carried `role="paragraph"`, both browsers left out the block of code's `pre`, and a focused link's raise would lie on its text's portions, since an element laid out as no box takes no z-index.
- **A portion offset from its row by one length,** its lines at their places in the text: Firefox 157 lost lines and parted others from their shapes, though every strip fit.
- **Each portion of a text an element of its own, with none around them:** it printed as the decided form, but a text would be several elements, where DEC-040 point 1 gives each text one, the element of its mark.
- **Rows' heights from their items:** where no shape spans a strip, its row collapses, and every later line rises.
- **Keeps as `break-after: avoid` on a row's items:** Firefox ignored them in a grid.
- **Keeps that never yield:** a keep taller than a sheet runs on, where Firefox 148 lost every line past the sheet's end when each text was kept whole.
- **Keeps merged where they overlap:** each browser left a line alone on a sheet where the merged keep yielded.
- **Keeps side by side also joined where their union does not fit:** it set far fewer headings apart, but cut one more picture that fit, and among paragraphs side by side Firefox left more lines alone and Firefox 157 lost some of them.
- **No places among pictures side by side but the gaps' own:** the browsers cut 13 pictures that fit, where the decided form cut 12, and set 15 headings apart, where it set 16.
- **A place where one text's step band ends, crossing another's line outside its ascender and descender,** for texts side by side that meet no common place, kept a px from those metrics: on the tight texts side by side it lost no line, cut few and set no heading apart, and it put the taller clusters that fit a sheet on fewer sheets, but a break there crosses a line's font height where an accent on a capital lies, which it cut on rows built against it, and lying nearer ink, it lost lines in Firefox 157 with no px kept and in Chrome under a clip this record rejects (point 3's rationale).
- **Those places inside a keep around each cluster of texts side by side,** so that a cluster that fits a sheet moves whole: it lost far fewer of the tight texts' lines than the decided form, but still set their heading apart on every print, set headings apart on rows where the decided form set none apart, and among paragraphs side by side broke a cluster's keep and left lines alone.
- **Those places only in a stretch of texts side by side that meets no common place and is taller than any upright sheet's area at the page's width,** the decided form's places elsewhere: weighed, not tested. Short clusters, such as the cards and the rows a capital bearing an accent opens, would plan as the decided form, and long tight texts as those places, with no keep added, where a keep around each cluster set headings apart. But its bound is a sheet the renderer assumes, where the sheet is the reader's (DEC-026 point 4); a narrow page, which prints at its own size, fits a stretch far taller than the bound; a stretch just short of it prints as the decided form's tight texts did; and where it offers those places it keeps their costs, point 1's rule and browsers' sensitivity to how near a place lies to ink.
- **Each layer in portions on screen too,** one form of a layer for both: no line's ink moved, but a layer's pictures drew a device px off, and under forced colours its portions drew otherwise than DEC-040's elements.
- **A shape's paint on its one definition,** not on each reference: under forced colours Chrome drew such a shape in its own colours, past the file's rule (DEC-023 point 7).
- **Each portion clipped at its strip's edges:** no two portions overlap, so no device row across a shape prints darker and no column down its side, but Chrome printed a white hairline through shapes and arrowheads on most of its prints, wherever a strip's edge lay half a px off its grid of whole CSS px, where the darker row and the column lie beside a shape's own ink.
- **Each portion half a CSS px taller at each edge it shares,** clipped at its own box: Firefox 148 drew no column, but Chrome left hairlines across boxes filled by a gradient or a pattern, paint for which this record must hold once the renderer draws it, and lost lines of the tight texts on plans of places outside a line's ascender and descender, which each portion clipped half a CSS px past its box printed.
- **Each portion clipped by a path at its own box,** which Chrome does not snap to whole px: Chrome printed no hairline, and each check judged its prints alike, but Firefox cut the rest of a picture's portion that ran on past a sheet's end.
- **No role on the element of a text marked as a paragraph,** its name alone: no role repeats on screen what the name says, but Chrome's tagged PDF lost each paragraph.
- **Rows' tops unrounded,** with no `round()`, no `@supports` and no floor on browsers: down a wide page laid out at an area's width, each line drifted from its place in the scene. It drifted with its shapes, so it harmed no print, but a layout check could no longer hold a line at its place.
- **A picture with a text over it as a ground, not a keep,** parting between the text's lines with only the text's keeps holding: a box for each keep already keeps that text's lines together, and a picture that would fit a fresh sheet would part.
- **A line's extent as its ink:** it differs with each string, so the place moves as the words change, and the renderer would have to draw each line to find it.
- **A line's extent as its step band alone:** texts side by side at different steps may then touch at no height, leaving no place.
- **A line's extent as its font's height, uncut:** a text set closer than its font is tall has no place between its lines, so where it is taller than a sheet, the sheet's end cuts it through a line, as Chrome and Firefox did.
- **One place in a gap's middle:** a strip then holds half of each gap around a picture, so a picture that fits a sheet may lie in a strip that does not, which Chrome cut.
- **A heading kept with what follows past its instance:** it would keep one instance's heading with another's, which it does not head.
- **Each instance kept whole where it fits,** such as a card or a diagram: one taller than what remains of a sheet leaves the rest of it empty.
- **Every picture cut as any shape, or each one marked as needing no text:** a picture may hold words, and one the content gives an empty text may too.
- **A shape a strip's edge parts copied into each portion:** each id would lie in every strip the shape reaches (DEC-020 point 4), and the test's file grew many times over.
- **A break forced before a page or a section:** the renderer does not know the sheet, and DEC-026 point 1's print is compact.
- **A page cut at a sheet the renderer assumes:** the sheet is the reader's (DEC-026 point 4).
- **A text's lines in the flow inside one element:** its shapes would still lie in layers the size of the page, which Firefox printed on the first sheet alone.
- **Strips on a fixed page too:** a slide never runs on, so its texts would part for nothing.
