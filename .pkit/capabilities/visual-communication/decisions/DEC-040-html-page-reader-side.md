**In plain terms:** a reader who does not see an HTML page gets each text with what it is from the elements the page already holds: a heading's text is a heading, a list's entries are a list. Each word lies once, but a link or a heading whose line broke where no space lies, as after a hyphen, is also named whole. None of it adds to the drawing. The page is written in the order a reader takes it, a picture's text is an element at the picture's place, and a copy gives the words as written. Find across a line break fails in Chrome and Firefox, which this record accepts for now. For example, a link the engine broke after "decisions never" takes one press of Tab, and a reader's tools get one link named "decisions never drift to match the code".

Terms:
- A *reader's tools* are as DEC-033 has them: a screen reader and its like, reading a page's *accessibility tree*, the tree a browser builds from the file for them.
- A line's *break character* is what the engine used up where it broke a paragraph's line: the spaces where it broke at a space; nothing where it broke after a hyphen, after a slash, between characters that take no space, or inside a word; and a line feed where a paragraph ends.
- A *run of texts* and its *grounds* are as DEC-039 has them: texts that follow one another in draw order with no other part between them, and the ground the file draws for each of their lines under forced colours.

## Decision

**An HTML page file spells the reader's side in HTML's own elements, on the elements its texts already are, adds an element only for what no text holds, and draws none of it.**

### 1. Each word takes the element HTML has for it

- **HTML's own element** spells each word of DEC-033 point 1: a text's mark, a list and its entries, a run's structure and a run's link. An ARIA role spells a word only where HTML has no element for it, or where its element alone does not reach a reader's tools. So each list also carries `role="list"`, since WebKit, by its known behaviour, reads a list whose marker is taken away (point 2) as no list.
- **On what is already there.** A text's own element takes its mark, and each of its lines is a phrasing element, so a paragraph's element holds them. Each span sits inside the element of its run's structure, and the element of a list, or of an instance's text, holds the elements of what it holds. So each text still lies once to a selection and to find (DEC-020 point 1).
- **A heading past level six** is written as the sixth, and reported `approximated` (DEC-033 point 4).
- **A caption** is a `figcaption`, which one `figure` holds with the element of what it captions, and which no attribute copies into the figure's name (DEC-033 point 4):
  - **at the place of whichever comes first** in the reading order, so a part between them follows the figure, and point 5 counts that move as it counts any element's place;
  - **over an instance with an instance's text,** in that instance's own `figure`, whose name stays the instance's text (point 7);
  - **over a picture that needs no text,** alone in its `figure`, since that picture takes no element (point 7).
- **An entry left out of its list,** having nothing to read (DEC-033 point 4), takes no entry element, and what it draws lies where point 5 places it. So a list's element holds entry elements alone, and a reader counts only its entries.
- **A list with no entry to read, or one marked as no list,** takes no list element, and its entries' parts lie where point 5 places them.
- **A run's structure across a line break** splits at the line's edge, one element on each line, each read with its structure. A link stays one link to a reader (point 3).

### 2. None of it is drawn

- **The elements draw nothing of their own.** The file sets back each look a browser gives them, such as a heading's size, a link's colour and underline, a list's marker, a paragraph's margin or the white space a block of code keeps, so the page draws the scene alone (DEC-001 point 2, DEC-033 point 3). At a device scale other than 1, Chrome may move the ink of a line that holds one by under a device pixel, for a cause not found.
- **Nor do they change what a reader selects or what draws over what.** The picture's own element (point 7) takes no pointer, as a layer does. The elements point 1 adds around texts, such as a list, its entries and a figure, form no stacking context.
- **What stays the reader's:** the focus the browser shows (point 4), and the colours a reader forces, a link's among them. So each element of point 1 inside a line, a link or a run's structure, is forced as DEC-039 point 1 forces a span, and takes no background. The line stays the only element whose own lines hold text, the only one a backplate could be painted for, so none returns.
- **Hidden from a reader's tools:** every element the file draws that holds no word a reader gets, such as a layer, what the file defines once for its layers, a run's sign ([visual-communication:DEC-036-concept-signs] point 5), the grounds of a run of texts, and a text marked decoration, whose links alone stay readable (DEC-033 points 3 and 6).

### 3. A link across a line break is one link

- **One link to a reader's tools and to the keyboard.** Its first part is the link they reach, named by the words of all its parts. Each later part follows it under the pointer alone, hidden from a reader's tools and out of the keyboard's order.
- **Named by its parts' own words,** through `aria-labelledby`, so no word lies twice. Where two of its parts meet at a break that used up no space, it is named instead by its words whole in `aria-label`, since `aria-labelledby` joins parts with a space.
- **Its focus shows on every part.**

### 4. A keyboard reader sees where the focus lies

- **Links take focus in the document's order,** which is the reading order (point 5). The file sets no order of its own, and adds no focus stop (DEC-023 point 4).
- **The browser's own sign of focus** stays on every link.
- **The text that holds a focused link draws over every shape of its page** while the link has focus, so a later shape never hides where the focus lies. Its rule is marked `!important`, so it wins over the z-index point 5 writes into a text's own style.
- **Both rest on CSS's `:has()`,** the focus on every part (point 3) and the raise. A browser without it shows the focus on a link's first part alone, and raises nothing.

### 5. The document's order is the reading order

- **A page's elements follow its reading order** (DEC-033 point 6), so a reader's tools, the keyboard and a selection take them in it.
- **What takes no place in the reading order,** such as a shape or a text marked decoration that holds no link, lies just before the next part, in draw order, that takes one, or last on its page where none follows. What lies just before a part lies inside the element that holds that part or, where that part is a caption that opens its figure, just before the figure. What lies at one place keeps its draw order there. A layer lies where its first shape falls.
- **A text marked decoration that holds a link** lies where DEC-033 reads its links: in its draw order, or, where its component names an order, after the parts the component lists (DEC-033 points 3 and 6).
- **A list's entries lie together,** since the part that repeats over its field takes one place in its component's order. So a list's element holds entry elements alone (point 1).
- **The grounds of a run of texts lie together,** just before the first of the run's texts in the document. In the draw order they count as one element, between the part before the run and the run's first text, and on a page that carries z-index they take that place's z-index (DEC-039 point 1).
- **Where the document's order of a page's texts, layers and grounds differs from their draw order,** each of them carries its place in the draw order by z-index, so the page draws as before (DEC-020 point 3). Nothing else carries a z-index, and each page is a stacking context of its own.
- **Whatever reads the draw order from the file,** in the renderer or in the step, reads it from the browser's painting, or from each element's z-index and, among elements of equal z-index, their order in the document; never from the document's order alone.

### 6. A line ends with what the engine used up at its break

- **Each line ends with its break character,** taking no width. The line feed that ends a text lies in an element hidden from a reader's tools, outside every span the step reads. Both lie inside the line's element, so the line's opt-out of forcing covers them (DEC-039 point 1). So a copy gives a text's words as written, and two texts apart, and no name ends with a space.
- **The renderer reads what the engine used up** from the scene's lines and paragraphs, so the seam is unchanged ([visual-communication:DEC-005-engine-and-plugins] point 8).
- **A heading whose lines meet at a break that used up no space** is named by its words whole in `aria-label`, as a link is (point 3), since Chrome names a heading from its lines with a space at each line placed alone. Its words then lie twice in the file.
- **What lines placed alone cost** (DEC-020 point 2): Chrome's and Firefox's find meets no phrase across a line break; a reader's tools get a paragraph's lines as pieces, which may split a word, as between two characters of a Japanese word; and Firefox and WebKit may name a caption's figure from its lines, which this record has not seen. The renderer's reference page states these, and no check reports them.

### 7. A picture's text is an element at the picture's frame

- **A picture's text is a picture to a reader's tools,** an element of its own at the picture's frame, in the picture's place in the reading order, named by that text. The picture stays in its layer (DEC-020 point 1).
- **A picture with no text** takes the same element with no name, so a reader's tools show that its text is missing (DEC-033 point 5). A picture that needs none takes no element.
- **A picture not drawn** keeps its element and its text, over nothing drawn (DEC-033 point 8).
- **An instance's text names a `figure`** that holds the elements of what the instance holds, so a reader's tools give it before them (DEC-033 point 5).
- **Neither text is drawn, selected or found.** Each lies only in what the file tells a reader's tools.

### 8. The viewing step reports none of it dropped

- **Its reads stay on spans** (DEC-020 point 6, DEC-037 point 3). What is no span, such as a picture's text, a link's name or a break character, draws nothing, so the step never reports it dropped (DEC-015 point 3). Putting the document in reading order breaks no read: the words read matches each text by its trace id, the ink read both lists the spans and marks the one it draws alone by their order in the document, and DEC-037's cover read and its pre-filter take what is drawn after a span from the browser's painting, never from the document's order alone (DEC-037 point 3, point 5 here).
- **A screen reader stays a person's** (DEC-020 point 5).

## Rationale

**Point 1, HTML's own elements.** An element of HTML carries its meaning to a reader's tools and to other tools that read a page's elements, such as a reader view. A role tells a reader's tools alone, and restates what an element would say, so it is kept for a word no element carries to them, as a list without its marker in WebKit. Putting the meaning on the elements the texts already are keeps what DEC-020 chose: each text lies once, so a reader's tools, a selection and a find meet it once. A line that were no phrasing element would close the paragraph around it, as an HTML parser closes one. A run's structure split at a line reads as before, as DEC-033 point 3 foresaw, since a reader's tools read emphasis from each part. A `figure` is HTML's element for a whole, captioned or named, such as a diagram, so a captioned instance is one figure. A caption reaches a reader's tools inside its figure, so a name copied from it would be heard twice. A list's element holds only entries in HTML, so what a left-out entry draws lies where point 5 places any shape, and a list no reader takes has no element.

**Point 2, nothing drawn.** A renderer draws the scene and adds no look (DEC-001 point 2). The test showed the reset elements leave each line's layout where it was, and Chrome's move of ink at another scale adds no look. A picture's element lies at the picture's frame, so taking the pointer it would stop a text drawn over the picture from being selected. A container that formed a stacking context would hold its texts' z-index, and the focus's raise, inside it. The focus and forced colours belong to the reader's own settings, which a renderer leaves alone. An element inside a line would inherit the line's opt-out of forcing, and with it the file's reset of its colour, so a link's letters would keep the reader's link colour only where an engine gives it to a forced span inside such a link. Forced, the link's element itself takes the link colour, so its letters rest on no engine's handling of that span, and its focus ring takes the palette's highlight colour where it took the line's text colour. An inline element brings no backplate. An element that draws and holds no word a reader gets is noise to a screen reader, as a layer read as a picture with no name is, and DEC-033 keeps a decoration's text out of the reading order but for its links.

**Point 3, one link.** DEC-033 point 3 keeps a link across runs one link, since two would read as two places. The lines are placed apart, so no one element can hold the link's words alone. Naming the first part by its parts' words keeps the words once, where a copy in an attribute would hold them twice. Where a break used up no space, as in an address broken after a slash, the space that joins the parts would split a word, so there a copy is the lesser loss. The pointer still follows the link from any part, as a seeing reader expects.

**Point 4, the focus.** A keyboard reader who cannot see the focus is lost. DEC-020 lets a later shape lie over a text, and the test showed such a shape hides the focus too. A text that carries a z-index keeps its link's own raise inside it, so the whole text is raised, its other lines with it, and only while its link has focus, the reader's own state. An element's own style wins over the file's rules but one marked `!important`. Every browser the test reached holds `:has()`.

**Point 5, the reading order.** A reader's tools, the keyboard and a selection all follow the document's order, so one order serves all three. DEC-020's own test drew the same image with z-index, and so did this test. DEC-033 point 6 gives a shape, a decoration's text and a picture that needs none no place in the reading order, so where they lie in the document is this record's choice, and placing them edits no accepted record. Placed before the next part that takes one, they keep their draw order, and a page whose texts and pictures keep theirs carries no z-index: a component that draws a shape that backs a title and a body, then the two, and names the order title, body, keeps its file in its draw order, with no z-index. A decoration's links read where DEC-033 reads them, so a component that draws a footer first and wants its links read last names its order, and its page carries z-index. Layers and grounds are compared with texts, since where the reading order moves a picture, the layer that holds it moves too, and must still draw under a text drawn after it. A run's grounds lie beneath all of its texts (DEC-039 point 1), so they take one place before the first: laid beside each text, a later text's grounds would follow an earlier text and cover its descenders, and on a page with z-index, grounds that carried none would lie beneath every layer. A page that is a stacking context of its own keeps its z-index from meeting another page's, where a reader's minimum size pushes a line past its page (DEC-020 point 4), and keeps a low z-index from falling beneath the page's own ground ([visual-communication:DEC-031-html-page-backdrop]). Once the document's order may differ from the draw order, it tells the draw order only among elements of equal z-index, as the browser paints them; on a page that carries no z-index, that is all of them.

**Point 6, copying and find.** A copy that runs two words together, or splits one, breaks the text a reader takes elsewhere, and characters that take no width mend it at no cost to the drawing. The line feed that ends a text keeps two texts apart in a copy, and hidden from a reader's tools it adds no space to a name. Kept inside the line, both keep the white space it keeps, and lie outside forcing with it, where no backplate is painted. A reader's tools list a page by its headings' names, so a name that splits a word is worth a copy of the words, as a link's is. Find in Chrome and Firefox stops at each line placed alone, about one two-word phrase in ten in a text that wraps, and a link to such a phrase finds nothing. Lines kept in one row and each moved into place were found, but their offsets rest on the browser's widths of the lines before, which a renderer does not know, so whether they can keep a line where DEC-020 point 2 holds it is a follow-up's question. A phrase is found within each of its lines, and a reader's tools read it whole, so the loss is accepted for now, and stated once rather than on every text that wraps, though a reader who finds nothing is not told why.

**Point 7, a picture's text.** A layer holds pictures and shapes in draw order, hidden whole, so a picture's text cannot lie there in reading order. An element of its own at the picture's frame stands in the reading order, and a reader's tools that show where they are show the picture's place. A picture with no name is how a reader's tools learn a text is missing.

**Point 8, the step.** A reader's text draws nothing by design, so a read of ink would report each one. Whether the step reads the reader's side, and against what, waits on a comparison and a timing no test has made.

### Alternatives considered

- **A copy of each text for a reader's tools, in either of #2740's two forms that hold one:** DEC-020's elements hold each text once, and a find meets a copy twice.
- **ARIA roles on the renderer's plain spans and blocks:** a role reaches a reader's tools alone, where an element of HTML reaches other tools too.
- **A group with a role for an instance's text:** a role where HTML has `figure`.
- **A captioned instance as a figure inside the caption's figure:** the outer one reads unnamed, so a reader meets one whole twice.
- **A caption's figure named by `aria-labelledby` pointing at its caption:** Chrome then names the figure, but a listener may hear the caption twice, and the name, built from lines placed alone, splits a word broken after a hyphen.
- **An entry element of its own, hidden from a reader's tools, for an entry left out of its list:** an element point 5's place for what draws makes needless.
- **Two links where a link crosses a line:** two stops of the keyboard and two links to one place.
- **A link named by a copy of its words wherever it crosses a line:** the same tree, with its words twice.
- **A reading order by an owner attribute or a tab index, the document left in draw order:** each reaches only one of a reader's tools, the keyboard and a selection.
- **What takes no place in the reading order placed where its component's list and the draw order put it, after the parts the list names:** one rule for every part, but a page whose texts keep their draw order then carries z-index wherever its component leaves out a shape it draws first, behind its texts.
- **Each text's grounds just before that text:** a later text's grounds follow an earlier text and cover its descenders, the cover DEC-039 removes.
- **A run's grounds left with no z-index on a page that carries it:** a layer drawn before the run lies over them.
- **A link and a run's structure left to the line's opt-out of forcing:** a link's letters keep the link colour only through how an engine colours a forced span inside it, and a focused link's ring takes the line's text colour.
- **What takes no place in the reading order gathered at a page's end:** a decoration's links would leave the place DEC-033 point 3 reads them at.
- **Only what takes a place in the reading order compared with the draw order:** a layer moved after the texts it lies under would draw over them.
