**In plain terms:** the check of edges that nearly line up compares like with like: a left edge with a left edge, a centre with a centre, a top with a top. A dot centred on a card's top edge has its top half its height above the card's, so the check reports the two tops as a near miss. Between groups it reports them again with every other card whose top shares that line, so a row of four such cards gives sixteen findings, though the dots are meant. Under this record, where one part's centre lies within a pixel of another's edge, the check does not compare the two at that edge: the dot's top is not compared with the card's. It compares them on their other lines on that axis as it compares any pair, among siblings and between groups alike. The row then gives none.
- **A judgement.** In the boxes, a dot set by its centre on the edge and a dot set by its top that slipped half its height are one fact. The check reads it as meant, on the judgement that a centre set on an edge is the commoner intent, and the reviewer judges the slip on the image.
- **The slip of a part set by its edge on another's centre stays named.** A dot set by its left edge on a heading's centre is compared with the heading at their centres, 3 px apart.
- **Nothing new is named.** A centre that nearly lies on an edge is no near miss the check names.

## Context

[visual-communication:DEC-046-faults-lower-grades] point 3 has the check of edges that nearly line up compare two siblings on each axis by three lines, like with like, and pass over the other lines of an axis on which a pair lines up on one: "that near miss follows from the line chosen". [visual-communication:DEC-067-near-alignment-between-groups] point 2 reads the line chosen alike between groups, and leaves to a later record whether a part centred on another's edge has chosen a line. [visual-communication:DEC-066-meant-overlaps] point 3 counts the near misses such a part gives among its costs, and leaves open whether, for a pair named under `lies-over`, a centre set on the other's edge counts as a line chosen. This record decides it, for every pair ([project-kit#3405](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3405)).

**The row of dots.** Four cards stand in a row, their tops on one line, under a brand whose smallest step of space is 4 px. Each card is a group holding a box 140 by 100 px and a dot 6 px square, the dot's middle on the box's top edge and its centre on the box's centre across. Across, the dot and its box line up at their centres. Down, the dot's middle lies on the box's top, two lines the check never compares with each other, and the dot's top lies 3 px above the box's top: a near miss, in each card. Between groups, each dot lies 3 px above each other card's box in the same way, and gives a finding of its own with each other card (DEC-067, "What it costs"): twelve, beside the four among siblings. Set on a card's top right corner, the same dot gives two near misses in its card, one on each axis.

**What follows from a centre on an edge.** A part of size s whose centre lies on an edge of a part of size b, on an axis, has its own edge there s/2 from that edge, whatever b is. Its centre lies b/2 from the other's centre, and its far edge as far from the other's far edge as b and s/2 differ. Each lies up to a pixel nearer or further where its centre lies up to a pixel of rounding off the edge. So a near miss at that edge needs s shorter than two steps and two pixels, under 10 px where the step is 4 px. One at their centres needs b as short, and one at their far edges needs b within a step and a pixel of s/2. A card, a heading or a timeline's rule down its length is longer than that, so a small part centred on its edge shows a near miss at that edge alone.

**What the boxes cannot tell.** The check reads the parts' boxes (DEC-046 point 3). A part set by its centre on an edge, and a part set by its edge that slipped by half its size, give the same two boxes. A dot 6 px across whose middle lies on a card's top, its top 3 px above the card's, may be a dot centred there, or one meant to stand inside the card's top, its top on the card's, that slipped 3 px up. For a part of size s, a slip within a pixel of s/2 lands its centre within a pixel of the edge. For that dot, under a step of 4 px, a slip of 2 px or more does so, of the slips of more than 1 px and less than 4 px that give a near miss there.

**The libraries the capability ships.** No component the capability ships draws a part that straddles another's edge. When DEC-066 was drafted, each part that lay on another by design lay within it, or on a line (DEC-066, Implications). Drawn on the starter's reference pieces as its tests draw them, one for each of its components, at commit 710f6d672 of project-kit's default branch, two pairs the check compares by their lines, on no line chosen, have one part's centre within a pixel of the other's edge. In each, a bar down a part's left side has its middle 0.8 px from the bottom of the head beside it, by chance, and lies beside the head, not across it. The pieces draw each word of each option at least once, never every combination nor every content, so they speak only for what they draw.

The words it uses:
- **A part**, **siblings**, **a leaf**, **two leaves of different groups**, **the lines of an axis**, **nearly agree**, **a near miss**, **a text's room** and **across** and **down** are DEC-067's. **The line chosen** is the test DEC-046 point 3 makes of siblings and DEC-067 point 2 makes between groups: a pair that lines up within a pixel of rounding on one line of an axis is not reported on another line of it.
- **A step** is the brand's smallest step of space at the scene's key, which bounds what nearly agrees, and **two steps** are twice its length.
- **An edge** of a part on an axis is its start or its end there: across, its left or its right edge, and down, its top or its bottom. **Its centre** on an axis is its centre across and its middle down. **Its size** on an axis is its box's width across and its height down.
- **Like edges** of two parts on an axis are their two starts, or their two ends. Two parts are **compared at an edge** of one where the check compares their like edges there: their starts at a start, and their ends at an end.
- **A line a part is compared on** is a line of an axis on which the check compares it: every line, but across, for a text that does not fill its width, the line it stands its lines on alone (DEC-067 point 3).
- **Centred on another's edge:** a part is centred on another's edge on an axis where its centre lies within a pixel of rounding of an edge of the other there, its centre a line it is compared on and that edge a line the other is compared on.

## Decision

**Where one part of a pair is centred on the other's edge on an axis, the check of edges that nearly line up does not compare the two at that edge, and compares them on their other lines on that axis, among siblings and between groups alike.**

### 1. A part centred on another's edge is not compared with it at that edge

- **The test.** On an axis, where one part of a pair is centred on the other's edge, the check does not compare the two at that edge: their starts where that edge is a start, and their ends where it is an end. It compares the pair on its other lines on that axis, and names a near miss on them as it names any.
- **Either part, whatever its size.** The test reads each part of the pair as the one centred, whichever is drawn first and whichever is the larger. Where each is centred on an edge of the other, as two parts of one size set half their size apart are, the check compares the two at neither edge, and compares their centres.
- **The lines each is compared on.** A part is centred on another's edge only where its centre is a line it is compared on, and that edge a line the other is compared on (DEC-067 point 3). So across, a text that does not fill its width offers the edge it stands its lines on alone, and is centred on an edge only where it stands its lines in the middle. Down, a text is compared on every line, as any part is.
- **Lines alone.** The test reads a pair the check compares by their lines. A pair one of which holds the other within a pixel is compared by the inner part's insets, as DEC-046 point 3 decides, and the test does not read it.
- **It names nothing.** The test only leaves edges uncompared. A centre that nearly lies on an edge, more than a pixel and less than a step off it, is no near miss the check names.

### 2. Between groups as among siblings

- **The same test.** Point 1 holds of two leaves of different groups, each where it lies on its page, as DEC-067 point 2 reads the line chosen between groups. So in the row of dots, no dot is compared at the tops with any card's box, its own or another's, and the row gives no finding.
- **No finding names those edges.** At an edge the test leaves uncompared, the check names no near miss: no finding of two siblings names it, no finding of two lists and no finding of its own (DEC-067 points 4 and 5).

## Rationale

**Why the near miss at that edge is read as meant.** The check reads boxes, and in them a centre on an edge and the near miss at that edge are one fact ("What the boxes cannot tell", Context). So the check reads that near miss one way wherever it meets it. Read as a slip, it is reported in every piece that draws a small part centred on an edge, such as a badge on a card's corner, a dot on its top or a timeline's markers on the ends of its rule. Between groups it is reported once more with each side whose edge shares the line: sixteen findings in the row of four dotted cards, each asking the reviewer to judge a placement no one got wrong. Read as meant, a slip of about half a small part's size at an edge goes unsummed, and the reviewer meets it on the image ("What it costs"). No component the capability ships draws a part that straddles another's edge (Context), so its libraries count neither. The choice rests on which intent is commoner in the pieces the check reads: a small part centred on an edge, a common way to draw a badge, a status dot or a timeline's marker, or a part set by its edge that slipped by about half its own size. This record judges the first the commoner, and reads the near miss as meant.

**Why that edge alone.** The centre on the edge fixes one line of the centred part: its own edge there, half its size from the other's edge, whatever the other's size. That near miss is the fact the boxes cannot tell from a slip. The pair's other lines lie where the other's size puts them, and a near miss on them is a fact of its own, which a reader sees: a small part set by its edge on a larger part's centre, its centre 3 px off; a column whose right edge lies on a header's centre, its left edge 3 px off the header's, on the page's margin; two swatches of one size, one set 3 px past the other, their centres 3 px apart. So the check compares the pair on its other lines on that axis. A meant placement of a small part on the edge of a card, a heading or a rule shows its near miss at that edge alone (Context), so comparing the other lines costs it nothing.

**Why no test of size.** The test leaves the two parts uncompared at that edge whichever is the larger. A part two steps and two pixels long or more, centred on another's edge, shows no near miss at that edge (Context), so there the test leaves nothing uncompared that the check would name. Where both parts are small, the test leaves that edge uncompared and compares the rest, where the slip shows. A test that let only the part no larger than the other be the centred one, and then passed over the whole axis, would turn on which of two parts of near-equal size is the larger. At sizes within about two pixels of each other, the smaller part's centre lies within a pixel of the larger's far edge too, so it would pass over the slip at the centres: a mark 5 px across set by its left edge on the centre of a dot 7 px across, and two swatches of one size, one 3 px past the other ("Alternatives considered").

**Why between groups too.** DEC-067 point 2 has a later record that refines a test among siblings say what it does between groups, since its reason may rest on the two parts sharing a group. This one rests on the boxes alone: a centre within a pixel of an edge, and the like edges there half the centred part's size apart, are one fact whichever groups hold the two. So the check reads it between groups as among siblings: a part is not compared with any leaf of its page, in any group, at an edge of that leaf on which its centre lies. In the row of dots, that is each dot with each other card's box, whose top lies on the dot's middle too, by the same placement. It is also any small part whose centre lies by chance within a pixel of the edge of a leaf of another group, a placement nobody may have set ("What it costs"). Read among siblings alone, the row would still give twelve findings, each a dot with another card's box.

**Why from the boxes, with no word.** DEC-066 point 3 left open whether a centre set on the other's edge counts as a line chosen for a pair named under `lies-over`. The fact this record reads lies in the two boxes, and a word would have each component say again what its parts' places show. `lies-over` speaks of an overlap, not of a line (DEC-066 point 3), and names siblings alone, so read through it, the twelve findings between groups in the row of dots would stay. Whether a component may say that a near miss is meant stays open, for a meant near miss the boxes cannot tell, such as optical overshoot: a round icon set a little past a margin, so that it looks in line with the text beside it.

**Why it names nothing.** The check compares a centre only with a centre (DEC-046 point 3). Naming a centre that nearly lies on an edge would compare each part's centre with each edge of every part it is compared with, a kind of place the check has never measured, whose cost no piece has shown. This record decides only what the check leaves uncompared.

**Why this refines, and overturns nothing.** DEC-046 point 3 decides that the check passes over the near misses that follow from a line chosen. This record adds a kind of line chosen, a centre set on an edge, from which the near miss at that edge follows, and narrows what the check reports, and that decision stands. DEC-067 point 2 leaves the question to a later record, which this is. DEC-066 point 3 leaves it open for named pairs, and this record answers it for every pair, with no word, so DEC-066's words on the cost and on what it leaves open come to say so, and its question of a word for a near miss that is meant stays open (Implications).

### What it costs

- **A slip of about half a small part's size, at an edge, goes unsummed.** A dot 6 px across meant to stand inside a card's top, its top on the card's, that slipped up by 2 px or more and less than 4 px, has its centre within a pixel of the card's top, and is not compared with the card at their tops. Of its slips there that nearly agree under a brand whose smallest step is 4 px, the check names those of less than 2 px alone. Where the test leaves an edge uncompared, the reviewer judges it on the image first, as a kind of place the check's reach leaves out ([visual-communication:DEC-063-where-a-check-measures] point 4).
- **The slip of a part small on one axis alone goes unsummed too.** A stripe the card's width and 4 px tall, meant to lie flush inside the card's top, that slipped up by 2 px, half its height, has its middle on the card's top, and is not compared with it at their tops. In a planted case it gives none, where it gave one. The reviewer's look at the image covers it, as above.
- **Between groups, by chance.** A small part whose centre lies within a pixel of the edge of a leaf of another group, which nobody set it by, is not compared with that leaf at that edge. In a planted case, a dot in one group whose middle lies on the top of a card's box in another, its top 3 px above the box's, gives none, where it gave one. The reviewer judges it on the image first, as above.
- **A centre a little off an edge stays unnamed:** a dot whose middle lies 2 px below its card's top is named only where its top or its bottom nearly agrees with a line of the card's. The reviewer judges it on the image first.
- **On the pages measured, nothing.** The repository the capability is authored in holds a showcase deck and its figure of ties (DEC-067, Context). Computed at commit 710f6d672 of project-kit's default branch, in each scene each draws, with this test and without it, the check gave the same findings: no pair there that the check compares by its lines, on no line chosen, has one part's centre within a pixel of the other's edge. On the starter's reference pieces (Context), it gave the same findings too.
- **In planted cases,** under a brand whose smallest step of space is 4 px:
  - the row of four dotted cards gives none, where it gave four findings among siblings and twelve between groups. The same row with a dot on each card's top right corner gives none, where it gave eight and twelve, and one such card gives none, where it gave two;
  - a timeline whose rule, 2 px wide, runs from the first step's marker's centre to the last's, each marker 6 px across, gives none, where it gave two between groups. With numbers 24 px across in place of the markers, it gives none with this test and without it, since a number that size shows no near miss at the rule's ends;
  - a mark 6 px across centred on the left edge of a text that stands its lines at the start gives none, where it gave one;
  - each of these still gives one finding: the slip, a dot 6 px across set by its left edge on a heading's centre, named at their centres, 3 px apart; the column, named at their left edges, 3 px apart; a mark 5 px across set by its left edge on the centre of a dot 7 px across, named at their centres, 2.5 px apart, and on a dot 7.5 px across, at their centres and their right edges, 2.5 px and 1.25 px apart; and two swatches 6 px across, one set 3 px right of the other, named at their centres, 3 px apart;
  - a tag set 2 px above each card's top edge, centred on no edge, still gives four and twelve.

### Alternatives considered

- **Every edge compared,** as DEC-046 point 3 compares a pair on no line chosen. Rejected: a meant placement repeats its findings in every piece that draws it, sixteen in the row of four dotted cards, each a look for the reviewer at a placement no one got wrong.
- **The whole axis passed over, where the centred part is no larger than the other.** Rejected, for the reason above: at sizes within about two pixels of each other, the smaller part's centre lies within a pixel of the larger's far edge too, so it passes over the slip at the centres. In planted cases, a mark 5 px across set by its left edge on the centre of a dot 7 px across, and two swatches 6 px across, one set 3 px right of the other, each give no finding, where this record gives one.
- **The whole axis passed over, whichever part is the larger.** Rejected: it passes over the slip of a small part set by its edge on a larger part's centre, and a margin missed where a part about half another's size has its edge on that part's centre. In planted cases, the slip and the column each give no finding, where this record gives one.
- **The whole axis passed over, where the centred part is shorter than two steps of space, whatever the other's size.** Rejected: it reads a small part centred on a smaller one's edge as a line chosen, so it passes over the slip at a smaller size. In a planted case, a mark 5 px across set by its left edge on the centre of a dot 7.5 px across gives no finding, where this record gives one.
- **Among siblings alone.** Rejected, for the reason above: the twelve findings between groups in the row of dots would stay.
- **Only for a pair named under `lies-over`,** as DEC-066 point 3 left open. Rejected, for the reason above: `lies-over` speaks of an overlap and names siblings alone, and the boxes show the centre on the edge.
- **Two parts that meet, an edge of one on the other's opposite edge,** as a tab set on a card's top edge has its bottom there, read as a line chosen too. Not taken: their tops differ by the tab's whole height, a near miss only for a part shorter than a step, and the follow-up this record answers asks only of a part centred on another's edge.
- **Naming a centre that nearly lies on an edge as a near miss.** Not taken, for the reason above ("Why it names nothing").
- **A word by which a component says a near miss is meant.** Not this record's: DEC-066 point 3 leaves it open, for the meant near misses the boxes cannot tell.
