**In plain terms:** the check of edges that nearly line up compares like with like: a left edge with a left edge, a centre with a centre, a top with a top. A pair that lines up on one line of an axis is not reported on the others, since their near misses follow from the line chosen. A dot centred on a card's top edge lines up with the card on no such line, so the check reports the dot's top, half its height above the card's, as a near miss. Between groups it reports it again with every other card whose top shares that line, so a row of four such cards gives sixteen findings, though the dots are meant. This record has a part centred on another's edge choose a line too, where it is no larger than the other on that axis: the pair is not reported on that axis, among siblings and between groups alike. The row then gives none.
- **No larger.** A part larger than the other, centred on its edge, chooses no line by it. Were it read as one, a slip would go unreported: a dot set by its left edge on a heading's centre, where its centre was meant, its centre 3 px off the heading's.
- **What it passes over** are the near misses of small parts alone, less than about twice the brand's smallest step of space long on that axis, such as dots, small badges and bullets, which a reader sees by where their centre sits.
- **Nothing new is named.** A centre that nearly lies on an edge is no near miss the check names, since it compares a centre only with a centre.

## Context

[visual-communication:DEC-046-faults-lower-grades] point 3 has the check of edges that nearly line up compare two siblings on each axis by three lines, like with like, and pass over the other lines of an axis on which a pair lines up on one: "that near miss follows from the line chosen". [visual-communication:DEC-067-near-alignment-between-groups] point 2 reads the line chosen alike between groups, and leaves to a later record whether a part centred on another's edge has chosen a line. [visual-communication:DEC-066-meant-overlaps] point 3 counts the near misses such a part gives among its costs, and leaves open whether, for a pair named under `lies-over`, a centre set on the other's edge counts as a line chosen. This record decides it, for every pair ([project-kit#3405](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3405)).

**The row of dots.** Four cards stand in a row, their tops on one line, under a brand whose smallest step of space is 4 px. Each card is a group holding a box 140 by 100 px and a dot 6 px square, the dot's middle on the box's top edge and its centre on the box's centre across. Across, the dot and its box line up at their centres. Down, the dot's middle lies on the box's top, two lines the check never compares with each other, and the dot's top lies 3 px above the box's top: a near miss, in each card. Between groups, each dot lies 3 px above each other card's box in the same way, and gives a finding of its own with each other card (DEC-067, "What it costs"): twelve, beside the four among siblings. Set on a card's top right corner, the same dot gives two near misses in its card, one on each axis.

**What follows from a centre on an edge.** Where a part is centred on another's edge on an axis, its other lines on that axis lie where the two sizes put them. A part of size s, centred on an edge of a part of size b, with s no greater than b, has its near edge s/2 from that edge, its centre b/2 from the other's centre, and its far edge b − s/2 from the other's far edge. Each is at least s/2, less a pixel where its centre lies up to a pixel of rounding off the edge. So a near miss on another line of that axis, less than the brand's smallest step, needs s shorter than two steps, or than two steps and two pixels where its centre lies a pixel off the edge.

The words it uses:
- **A part**, **siblings**, **a leaf**, **two leaves of different groups**, **the lines of an axis**, **nearly agree**, **a near miss**, **a text's room** and **across** and **down** are DEC-067's. **The line chosen** is the test DEC-046 point 3 makes of siblings and DEC-067 point 2 makes between groups: a pair that lines up within a pixel of rounding on one line of an axis is not reported on another line of it.
- **A step** is the brand's smallest step of space at the scene's key, which bounds what nearly agrees, and **two steps** are twice its length.
- **An edge** of a part on an axis is its start or its end there: across, its left or its right edge, and down, its top or its bottom. **Its centre** on an axis is its centre across and its middle down. **Its size** on an axis is its box's width across and its height down.
- **A line a part is compared on** is a line of an axis on which the check compares it: every line, but across, for a text that does not fill its width, the line it stands its lines on alone (DEC-067 point 3).
- **Centred on another's edge:** a part is centred on another's edge on an axis where its centre lies within a pixel of rounding of an edge of the other there, its centre a line it is compared on and that edge a line the other is compared on.

## Decision

**Where one part of a pair, no larger than the other on an axis, is centred on the other's edge, the pair has chosen a line on that axis, and the check of edges that nearly line up reports it on none of that axis's lines, among siblings and between groups alike. A part larger than the other, centred on its edge, chooses no line by it.**

### 1. A part centred on another's edge, and no larger than it, has chosen a line

- **The test.** On an axis, where one part of a pair is centred on the other's edge and is no larger than the other there, the pair has chosen a line on that axis. The check reports it on none of that axis's lines, as it reports a pair that lines up on one line on none of the others (DEC-046 point 3).
- **Either part.** The test reads each part of the pair as the one centred, whichever is drawn first. Two parts of one size, each set half its size past the other, as overlapping avatars are, are each centred on the other's edge.
- **The lines each is compared on.** A text that does not fill its width is centred on an edge across only where it stands its lines in the middle, and offers across only the edge on which it stands its lines (DEC-067 point 3). So a bullet centred on the left edge of a text that stands its lines at the start has chosen a line with it. Down, a text is compared on every line, as any part is.
- **Lines alone.** The test reads a pair the check compares by their lines. A pair one of which holds the other within a pixel is compared by the inner part's insets, as DEC-046 point 3 decides, and the test does not read it.
- **It names nothing.** The test only passes pairs over. A centre that nearly lies on an edge, more than a pixel and less than a step off it, is no near miss the check names, since it compares a centre only with a centre (DEC-046 point 3).

### 2. Between groups as among siblings

- **The same test.** Point 1 holds of two leaves of different groups, each where it lies on its page, as DEC-067 point 2 reads the line chosen between groups.
- **Its reason rests on no group the two share.** A dot centred on its card's top edge lies on that edge's line, which the other cards' tops share, and its near misses with them follow from the same placement and the same sizes. So in the row of dots, each dot has chosen a line with each other card's box, and the row gives no finding, among siblings or between groups.
- **No finding names such a pair.** A pair that has chosen a line on an axis shows no near miss there, so no finding names it on that axis: no finding of two siblings, no finding of two lists and no finding of its own (DEC-067 points 4 and 5).

### 3. A part larger than the other, centred on its edge, chooses no line by it

- **Compared as before.** Where the only part of a pair centred on the other's edge is larger than the other on that axis, the pair is compared on that axis's lines as before.
- **The near misses it would pass over.** Such a part's near edge lies half its size from the other's edge. A near miss of the pair lies at their centres, where the other is less than about two steps long, or at their far edges, where the other is about half the centred part's size. The first is the slip of a small part set by its edge where its centre was meant: a dot 6 px across, set by its left edge on a heading's centre, its centre 3 px off the heading's. The second may be chance or a margin missed: a column whose right edge lies on a header's centre, its left edge 3 px off the header's, on the page's margin. A reader sees both, and the check names both.

## Rationale

**Why a centre on an edge is a line chosen.** A component that sets a part's centre on another's edge sets it by that edge: a dot on a card's top, a badge on its corner, a bullet on a text's margin, a step's number on a timeline's rule, a legend on a frame's top. The other lines of the axis then lie where the two sizes put them, as two cards of different widths on one margin differ at their right edges (DEC-046 point 3). The dot's top lies half its height above the card's top wherever the card stands. A near miss there names no placement missed. Its finding asks the reviewer to judge a choice no one got wrong, in every piece that draws the component, and between groups once more with each side whose edge shares the line.

**Why no larger than the other.** What follows from a centre on an edge (Context) bounds what the test passes over. With the centred part no larger than the other, every near miss it passes over needs that part shorter than two steps on the axis, or than two steps and two pixels where its centre lies a pixel off the edge: under 8 px, or 10 px, where the step is 4 px. Such a part's edges lie less than a step from its centre, and a reader sees it by where it sits, its centre on the edge, so its edges are no lines a reader reads. With the centred part the larger, the test would pass over the near misses of the other's centre, where the other is small, and of the two parts' far edges, where the other is about half its size: the slip and the column of point 3, which a reader sees. Two parts of one size pass the test from either side, since each is no larger than the other.

**Why between groups too.** DEC-067 point 2 has a later record that refines a test among siblings say what it does between groups, since its reason may rest on the two parts sharing a group. This one rests on none: a centre is set on an edge's line, which runs the length of the page as a margin does, and which parts of other groups share. Read among siblings alone, the row of dots would still give twelve findings, each a dot with another card's box, by the placement the test passes over in its own card.

**Why from the boxes, with no word.** DEC-066 point 3 left open whether a centre set on the other's edge counts as a line chosen for a pair named under `lies-over`. The check tells the line chosen of every other pair from the two boxes alone, and the boxes show a centre on an edge as plainly as two centres that agree. A word would have each component say again what its parts' places show. `lies-over` speaks of an overlap, not of a line (DEC-066 point 3), and names siblings alone, so read through it, the twelve findings between groups in the row of dots would stay. Whether a component may say that a near miss is meant stays open, for a near miss the boxes cannot tell, such as a part set on purpose a little off an edge.

**Why this refines, and overturns nothing.** DEC-046 point 3 decides that the check passes over the near misses that follow from a line chosen. This record adds a kind of line chosen, and narrows what the check reports, and that decision stands. DEC-067 point 2 leaves the question to a later record, which this is. DEC-066 point 3 leaves it open for named pairs, and this record answers it for every pair, with no word, so DEC-066's words on the cost and on what it leaves open come to say so, and its question of a word for a near miss that is meant stays open (Implications).

### What it costs

- **A near miss of a small part centred on an edge by chance** is passed over: a dot meant to stand on a card's top by its own top, whose centre lies on it instead, so that it rises half its height above. Between groups the chance is wider, since a small part's centre may lie within a pixel of the edge of a larger part in any group of its page. Where the check passes a pair over so, the reviewer judges it on the image first, as a kind of place the check's reach leaves out ([visual-communication:DEC-063-where-a-check-measures] point 4).
- **A centre a little off an edge stays unnamed:** a dot whose middle lies 2 px below its card's top is named only where its top or its bottom nearly agrees with a line of the card's. The reviewer judges it on the image first.
- **On the pages measured, nothing.** The repository the capability is authored in holds a showcase deck and its figure of ties (DEC-067, Context). Computed at commit 710f6d672 of project-kit's default branch, in each scene each draws, with this test and without it, the check gave the same findings: no pair there has a part centred on another's edge with a near miss on another line of that axis. Read whatever the two parts' sizes, it gave the same findings too.
- **In planted cases,** under a brand whose smallest step of space is 4 px: the row of four dotted cards gives none, where it gave four findings among siblings and twelve between groups. The same row with a dot on each card's top right corner gives none, where it gave eight and twelve. A bullet centred on the left edge of a text that stands its lines at the start gives none, where it gave one, and so do two dots of one size, one set half its size past the other. The slip and the column of point 3 each still give one, and read whatever the parts' sizes, they would give none. A tag set 2 px above each card's top edge, centred on no edge, still gives four and twelve.

### Alternatives considered

- **No centre on an edge as a line chosen,** as before. Rejected: a meant placement repeats its findings in every piece that draws it, sixteen in the row of four dotted cards, each a look for the reviewer at a choice no one got wrong.
- **A centre on an edge, whichever part is the larger.** Rejected, for the reason above: it passes over the slip of a small part set by its edge on a larger part's centre, and a margin missed where a part about half another's size has its edge on that part's centre. In planted cases, the slip and the column each give no finding, where this record gives one.
- **A centred part shorter than two steps of space, whatever the other's size,** in place of "no larger than the other". Not taken. It bounds by the brand's step what the sizes already bound, since a part no larger than the other shows a near miss on another line only where it is about that short. And it reads a small part centred on a smaller one's edge as a line chosen, so it passes over the slip at a smaller size: in a planted case, a mark 5 px across set by its left edge on the centre of a dot 7.5 px across, its centre 2.5 px off the dot's, gives no finding, where this record gives one.
- **Among siblings alone.** Rejected, for the reason above: the twelve findings between groups in the row of dots would stay.
- **Only for a pair named under `lies-over`,** as DEC-066 point 3 left open. Rejected, for the reason above: `lies-over` speaks of an overlap and names siblings alone, and the boxes show the centre on the edge.
- **Any line of one part on another line of the other,** an edge on the other's centre and an edge on the other's far edge among them. Not taken. An edge on a larger part's centre is the slip, read from the smaller part's side, which point 3 keeps named. An edge on the other's far edge is two parts that meet, such as a tab set on a card's top edge, whose other lines on that axis differ by a whole part's size, a near miss only for a part shorter than a step, and the follow-up this record answers asks only of a part centred on another's edge.
- **Naming a centre that nearly lies on an edge as a near miss.** Not taken: it would compare each part's centre with each edge of every part it is compared with, a kind of place the check has never measured, whose cost no piece has shown. This record decides only what the check passes over.
- **A word by which a component says a near miss is meant.** Not this record's: DEC-066 point 3 leaves it open, for the near misses the boxes cannot tell.
