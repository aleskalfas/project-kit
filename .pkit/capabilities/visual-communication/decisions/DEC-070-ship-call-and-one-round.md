**In plain terms:** whether a piece ships is the person's call, at every level of how much agents decide alone. The graphic-design review informs that call and never makes it. It reports its findings, with no grade for a rule, no mark for a page and no verdict. No project sets a bar, no hold ends the designer's loop, and no calibration runs. The loop is one round. The designer draws, and the review reads every page. The designer then mends once each fault, each brief finding, each break a rule's text settles, each fact its source contradicts and each difference told by colour alone, and leaves readings and notes of taste to the person. The review reads every page again, and the piece goes to the person. The rule sets stay the canon both agents read and cite. Whose words the designer changes, what the review is handed and in what order, and where a fix belongs carry over from the record of the grades, reworded for findings. This record supersedes that record and the record of what lapses the hold.

## Context

[visual-communication:DEC-042-grades-bar-and-loop] had the review grade each rule in words, and a project set a *bar* in the capability's configuration. A command read a *verdict* on the piece from the grades and the bar. Once whoever maintains the capability accepted a *calibration* of the review, the verdict ended the designer's loop at the bar, with polish within a bound, and that switch was the *hold*. [visual-communication:DEC-065-checks-lapse-the-hold] had any change to the capability's code lapse the hold.

No calibration met its conditions. The first passed no design (DEC-042, Context). The calibration of [project-kit#3230](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3230) graded more leniently than its outside judges, and its report was never accepted ([visual-communication:DEC-068-focal-point-in-the-content], Context).

**The tests.** The maintainer then judged a development set: one presentation that explains, in twelve designs. A loop under a critic drew six, two were drawn weak on purpose, the capability drew one, and a fresh session given only the words and the brief drew three. The tests and their figures are in the scratchpad note `design-review-ship-call` of the repository the capability is authored in ([project-kit#3468](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3468)).
- **The person shipped only the fresh session's designs.** The polished rounds of the loop ranked below an earlier round, and its first draft did not ship either. A second fresh session drew another presentation three ways, and the person shipped all three.
- **No verdict drew the person's line.** A model that judged a design alone passed the loop's rounds. Given a principle, or the person's shipped work beside it, its verdict on a whole design failed designs the person shipped, or every design.
- **Page by page, beside the person's shipped work and with the principle, it saw what the person saw.** Its marks on each page split the shipped designs from the rest. A count of marks matched the person on eleven of twelve designs in each of three runs, but the count was fixed after a run, and every miss sat at its edge.
- **How far it goes.** The sample is small: two fresh sessions drew every design the person shipped, and one loop drew most of those that failed. It shows nothing of why the fresh designs worked, or whether the model would fail a weak free draft.

The words it uses:
- **The person** is the person [COR-064](../../../decisions/core/COR-064-answer-delegate.md) names, for whom the designer works, and whom it tells and asks ([visual-communication:DEC-030-design-agents] point 2).
- **The ship call** is the decision that a piece goes to its audience as it stands.
- **The review** is what the capability's reviewer does: it reads the pages drawn, and reports findings of four kinds, a fault, a rule finding, a brief finding and a note (DEC-030 point 6).
- **A settled break** is a rule finding whose rule's text settles the break with no reading left to choose. A *reading* is a rule finding that needs a reading the rule's text does not settle (DEC-030 point 6).
- **A round** is one mend by the designer, then a render, a look and a review of every page. The designer's *loop* is its rounds, from the first review on.
- **The asker** is whoever asked for the piece ([visual-communication:DEC-029-piece-brief] point 4).
- **The four gaps** are what the first calibration found the review judging weakly: craft, typography, legibility at the medium's size and colour. [visual-communication:DEC-046-faults-lower-grades] placed each.
- **A level** is one of the levels of the delegation setting, by which a person chooses how much agents decide in their place ([COR-072](../../../decisions/core/COR-072-delegation-levels.md)).

## Decision

**The person makes the ship call on a piece, at every level, and the review informs it with its findings alone. The designer's loop is one round: it mends once what this record names, the review reads every page again, and the piece goes to the person. Whose words the designer changes, what the review is handed and where a fix belongs carry over from DEC-042, and the four gaps stay where DEC-046 placed them.**

### 1. The person makes the ship call, and the review informs it

- **The ship call is the person's.** The person makes it on the piece and on the review's report, which the round hands over (point 2).
- **The review informs it.** The review reports its findings, with no grade for a rule, no mark for a page and no verdict on the piece. Nothing it reports passes or fails a piece. One observation that fails the words of several rules is one finding that cites each (DEC-030 point 6).
- **No bar, no hold, no calibration.** A project sets no bar, and no command reads a verdict from the review. Nothing ends the designer's loop but the round's end and the person, so no hold exists for a calibration to switch on, and none runs.
- **At every level.** This record places the ship call at no level (COR-072 point 13), so it stays the person's at every level. No agent makes it in the person's place, the designer, the reviewer and a delegate among them. The designer hands each piece to the person, and never reports it done.
- **The rule sets stay.** `COMM` and `GRAPHIC` stay the canon the designer and the review read and cite ([visual-communication:DEC-028-principles-of-visual-communication] point 5). Nothing grades their rules one by one.

### 2. The designer's loop is one round

- **The round.** The designer draws the piece. It mends each failure of a check before any review, since what a check measures stays the check's (DEC-030 point 7). The review reads every page in every language, mode and size the piece has, each band of a tall page included (DEC-030 point 2). The designer mends once what point 3 names, renders, and mends what the checks then find. The review reads every page again. Then the piece goes to the person.
- **What goes to the person.** The piece as the round left it, and the second review's report as the review wrote it. With them go what the designer changed and why (DEC-030 point 2), each finding it left with why, and each question still open (point 4).
- **No polish.** The designer runs no round of its own after the second review, whatever that review finds. What the round did not clear reaches the person there, and is no question of its own.
- **A round the person asks for.** Where the person asks for mends rather than ship the piece, the designer mends what the person names, within point 4, and the review reads every page again. The piece then goes back to the person, as at the first round's end.

### 3. What the round mends

The designer mends, in the round:
- **each fault,** an error of drawing and never a judgement of design (DEC-030 point 6);
- **each brief finding,** a page that does not show what its brief asks;
- **each settled break** of an accepted rule, whether or not the rule binds;
- **each fact its source contradicts,** whether the review reports it as a rule finding or as a note (DEC-030 point 7). It corrects the fact to its source, within point 4;
- **each difference told by colour alone,** which `RS-GRAPHIC-006` judges, a reading among them.

How it mends them, and what it leaves:
- **A mend, not a redesign.** Each mend changes what its finding names, at the place the finding says its fix belongs (point 6), and nothing the finding does not need.
- **A finding it judges wrong,** such as a fault it reads as meant, it leaves, and says why. The second review reads that page again, so the person sees both.
- **What it leaves to the person:** a reading, a note, and a finding the review marked (point 5). It says of each what it would do, and mends none of them unless the person asks (point 2).

### 4. Whose words the designer changes alone, and what it asks

Whose words are whose holds wherever the designer mends a piece.
- **The asker's words.** A piece's intent belongs to the asker (DEC-029 point 4; DEC-030 point 2). The *asker's words* carry it: the brief's goals and relations; each page's lead and title, and each takeaway, once the asker has seen them; any word a goal names as the asker's, such as a quote; and their translations. The designer changes none of them without the asker's word, and mends a translation only to make it say what its source says.
- **By where they came from.** Words are the asker's once the asker has seen them: those the asker wrote, and those the designer drafted and showed the asker, as it shows the goals and relations (DEC-030 point 2). Until then a lead, title or takeaway the designer drafted is its own to change. Returning it to whoever dispatched the designer is not showing it to the asker.
- **The makers' record of drafts.** For each lead, title and takeaway that the designer drafted and the asker has not seen, the brief records which one it is, by its page, its kind and the language of its text, and a digest of that text, never the text. The record lies in the page's part, or in the part for the whole piece on a piece of one page. The designer records a draft as it writes it, and clears it once the asker has seen it. A lead, title or takeaway whose text no longer matches its recorded digest is the asker's, and so is one the record lists nothing for, a piece with no brief included. So a record lost, stale or never written leads the designer to ask.
- **Every other word is the designer's, within DEC-030 point 4.** It may reword, cut or move any other text, in every language, where each goal and relation still holds. It never bends the meaning, nor changes what the piece says to fit what a library draws (DEC-030 point 4). It may correct a fact to its source, and cut one with no source unless a goal or relation rests on it. A fact it adds names its source (`RS-COMM-005`). It tells the person afterwards what it changed (DEC-030 point 2).
- **What it asks.** It asks the person only where a fix would change the asker's words or cut a fact a goal or relation rests on, and where a fix needs a write it may not make (DEC-030 point 5). Each time it says what it recommends.
- **At every level.** This record places none of these questions at a level (COR-072 point 13), so they stay the person's at every level, as the ship call does (point 1). What the designer changes alone, it changes at every level: it tells the person afterwards what it changed, and the second review reads the pages it changed. Nothing else checks it.

### 5. What the review is handed, and which findings it marks

- **What it reads.** Every image the viewing step drew, with the frames and the report; the rule sets; the design knowledge (DEC-030 point 8); and the sources the piece's facts name (DEC-030 point 7).
- **The brief's intent first.** A command of the capability hands the review the brief's goals, relations and takeaways, without their reasons. The review judges each page against them (DEC-030 point 6), and reads the brief's file only once its findings are written.
- **Its first report, only after its second findings.** Whoever hands the review its second reading of a round hands it its report of the first, as it wrote it, only once its findings for the second are written. Nobody summarises, filters or answers that report first, and the review reads no account of what the designer changed. It then says of each finding of its first report whether the page still shows it.
- **What the order rests on.** For the reasons and its first report, the order rests on the reviewer's restraint, since its tools read the repository. Its report says what it read before writing its findings, so a breach shows.
- **A need.** Where only a tool the engine lacks would clear a finding, the review marks the finding a *need*, and names the tool. The need goes to the person as a question that names what the design needs, as DEC-030 point 4 has a stop do. It stops only the work that rests on it.
- **The asker's.** Where only the asker's words (point 4) would clear a finding, the review marks it as the asker's and names the words, and it goes to the person as a question.
- **Marked by the review alone.** A finding is marked only where no fix the designer may make would clear it. The designer marks nothing, and the round mends no marked finding.

### 6. A finding names where its fix belongs

A finding names where its fix belongs, and never what the fix is, as DEC-030 point 7 decides. The places a fix belongs take in two more: the asker's words, for a finding only they would clear, and a tool the engine lacks, for a need (point 5).

### 7. Where the gaps belong

- **The four places.** A gap in what the review judges belongs, in this order, to the reading, in the reviewer's body or the design knowledge, where a rule or a fault already names it; to a check, for each part that needs no judgement; to a brand's values, where it is the look; and to a rule of the canon, with its source, for what is left (DEC-028 point 1; DEC-046 point 1).
- **The four gaps stay where DEC-046 placed them** (DEC-046 points 3 to 6). Typography is `RS-GRAPHIC-008`'s. Craft is the faults', `RS-GRAPHIC-002`'s and, for a leader, `RS-GRAPHIC-009`'s, with the two checks of boxes. Legibility at the medium's size is `RS-GRAPHIC-005`'s and the check of a text's size's. Colour is `RS-GRAPHIC-001`'s and `RS-GRAPHIC-007`'s, a brand's and a note's. Each holds with no grade: the review reports what it finds there as a fault, a rule finding or a note.
- **No borrowed measure becomes a rule.** An outside judge's measures, and the person's reasons for a ship call, show where the canon is thin, and are never a rule's source (DEC-028 point 1).
- **A defect of design the person finds blocking** becomes a rule's, a new one with its source or one that already names it, through a record (DEC-028 point 1). It never joins the faults, which judge no design (DEC-030 point 6). The round mends it where that rule's text settles it (point 3).
- **What stays the checks'.** Contrast, a text that overflows its box and a text smaller than its medium allows stay the checks' (DEC-028 point 1).

### 8. A fact its source contradicts and colour as the only signal stay out of the faults

- **Each is a rule's already.** `RS-COMM-005` asks that each fact a piece that explains shows be true against its source, and `RS-GRAPHIC-006` that colour is never the only signal.
- **Neither is a fault.** A fault is a drawing that does not show what the content, the brand or a passing check says it should (DEC-030 point 6). A false fact is the content's own error, which the drawing shows as the content gives it. A difference told by colour alone is a choice of form, which its rule judges. As faults, either would change what a fault is.
- **The round mends both all the same** (point 3). A fact its source contradicts in a piece that does not explain, which no rule judges, is a note that names its source (DEC-030 point 7), and the round mends it too.

### 9. The triggers that waited on a calibration

A trigger to revisit that waited on what a calibration finds waits on the same finding where it now shows: in the person's ship calls, in the review's reports across the pieces a project makes, or in a test that compares the review with the person, as the tests of Context did. Each such trigger reads so:
- **DEC-046's alternative "A rule on every text of more than one line":** the person, or the review's reports across pieces, find headings or items of a list that wrap badly, and a source states a test for them.
- **DEC-046's alternative "`RS-GRAPHIC-005` split in two rules":** the review judges whether a text can be read by how much a page holds.
- **The alternative "A released word gives the review the fact" of [visual-communication:DEC-066-meant-overlaps]:** a test that compares the review's judgement of overlaps with a person's.
- **"When to revisit" in [visual-communication:DEC-067-near-alignment-between-groups]:** the review's reports across the pieces a project makes show most findings labelled `near-alignment-between-groups` judged no fault. The count is read from those reports, which say of each such finding whether it is a fault.
- **The alternative "The course's clauses for every line of a page" of [visual-communication:DEC-069-leaders-and-layers]:** the person, or the review's reports across pieces, find a base's lines crossing at glancing angles that no rule judges, and a source states a test for them.

A trigger whose alternative weighs a calibration, a bar or the hold itself takes none, since nothing is left for it to reopen.

## Rationale

**Why the person makes the ship call.** The person's call was the one judge that held. A model that judged a design alone passed what the person failed. Given the person's shipped work, its verdict on a whole design failed what the person shipped, and only a count fixed after a run came near the person's line. Two calibrations met no condition, so no verdict was ever shown to draw that line, and a verdict that ended a loop without it would ship what the person would not.

**Why no grades, bar or hold.** The grades served the bar, and the bar the hold. With no hold, a grade in words is a reading the person weighs beside the findings, and the guards of the hold buy nothing: the digests that lapse it, and a fresh ladder and an outside judge for each calibration. A finding carries what the person reads in a grade: the rule, the page and the evidence.

**Why at every level.** A record that places a question at a level names what checks the agent's choice, or says that the person is told and nothing checks it (COR-072 point 13). The tests found nothing that can check the ship call, and a person told afterwards learns of a piece already gone to its audience. The asker's words carry what the asker meant, which only the asker can say. A write outside the designer's grant would widen what it may write, which COR-072 point 7 keeps for a person at every level. So none of these questions is placed at a level.

**Why one round.** The polished rounds ranked below an earlier round of the same loop. A round that mends what is plainly wrong spares the person faults the designer could have mended alone, and the second reading shows the person what is left. A second round of the designer's own would be polish. A round the person asks for is the person's own judgement at work, so it runs on what the person names.

**Why the round mends these, and leaves the rest.** A fault, a brief finding, a settled break, a false fact and a difference told by colour alone each have one answer, which the designer finds without taste. A reading and a note of taste are where the polish happened, so the person weighs them with the review's report. A finding the designer judges wrong it leaves with its reason, so the round never distorts a page to quiet a false finding, and the second reading shows the person both. A difference told by colour alone is mended even where the review reads it rather than settles it, since a reader who cannot tell the colours apart loses the difference either way.

**Why the review's order and marks carry over.** A review that reads the brief's reasons first sees the page through them (DEC-030, "Why the reasons are read last"), and its first report, read first, would anchor its second reading the same way. Marks keep the round from spending a mend where no mend clears the finding. A designer that marked findings would take them out of its own test.

**Why a finding names where its fix belongs.** Only the designer renders, so only it learns whether a fix fits. A prescription also meets limits the review does not weigh, such as the asker's words.

**Why whose words carry over.** DEC-029 gives the goals and relations to the asker (point 4), and makes a lead the page's takeaway (point 2). A lead states the page's point, the intent the asker owns, and a title is read with its lead (`RS-COMM-001`). Words the designer drafted carry its reading until the asker has seen them, so it mends its own draft freely, and words the asker has read never change behind the asker's back. The record of drafts lives in the brief, the makers' own file, and keeps a digest, since the text already lies in the content (DEC-029 points 1 and 2). A designer that asked before each change of words would wait on the person at every mend, so every other word is its own, within a meaning it never bends.

**Why the gaps stay where DEC-046 placed them.** DEC-046 placed each gap where a rule, a fault, a check or a brand already judged it, or gave it a rule from the canon. None of those places needed a grade. Only how far a fault moved a grade did, and that goes with the grades.

**Why neither joins the faults.** A fault stays the one kind of finding that judges neither the content's truth nor the design, so the review and the designer read it alike. The round mends a false fact and a difference told by colour alone all the same, so making them faults would gain nothing.

**Why the triggers move.** A trigger that waits on a calibration would never fire, since none runs. The finding it waits on now shows in the person's calls, in the review's reports, or in a test that compares the two.

### Alternatives considered

- **The grades kept, to inform.** Rejected: they served the bar, and the person reads the findings directly.
- **A verdict on the whole piece from page marks,** such as a design that ships where at least eight of its ten pages are marked at the level. Not taken: the count was fixed after a run, its margin is thin, and a long piece that fails only its opening and dividing pages and its pictures still passes. Its trigger to revisit: the person's review becomes the bottleneck (COR-007), and a test with its count fixed before it draws the person's line on pieces of several lengths.
- **The bar and the hold kept, for a calibration still to come.** Rejected: two calibrations met no condition, each costs a fresh ladder and an outside judge, and the hold would lapse at the next change to the capability's code.
- **Two rounds, then the person,** as DEC-030 point 2 had it. Rejected: the second round polishes.
- **No round of mends.** Rejected: the person would meet faults and false facts the designer can mend alone.
- **Every finding mended in the round.** Rejected: the readings and the notes are where the polish happened.
- **A fact its source contradicts, and colour as the only signal, as faults.** Rejected: point 8.
- **Page marks against a project's shipped examples, now.** Not taken: the test [project-kit#3468](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3468) runs weighs them first. Its trigger to revisit: that test.
- **The ship call placed at a level,** such as land. Rejected: nothing yet checks it in the person's place.
- **DEC-042 overturned in part, its points 4, 5 and 7 left standing there.** Not taken: they would stand in a record whose title, summary and other points decide the grades, the bar and the hold. Here each reads in the terms of findings.
