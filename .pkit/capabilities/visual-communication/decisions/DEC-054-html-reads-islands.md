**In plain terms:** when the viewing step draws a page through a browser, its report names a full stop, a decimal point or the dot of ż that a later part hides, or that the page's edge cuts, however few its pixels. DEC-037's read names such a loss where it passes a *sliver*, a share of the text's em squared, and a small dot can hold less than that. In the deck's faces, a probe found that read leaving a full stop or a decimal point hidden whole unnamed at many sizes below an em of about 21 device pixels, and the dot of ż up to 38. So a cause is named too where it takes an *island* of the text's ink, a piece that stands apart from its letter, by the measure the SVG renderer's report already uses (DEC-045). A loss that takes no island is still judged by the sliver. A box's edge across the foot of a line mostly takes none. Stepped across a line's foot at ems of 10 to 20, and in Regular up to 40, in a light key, a dark one and both forced palettes, in the deck's faces, it took an island the sliver left at an em of 11.7 in Source Sans Pro Regular and of 11.5 in Source Code Pro alone, in the light palettes, where it hid one strong pixel that stood apart at a descender's foot. Such a graze is named, and the layout mends it by moving the box clear, as it mends each graze the sliver names. One look of the deck in each key and forced palette gained no entry. The entry says when a cause took an island, as through resvg. The first read of each browser start plants a small full stop under a box, so a start whose read misses it stops. A box's edge across a dot, a translucent part over one, and a dot past the page's edge were not drawn through a browser. For example, where a later box hides the decimal point of a small "3.5", the report lists that text as covered, and says that the cause took an island of its ink.

## Decision

**Through a browser, the viewing step names a cut or a cover that takes an island of a span's ink, as DEC-045 has it through resvg, beside each loss that passes DEC-037's sliver, and the entry says that its cause took an island.**

### 1. A cause that takes an island is named, whatever the sliver holds

- **Named:** a cut or a cover that loses at least the sliver of the span's strong ink, or that takes an island of the span's counted ink, however few its pixels. It takes one as DEC-045 point 1 has a cause take one: it hides some pixel of the island wholly and leaves none of it showing at the island's level. Past the page's edge, where a pixel is cut or shows, a cause takes an island only by cutting all of it.
- **The sliver stays an area.** DEC-037 point 1's area, in the span's em², which the outputs page states, judges a loss by its size. A loss under it that takes no island is a sliver, and names no entry.
- **Cut and covered, on counted ink.** Counted ink past the page's edge is cut, and counted ink on the page that the page drawn whole hides under a part drawn after it is covered. A loss's size is judged on strong ink, and its shape on islands of counted ink, as through resvg. So a faint dot with no strong ink can be named.
- **Each cause alone,** as DEC-037 point 1 has it, and an island is taken only by one cause.
- **A graze that takes an island is named too.** Where a box's edge across a line hides wholly a strong pixel that stands apart from the rest of its letter at its level, such as one at a descender's foot, the cause takes an island, and the entry names it with `island`, though its loss lies under the sliver. The probe found such grazes at one em in each of two faces, in the light palettes alone (Context). Each is mended where its cause lies, as DEC-037 point 1 has any covered span mended: by moving the box clear of the line.
- **Left unnamed:** a loss under the sliver that takes no island, such as part of a stroke, most grazes among them, or a dot of which a pixel still shows at its level.
- **Known gaps,** as DEC-045 point 1 gives them through resvg. A dot the face draws fused to its letter at every level is no island of its own, and a mark joined to its letter, such as an ogonek, is none either: the sliver alone judges each. The probe could not set Regular's dot of ż apart from the z at ems of 10.7 and 10.9 in the light palettes, nor at 15 to 20 draws in the dark ones, nor the point inside "3.5" from its digits at 5 draws in the dark ones. A mark partly past the page's edge and partly under a part is left unnamed where neither cause takes an island of it or loses the sliver. A small dot that a box's edge fades without hiding any pixel of it wholly takes no island.

### 2. Darkness, read in the browser's two colours

- **Darkness.** Through a browser, a pixel's darkness is how far the span's letters alone change it between the two colours in which DEC-037 point 3 measures strength, written as DEC-045 writes darkness: 0 where they change it by the whole difference between the two colours, and 255 where by none. Strong ink stays as DEC-037 point 3 counts it, where they change it by more than half, which this darkness writes as 127 or darker.
- **Hidden wholly, and what shows.** A pixel is hidden wholly where the page drawn whole does not change it at all as the span's letters change between the two colours (DEC-037 point 3). It shows at a level where the page drawn whole changes it by so much of the difference that, drawn black on white, it would be that dark or darker.
- **In every key and forced palette** the two colours are those DEC-037 point 3 takes there, so each image reads islands as it reads strength.
- **The levels and the joining** are those the outputs page states for the measure of islands, one set for both renderers, and they change there with no new record (DEC-045 point 1). A change weighs both renderers. The figures were found at the values the page holds: the levels 127, 159 and 191, pixels joined by a side or a corner.

### 3. Its entry says so

- **`island`** is true on a `cut` or `covered` entry whose cause takes an island of the span's counted ink, whatever its share, as DEC-045 point 2 gives it through resvg. Another entry holds no such key. So through either renderer a reader tells a dot lost from a graze, and the command's line says so.
- **`share`** stays the share of the span's strong ink that the cause loses (DEC-037 point 2). Where the span holds no strong ink, it is the share of its counted ink, as DEC-045 point 2 has it through resvg, so a faint full stop hidden whole reads 1.

### 4. The first read plants a dot, and names the háček plant by its size

- **The dot plant:** a full stop, a run of its own, under a later box. Each start's first read sets it at the command's text floor in device pixels (DEC-015 point 5), as it sets the háček plant, from its page's drawn width and its start's device scale. So it holds the same share of its em in every start.
- **Named with `island`.** Each start's first read names the dot plant as covered, with `island`. Where the read leaves it unnamed, or names it without `island`, the run stops, as it stops for a plant missed ([visual-communication:DEC-020-html-page-drawing] point 7).
- **The háček plant passes the sliver by its size.** The measure takes an island of the háček plant too (Context), so naming it does not show that its loss passes the sliver. Each start's first read works out the plant's loss from its entry's `share` and the plant's strong ink, and where that loss lies under the sliver, the run stops, as where the read leaves the plant unnamed. So a start in which the sliver lies above the háček plant's loss stops, and the first read holds the sliver's lower side, which a mark fused to its letter needs, since the sliver alone judges it (point 1).
- **The edge control is planted as DEC-037 point 5 plants it, and holds only that its own placement stays unnamed.** Its last device row holds more strong ink than the sliver at its em, in every key and forced palette the probe drew, and its box's edge sits where it hides none of that row wholly (Context). So the control holds that a graze where its own box's edge sits stays unnamed. It does not hold the sliver's upper side, that a graze of that row stays unnamed wherever the box's edge falls, and the sliver is not set above that row: this record narrows DEC-037 point 5 to the sliver's lower side, which the háček plant holds (Implications), and [project-kit#3279](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3279) follows up what is to hold the upper side. Its box hides none of its counted ink wholly, in any key or forced palette, so the measure has nothing there to take, and the control does not hold that a graze takes no island either. Nothing in a start holds that: it rests on the probe (Context). Where the read names the control, by the sliver or by an island, the run stops (DEC-020 point 7).

## Rationale

**Why a measure of shape, and no smaller area.** The sliver judges a loss by its size against the em. A dot hidden whole loses all of its strong ink, which in the deck's faces fell to 0.0038 of the em squared, and to none for a faint dot. An area small enough to name every dot that holds strong ink would name grazes the sliver leaves: one strong pixel passes 0.0038 of the em squared at any em up to 16 device pixels, and past an em of 10 the sliver leaves a graze of one strong pixel to the reviewer (DEC-037, Rationale). And no area of strong ink names a dot that holds none. Shape tells them apart, as DEC-045 found through resvg: a graze takes part of a stroke, which still shows at its level, and a box over a dot takes an island. Through a browser it tells them apart at all but a few graze steps, where a strong pixel at a descender's foot stands apart from its stroke (Context, and "The grazes it names" below). A dot lost unnamed runs two sentences together, or turns 3.5 into 35 (DEC-045, Context), and DEC-037 point 1 accepts no such loss.

**The grazes it names, a cost.** At an em of 11.7 in Regular and of 11.5 in Source Code Pro, in the light palettes, the browser drew a descender's foot with a strong pixel that touched no other strong pixel, so a box whose edge hid it whole took an island, though the loss lay under the sliver. Through resvg, DEC-045's threshold names a loss of one whole pixel below an em of about 14.1, so its read names such a graze by its size, and through either renderer the report names it alike. Like any covered span, it is mended by moving the box clear of the line (point 1). It errs toward naming, as DEC-037 point 3 accepts that a dark image's residue does. The probe found 32 such steps among the 75,750 it drew in the light palettes from an em of 10 to 20, and none among Regular's 50,000 there from 20.1 to 40. Every other island the measure took, the sliver named too. One look of the deck gained no entry. Which steps meet such a pixel turns on where a text falls among the device's pixels, so a piece may meet one at an em the probe did not, and the record is revisited if it comes to name grazes in looks of pieces.

**Why DEC-045's way of taking an island, by name.** One rule in both reads gives `island` one meaning in both reports, so each reader of the key, the reviewer and the build loop among them, reads it alike through either renderer. DEC-045 chose its rule among seven on resvg's figures: it names no graze step that the threshold leaves unnamed and takes no island under a translucent part alone, and of the rules that pass both, it leaves the fewest steps of a box's edge at which a dot is gone and unnamed. Through a browser no threshold in whole pixels stands beside the measure. The lone strong pixel the probe found at a descender's foot touches no other strong pixel by a side or a corner, so joining by a corner, which kept such a pixel in its stroke through resvg (DEC-045, Rationale, "The values the outputs page holds"), does not keep it there, and a box that hides it hides an island whole, which each of the seven takes, as each takes a dot hidden whole. So the probe's grazes and dots cannot choose among them, and it found no reason to choose otherwise. A box's edge across a dot was not drawn through a browser (Context, "Not reached"). The dot plant holds in each start that the measure takes a mark (point 4).

**Why the sliver stays an area.** The measure took every dot the sliver left, as it must by how the probe finds a dot (Context), and DEC-045's threshold in whole pixels would have named fewer. A threshold would add a rounding rule to the browser's read, and to what DEC-037 point 5's plants hold, for no mark the measure leaves. At an em of exactly 10, the renderer's rounding left a dot of one strong pixel under the area, which whole pixels would cure. The measure took each such dot, and the háček plant loses two pixels there.

**Why the cut as well as the cover.** A dot's strong ink is the same whichever cause loses it, so the sliver leaves a dot cut past the page's edge unnamed wherever it leaves the same dot covered. One rule for both causes keeps a full stop past the edge from passing where the same full stop under a box is named. DEC-045's probes found through resvg that the cut takes the measure as the cover does. Through a browser the cut was not probed.

**Why `share` of counted ink where a span holds no strong ink.** DEC-045's reason holds through a browser: a share of 0 would list a faint mark hidden whole as the least loss in the list, though it lost all of its ink. So `share` means the same through both renderers. The probe found no such span among its five dots, since the dot of ż without strong ink shares its span with the z, so this rule meets a mark set alone in a face or at a size the probe did not draw.

**Why a dot plant.** Each start's first read is planted with one fault of each kind the check reads (DEC-020 point 7), so a start whose browser draws otherwise stops, and never passes the loss in silence. The háček plant's loss passes the sliver, and the measure takes an island of it too (Context). Named by an island alone, it would let every start pass with a sliver raised past a háček's loss, though the sliver alone judges a mark fused to its letter (point 1). So the first read holds it to its size (point 4): it holds the sliver's lower side, and not the measure. The edge control holds only that its own placement stays unnamed, and its box hides none of its counted ink wholly, so it holds nothing of the measure either (point 4). `island` on the dot plant's entry shows that the measure named it, whether or not the sliver did too. A full stop at the text floor is the smallest mark a start keeps. In Regular, the planted file's face, it held one strong pixel at an em of 10, and the probe found it apart from its letter at every draw in every face. It could not set the dot of ż apart from its z at two ems near the floor, and found it with no strong ink at others.

**The cost, not measured.** Through resvg, the measure's own work took under a twentieth of a second over the 208 calls of one look of the showcase deck (DEC-045). Through a browser it needs, for each pixel of a span's ink, its darkness and what the page drawn whole shows of it, where DEC-037's read takes counts alone. It needs them only for a span a cause hides some pixel of wholly, or puts some of past the page's edge. That was not measured, so #3262's change measures one look of the deck with it, and DEC-037's Revisit, a look past a minute, holds.

### Alternatives considered

- **A smaller area:** one small enough to name every dot that holds strong ink names grazes of one strong pixel that the sliver leaves, and none names a dot with no strong ink.
- **DEC-045's threshold in whole pixels through a browser too, beside the measure:** it names no dot the measure leaves, and adds a rounding rule to the read and to what the first read's plants hold.
- **The threshold alone, without the measure:** it would leave dots hidden whole unnamed, 149 dots of ż in Regular, 10 in Semibold and 5 in Source Code Pro, and 5 to 15 of each other dot in Regular.
- **A report that says plainly that the browser's read does not name a small dot,** which leaves it to the reviewer: the read left dots unnamed up to an em of about 21 for a full stop or a point, and 37.7 for the dot of ż, and DEC-037 point 1 accepts no such loss. The measure takes every one the probe found hidden whole.
- **A rule of taking an island of the browser's own:** `island` would mean two things in one report's key, and the probe gives nothing to choose another rule by. A rule that left the grazes the measure names would need to leave an island of one strong pixel at a line's foot and take a dot, and no such rule was drawn.
- **The measure for the cover alone:** a dot past the page's edge would pass where the same dot under a box is named.
- **No dot plant:** the measure would be the one rule of the read that no start holds, so a browser that read it otherwise would pass a lost mark in silence.
- **The háček plant named by an island alone:** a sliver raised past a háček's loss would stop no start, and the sliver alone judges a mark fused to its letter.
