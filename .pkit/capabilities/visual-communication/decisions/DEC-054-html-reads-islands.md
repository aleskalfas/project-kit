**In plain terms:** when the viewing step draws a page through a browser, its report names a full stop, a decimal point or the dot of ż that a later part hides, or that the page's edge cuts, however few its pixels. DEC-037's read names such a loss where it passes a *sliver*, a share of the text's em squared, and a small dot can hold less than that. In the deck's faces, a probe found that read leaving a full stop or a decimal point hidden whole unnamed at many sizes below an em of about 21 device pixels, and the dot of ż up to 38. So a cause is named too where it takes an *island* of the text's ink, a piece that stands apart from its letter, by the measure the SVG renderer's report already uses (DEC-045). A loss that takes no island is still judged by the sliver. The probe stepped one kind of box's edge across a text, a *graze*: a box's top edge stepped across the foot of a line's descenders, at ems of 10 to 20, and in Regular up to 40, in a light key, a dark one and both forced palettes. There the measure took an island the sliver left at 32 of the 75,750 steps it drew from an em of 10 to 20 in the light palettes, where the box hid a strong pixel that stood apart at a descender's foot, and at none in the dark palettes. Which steps meet such a pixel turns on where a text falls among the device's pixels, so a piece may meet one at another em, and no other edge was stepped across a text, nor a lighter weight drawn. Such a graze is named, and the layout mends it by moving the box clear, as it mends each graze the sliver names. One look of the deck in each key and forced palette gained no entry. The entry says when a cause took an island, as through resvg. The first read of each browser start plants a small full stop under a box, so a start whose read misses it stops. A box's edge across a dot, and a dot past the page's edge, were not drawn through a browser. For example, where a later box hides the decimal point of a small "3.5", the report lists that text as covered, and says that the cause took an island of its ink.

## Decision

**Through a browser, the viewing step names a cut or a cover that takes an island of a span's ink, as DEC-045 has it through resvg, beside each loss that passes DEC-037's sliver, and the entry says that its cause took an island.**

### 1. A cause that takes an island is named, whatever the sliver holds

- **Named:** a cut or a cover that loses at least the sliver of the span's strong ink, or that takes an island of the span's counted ink, however few its pixels. It takes one as DEC-045 point 1 has a cause take one: it hides some pixel of the island wholly and leaves none of it showing at the island's level. Past the page's edge, where a pixel is cut or shows, a cause takes an island only by cutting all of it.
- **The sliver stays an area.** DEC-037 point 1's area, in the span's em², which the outputs page states, judges a loss by its size. A loss under it that takes no island is a sliver, and names no entry.
- **Cut and covered, on counted ink.** Counted ink past the page's edge is cut, and counted ink on the page that the page drawn whole hides under a part drawn after it is covered. A loss's size is judged on strong ink, and its shape on islands of counted ink, as through resvg. So a faint dot with no strong ink can be named.
- **Each cause alone,** as DEC-037 point 1 has it, and an island is taken only by one cause.
- **A graze that takes an island is named too.** Where a box's edge across a line hides wholly a strong pixel that stands apart from the rest of its letter at its level, such as one at a descender's foot, the cause takes an island, and the entry names it with `island`, though its loss lies under the sliver. The probe found such grazes at one em in each of two faces, in the light palettes alone (Context). Each is mended where its cause lies, as DEC-037 point 1 has any covered span mended: by moving the box clear of the line.
- **The reach of what the probe found of edges.** Every edge the probe stepped across a text was a graze, a box's top edge across the foot of a line's descenders, in the deck's three faces. What this record says of how few grazes the measure names reaches that far, and no other edge was stepped across a text (Context, "Not reached").
- **Left unnamed:** a loss under the sliver that takes no island, such as part of a stroke, most grazes among them, or a dot of which a pixel still shows at its level.
- **Known gaps,** as DEC-045 point 1 gives them through resvg. A dot the face draws fused to its letter at every level is no island of its own, and a mark joined to its letter, such as an ogonek, is none either: the sliver alone judges each. A mark partly past the page's edge and partly under a part is left unnamed where neither cause takes an island of it or loses the sliver. A small dot that a box's edge fades without hiding any pixel of it wholly takes no island.
- **Not known:** whether the measure takes a dot at the draws where the probe found no empty row or column between it and its letter at any level: Regular's dot of ż at ems of 10.7 and 10.9 in the light palettes and at 15 to 20 draws in the dark ones, and the point inside "3.5" at 5 draws in the dark ones (Context). The measure joins pixels by a side or a corner, so such a dot is an island of its own wherever it meets its letter by neither, and a known gap only where the face fuses it.

### 2. Darkness, read in the browser's two colours

- **Darkness.** Through a browser, a pixel's darkness is how far the span's letters alone change it between the two colours in which DEC-037 point 3 measures strength, written as DEC-045 writes darkness: 0 where they change it by the whole difference between the two colours, and 255 where by none. Strong ink stays as DEC-037 point 3 counts it, where they change it by more than half, which this darkness writes as 127 or darker.
- **Hidden wholly, and what shows.** A pixel is hidden wholly where the page drawn whole does not change it at all as the span's letters change between the two colours (DEC-037 point 3). It shows at a level where the page drawn whole changes it by so much of the difference that, drawn black on white, it would be that dark or darker.
- **In every key and forced palette** the two colours are those DEC-037 point 3 takes there, so each image reads islands as it reads strength.
- **The levels and the joining** are those the outputs page states for the measure of islands, one set for both renderers (Rationale, "Why one set of levels and joining"), and they change there with no new record (DEC-045 point 1), a change weighing the figures of both renderers. The figures were found at the values the page holds: the levels 127, 159 and 191, pixels joined by a side or a corner.

### 3. Its entry says so

- **`island`** is true on a `cut` or `covered` entry whose cause takes an island of the span's counted ink, whatever its share, as DEC-045 point 2 gives it through resvg. Another entry holds no such key. So through either renderer a reader tells a dot lost from a graze, and the command's line says so.
- **`share`** stays the share of the span's strong ink that the cause loses (DEC-037 point 2). Where the span holds no strong ink, it is the share of its counted ink, as DEC-045 point 2 has it through resvg, so a faint full stop hidden whole reads 1.

### 4. The first read plants a dot, and names the háček plant by its size

- **The dot plant:** a full stop, a run of its own, under a later box. Each start's first read sets it at the command's text floor in device pixels (DEC-015 point 5), as it sets the háček plant, from its page's drawn width and its start's device scale. So it holds the same share of its em in every start.
- **Named with `island`.** Each start's first read names the dot plant as covered, with `island`. Where the read leaves it unnamed, or names it without `island`, the run stops, as it stops for a plant missed ([visual-communication:DEC-020-html-page-drawing] point 7).
- **The háček plant passes the sliver by its size.** The measure takes an island of the háček plant too (Context), so naming it does not show that its loss passes the sliver. Where the háček plant's loss lies under the sliver, the run stops, as where the read leaves the plant unnamed. So each start's first read holds that the sliver lies below the háček plant's loss, as DEC-037 point 5 has it, which a mark fused to its letter needs, since the sliver alone judges it (point 1).
- **The edge control is planted as DEC-037 point 5 plants it.** Its box hides none of its counted ink wholly, in any key or forced palette the probe drew, so the measure has nothing there to take (Context), and where the read names the control, by the sliver or by an island, the run stops (DEC-020 point 7). The control does not hold that a graze takes no island: nothing in a start holds that, and it rests on the probe (Context). What the control holds of the sliver is DEC-037 point 5's, and this record decides nothing of it. [project-kit#3279](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3279) takes up what the probe found of it (Context).

## Rationale

**Why a measure of shape, and no smaller area.** The sliver judges a loss by its size against the em. A dot hidden whole loses all of its strong ink, which in the deck's faces fell to 0.0038 of the em squared, and to none for a faint dot. An area small enough to name every dot that holds strong ink would name grazes the sliver leaves: one strong pixel passes 0.0038 of the em squared at any em up to 16 device pixels, and past an em of 10 the sliver leaves a graze of one strong pixel to the reviewer (DEC-037, Rationale). And no area of strong ink names a dot that holds none. Shape tells them apart, as DEC-045 found through resvg: a graze takes part of a stroke, which still shows at its level, and a box over a dot takes an island. Through a browser, on the grazes the probe drew, it tells them apart at all but a few steps, where a strong pixel at a descender's foot stands apart from its stroke (Context, and "The grazes it names" below). A dot lost unnamed runs two sentences together, or turns 3.5 into 35 (DEC-045, Context), and DEC-037 point 1 accepts no such loss.

**The grazes it names, a cost.** At an em of 11.7 in Regular and of 11.5 in Source Code Pro, in the light palettes, the browser drew a descender's foot with a strong pixel that touched no other strong pixel, so a box whose edge hid it whole took an island, though the loss lay under the sliver. Through resvg, DEC-045's threshold names a loss of one whole pixel below an em of about 14.1, so its read names such a graze by its size, and through either renderer the report names it alike. Like any covered span, it is mended by moving the box clear of the line (point 1). It errs toward naming, as DEC-037 point 3 accepts that a dark image's residue does. Of the 75,750 graze steps, a box's top edge across a descender's foot, that the probe drew in the light palettes from an em of 10 to 20, it found 32 such steps, and none among Regular's 50,000 there from 20.1 to 40. Every other island the measure took, the sliver named too, and one look of the deck gained no entry. The figures reach that far. No other edge was stepped across a text, such as a box's top edge at the baseline, its side across a round letter or its foot across a letter's top, nor a graze in a lighter weight or another family. And which steps meet such a pixel turns on where a text falls among the device's pixels, so a piece may meet one at an em the probe did not. So the record is revisited if it comes to name grazes in looks of pieces, or if a piece comes to set text in a lighter weight or a family the probes did not draw.

**Why DEC-045's way of taking an island, by name.** One rule in both reads gives `island` one meaning in both reports, so each reader of the key, the reviewer and the build loop among them, reads it alike through either renderer. DEC-045 chose its rule among seven on resvg's figures: it names no graze step that the threshold leaves unnamed and takes no island under a translucent part alone, and of the rules that pass both, it leaves the fewest steps of a box's edge at which a dot is gone and unnamed. Through a browser no threshold in whole pixels stands beside the measure. The lone strong pixel the probe found at a descender's foot touches no other strong pixel by a side or a corner, so joining by a corner, which kept such a pixel in its stroke through resvg (DEC-045, Rationale, "The values the outputs page holds"), does not keep it there, and a box that hides it hides an island whole, which each of the seven takes, as each takes a dot hidden whole. So the probe's grazes and dots cannot choose among them, and it found no reason to choose otherwise. A box's edge across a dot was not drawn through a browser (Context, "Not reached"). The dot plant holds in each start that the measure takes a mark (point 4).

**Why one set of levels and joining.** `island` is one key in both reports, read alike through either renderer (above). The levels and the joining say which pixels make an island, so one set keeps the key's meaning one: a pixel of a given darkness joins the same island through either renderer. The probe found DEC-045's values serve a browser: each dot it found hidden whole was an island at one of them, by how it finds a dot, and of the grazes it drew they named few that the sliver left (Context). Each renderer's own values under one rule were weighed, and are not taken: the rule would be one, but the key would say different things of the same pixels in the two reports, and the probe drew no other values through a browser to set a browser's by. So a change of them on the outputs page weighs the figures of both renderers (point 2).

**Why the sliver stays an area.** The measure took every dot the sliver left, as it must by how the probe finds a dot (Context), and DEC-045's threshold in whole pixels would have named fewer. A threshold would add a rounding rule to the browser's read, and to what DEC-037 point 5's plants hold, for no mark the measure leaves. At an em of exactly 10, the renderer's rounding left a dot of one strong pixel under the area, which whole pixels would cure. The measure took each such dot, and the háček plant loses two pixels there.

**Why the cut as well as the cover.** A dot's strong ink is the same whichever cause loses it, so the sliver leaves a dot cut past the page's edge unnamed wherever it leaves the same dot covered. One rule for both causes keeps a full stop past the edge from passing where the same full stop under a box is named. DEC-045's probes found through resvg that the cut takes the measure as the cover does. Through a browser the cut was not probed.

**Why `share` of counted ink where a span holds no strong ink.** DEC-045's reason holds through a browser: a share of 0 would list a faint mark hidden whole as the least loss in the list, though it lost all of its ink. So `share` means the same through both renderers. The probe found no such span among its five dots, since the dot of ż without strong ink shares its span with the z, so this rule meets a mark set alone in a face or at a size the probe did not draw.

**Why a dot plant.** Each start's first read is planted with one fault of each kind the check reads (DEC-020 point 7), so a start whose browser draws otherwise stops, and never passes the loss in silence. The háček plant's loss passes the sliver, and the measure takes an island of it too (Context). Named by an island alone, it would let every start pass with a sliver raised past a háček's loss, though the sliver alone judges a mark fused to its letter (point 1). So the first read holds it to its size (point 4): it holds that the sliver lies below the háček plant's loss, and nothing of the measure. Nor does the edge control, whose box hides none of its counted ink wholly (point 4). The dot plant is a run of its own, so its ink is the whole of its span's, and a box that hides it whole takes its island however a browser draws it: it is an island by construction. So it holds that the browser hides it and that the read takes the measure's path to `island`. It does not hold that a browser draws a dot apart from its letter, which rests on the probe, in Chrome on one Mac (Context). `island` on the dot plant's entry shows that the measure named it, whether or not the sliver did too. A full stop at the text floor is the smallest mark a start keeps. In Regular, the planted file's face, it held one strong pixel at an em of 10.

**The cost, not measured.** Through resvg, the measure's own work took under a twentieth of a second over the 208 calls of one look of the showcase deck (DEC-045). Through a browser it needs, for each pixel of a span's ink, its darkness and what the page drawn whole shows of it, where DEC-037's read takes counts alone. It needs them only for a span a cause hides some pixel of wholly, or puts some of past the page's edge. Its cost through a browser was not measured, so this record accepts none on figures. It stands under DEC-037's Revisit, a look of one of the pieces of the repository the capability is authored in, divided by its languages and keys, past a minute in that repository's CI, and #3262's change measures one look of the deck with it.

### Alternatives considered

- **A smaller area:** one small enough to name every dot that holds strong ink names grazes of one strong pixel that the sliver leaves, and none names a dot with no strong ink.
- **DEC-045's threshold in whole pixels through a browser too, beside the measure:** it names no dot the measure leaves, and adds a rounding rule to the read and to what the first read's plants hold.
- **The threshold alone, without the measure:** it would leave dots hidden whole unnamed, 149 dots of ż in Regular, 10 in Semibold and 5 in Source Code Pro, and 5 to 15 of each other dot in Regular.
- **A report that says plainly that the browser's read does not name a small dot,** which leaves it to the reviewer: the read left dots unnamed up to an em of about 21 for a full stop or a point, and 37.7 for the dot of ż, and DEC-037 point 1 accepts no such loss. The measure takes every one the probe found hidden whole.
- **A rule of taking an island of the browser's own:** `island` would mean two things in one report's key, and the probe gives nothing to choose another rule by. A rule that left the grazes the measure names would need to leave an island of one strong pixel at a line's foot and take a dot, and no such rule was drawn.
- **Islands nested across levels,** an island at a darker level taken only where it stands apart at each lighter level too: it would spare the lone strong pixel at a descender's foot, which joins its stroke at the lighter levels (Context), and with it the 32 graze steps. But it would leave unnamed each dot that meets its letter at a lighter level and stands apart only at a darker one, which DEC-045's levels are there to take (DEC-045 point 1, and its Rationale, "The values the outputs page holds"), and `island` would mean one thing through a browser and another through resvg.
- **Each renderer's own levels and joining, under one rule:** the key would say different things of the same pixels in the two reports, and the probe drew no other values through a browser to set a browser's by (Rationale, "Why one set of levels and joining").
- **The measure for the cover alone:** a dot past the page's edge would pass where the same dot under a box is named.
- **No dot plant:** the measure would be the one rule of the read that no start holds, so a browser that read it otherwise would pass a lost mark in silence.
- **The háček plant named by an island alone:** a sliver raised past a háček's loss would stop no start, and the sliver alone judges a mark fused to its letter.
- **The háček plant held both by its size and with `island`, and no dot plant:** a háček at the text floor may meet its letter at every level where another rasterizer draws it, as Semibold's met its letter at ems of 11.3 to 11.5 through resvg (DEC-045). A start would then stop for a known gap of the measure, not a fault of its own, and the plant would hold the measure only where a face keeps the háček apart. A full stop that is a run of its own is an island however a browser draws it.
- **The dot plant above the text floor,** so that a rasterizer that draws a small dot lighter still meets it: the measure takes a faint dot at its lighter levels (point 1), and a start whose browser draws the floor's full stop too faint even for those would leave such dots unnamed on its own pages too, which is what the plant is there to stop. Set higher, it would let that start pass.
- **The dot of ż as the dot plant:** it shares its span with its z, so it is an island only where the face draws it apart, and in Regular whether it stands apart at two ems near the text floor is not known (Context). A full stop that is a run of its own is an island by construction.
