**In plain terms:** a web page is read on a screen, and a reader sees only part of a tall one at once. This record gives a site's page, and the page of any other top whose layouts are its pages' forms and which asks for no height, a *screen* at each size it is drawn at: as wide as the page, and as tall as a share of that width fixed for the size, landscape at a desktop and a projector, upright at a tablet and a phone. The *first screen* is that screen at the page's top, where every reader starts. Where the page is taller than its screen, the reader scrolls, and the rule that a page has one focal point, `RS-GRAPHIC-001`, asks, beside what it asks of the page whole, that the first screen show at least one of the page's head, its focal point and the action it asks, an action counting by its strongest instance. Where one screen holds the page whole, the reader sees it at once, so a page of sections is judged whole there, as a poster is, and its sections may not compete for one eye. The rule that the form says what the content says, `RS-GRAPHIC-007`, weighs a page of sections the same way, and weighs no screen apart. The viewing step draws the first screen of each page taller than its screen, and the reviewer reads it.

## Context

[visual-communication:DEC-068-focal-point-in-the-content] point 3 has `RS-GRAPHIC-001` judge a page a reader scrolls through, such as a site's page, as drawn, never by what its first screen shows. It judges such a page of sections section by section, whatever its height. Both rest on one gap: the viewing step cuts a tall page into bands by the size of an image a harness reads whole, never by a screen, so the capability draws no unit of what a reader sees at once ([visual-communication:DEC-022-web-site-and-parts] point 7). DEC-068 names the two holes this leaves, each accepted knowingly until such a unit is drawn:
- **The first screen.** A page whose first screen shows none of its head, its focal point and its action passes, where they lie lower and stand above the rest of the page, or of its section.
- **A short page of sections.** A page of sections a reader scrolls through, short enough to be seen at once, is judged section by section, so two sections' focal points may compete for one eye unseen.

DEC-068's Alternatives leave two choices to revisit once the step draws a reader's screen at each size: each part judged on the first screen, and a page of sections told by its height against its screen. DEC-022 leaves open a page shorter than a screen, and where a screen's first view ends. The review of [project-kit#3396](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3396) found the holes, and [project-kit#3413](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3413) is the follow-up.

The words it uses:
- **The three** are a page's head, its focal point and the action it asks of its reader, as `RS-GRAPHIC-001` names them (DEC-068 point 1). On a page with no head they are two.
- **A size** is a mode of the size axis `device` that a piece is drawn at ([visual-communication:DEC-021-size-and-shape] point 1).
- **A band** is a strip of a page's height that the viewing step draws as one image, at the page's whole width (DEC-022, Context).
- **A top whose layouts are its pages' forms** is a top with a slot of pages, each of which takes one of its layouts, such as a deck or a site (DEC-022 point 1).
- **A page alone** is a top with no slot of pages, such as a figure, whose one page is the top's own (DEC-022, Context).
- **A page that has a screen** is a page of a top whose layouts are its pages' forms and which asks for no height, as point 1 says.
- **A page of sections** is a page whose content gives its sections as a list, met one after another (DEC-068 point 3).
- A page's **screen** and its **first screen** are what point 1 says.

## Decision

**A page of a top whose layouts are its pages' forms and which asks for no height has a screen at each size, as wide as the page and as tall as a share of that width the rule fixes for the size. Where the page is taller than its screen, a reader scrolls through it, and its first screen shows at least one of its head, its focal point and its action. Where its screen holds it whole, a reader sees it at once, and a page of sections is judged whole there. The viewing step draws the first screen of each page taller than its screen, and the reviewer reads it.**

### 1. A page's screen is as wide as the page, and as tall as a share of its width fixed for each size

- **Which pages have one.** A page of a top whose layouts are its pages' forms and which asks for no height, so that each page is as tall as its lowest part, as DEC-022 point 3 makes a site's. This record calls it a *page that has a screen*. The files show both: the top's slot of pages, and the height it asks for, or none. A page alone, such as a figure, and a page of a top that asks for a height, such as a deck's slide, have none, whichever renderer draws them: a reader sees them at once by their kind (DEC-068 point 3). A height a layout says for its page under a top that asks none (DEC-022 point 3) leaves the page its screen, since the class is told by what the top asks for.
- **Its width** is the page's, at the size drawn. The brand's width at a size is the one statement the files make of the window a page is shown in ([visual-communication:DEC-055-smallest-text-size], Rationale, "Why the brand's width stands for the width a page is shown in").
- **Its height** is a share of that width, one for each size: nine sixteenths at `desktop` and at `projector`, five quarters at `tablet`, and sixteen ninths at `phone`. A page drawn at no size, or at a size the rule gives no share, takes `desktop`'s, as a component draws the default's shape at a mode it does not name (DEC-021 point 3).
- **Its first screen** is its screen at the page's top.
- **Scrolled, or seen at once.** At a size where a page that has a screen is taller than it, a reader scrolls through the page. At a size where its screen holds it whole, a reader sees it at once. The line is the screen's height, with no margin.
- **The shares are the rule's words.** `RS-GRAPHIC-001` gives them, so that what the rule judges stays within its set and its pin, as the page's frame does in `RS-GRAPHIC-003` (DEC-068 point 4). The viewing step holds the same shares, and a change to one changes the other in the same change.

### 2. The first screen shows at least one of the three

- **The test.** At a size where a reader scrolls through a page that has a screen, its first screen shows at least one of the page's three, whole or so far that a reader takes it for what it is.
- **The action by its strongest instance.** An action drawn more than once counts on the first screen only by the instance it ranks by, its strongest (DEC-068 point 1). A quieter instance there, such as a link in the header the site gives every page, shows no action where the strongest lies lower.
- **On a page of sections,** the page's three are its head and its action, where it has them (DEC-068 point 3), and the focal point of the section that holds its head, which bears out the point that head states. Where its content places its head above its sections, in none of them, no section's focal point counts, so its first screen shows its head or its action.
- **A page of sections with no head of its own,** none of whose headings names it, has no opening of its own. There the three of any section its first screen shows, whole or in part, count, beside the page's action where it has one.
- **What it passes and fails.** A page whose first screen shows its head passes, and so does one whose hero's picture fills that screen with the page's head just below it, and one whose first screen shows only the strongest instance of its call to act. A page whose first screen shows a strip of logos and a band of news above its hero fails, though each is a section whose heading that screen shows, since the page has a head of its own. So does one whose first screen shows only a quiet sign-up link in the header the site gives every page, with its loud call to act lower down, and one whose first screen shows only that header, which is frame, and a picture that carries none of its content.
- **Beside the page whole.** `RS-GRAPHIC-001` still judges the page whole, or each of its sections, as point 3 decides. The first screen adds a test of its own: that a reader meets one of the three where every reader starts.
- **Which of the three is the design's,** as the order among them is (DEC-068 point 1). A page may open on its head, on the picture that bears its point out, or on its call to act.
- **The page's frame,** as `RS-GRAPHIC-003` defines it, counts as it does on the page whole: a part of it shows one of the three only where it holds one, and the action only where it holds its strongest instance.
- **The first screen alone.** No screen below it is judged.
- **`RS-GRAPHIC-007` weighs no screen apart.** It weighs the strongest difference across the page, or within each section where `RS-GRAPHIC-001` judges the page by its sections (point 3). Whether a reader meets one of the three on the first screen is `RS-GRAPHIC-001`'s alone.

### 3. A page its screen holds whole is seen at once, and judged whole

- **Judged whole.** At a size where its screen holds a page that has a screen whole, `RS-GRAPHIC-001` judges it whole there, sections and all, with one focal point, as it judges a page seen at once by its kind (DEC-068 point 3). At a size where the page is taller than its screen, it judges a page of sections section by section, as DEC-068 point 3 decides.
- **Each size apart.** So one page may be judged whole at `desktop` and by its sections at `phone`.
- **Its sections are no group by being sections.** Judged whole, a page's sections are one focal point only where DEC-068 point 2 makes them a group the content gives as one: equal parts that show the page's point together, or a list whose point is its order. That its content gives them as a list does not by itself make them one, or every page of sections its screen holds whole would pass.
- **`RS-GRAPHIC-007` reads it alike.** It weighs the strongest difference across the page where `RS-GRAPHIC-001` judges it whole, and within each section where that rule judges it by its sections, so the two rules never read one page's sections apart (DEC-068 point 5).

### 4. The viewing step draws the first screen of each page taller than its screen, and the reviewer reads it

- **Which pages.** Each page that has a screen, at each key drawn where it is taller than its screen (point 1), through either renderer.
- **What it draws.** An image of its first screen: the strip from the page's top, as wide as the page and as tall as its screen. It is drawn at the scale of the page's own images, so its smallest text reads as theirs does, and under no forced palette. Beside it lie the frames of each element it shows, in part or whole, as beside a band (DEC-022 point 7), and it takes its page's findings, as a band does ([visual-communication:DEC-062-checks-beside-the-frames] point 1).
- **A first screen too tall for one image.** Where the first screen at that scale passes the long edge the step allows for one image, the step cuts it into bands as it cuts a page's (DEC-022 point 7), and the report gives them in order as that first screen. Drawn smaller instead, its smallest text might not read, which every image of the step must allow ([visual-communication:DEC-015-viewing-step-and-reviewer] point 5).
- **Its foot is no edge of the page.** A part the first screen cuts there shows whole in the page's bands, and is no fault.
- **A page its screen holds whole** takes no image apart: the page's own image shows its first screen.
- **The report** gives each page that has a screen, at each key, its screen's height, in the page's px, and says whether its screen holds it whole.
- **The reviewer** reads each first screen beside the page's bands, and judges point 2's test on it. It reads from the report whether a page's screen holds it whole, and judges a page of sections whole or by its sections by that, as point 3 says. Its report names each first screen it read, and each it did not.

## Rationale

**Why judge the first screen.** Readers spend most of their viewing time on a page's first screen, and less on each screen below it, as the Nielsen Norman Group's eye-tracking studies of scrolling found (Jakob Nielsen, "Scrolling and Attention (Original Research Study)", 2010; Therese Fessenden, "Scrolling and Attention", 2018). What that screen shows tells a reader whether to scroll on (Amy Schade, "The Fold Manifesto: Why the Page Fold Still Matters", 2015). A page whose first screen shows none of the three gives its reader nothing of its point where most reading happens, and no reason to look for it lower.

**Why one of the three, and any one.** DEC-068 point 1 leaves their order to the design: a reader may enter through a dominant picture, and a page may draw its call to act loudest. So the first screen asks for one of them, and leaves which to the design. Asked for all three, a phone's page whose head and picture fill its first screen would fail for an action just below it.

**Why the action counts by its strongest instance.** The rule ranks an action drawn more than once by its strongest instance (DEC-068 point 1), which is the one the page asks its reader most to take. Counted by any instance, a quiet link the site's header gives every page would meet the test on every page of the site, wherever the page draws its call to act, and the test would fail no page that opens on what its point does not need.

**Why a section's three count only on a page with no head of its own.** Nearly any first screen shows some section's heading, so a test met by any section's would pass a page that opens on a strip its point does not need. A page with a head of its own has an opening of its own. Where a section holds that head, that section opens the page, and its focal point bears out what the head states, so a page may open on that picture, as point 2 lets any page. Where its content places its head above every section, that head opens the page by the content's own order. A page with no head of its own has only its sections, met one after another, and its action, which its design may place anywhere. Asked for its action alone, it would fail for a call to act at its foot, where the same page without one passes on any section's three, so adding a call to act would make it fail. Its sections' three count whether or not it has an action.

**Why a page its screen holds whole is judged whole.** Its reader takes in its sections together, so they compete for one eye, as on any page seen at once (DEC-068 point 3). DEC-068 rejected judging a site's page of sections whole, since one whose sections each rank their own parts well would fail where none bears the page's point out above the others. That holds for a reader who meets the sections one after another, and not for one who sees them at once, where a page with no focal point among them shows no point first, as a poster of equal sections does. DEC-068 told a page of sections by its kind alone only because no unit of a screen was drawn.

**Why the first screen alone.** Every reader starts at the page's top, so its first screen is the same for each. A later screen falls where a reader stops scrolling, so no two runs would cut it alike.

**Why a page alone and a page of a top that asks for a height have no screen.** They are kinds made to be seen at once, which DEC-068 point 3 judges whole. Given a screen, a tall figure would be judged by two units at once, whole and by its first screen. The class is told by what the files show, the top's slot of pages and the height it asks for, so the step tells it alike on every run, whichever renderer draws the page. A height a site's layout says for its page does not take the page out: the site still asks no height of it, and a reader still scrolls it where it is taller than the screen.

**Why the page's width, and a share of it.** The record takes a page's window as wide as the page at every size, as DEC-055 takes the brand's width for the window's width, since no file states a window's height and the brand's width is the files' one statement of a window. The shape of the window then gives its height, so each size has one share.
- **At `desktop`,** nine sixteenths is the screen the 2018 study measured, whole: Fessenden counted a screenful as 1,080 px on screens 1,920 px wide, nine sixteenths of the width. The point 2 test rests on that study's finding, so it takes the screen that study measured.
- **At `projector`,** nine sixteenths is a judgement: a room's screen is landscape, and shows a page whole across it, as it shows a slide.
- **At `phone`,** sixteen ninths is a judgement: a phone held upright, less its browser's bars and its system's.
- **At `tablet`,** five quarters is a judgement, of a tablet held upright, less its bars. Upright, because a tablet held sideways leaves a window near a small laptop's, which `desktop`'s share already judges, while the window a tablet leaves upright is one no other size has.
- **One number for each size,** so two runs judge alike, which DEC-068's Alternatives found missing.

**Why the shares are the rule's words.** A share decides what the rule passes. Held by the viewing step alone, it would move with the capability's code, outside the set's pin, as DEC-068 rejects for the page's frame ("The frame defined in this record").

**Why the cut-off at the screen's height, and not the sections the first screen holds.** One line, the page's height against its screen, sorts every page, and each rule then reads the page by a unit DEC-068 already defines: the page whole, or each section. Judging together the sections a first screen holds whole would make a new unit, a group of sections with no head and no rest a record defines, and would move the cliff to the section the screen's foot cuts rather than remove it.

**Why `RS-GRAPHIC-007` weighs no screen apart.** It already weighs where the strongest difference goes across the page, or within each section. The first screen asks only whether a reader meets one of the three there, which is a question of rank, `RS-GRAPHIC-001`'s.

**Why an image of the first screen.** The reviewer judges what a reader sees at once, as the squint test asks of a page ([visual-communication:DEC-028-principles-of-visual-communication] point 8). A line across the bands would have it piece that screen together from strips cut by another measure.

### Alternatives considered

- **All three on the first screen.** Rejected, for the reason above.
- **The head on the first screen.** Rejected: a page that opens on the picture that bears its point out, with its head below, is drawn so on purpose, and DEC-068 point 1 leaves the order to the design.
- **The action by any of its instances on the first screen.** Rejected, for the reason above.
- **The three of any section the first screen shows, on every page of sections.** Rejected: a page that opens on a strip of logos would pass, though its first screen shows nothing of its point.
- **The page's action alone on a page of sections with no head of its own,** where it has one. Rejected, for the reason above.
- **The first section counted as holding a head its content places above every section.** Not taken: that head opens the page by the content's own order, and a picture drawn above it opens the page on a part the content gives after it. Its cost is named below.
- **The first screen judged whole, as a page seen at once.** Rejected. A reader takes in the first screen at once, as one takes in a page its screen holds, and what parts the two is only that this reader goes on to scroll, and meets the sections below in turn: the cut-off at the screen's height, whose cliff What it costs concedes. Judged whole, a page of sections whose first screen shows its hero and, at its foot, the next section's head drawn as large would fail, though its head need not stand above that section's there (DEC-068 point 3), and its verdict would turn on where the screen's foot falls, which the share sets and the design does not.
- **The sections the first screen holds whole judged together,** and the rest section by section, in place of the cut-off. Not taken, for the reason above.
- **Every screen judged, cut down the page at the screen's height.** Rejected: a reader's later screens fall where they stop scrolling, so the cuts would be the step's, not a reader's.
- **The screen's height as a brand's role.** Rejected. A shared role the foundations add after their first version takes a default that aliases an older role ([visual-communication:DEC-004-brands-and-notations] point 7), and no older role gives a height near a screen's at each size. And a height that only this test reads would answer to nothing else a brand draws, where the width the share follows is the one every part of the page is laid out at, and DEC-055's check measures against.
- **`desktop`'s screen a fixed height in px, and the other sizes a share,** as DEC-055 splits a medium's smallest text. Not taken: it would take a desktop's window as wider than the page and every other size's as wide as it, and a fixed height needs the height of a window less its bars, which neither the files nor the studies give.
- **The screen declared by the top, as its smallest text is (DEC-055).** Rejected. A screen's shape follows the device a size names, not the kind of page, and a project's own top could declare one that holds every page.
- **The screen named by the piece.** Rejected: a size would have two owners (DEC-021 point 1).
- **No screen at a size the piece's `media` gives to paper alone** (DEC-055 point 1). Not taken: a piece need not give `media` (DEC-055, Alternatives, "`media` required of every piece"), so the class would turn on a field a piece may leave out, where the top's slot of pages and the height it asks for are always there. Its cost is named below.
- **The shares left to the viewing step.** Rejected, for the reason above.
- **A margin at the screen's height,** so that a page a little taller is still seen at once. Rejected: its reader does scroll, and the margin would be a second number with no source.

### What it costs

- **A share is not a reader's window, and it errs both tests one way at once.** A share taller than a reader's window passes a first screen that reader does not see whole, so point 2 leans lenient, and judges whole a page that reader scrolls, so point 3 leans strict. A shorter share does the reverse.
  - At `desktop`, for a page as wide as the measured screen, the share is that screen whole, bars and all, taller than the window a browser leaves on it, so it leans lenient on point 2 and strict on point 3. A page narrower than a reader's window keeps its own size there ([visual-communication:DEC-020-html-page-drawing] point 4), so the window's height does not shrink with the page's width as the share does, and for such a page the share may lean the other way.
  - At `tablet`, the share is upright's, taller than a sideways tablet's window: lenient on point 2 and strict on point 3 for a reader who holds it sideways.
  - At `phone` and `projector`, the shares are judgements near the window, and may err either way by a little.
  - A brand that draws its pages wider than its devices' windows gets a taller screen, and so leans lenient on point 2, as DEC-055 concedes the same width for the smallest text.
- **The cut-off has a cliff.** A page a little taller than its screen is judged by its sections, though its reader sees all but its foot at once. And on a page taller than its screen, the sections its first screen shows together are each judged alone, so their focal points may compete for one eye unseen. Both lean lenient on point 3.
- **A head above every section.** On a page of sections whose content places its head above its sections, in none of them, no section's focal point counts on the first screen. So a design that opens on a picture, with that head below it, fails, where the same drawing passes with the head inside the picture's section.
- **A printed site has a screen too.** A piece may say that it is printed, in its `shown` field and its `media` (DEC-055 point 1; [visual-communication:DEC-026-html-page-print]), and its pages keep their screen, at a height no sheet has. Its first screen is judged as a screen's, and a page of sections its screen holds whole is judged whole, wherever a sheet ends.
- **One more image** of each page taller than its screen, at each key drawn, for the reviewer to read.
- **A new major of `GRAPHIC`,** which each project that inherits it reads, and moves its pin to (Implications).
