**In plain terms:** the capability's review grades each rule in words: below competent, competent, professional or excellent, measured against professional work. A rule on one page takes a grade on each page, and a rule on the pages together takes one for the whole piece. A project sets its *bar* in the capability's configuration, such as "professional on every rule of `COMM` and `GRAPHIC`", and a command reads from the review's grades a verdict against it. The review marks each grade that only a missing tool or the asker's own words could raise, and the verdict leaves those out. The designer's loop runs until the verdict meets the bar, goes on into polish within a bound, and hands the person the last round that met the bar, with what still blocks it. The designer changes alone every word that is not the asker's. A failing finding still names only where its fix belongs. The reviewer stays advisory, and the verdict ends the designer's loop and holds nothing else. Where the canon is thin, a refinement of the record of the principles decides where each gap belongs. The verdict starts to end the loop only once whoever maintains the capability accepts the report of a second calibration, on another ladder, whose conditions were fixed before it ran. From then on it does so in any project whose bar and reviewer stay within what was calibrated.

## Context

The record of the design agents has the designer mend what it judges right, and stop when the reviewer's reading of every page reports nothing it would mend ([visual-communication:DEC-030-design-agents] point 2). The reviewer's findings inform, and hold nothing (DEC-030 point 6). No finding says how far a page is from good work, and no project says how good is enough. So the designer stops once it no longer agrees with what is left, however far that is from professional work.

The Feature [project-kit#3148](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3148) asks the designer to work under its own review until every page reaches a bar the project sets, and then hand the person only what still blocks it. Its Task, [project-kit#3151](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3151), ran two experiments before this record was drafted. That Task's pull request gives their figures.

**A calibration on a ladder.** The reviewer graded a ladder of earlier designs of one deck, from a first draft to designs a senior critic had judged professional and then polished. It also graded the deck as the default branch of the repository the capability is authored in drew it. Each page's lead stayed the same in every design of the ladder. The other words changed a lot between the early designs, mostly by cuts, less in the middle of the ladder, and almost not at all among its last designs. The reviewer graded some designs several times and the others once, in two arms: the canon's rules alone, and the canon's rules beside the senior critic's own measures. What it showed:
- **No design passed,** in either arm and in any run, the critic's professional designs included. So the conditions the Task set before the review may hold the loop were not all met (point 9).
- **The canon alone ordered the ladder closely against the scores the critic's measures gave it,** and less closely against the order of the designs and the senior critic's own judgement. Its last design scored among its first, mostly through a rule on words that graded a real change of words down (below). On the `GRAPHIC` rules alone, the two stood apart, and the canon ordered the ladder much as the critic had.
- **On pages that were the same image in two designs, the page verdicts of the canon alone split from run to run.** With the measures beside it, the canon's grades on those pages varied about as much. Its verdicts there agreed only because the critic's bar read them, and those pages sat further from that bar.
- **The rules on words failed pages across the whole ladder alike.** The leads held, so the rule on the order of the leads had nothing to tell apart. The other words changed most among the early designs, yet the rule on words that fit the audience graded those designs alike. Either the cuts left what it judges as it was, or it graded them badly, and the calibration cannot tell which. Where a later design dropped a source an earlier one named, the rule on sources graded the change down.
- **Four things the critic's measures judge had no counterpart among the rules, or only a weak one:** craft, typography, legibility at the medium's size, and colour. Part of the gap may lie in the reading rather than the rules. The review graded the rule on legibility by how much a page holds, not by whether its text reads at its size. With the critic's measures beside it, it graded the rule on nothing extra below professional on pages where, by the canon alone, it never did. One fault the critic blocked on judges design, and no rule names it.
- **Each review's verdict followed from its own grades,** read by the bar's text, in every run.

**Convergence on one page.** A page outside that deck was designed twice from the same starting page, by the same designer and reviewer agents. In both, the designer could change every word but the page's lead. The two loops differ only in how a failing finding states its fix:
- **Findings that name only where the fix belongs:** the first review failed the page, and the review after one round of mending passed it, with no rule graded excellent.
- **Findings that prescribe the fix:** the page passed after three rounds of mending. In the first review, two prescriptions changed what the designer could not: the page's lead, and a part that a relation of the brief names. The designer took neither. In a later round the review judged, from the browser's drawing, that the wording it prescribed would fit on one line. The engine's own measure broke it onto two, the render failed, and the designer took the review's fallback.
- **Neither loop waited on the person.** The one question the designer raised went unanswered, and the next review's grade settled it.
- **The reasons were in view.** In both loops, the review read the brief in one read, its reasons included, and could only say that it weighed them after grading.

Each loop ran once, so the second experiment shows a direction, not a measure.

## Decision

**The review grades each rule in words, against professional work: on each page, or once for the piece where the rule judges the pages together. A project sets its bar in the capability's configuration, and a command reads from the review's grades a verdict against it. The review is handed its earlier verdicts and the designer's reasons only once its grades are written, marks each grade only a missing tool or the asker's words could raise, and names where a fix belongs without prescribing it. The designer's loop runs to the bar, on into polish within a bound, and then hands the person the last round that met the bar and what blocks it. The designer changes alone every word that is not the asker's. The reviewer stays advisory. The verdict ends the designer's loop and nothing else, once whoever maintains the capability accepts a calibration's report as point 9 says, in any project whose bar and reviewer stay within what was calibrated. Where the canon is thin, a refinement of [visual-communication:DEC-028-principles-of-visual-communication] decides where each gap belongs.**

### 1. The review grades each rule in words

- **A grade for each rule.** The review grades each rule it reads from the rule sets (DEC-030 point 6) that judges the piece (DEC-028 point 3): the rules of `COMM` and `GRAPHIC`, and those of a set of the project's own that inherits one. A *grade* is one of four words, from the lowest: *below competent*, *competent*, *professional* and *excellent*. It judges the rule on every image it reads, so a break in one language, mode or size counts. Each grade gives its evidence in a line.
- **On each page, or once for the piece.** A rule that judges a page takes a grade on each page. A rule whose text judges the pages together takes one grade for the whole piece. These are the rule that the pages tell the story in order (`RS-COMM-003`), and the rule that one meaning looks the same everywhere (`RS-GRAPHIC-004`). A rule that judges both takes both: the rule that the big picture comes first (`RS-COMM-002`) takes a grade for the piece on what the pages say in their order, and one on each page on whether its picture shows the whole before its parts. Where a rule's text leaves a page or a piece out, it takes no grade there, and the review says so. So a page whose layout has no lead takes none on `RS-COMM-001`, and a piece of one page none on `RS-COMM-003`.
- **Against professional work.** A grade measures what a reader sees against the work a professional designer hands over for that audience and medium. It never weighs effort, what the engine can draw today, or how far a page has come since an earlier round.
- **A rule finding carries its grade.** Each grade below professional, or below what the project's bar asks of its rule, is a rule finding as DEC-030 point 6 defines one, and carries the grade.
- **The verdict.** Where a project sets a bar (point 3), the *verdict* says whether each page meets the bar, and whether the piece does. A page meets it where each of its grades on the rules the bar covers does. The piece meets it where every page does, and each grade it takes for the whole piece does too. The verdict counts only the grades the review did not mark (point 4).
- **Given by a command.** The verdict needs no judgement, so a command of the capability gives it, as what needs no judgement is a check's (DEC-028 point 1). The review writes its grades and its marks in a report of a fixed form, and the command reads them with the bar from the configuration. So the designer and a person can give the verdict again from the same report. The reviewer runs no command (DEC-030 point 7), so whoever reads its report runs this one. The command gives no verdict where the check fails the bar (point 3), and says why.
- **Settled, or for a person.** A grade is settled only where a rule's text settles it, as a rule on a blocking fault settles *below competent* for a page that shows the fault (point 2). Every other grade is a reading. Once its rule binds, a rule finding on such a grade is for a person (DEC-030 point 6), so it never fails a merge alone, should a record give the review a place at the merge (point 8).
- **What stands.** A fault never judges how well a page is designed, and a note never fails (DEC-030 point 6).

### 2. Where the canon is thin, a refinement of the record of the principles decides where each gap belongs

- **The gaps.** The calibration found four measures that the rules name weakly or not at all: *craft*, how cleanly each part is finished and joined to the next; *typography*, how the type is set; *legibility at the medium's size*, whether the text reads at the size the medium shows it, apart from whether the page is taken in at a glance; and *colour*, whether the colours work as one scheme, in each mode the piece is shown in.
- **Where each belongs.** A refinement of [visual-communication:DEC-028-principles-of-visual-communication], which a person accepts in a change of its own, decides for each gap where it belongs (DEC-028 point 1):
  - **a rule from the canon,** with its source, where no rule names it;
  - **a check,** where it needs no judgement, as a text smaller than its medium allows needs none;
  - **the brand's values,** where it is the look, such as a scale of type sizes or a set of colours;
  - **the reading,** in the design knowledge (DEC-030 point 8) or in the reviewer's body, where a rule already names it and the review read the rule otherwise, as it read `RS-GRAPHIC-005` for how much a page holds and not for whether its text reads. A change to the reading moves the grades as a new rule does, so it calls for a new calibration's report (point 9).

  The calibration cannot tell a gap in the canon from a gap in the reading, so the refinement weighs each.
- **No borrowed measure becomes a rule.** The measures the calibration set beside the canon were one critic's, written for one deck. They show where the canon is thin, and are never a rule's source (DEC-028 point 1).
- **A blocking fault that judges design is a rule's,** graded at the bottom: a page that shows it takes *below competent* on that rule. The rule is a new one, or one that already names the fault, as the refinement decides. The fault never joins the reviewer's list of faults, which never judge design (DEC-030 point 6).
- **What stays the checks'.** Contrast, a text that overflows its box and a text smaller than its medium allows stay the checks' (DEC-028 point 1). A rule judges only what no check measures.

### 3. A project sets its bar in the capability's configuration

- **What a bar is.** A project's *bar* names what it covers among the rules the review grades (point 1), whole rule sets or single rules, each with its set's major version, and the grade each page, and the piece, must reach on them: professional or excellent. It is data, in a shape such as `bar: {covers: [COMM 1, GRAPHIC 1], at-least: professional}`, whose spelling the configuration's schema fixes. A bar asks no grade below professional: a grade below professional is a rule finding (point 1), and a bar met with one would end the loop on a finding the designer could still mend.
- **The loop's stopping parameter.** The bar says when the designer's loop may end (point 6), and requires nothing of a piece. A piece below it fails no validation and no merge. So it is no rule, and binds nothing. It is a setting of the capability's configuration, which holds the project's choices as a whole ([visual-communication:DEC-002-files-and-validation] point 2), beside the loop's bound.
- **Checked as the configuration is.** The configuration's schema refuses a bar of another shape, or one that asks below professional. The capability's check fails a bar that covers a set or a rule the review does not grade. It also fails a bar whose major for a set differs from the major the project holds, as a pin behind its set fails until the project reviews the new major (COR-051 point 7). The project corrects either in its configuration (DEC-002 point 7). Until it does, the command gives no verdict, and says why.
- **Read from the configuration, never from a dispatcher.** The review reads the bar from the configuration, to report each grade below it (point 1), and grades no bar. The command reads it there too. Nobody who dispatches the review or the designer hands either a bar.
- **One bar.** A project keeps one bar, read from that one key. A second, such as one for talks and another for sketches, would need each piece to name the bar that holds it. The evident way is a field of the piece's own file or of its brief, as a piece's own field already decides which pieces `COMM` judges (DEC-028 point 3). Such a field is a format other work builds on, so it waits for a record of its own, once a project asks for a second bar.
- **Whose to change.** A change to a project's bar is a change to its configuration, reviewed in its pull request, as every file the project reviews is ([visual-communication:DEC-020-html-page-drawing] point 7). Who authorises it is that project's to say.
- **No bar, no hold.** A project that sets no bar holds its designer to none. The review still grades each rule, and no verdict is given. The designer's loop then ends as DEC-030 point 2 says.
- **No shipped bar.** The capability's configuration ships no bar. A default bar for adopters waits for the evidence of the capability's trials ([project-kit#3150](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3150)).

### 4. What the review sees

- **What it reads.** Every image the viewing step drew of the piece, with the frames and the report; the brief; the rule sets; the bar, from the capability's configuration; and the design knowledge (DEC-030 point 8). It checks the piece's facts against their sources, as DEC-030 point 7 says.
- **Two things only once its grades are written.** Whoever hands the review its work hands it the brief's reasons, and the reports of the loop's earlier rounds as the review wrote them, only once the review has written its grades for the round. Nobody summarises, filters or answers those reports first, the designer included. So it judges each page against the brief's relations, goals and takeaways first, as DEC-030 point 6 has it, and grades before an earlier grade can anchor it. It reads no account of the designer's of what it changed.
- **What that guarantees, and what it does not.** It guarantees the order in which the review is handed both, and the reviewer's body tells it to open neither before its grades are written. It does not put them out of the review's reach: the reviewer may read the whole repository, and the brief holds its reasons beside its goals ([visual-communication:DEC-029-piece-brief] point 1). So the review's report says what it read before its grades were written, and a person can see a breach. A way to put them out of reach, such as a brief split in two files or a reviewer that fetches them through a command, would change DEC-029 point 1 or DEC-030 point 7, and waits for a record of its own.
- **Each moved grade explained.** Once it has read its earlier verdicts, the review says why each grade differs from the one it gave in the round before, higher or lower.
- **A need.** Where the only fix that would raise a grade is a tool the engine lacks, the review marks the grade as a *need*, and names the missing tool. The need leaves the loop as a question to the person, as DEC-030 point 4's stop does.
- **The asker's.** Where the only fix that would raise a grade lies in the asker's words (point 7), the review marks the grade as the asker's, and names those words. It leaves the loop as a question to the person.
- **Marked by the review alone, grade by grade.** A grade is marked only where no fix the designer may make would raise it. A grade that a fix of the designer's would raise stays unmarked, and the review names its other cause apart. The verdict leaves the marked grades out, so the loop spends no round on them. The designer marks nothing.

### 5. A failing finding names where its fix belongs, and prescribes no fix

A failing finding names what fails, where, what it rests on and where its fix belongs, and never what the fix is, as DEC-030 point 7 decides. Only the designer renders, so only the designer learns whether a fix fits. The engine's own measure of a text decides its line breaks, and a review that judges from a drawing can be wrong about them. A prescription also meets limits the designer weighs and the review does not, such as the asker's words or a relation of the brief. The convergence experiment shows both (Context). Its counts of rounds point the same way, but each loop ran once, which is no measure.

### 6. The loop runs to the bar, into polish, within a bound

This point holds where a project sets a bar, and the verdict ends the loop (points 8 and 9).
- **A round** is one mend of the piece by the designer, then a render, a look and a review of every page (DEC-030 point 2). The designer's *loop* is the rounds from the first review on.
- **To the bar.** While the verdict fails the bar, each round mends each failing finding the designer may mend (point 7), as DEC-030 point 2 has it mend.
- **Into polish.** Once the verdict meets the bar, the loop goes on into *polish*: rounds that mend what the review graded below excellent, and the notes the designer agrees with. Where a polish round's verdict fails the bar, the next round mends that first. Polish ends when the review grades nothing below excellent and reports no note the designer would act on, or at the bound. The bound is its real stop: in the calibration, the canon alone graded one rule professional on every page, and the page that passed in the convergence experiment took excellent on no rule.
- **Within a bound.** The loop runs no more than a bound of rounds to the bar, and a bound of rounds of polish after it. A count of rounds is no requirement a piece meets, so the bound is no rule, and no part of the bar. It is a setting of the capability's configuration, beside the bar, in a shape such as `rounds: {to-bar: 5, polish: 2}`. The capability's default is five rounds to the bar and two of polish, and a project may change it. Whoever dispatches the designer may lower either bound for one run, and never raise it.
- **A grade two rounds did not raise.** A grade on the same rule, on the same page or for the piece, that still fails the bar two rounds after the round that first graded it so, still counts against the bar. The loop goes on with the rest, and the grade goes to the person when the loop ends. Where every grade that still fails the bar is one two rounds did not raise, the loop ends there. The review says why each grade moved (point 4), so a grade that rose by the review's own variation shows as such.
- **Then the person, with the last round that met the bar.** When the loop ends, the designer hands the person the piece as it stood at the last round whose verdict met the bar, or at its last round where none did. With it go each grade that still fails the bar, each grade two rounds did not raise, each marked grade, and each question it could not settle. It hands over that round's review as the review wrote it. It says what it changed (DEC-030 point 2), and what the rounds after that round tried.
- **What a check measures stays the check's.** The designer mends what a check fails before any round's review, as it renders and checks (DEC-030 point 2), and no round counts a check's failure.

### 7. What the designer changes alone, and what it asks

Whose words are whose holds wherever the designer mends a piece, whether or not a verdict ends its loop. It holds too at every level of the setting by which the core will decide how much agents decide without asking ([project-kit#2801](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2801)). That setting replaces only how much the designer changes alone and when it asks, the last two bullets below, which are the capability's default until then.
- **The asker's words.** A piece's intent belongs to whoever asked for it, the *asker* ([visual-communication:DEC-029-piece-brief] point 4; DEC-030 point 2). The *asker's words* carry that intent: the brief's goals, relations and takeaways, each page's lead and title, and any other word of the content a goal names as the asker's, such as a quote. The designer changes none of them without the asker's word.
- **By where they came from.** Words are the asker's once the asker has seen them: those the asker wrote, and those the designer drafted and showed, as DEC-030 point 2 has it show the goals and relations before it designs against them. So a title the asker dictated stays the asker's. A lead or a title the designer drafted, on a page it added or on any other, stays its own to change until the asker has seen it.
- **Where that is recorded.** A page's part of the brief records each lead and title of the page that the designer drafted and the asker has not seen. The designer records one when it drafts it, and clears the record once it has shown it. Where the brief records nothing, the page's lead and title are the asker's, a piece with no brief included. So a later session knows which it may change, and a record lost or never written leads it to ask, never to change.
- **A translation follows its source.** The translation of the asker's words is the asker's too. The designer mends it alone only to make it say what its source says.
- **Every other word is the designer's.** It may reword, cut or move any other text, in every language alike, where each goal and relation of the brief still holds. It may correct a fact to what its source says, and cut a fact with no source unless a goal or a relation rests on it. A fact it adds names its source (`RS-COMM-005`). It tells the person afterwards what it changed and why, as DEC-030 point 2 has it tell.
- **What it asks.** It asks the person only where a fix would change the asker's words or cut a fact a goal or relation rests on, where a fix needs a write it may not make (DEC-030 point 5), and where point 6 hands over what the loop could not clear. Each time it says what it recommends. A grade only the asker's words could raise, the review marks (point 4).

### 8. Where the verdict binds

- **On the designer's loop on a piece.** The designer's rule for ending its loop reads the verdict (point 6). While the verdict fails the bar, the designer neither ends the loop as passed nor hands the piece over as meeting the bar. The hold is that rule's alone, which the record of the design agents keeps among the designer's (DEC-030 point 2).
- **The reviewer stays advisory.** Its findings, and the verdict read from its grades, inform wherever they are read. The reviewer holds nothing itself: one agent makes, and one reads (DEC-030 point 1).
- **It binds no rule.** The verdict holds the behaviour of one of the capability's agents, which a record of the capability decides, as it decides any of its agents' bodies (COR-013). The rules it is read from still bind on no artefact: a break of one informs, and holds no file, no validation and no merge (DEC-028 point 5). The bar enters no rule set and no route, so which rules bind on what stays the project's choice, through its sets, their statuses and their pins (COR-051 point 7).
- **Nowhere else.** Elsewhere the reviewer's findings inform their owner, who decides, and a verdict holds nothing. That covers the starter's entry review ([visual-communication:DEC-007-starter-library] point 3), a role's default as a fair stand-in ([visual-communication:DEC-008-library-role-defaults] point 2), a picture with the piece's words drawn into it ([visual-communication:DEC-006-work-outside-the-engine-and-outputs] point 2), and a review a person asks for outside a loop.
- **No validation, no merge.** The verdict joins no validation, since the grades it is read from are readings, and a reading of taste never fails (DEC-002 point 7). It holds no merge. Whether the review is required at the merge is [project-kit#2676](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2676)'s.

### 9. The verdict ends the loop once a calibration's report is accepted

- **The Task's conditions.** The Task that calibrated the review let it hold the designer's loop only where four conditions all hold on the ladder ([project-kit#3151](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3151)). In its words: "the order holds: round 1 below round 4, round 4 below round 5, and round 5 about equal to round 7"; "round 4 fails and round 5 passes"; "the verdict is stable across the runs"; and "main's drawing fails". The rounds are the ladder's designs in order: round 4 the last the senior critic failed, round 5 the first it judged professional, and round 7 the last it polished. Main's drawing is the deck as the default branch drew it (Context).
- **Not met by the first calibration.** On the canon alone, the order held, though weakly: round 4 sat below round 5 on the average alone, within the spread between runs. Round 5 passed in no run, so the second condition failed. Round 4's fail, main's fail and the verdict's stability held only because every verdict was a fail. So the verdict ends no loop yet.
- **What ships, however the Task's first condition is read.** The Task also let the record ship the canon graded alone if it ordered the ladder as the canon with the critic's measures beside it did. However that is read, the canon alone is what this record ships. The critic's measures can never be a rule's source (DEC-028 point 1), so no other measure could ship. Until the verdict ends the loop, the grades only inform, so shipping them holds nothing.
- **Until then.** The review grades each rule, and the command gives the verdict, which informs. The designer's loop ends as DEC-030 point 2 says.
- **The second calibration.** It runs once the refinement of point 2 has landed, grading by the canon alone, since the canon is what the capability ships. Its Task fixes four things before it runs, and nobody accepts the report of a run that fixed them later:
  - **The bar it reads,** in the shape point 3 gives a bar. The conditions depend on the bar as much as on the grades.
  - **A held-out ladder:** the designs of another piece, which a senior critic ranked before the calibration runs, and from which the refinement of point 2 was not written.
  - **How each rule the bar covers is tested:** by a difference the ladder makes in what that rule judges. The held-out ladder varies its leads and its other words between designs, as well as their look. A rule whose part the ladder holds fixed is left out of the bar the calibration reads.
  - **The four conditions,** each read on its counterpart in the held-out ladder. Its first design grades below the last the critic failed, that one below the first it judged professional, and that one about equal to its last. The last it failed fails, and the first it judged professional passes. The verdict is stable across the runs. A design known to be weak fails.
- **What its report shows,** so that whoever reads it reads evidence that can bear the decision:
  - what was calibrated: the bar and the rules it covers, the reviewer's model, the major of each set the bar covers, and the version of the capability whose reviewer's body and design knowledge ran;
  - the ladder, and the senior critic's ranking of it, made before the runs;
  - each condition as the Task fixed it, whether it held, and the runs and grades it rests on, with a condition that held only because every verdict failed told apart;
  - for each rule the bar covers, the difference the ladder made in what the rule judges, and how its grades followed it;
  - how far each page's verdict held from run to run, on the same images above all;
  - each verdict beside the grades it was read from;
  - where a condition failed, the rules behind the failure.
- **What switches the hold on.** Whoever maintains the capability accepts the report. The acceptance is recorded as a refinement of this record, which names what was calibrated: the bar, the reviewer's model, the major of each canon set the bar covers, and the reviewer's body and the design knowledge by the capability's version. The conditions are the evidence that acceptance weighs, not a switch of their own: where one did not hold, the refinement says which, and why the report was accepted. Turning the hold on changes what the capability's designer and reviewer do in every project, so a person decides it.
- **Where it holds.** Once a report is accepted, the verdict ends the loop in any project whose bar asks professional or above on rules the calibration tested, at the majors it tested, and whose reviewer runs on the model it calibrated. The reviewer's definition names that model. Where a project overrides that model, or its bar covers anything else, the review informs and ends no loop, and the capability's check says why, as a fact (DEC-002 point 7). A project that adopts the capability calibrates nothing.
- **If it is not accepted.** The verdict ends no loop, and the grades go on informing. The report names each condition that failed, and the rules behind it. The next calibration runs only after a change that answers that report, which a person accepts: a refinement of the canon, of the reviewer's body, or of this record.
- **What calls for a new report.** Each of these calls for a new report and a new acceptance before the verdict ends a loop under what changed:
  - a set the calibrated bar covers takes a new major, as a new rule gives it (COR-051 point 7);
  - the model the reviewer's definition names changes;
  - the reviewer's body or the design knowledge changes, as point 2's reading can change them.

  A check can see each: a set's major in its file, the model in the reviewer's definition and the project's overlay, and the reviewer's body and the design knowledge by the capability's version.

## Rationale

**Why grades in words.** A pass or a fail on each rule hides how far a page is from the bar, and leaves polish nothing to aim at. Words read alike to the designer, the reviewer and a person, and each names a level of work. A number would invite sums that no rule supports, such as a mean in which one strong rule hides a weak one. The bar reads each rule apart. In the calibration, every review's verdict followed from its grades. A verdict that needs no judgement is a command's, so the designer and a person can give it again from the same report, and what the calibration observed, the command guarantees.

**Why a rule on the pages together takes one grade for the piece.** A rule on the order of the pages, or on one meaning looking the same everywhere, says nothing of one page alone. Graded on each page, it would fail or pass a page for what lies on others, and the same flaw would count once for each page. The calibration graded such rules on each page, which may be part of why the rule on the story's order failed about half the pages, alike across the ladder.

**Why against professional work, and not against progress or the engine.** A review that graded progress would pass a weak page that improved. One that graded what the engine draws today would pass the engine's limits as the piece's quality. A missing tool is a need instead (point 4), so the person sees it, and the grade stays honest.

**Why the gaps are placed through the record of the principles.** The rules are the canon's, each with its source, and a project or a talk that shows a rule at work confirms it and is never its source (DEC-028 point 1). One critic's measures for one deck would carry that critic's taste to every project. Not every gap is a missing rule. A part of design that needs no judgement is a check's, and a type scale or a colour scheme is a brand's values (DEC-028 point 1). Where a rule names the measure and the review read it otherwise, the gap is in the reading, which the design knowledge and the reviewer's body carry. The calibration shows where the review graded weakly, not why, so the record that places the rules weighs each gap.

**Why a blocking fault on design is a rule at the bottom, and its grade settled.** The reviewer's faults stay facts about the drawing, which is why a fault never judges design (DEC-030 point 6). A fault of design is a reading. As a rule graded at the bottom, it blocks the bar while it keeps the citation, the source and the acceptance of every rule. Its rule names the fault, so a page that shows it breaks the rule by the rule's text alone, as a break the text settles is settled (DEC-030 point 6). Every other grade weighs how well, which no rule's text fixes, so a person decides it.

**Why the bar is a setting of the configuration, and not a rule.** The Task asked for the bar as an accepted rule in a project's own set that inherits `COMM` and `GRAPHIC`. A rule is a statement every artefact it binds on must satisfy (COR-051 point 1). The bar requires nothing of a piece: a piece below it fails nothing, and reaches the person with what blocks it. It says when the designer's loop may end, so it is the loop's stopping parameter, as the bound is, and the configuration holds such choices of the project as a whole (DEC-002 point 2).
- **As a rule in a set that inherits the canon,** it would, once a piece's files are artefacts, sit in the very set through which the canon's rules bind on them, on drawings no reviewer of a change can judge (COR-051 point 7, COR-057 point 3). Point 1 would also grade it as a rule of the project's own.
- **As a rule in a set with no scope that inherits nothing,** it would keep a rule's id and acceptance without its binding. Nothing would guard it from a scope or an inheritance added later. The review would find it by reading the sets' prose. The backbone's tools would report it as binding nothing while it ended a loop.
- **As data,** the configuration's schema and the capability's check guard it, one key names it, and the check compares the majors it covers as a pin would, so a new major of the canon shows.

What it gives up is a rule's acceptance. A change to it is reviewed in its pull request, as every change to the configuration is (DEC-020 point 7), and who authorises it is the project's to say. A shipped bar would carry one project's choice to every project (COR-014). A bar asks professional at least, so the loop never ends as met on a grade the review reports as a rule finding. One bar serves the loop the Feature asks for, and a second needs a field by which each piece names its bar, a format of its own.

**The first of a pattern.** This is the first case of a project's stopping parameter that a component's agent loop reads. Should a second component's agent loop come to stop at a parameter of the project's own, such as a loop of code review, the core decides one shape for both (COR-007).

**Why the review is handed its earlier verdicts and the reasons only once its grades are written.** DEC-030 has the review read the brief's reasons last, so that it never sees a page through them. Earlier verdicts read first would anchor its grades the same way. The convergence experiment shows that a rule of restraint is not enough: in both loops, the reasons were in view from the review's first read, and the review could only say it weighed them later. So one way serves both: each is handed over only once the grades are written. Handed over in that order, neither is in the review's first read. Neither is out of its reach, since its tools read the repository and the brief holds its reasons beside its goals. So the record says so, and the review's report shows what it read before it graded. Putting them out of reach would split the brief or give the reviewer a command. Each is a boundary other work builds on (COR-065 point 3), so it waits for a record of its own.

**Why the review says why each grade moved.** In the calibration, the canon's grades on the same images varied from run to run. A review that explained only a lower grade would let a grade raised by that variation stand, and a loop could reach the bar by it. One that explains every change, higher or lower, shows the person which changes came from the page, and which from the reading. An account of what the designer changed would show the review the page through the designer's eyes, as reading the reasons first would (DEC-030, "Why the reasons are read last").

**Why the review marks a need, and a grade only the asker's words could raise.** No mend of the designer's raises either. A round spent on one would end at the bound with no news for the person. The review marks them, never the designer: a designer that took its own findings out of its own test for ending the loop would hand the answer that decides whether those findings hold, as DEC-030 refuses ("The answer of which rules bind, handed to the reviewer by whoever dispatches it"). A mark sits on a grade, as the bar does, so a grade that one mend could raise stays in the verdict, whatever else holds it down.

**Why a finding names where its fix belongs.** Only the designer renders, so only the designer learns whether a fix fits. The engine's measure of a text decides its line breaks, and a review that judges from a drawing can be wrong about them. A prescription also meets limits the review does not weigh, such as the asker's words. That reason holds whatever the counts of rounds, which one run of each loop cannot settle.

**Why polish, within two bounds, and the last round that met the bar.** A loop that stopped at the first pass would hand over work just at the bar. Polish spends a few rounds on what the review still grades below excellent. The review varies from run to run, so a loop with no bound could chase that variation for ever. A count of rounds is no requirement a piece meets, as a rule is (COR-051 point 1), so the bound is a setting, which a project may change. Its default starts from the one whole deck the Feature cites: a designer under a blocking critic, outside the capability, reached professional work in five rounds and polished it in two more (#3148). The trials revisit it (#3150). A bound of its own for polish keeps a slow climb from eating polish, and keeps polish from running on. A dispatcher may lower a bound for one run, since that only hands over sooner. Raising one would spend more of the person's wait than the project chose. A polish round can fail the bar a round before it met, so the designer hands over the last round that met it, and the person never gets a piece worse than one the loop already had.

**Why a grade two rounds did not raise goes to the person at the loop's end.** It still counts against the bar, so nothing exempts it, and nobody has to mark it. Asked about midway, the person would hold up a loop that can still mend the rest. Held to the end, the loop converges alone, and the person hears of each such grade with everything else. Once only such grades are left, a further round would bring no news, so the loop ends there.

**Why the asker's words are decided by where they came from.** The record of the brief gives the goals and relations to the asker (DEC-029 point 4), and makes a page's lead its takeaway (DEC-029 point 2). It says nowhere whose a lead's words are, so this record decides it. A lead states the page's point, which is the intent the asker owns, and a title is read with its lead (`RS-COMM-001`), so a title the asker gave means what a lead means. Words the designer drafted carry the designer's reading of that intent until the asker has seen them. Once the asker has seen them and let them stand, they state the asker's intent, as the goals and relations do once the designer has shown them (DEC-030 point 2). So the designer stays free to mend its own draft, and a lead or a title the asker has read never changes behind the asker's back. Where a seen title and lead hold a rule below the bar, the grade goes to the person as the asker's (point 4). A translation says what its source says, so it has its source's owner.

**Why the brief records the designer's unseen drafts.** Whether the asker has seen a word must outlast the session that showed it, or a later session could not keep the rule. The brief holds a piece's intent and no command changes it (DEC-029 point 4), so it keeps the record, by page. It records the drafts the asker has not seen, not those the asker has, so a record lost or never written leads the designer to ask, never to change the asker's words.

**Why every other word is the designer's.** The other words carry the asker's intent as the form does, and the designer shapes them as it shapes the form, within goals and relations that still hold. A designer that asked before each change of words would wait on the person at every mend, with a loop or without one: DEC-030 point 2 has it mend each finding it judges right, and many findings are on words. In both loops of the convergence experiment, the designer, given this authority, changed titles, cut facts with no source and reworded parts in most rounds of mending. That shows how often a mend touches words, not that the authority is right. The authority rests on the asker's intent staying in the asker's words, which the designer never changes alone. A fact that a goal or relation rests on is part of that intent, so the designer asks before it cuts one. How much the designer changes alone is a level of autonomy, which the core's setting will govern (#2801). Whose words are whose is a fact of the piece's intent, which no level changes.

**Why the verdict holds only the loop, and binds no rule.** A grade is a reading, which never fails validation (DEC-002 point 7). What ends the designer's loop is the designer's own rule, which this record decides as a record of the capability decides any of its agents (COR-013). The reviewer stays advisory, so one agent makes and one reads (DEC-030 point 1), and the hold lives where the loop does. The rules the verdict is read from still bind on nothing, and the bar enters no set and no route, so which rules bind stays the project's choice through its sets (COR-051 point 7). Elsewhere, an owner judges by a bar of their own, such as the starter's bar for entry, and the review informs that owner.

**Why the verdict waits for an accepted report, on another ladder.** A verdict that ends a loop must pass work a senior critic judged professional, fail work it judged weak, and say the same of the same page twice. The first calibration passed no design, so a loop held to its verdict could never end at the bar. Its conditions were the Task's own, set before it ran, and the next calibration's are fixed before it runs, so nobody sets the test after seeing the result. A second calibration on the same ladder would test the canon on the designs its gaps were found on, and would test again the rules whose part that ladder held. It would also read whatever bar it was handed, while the conditions depend on the bar. So it states its bar, reads another ladder, and tests each rule by a difference the ladder makes.
- **A person's acceptance, not a switch.** Turning the hold on changes what the shipped designer and reviewer do, so a person decides it in any case. An acceptance recorded as a refinement names what was calibrated. A licence the conditions granted by themselves would be recorded nowhere, and would hold for whatever came after. The person reads the conditions as evidence, and can weigh a failure by its cause, such as a rule that judges beyond the senior critic. The report's form gives that reading what it needs.
- **What calls for a new report.** A change to any part of what was calibrated, the canon, the model or the reading, moves the grades, so each calls for a new report.

**Why the hold follows the reviewer, not the bar.** A calibration needs a held-out ladder ranked by a senior critic, which no adopter can be asked to build. Were the hold tied to a project's bar by its text, an adopter would get it only by copying the calibrated bar: a shipped default under another name, which this record and #3150 defer. Tied to what was calibrated, it reaches any project whose bar asks professional or above on rules that were tested, at the majors tested, with the reviewer on the model tested. The reviewer's definition names that model, since a reviewer that inherits its caller's model would run on another with each dispatch (the agents README, "Model and effort"). A project that overrides the model, or covers what was not tested, has a review no calibration measured. So it informs, and the check says why, so the project knows what it gave up.

### Alternatives considered

- **The critic's measures beside the canon, as the calibration graded them.** Rejected: they were one critic's, written for one deck, and no source of the canon's names them (DEC-028 point 1). The gaps they showed are placed through the canon instead (point 2).
- **The `GRAPHIC` rules alone as the measure.** Rejected: the calibration does not show that the rules on words grade badly. The leads held across the ladder, so the rule on their order had nothing to tell apart. The other words changed mostly by cuts, which may leave what the rule on words for the audience judges as it was. A change of sources the ladder did make, the rule on sources caught. Dropped, they would leave what a piece says unjudged. A project that wants a bar on `GRAPHIC` alone names that set in its bar, and the second calibration tests the rules on words on a ladder that varies them (point 9).
- **A pass or a fail on each rule, with no grade.** Rejected: polish would have nothing to aim at, and nobody could tell a page near the bar from one far below it.
- **Grades as numbers.** Rejected: a sum or a mean lets a strong rule hide a weak one, and no rule says what a mean means.
- **A grade on each page for every rule.** Rejected: a rule on the pages together says nothing of one page, and one flaw would count once for each page.
- **Every grade for a person, a blocking fault's included.** Rejected: a rule that names a fault settles the page that shows it by its text alone.
- **The verdict given by the review itself.** Rejected: it needs no judgement, so it is a command's, and only a command's can be given again from the same report.
- **A bar the capability ships.** Not taken now: it waits for the trials ([project-kit#3150](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3150)).
- **The bar as an accepted rule in a project's set that inherits `COMM` and `GRAPHIC`,** as the Task words it. Rejected: once a piece's files are artefacts, that set would bind the canon, and the bar, on them, and point 1 would grade the bar as a rule.
- **The bar as a rule in a set with no scope that inherits nothing.** Rejected: nothing would guard it from a later scope or inheritance, the review would find it by reading prose, and the backbone's tools could not see it.
- **A key in the configuration naming the bar's rule by its id.** Rejected: it would keep a rule that binds nothing, with a guard of the capability's own over a set, where the bar is a stopping parameter the configuration can hold outright.
- **Several bars in one project.** Not taken now: each piece would need a field naming its bar, a format that waits for a record of its own.
- **The review reading its earlier verdicts by restraint alone, and being handed the reasons late.** Rejected: one way for both, and the experiment shows restraint failing for the reasons.
- **A brief split in two files, or a reviewer that fetches the reasons through a command.** Not taken here: either changes a boundary other records decide (DEC-029 point 1, DEC-030 point 7), so it waits for a record of its own.
- **The review saying why only where it grades an unchanged part lower.** Rejected: a grade raised by the review's variation would stand, and the review, which reads no account of what changed, cannot know that a part did not change.
- **The designer marking a need, or a finding only the asker's words can mend.** Rejected: the designer would take its own findings out of its own test for ending the loop.
- **Findings that prescribe their fix.** Rejected: only the designer renders, and a prescription can be wrong where only the engine's measure tells, as one was. A prescription also meets limits the review does not weigh.
- **The verdict holding the loop at once, on the canon as it is.** Rejected: no design passed, the critic's professional ones included, so the loop could never end at the bar, and the Task's conditions forbid it.
- **A licence the four conditions grant once they hold.** Rejected: nothing would record it, turning it on changes the shipped agents anyway, and it would hold past changes to what it measured.
- **The conditions rewritten after the first calibration.** Rejected: that sets the test after seeing the result, and on the first data it would open nothing.
- **A second calibration on the same ladder.** Rejected: its gaps were found on it, and it held its leads, so it tests no rule on them.
- **The hold for any project whose bar has the calibrated bar's text.** Rejected: an adopter would get it only by copying that bar, a shipped default under another name.
- **Each project calibrating its own bar now.** Not taken now: it needs a held-out ladder and a senior critic, which no adopter has been asked for.
- **A loop with no bound, until the bar.** Rejected: the review varies from run to run, and a loop could chase that variation for ever.
- **The bound in the bar.** Rejected: a count of rounds is no requirement a piece meets, so it is no rule.
- **One bound for the whole loop, polish taking what is left.** Rejected: a slow climb would leave no polish.
- **A loop that stops once two rounds raise no grade.** Rejected: the review's variation raises and lowers grades with no change to the page, so a stall is hard to tell from noise.
- **A grade two rounds did not raise stopping the loop, as DEC-030 point 2 has a finding do.** Rejected: the loop would wait on the person midway, and would not converge alone.
- **A dispatcher raising the bound for one run.** Rejected: it would spend more of the person's wait than the project chose.
- **Polish that hands over its last round, whatever its verdict.** Rejected: a polish round can fail the bar a round before it met.
- **Every lead the asker's, whoever drafted it.** Rejected: the designer would ask to change its own draft, and a page it added would fix its lead the moment it was written.
- **A title the designer's, whoever wrote it, unless a goal names it.** Rejected: a title the asker dictated could change without asking.
- **A title the asker's only where the asker wrote it.** Rejected: a title the asker has read and let stand states the asker's intent as a lead does, and the rule would hold two ways.
- **The brief recording each lead the asker has seen.** Rejected: a record lost or never written would free the designer to change the asker's words.
- **The designer asking before any change of words.** Rejected: it would wait on the person at every mend, loop or none.
- **Point 7 only where a verdict ends the loop.** Rejected: the designer mends words with or without a bar, and would otherwise ask about each such mend until a calibration opens the loop.
- **The hold in the reviewer's posture.** Rejected: the reviewer reads and the designer makes (DEC-030 point 1), so the rule that ends a loop is the designer's.
