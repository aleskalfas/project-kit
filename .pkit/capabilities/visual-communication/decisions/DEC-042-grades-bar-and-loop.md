**In plain terms:** the review grades each rule of the capability's rule sets in words, from *below competent* to *excellent*, against professional work. A project may set a *bar* in the capability's configuration, and a command reads from the grades whether a piece meets it. Once whoever maintains the capability accepts a calibration of the review, and where the bar covers only rules that calibration gave evidence for, the designer's loop runs until the piece meets the bar, polishes it within a bound, and hands the person what still blocks it. Until then the grades only inform. The designer changes alone every word that is not the asker's.

## Context

The designer mends what it judges right, and stops when the reviewer's reading reports nothing it would mend ([visual-communication:DEC-030-design-agents] point 2). No finding says how far a page is from good work, and no project says how good is enough. [project-kit#3148](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3148) asks the designer to work under its own review until every page reaches a bar the project sets, then hand the person only what still blocks it. [project-kit#3151](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3151) ran two experiments first, and its change gives their figures.

**The outside judge.** An *outside judge* is a senior designer, a person or an agent briefed as one, outside the capability, who ranks a *ladder*, a series of designs of one piece, before any run of the review on it. The judge's *pass line* is where the judge passes a page.

**A calibration on a ladder.** The reviewer graded a ladder of one deck, from a first draft through designs an outside judge failed, then judged professional, then polished. It also graded the deck of the repository the capability is authored in, as it stood on its default branch when the calibration ran. Each page's lead stayed the same throughout, and the other words changed a lot among the early designs, mostly by cuts, and little among the last. The review graded by the canon's rules alone, and by the canon beside the judge's own measures.
- **No design passed** either way, the designs the judge judged professional included.
- **The canon alone ordered the ladder closely against the judge's measures, and loosely against the designs' order and the judge's ranking.** On the `GRAPHIC` rules alone, it ordered the ladder much as the judge had.
- **On pages that were the same image in two designs, the canon's verdicts split from run to run.** Beside the measures they agreed only because the judge's pass line read them.
- **The rules on words failed pages alike across the ladder.** The leads held, so the rule on their order had nothing to tell apart. The rule on words for the audience graded the early designs alike though their words changed most. The rule on sources graded the last design down for a source it dropped, and failed most of the pages the canon failed and the judge passed.
- **Four things the judge's measures judge had a weak counterpart among the rules, or none:** craft, typography, legibility at the medium's size and colour. The review graded the rule on legibility by how much a page holds, not by whether its text reads at its size. One fault the judge blocked on judges design, and no rule names it.
- **Each verdict followed from its review's grades.**

**The issue's conditions, not met.** The *hold* is the switch by which the verdict a command reads from the review's grades (point 1) ends the designer's loop at the bar. [project-kit#3151](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3151) would turn the hold on only where four conditions were met on the ladder: that the review order the ladder with the first draft below the last design the judge failed, that one below the first it judged professional, and that one about equal to the last it polished; that the last it failed fail, and the first it judged professional pass; that the verdict be stable across the runs; and that the deck as it stood on that default branch fail. On the canon alone, the last design the judge failed sat below the first it judged professional on the average alone, within the spread between runs. The first it judged professional passed in no run, and the other conditions held only because every verdict was a fail.

**Convergence on one page.** A page outside that deck was designed twice from the same start, by the same agents, with every word but its lead the designer's to change, and only how a failing finding stated its fix differed. Both loops converged, and findings that name only where the fix belongs took fewer rounds than findings that prescribe it. Some prescriptions changed what the designer could not, and it took none of them. One judged from the browser's drawing that a wording would fit on one line, and the engine's measure wrapped it. Neither loop waited on the person. In both, the review read the brief whole in one read. Neither loop was repeated, so this shows a direction, not a measure.

## Decision

**The review grades each rule in words; a project's bar, read by a command, decides when the designer's loop may end; and the verdict ends that loop only where an accepted calibration of the review reaches.**

### 1. The review grades each rule in words

- **A grade for each rule.** The review grades each rule it reads from the rule sets (DEC-030 point 6) that judges the piece ([visual-communication:DEC-028-principles-of-visual-communication] point 3), on every image it reads, with its evidence in a line. A *grade* is one of four words, lowest first: *below competent*, *competent*, *professional* and *excellent*. *Professional* is the work a professional designer hands over for that audience and medium, and the reviewer's body defines the other three against it. A grade never weighs effort, what the engine can draw, or progress.
- **On each page, or once for the piece.** A rule whose text judges the pages together, such as the rule that the pages tell the story in order (`RS-COMM-003`), takes one grade for the piece. A rule that judges both takes both, as `RS-COMM-002` and `RS-GRAPHIC-004` do, and the others take one on each page. The reviewer's body holds which take which. Where a rule's text leaves a page or a piece out, it takes no grade there, and the review says so.
- **A rule finding.** Each grade below professional is a rule finding (DEC-030 point 6), whatever the bar, and carries its grade. The bar only reads the grades, for the verdict.
- **Settled, or for a person.** A rule's text that settles a break settles that the grade is below professional, and settles the exact grade only where it names one, as a rule on a blocking fault names *below competent* (point 2). Every other grade is a reading, and once its rule binds, a finding on it is for a person (DEC-030 point 6).
- **The verdict, given by a command.** Where a project sets a bar (point 3), the *verdict* says whether each page meets it, and whether the piece does. A page meets it where each of its unmarked grades (point 4) on the rules the bar covers reaches the bar. The piece meets it where every page does, and each unmarked grade the piece takes as a whole does. The verdict names each grade that fails the bar, and the rules the bar covers. It needs no judgement, so a command of the capability gives it (DEC-028 point 1), from the review's report of a fixed form and the bar, and anyone can give it again from the same report. The reviewer runs no command (DEC-030 point 7), so whoever reads its report runs this one. Where the command gives no verdict, or one that ends no loop (points 3, 9 and 10), it says why.

### 2. A refinement of the record of the principles decides where each gap belongs

- **Where each belongs.** A refinement of DEC-028, accepted in a change of its own, decides where each gap the calibration found (Context) belongs (DEC-028 point 1): to a rule of the canon, with its source, where no rule names it; to a check, where it needs no judgement; to a brand's values, where it is the look; or to the reading, in the design knowledge (DEC-030 point 8) or the reviewer's body, where a rule names it and the review read the rule otherwise. The calibration cannot tell a gap in the canon from one in the reading, so the refinement weighs each.
- **No borrowed measure becomes a rule.** The outside judge's measures show where the canon is thin, and are never a rule's source (DEC-028 point 1).
- **A blocking fault that judges design is a rule's,** a new one or one that already names it. A page that shows it takes *below competent* on that rule. It never joins the reviewer's faults, which never judge design (DEC-030 point 6).
- **What stays the checks'.** Contrast, a text that overflows its box and a text smaller than its medium allows stay the checks' (DEC-028 point 1). The check DEC-028 point 1 assigns for the last was never built, so the gap in legibility is partly that check, which later work builds.

### 3. A project sets its bar in the capability's configuration

- **What a bar is.** A project's *bar* names what it covers among the rules the review grades, rule sets by their own ids or single rules, each with its set's major. It names the grade each page and the piece must reach on them: professional or excellent. It is data, in a shape such as `bar: {covers: [COMM@1, GRAPHIC@1], at-least: professional}`, which the configuration's schema fixes. A set of the project's own covers only its own rules, not those it inherits. A bar asks nothing below professional, since a loop that met such a bar would end on a rule finding the designer could still mend.
- **A stopping parameter, not a rule.** The bar says when the designer's loop may end (point 6), and requires nothing of a piece, so it binds nothing. It is a setting of the capability's configuration, which holds the project's choices as a whole ([visual-communication:DEC-002-files-and-validation] point 2). A project keeps one bar, under one key. A second would need each piece to name its bar, a format that waits for a record of its own.
- **Checked as the configuration is.** The schema refuses another shape, or a grade below professional. The capability's check fails a bar that covers what the review does not grade. It also fails one whose major for a set differs from the major the project pins for that set, or from the set's own where the project pins none, as a pin behind its set fails (COR-051 point 7). The project corrects either in its configuration (DEC-002 point 7).
- **Read from the configuration alone.** No agent reads the bar. The command and the capability's check read it from the configuration, a file the project reviews, as a browser a project names lies in such a file and never in a flag ([visual-communication:DEC-020-html-page-drawing] point 7). No dispatcher hands anyone a bar, and the review grades by none. Who authorises a change to the bar is the project's to say.
- **No bar, no hold.** Without a bar the review grades, no verdict is given, and the designer's loop ends as DEC-030 point 2 says. The capability ships no bar, since a default waits for the evidence of its trials ([project-kit#3150](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3150)).

### 4. What the review is handed, and which grades it marks

- **What it reads.** Every image the viewing step drew, with the frames and the report; the rule sets; the design knowledge (DEC-030 point 8); and the sources the piece's facts name (DEC-030 point 7).
- **The brief's intent first.** A command of the capability hands the review the brief's goals, relations and takeaways, without their reasons. The review grades each page against them (DEC-030 point 6), and reads the brief's file only once its grades are written.
- **Its reports of the loop's earlier rounds after its grades.** Whoever hands the review its work hands it its reports of the loop's earlier rounds, as it wrote them, only once its grades for the round are written. Nobody summarises, filters or answers them first, and the review reads no account of what the designer changed. It then says why each grade differs from the one it gave the round before, higher or lower.
- **What the order rests on.** For the reasons and its reports of the loop's earlier rounds, the order rests on the reviewer's restraint, since its tools read the repository. Its report says what it read before grading, so a breach shows.
- **A need.** Where only a tool the engine lacks would raise a grade, the review marks the grade a *need*, and names the tool. The need goes to the person as a question that names what the design needs, as DEC-030 point 4 has a stop do. It stops only the work that rests on it, and the loop goes on with the rest.
- **The asker's.** Where only the asker's words (point 7) would raise a grade, the review marks the grade as the asker's, names the words, and it goes to the person as a question.
- **Marked by the review alone, grade by grade.** A grade is marked only where no fix the designer may make would raise it. Otherwise it stays unmarked, and the review names its other cause apart. The verdict leaves marked grades out. The designer marks nothing.

### 5. A failing finding names where its fix belongs

A failing finding names where its fix belongs, and never what the fix is, as DEC-030 point 7 decides. The places a fix belongs gain two: the asker's words, for a grade only they could raise, and a tool the engine lacks, for a need (point 4).

### 6. The loop runs to the bar, into polish, within a bound

This point applies where points 9 and 10 let the verdict end the loop at a project's bar. Elsewhere the designer's loop ends as DEC-030 point 2 says.
- **A round** is one mend by the designer, then a render, a look and a review of every page (DEC-030 point 2). The designer's *loop* is the rounds from the first review on.
- **To the bar.** While the verdict fails the bar, each round mends each unmarked grade that fails it, within what the designer may change (point 7), whether or not the designer agrees with the grade.
- **Into polish.** Once the verdict meets the bar, *polish* mends the unmarked grades below excellent on the bar's rules, and the notes the designer agrees with. A polish round that fails the bar is mended first. Polish ends when neither is left, or at the bound.
- **Within a bound.** The loop runs no more than a bound of rounds to the bar, and one of polish. The bound is a setting of the capability's configuration, beside the bar and no part of it, with a default in the configuration's schema that a project may change. Whoever dispatches the designer may lower either bound for one run, and never raise it.
- **A stuck grade.** A grade is *stuck* where two consecutive rounds of mending did not raise it while it failed the bar. Its count starts again where it passes and later fails. A stuck grade still counts against the bar. Within its bound, the loop never ends on stuck grades while a grade that is not stuck still fails. It ends where every failing grade is stuck.
- **Then the person, with the last round that met the bar.** The designer hands over the piece as it stood at the last round whose verdict met the bar, or at its last round where none did. With it go each grade that still fails, stuck or not, each marked grade, each open question, and that round's review as the review wrote it. It says what it changed (DEC-030 point 2), and what the later rounds tried.
- **What a check measures stays the check's.** The designer mends a check's failure before any review (DEC-030 point 2), and no round counts one.

### 7. Whose words the designer changes alone, and what it asks

Whose words are whose holds wherever the designer mends a piece, under a bar or not, and at every level of the setting by which the core will decide how much agents decide without asking ([project-kit#2801](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2801)). That setting replaces only the last two bullets, which are the capability's default until then.
- **The asker's words.** A piece's intent belongs to whoever asked for it, the *asker* ([visual-communication:DEC-029-piece-brief] point 4; DEC-030 point 2). The *asker's words* carry it: the brief's goals and relations; each page's lead and title, and each takeaway, once the asker has seen them; any word a goal names as the asker's, such as a quote; and their translations. The designer changes none of them without the asker's word, and mends a translation only to make it say what its source says.
- **By where they came from.** Words are the asker's once the asker has seen them: those the asker wrote, and those the designer drafted and showed the asker, as it shows the goals and relations (DEC-030 point 2). Until then a lead, title or takeaway the designer drafted is its own to change. Returning it to whoever dispatched the designer is not showing it to the asker.
- **The makers' record of drafts.** For each lead, title and takeaway that the designer drafted and the asker has not seen, the brief records which one it is, by its page, its kind and the language of its text, and a digest of that text, never the text. The record lies in the page's part, or in the part for the whole piece on a piece of one page. The designer records a draft as it writes it, and clears it once the asker has seen it. A lead, title or takeaway whose text no longer matches its recorded digest is the asker's, and so is one the record lists nothing for, a piece with no brief included. So a record lost, stale or never written leads the designer to ask.
- **Every other word is the designer's, within DEC-030 point 4.** It may reword, cut or move any other text, in every language, where each goal and relation still holds. It never bends the meaning, nor changes what the piece says to fit what a library draws (DEC-030 point 4). It may correct a fact to its source, and cut one with no source unless a goal or relation rests on it. A fact it adds names its source (`RS-COMM-005`). It tells the person afterwards what it changed (DEC-030 point 2).
- **What it asks.** It asks the person only where a fix would change the asker's words or cut a fact a goal or relation rests on, where a fix needs a write it may not make (DEC-030 point 5), and where two rounds did not clear a finding, at the loop's end where point 6 holds. Each time it says what it recommends.

### 8. What the verdict governs

Once point 9 lets it, the verdict governs one thing: when the designer's loop on a piece ends (point 6), a rule DEC-030 point 2 keeps among the designer's. A record of the capability decides that rule, as records of their own decide its agents ([visual-communication:DEC-001-visual-communication-discipline] point 1). The bar enters no rule set and no route, so which rules bind on what stays the project's choice (COR-051 point 7). The rest stands as other records decide it: the reviewer stays advisory (DEC-030 point 1; [visual-communication:DEC-015-viewing-step-and-reviewer] point 7), the rules bind on no artefact (DEC-028 point 5), the reviewer's findings elsewhere inform their owner (DEC-030 point 6), and the verdict joins no validation (DEC-002 point 7). Whether the review blocks a merge is [project-kit#2676](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2676)'s.

### 9. The verdict ends the loop once a calibration's report is accepted

- **Until then.** The review grades, the command's verdict informs, and the designer's loop ends as DEC-030 point 2 says. The canon alone ships however one reads the condition [project-kit#3151](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3151) set for shipping it, apart from the four of Context: that grading by the canon alone order the ladder as grading beside the outside judge's measures does. Grading beside those measures can never ship, since they are never a rule's source (DEC-028 point 1).
- **A calibration** grades by the canon alone, as the capability ships it, and none runs before the refinement of point 2 has landed. Its work fixes four things before it runs, and the report of a run that fixed them later is never accepted:
  - **its bar,** in point 3's shape, since the conditions depend on the bar as much as on the grades;
  - **a held-out ladder,** of a piece that no change the calibration rests on was written from. An outside judge ranks it before the run by the rules the bar covers, so *professional* means the same to the judge and to the review. Each calibration takes a fresh ladder;
  - **how each covered rule is tested:** by a difference the ladder makes in what the rule judges. A rule whose part the ladder holds fixed leaves the bar. The next calibration's bar covers each rule on words (Context), on a ladder that varies what each one judges;
  - **the four conditions** of Context, each read on its counterpart in the held-out ladder, a design known to be weak standing for the deck of the repository the capability is authored in. They are read for the bar, and for each covered rule alone.
- **Its report** shows enough to weigh each condition and each covered rule's own evidence. The report of an accepted calibration is kept in version control, in the files of the repository the capability is authored in, outside the capability's shipped folder.
- **What switches the hold on.** Whoever maintains the capability accepts the report, and only where every condition was met: that is the acceptance's precondition. The hold it switches on reaches only as point 10 says. A report whose condition failed is never accepted. A change a person accepts answers it, to the canon, the reviewer's body or this record, and a new calibration follows with its conditions fixed first.
- **Where what was calibrated lives.** A change that refines this record records the acceptance. The refinement says that the report was accepted, links it, and points at the calibrated values as that change writes them. The values are data the capability ships, which the command reads, and never a record's prose. Only that change writes them, and the refinement governs them. Their shape is the work's that builds the command (Implications).
- **What lapses the hold.** Anything that could move the grades lapses the hold, and the command compares each with the accepted data:
  - a new major of a set the bar covers, which pins the bar (point 3);
  - the covered rules' text, by its digest, since a rule loosened or reworded keeps its major (COR-051 point 7);
  - the reviewer's definition and the design knowledge, each by a digest of its content, as the deploy computes for an agent's definition, and never by the capability's version;
  - the model and the effort the review ran on. Its report names the model's full name, and its effort where the harness has one. The reviewer's definition may name the model where the harness has the key, and the command never infers the model from the definition alone.

  A lapse is safe: the verdict informs, and the loop ends after two rounds and the person, as DEC-030 point 2 says, until a new report is accepted.

### 10. Where the hold reaches

- **Any project within what was calibrated.** Once a report is accepted, the verdict ends the designer's loop in any project whose bar asks professional or above only on rules that each have their own evidence in that report, while nothing point 9 names lapses the hold. The acceptance rests on each covered rule's own evidence, so a bar over some of those rules gets the hold, and a bar over any rule without its own evidence does not.
- **Elsewhere it informs.** Where a project's bar covers anything else, or the hold has lapsed, the verdict informs and ends no loop.
- **Nothing to calibrate.** A project that adopts the capability calibrates nothing.

## Rationale

**Why grades in words.** A pass or a fail hides how far a page is from the bar, and leaves polish nothing to aim at. A number invites sums no rule supports, such as a mean in which a strong rule hides a weak one. Words name a level of work, and read alike to the designer, the reviewer and a person.

**Why one grade for the piece, and against professional work.** A rule on the pages together, graded on each page, would fail a page for what lies on others. A review that graded progress would pass a weak page that improved, and one that graded what the engine draws would pass its limits as the piece's quality, which a need shows instead (point 4).

**Why a rule's text settles only the grade it names.** A break the text settles is settled (DEC-030 point 6), and a break is below professional, but how far below is a reading unless the text names it. A design fault made a rule that names the bottom grade blocks the bar, and keeps a rule's source and acceptance, where as a fault it would judge design.

**Why the gaps are placed through the record of the principles.** Not every gap is a missing rule, and one judge's measures for one deck would carry that judge's taste to every project (DEC-028 point 1).

**Why the bar is a setting of the configuration.** [project-kit#3151](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3151) asked for the bar as an accepted rule. A rule is a statement every artefact it binds on must satisfy (COR-051 point 1), and the bar requires nothing of a piece: a piece below it fails nothing, and reaches the person with what blocks it. It is the loop's stopping parameter, as the bound is. As data, the schema and the check guard it, and the check compares its majors as a pin would. It gives up a rule's acceptance, and a change to it is reviewed as any change to the configuration is. It is the first stopping parameter of a project's that a component's agent loop reads, and should a second loop stop at one, the core decides one shape for both (COR-007).

**Why the review gets the brief's intent first, and its reports of the loop's earlier rounds after its grades.** A review that reads the reasons first sees the page through them (DEC-030, "Why the reasons are read last"), and its report of an earlier round, read first, anchors a grade the same way. The convergence experiment shows the brief read whole in one read, so restraint alone did not hold. A command that copies the goals, relations and takeaways out of the brief lets the review grade with nothing else in hand, and nobody who made the piece chooses what it is judged against. Putting the reasons out of reach waits for a record of its own (Alternatives), so meanwhile the report of what was read makes restraint checkable. However each moved grade is explained, a grade the reading's variation raised would still stand. The guard against that noise is point 9's condition that the verdict is stable across runs.

**Why the review marks, grade by grade.** No mend of the designer's raises a need, or a grade only the asker's words could raise, so a round spent on one brings no news. A designer that took its own findings out of its own test would decide whether they hold, as DEC-030 refuses for the answer of which rules bind. A mark sits on one grade, so a grade a mend could raise stays in the verdict.

**Why a finding names where its fix belongs.** Only the designer renders, so only it learns whether a fix fits, as the engine's measure of a line showed against a review's estimate. A prescription also meets limits the review does not weigh, such as the asker's words.

**Why polish, within two bounds that have a default, and the last round that met the bar.** A loop that stopped at the first pass would hand over work just at the bar. The review varies from run to run, so a loop with no bound could chase that variation for ever. A loop always needs a stop, and a project that sets a bar need not set a bound, so the bound has a default. A bound of its own keeps a slow climb from eating polish. A lowered bound only hands over sooner, where a raised one would spend more of the person's wait than the project chose. A polish round can fail the bar a round after it was met, so the last round that met it is handed over.

**Why a stuck grade waits for the loop's end.** It still counts, so nothing exempts it, and nobody has to mark it. Asked about midway, the person would hold up a loop that can still mend the rest. A loop that stopped once two rounds raised no grade could stop on noise while a grade that can still rise fails, and this one ends on stuck grades only once nothing else fails.

**Why words are the asker's by where they came from.** DEC-029 gives the goals and relations to the asker (point 4), and makes a lead the page's takeaway (point 2), but says nowhere whose a lead's words are. A lead states the page's point, the intent the asker owns. A title is read with its lead (`RS-COMM-001`), and a takeaway stands in for a lead. Words the designer drafted carry its reading until the asker has seen them, and then state the asker's intent, as the goals and relations do once shown (DEC-030 point 2). So the designer mends its own draft freely, and words the asker has read never change behind the asker's back. The record must outlast the session that showed a draft. It lives in the brief because the brief is the makers' own file, one for each piece, never shown and never translated (DEC-029 point 1). A digest suffices: the record only has to tell whether the text is still the designer's draft, and the text already lies where it is kept, in the content, or in the brief for a takeaway. The text itself would put the content's words in the brief, which DEC-029 point 2 keeps out.

**Why every other word is the designer's.** A designer that asked before each change of words would wait on the person at every mend, since many findings are on words. The other words carry the asker's intent as the form does, so the designer shapes them within goals and relations that still hold, and a meaning it never bends (DEC-030 point 4). So the asker owns the asker's words, rather than all a piece says.

**Why the verdict waits for an accepted report, on a fresh ladder.** A verdict that ends a loop must pass work an outside judge ranked professional, fail work it ranked weak, and say the same of the same page twice. The first calibration passed no design. Turning the hold on changes what the shipped designer does in every project, so a person decides it, on conditions fixed before the run, since a condition waived after reading the report would set the test after seeing the result. A judge who ranked by measures of its own would fail the canon for differing from them, as the canon failed pages the first judge passed, mostly on the rule on sources. A ladder some change was written from tests the canon on the designs its gaps were found on, so each calibration costs a new ladder.

**Why the calibrated values are data.** A model's name or a digest is state, which a record does not hold, and a check that read a record's prose would read what no schema guards. As data the capability ships, the values reach every project that has it. Since only a refinement's change writes them, no edit of data alone turns the hold on.

**Why anything that could move the grades lapses the hold.** A calibration measures one reading: the covered rules' text, the reviewer's definition, the design knowledge, the model and its effort. A change to any of them moves the grades as a new rule does. A digest sees every change of text and nothing else, where the capability's version moves with every release, the one that ships the accepted data included. A major misses a rule loosened or reworded, so the covered rules' text is digested too, at the cost of a new calibration for each rewording. A definition says what was asked for, not what ran, and an alias moves to a newer model unseen, so the review's report names the model in full. The cost is that when that model retires, the hold lapses everywhere until a new calibration is accepted. Each lapse costs only the hold.

**Why the hold follows the reviewer, not the bar.** No adopter can be asked for a held-out ladder and an outside judge. Tied to a bar's text, the hold would reach an adopter only by copying the calibrated bar, a shipped default under another name. Tied to what was calibrated, it reaches any project whose bar stays on rules with their own evidence. A bar over some of those rules was never read whole, but each rule's evidence stands on its own.

### Alternatives considered

The Rationale gives the reasons against the other contenders it names: a pass or a fail, numbers, a loop with no bound or one that stops on two flat rounds, a stuck grade asked about midway, a designer that asks before each change of words, a waived condition, a reused ladder, the capability's version, an alias, and a hold tied to a bar's text or calibrated by each project.

- **Point 1.**
  - **A grade below the bar as a rule finding.** Rejected: the bar would decide which grades are findings.
  - **The verdict given by the review.** Rejected: it needs no judgement, so it is a command's.
- **Point 2.**
  - **The judge's measures beside the canon.** Rejected: one judge's measures for one deck, which no source of the canon names.
  - **The `GRAPHIC` rules alone.** Rejected: the ladder held its leads and cut its other words, so it does not show that the rules on words grade badly, and without them nothing judges what a piece says. A project that wants it names `GRAPHIC` alone in its bar.
- **Point 3.**
  - **The bar as an accepted rule in a project's set that inherits `COMM` and `GRAPHIC`,** as [project-kit#3151](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3151) words it. Rejected: that set would bind the canon, and the bar, on a piece's files once they are artefacts, and point 1 would grade the bar.
  - **The bar as a rule in a set with no scope that inherits nothing.** Rejected: nothing would guard it from a later scope, and the review would read it from prose.
- **Point 4.**
  - **The designer handing the review an excerpt of the brief.** Rejected: the designer would choose what its own work is judged against.
  - **A brief split in two files, or a reviewer that fetches the reasons through a command.** Not taken: either changes a boundary DEC-029 point 1 or DEC-030 point 7 decides, and waits for a record of its own.
  - **The designer marking grades.** Rejected: it would take its own findings out of its own test.
- **Point 5.** **Findings that prescribe their fix.** Rejected: only the designer renders.
- **Point 6.** **The bound in the bar.** Rejected: a count of rounds is no requirement a piece meets (COR-051 point 1).
- **Point 7.**
  - **Every lead and title the asker's, whoever drafted it.** Rejected: the designer would ask to change its own draft.
  - **A title the asker's only where the asker wrote it.** Rejected: a title the asker has read states the asker's intent as a lead does.
  - **The brief recording what the asker has seen.** Rejected: a record lost would free the designer to change the asker's words.
- **Point 9.** **A licence the conditions grant once they hold.** Rejected: nothing would record it, and it would outlast changes to what it measured.
