**In plain terms:** the check of edges that nearly line up compares only siblings, the parts one group holds directly. So it never compares a title in a header with the paragraph in the body below it, nor the titles of two cards in one row, though a reader sees whether those line up. This record widens the check to every two drawn parts of different groups on one page: texts, boxes, pictures and signs. It never compares a group there, since a group's box is only the room its component was given, which no reader sees. It names a near miss by the tests it already makes of siblings: two values that differ by more than a pixel and by less than the brand's smallest step of space nearly agree, and a pair that lines up on one line of an axis is not reported on another line of it. The near misses of one offset between the same two parts of a group, such as two cards of a row, are one finding, which names each pair of drawn parts that shows it. Where the check already reports those two parts by that offset, their finding names the pairs instead. It compares the spacings of two lists of like items on one page too.
- **Left out:** two parts on different pages, a group beside a part of another group, the gap between two parts of different groups, and the spacings of groups that are not two lists of like items.
- **A label of its own:** a finding across groups carries one, so that the reviewer can tell it apart, and a calibration can count it.
- **The reviewer** does no sums where the check measured. Where it did not measure two parts of different groups, such as at a key whose brand gives no step of space, the reviewer judges them on the image first, since no reader sums over most pairs of a page.
- **The check of boxes that overlap** is not widened: it compares siblings alone.

## Context

[visual-communication:DEC-046-faults-lower-grades] point 3 adds two checks of boxes, which compare the siblings of each scene: one reports edges that nearly line up, the other boxes that overlap. Each states its *reach*, the kinds of place it measures and those it leaves out, and a reader reads its silence as measured only at a kind its reach measures ([visual-communication:DEC-063-where-a-check-measures] points 1 and 3). Both compare siblings alone, so the reach of both leaves out two parts of different groups. There the reviewer judges on the image first, and reads the frames only where the image shows a near miss or an overlap (DEC-063 point 4). DEC-063 names the cost: a near miss too small to see goes unsummed there, in the craft the first calibration found the review weak on. It names this widening as the follow-up that makes the commonest of those places the check's (DEC-063, Rationale and Implications). DEC-046 point 3 decides what the check compares among siblings, so which pairs across groups it compares, and how it names a near miss among them, is this record's.

Three places a reader lines up across groups:
- **A margin.** A header group holds a title, and a body group holds a paragraph and a list. The header starts 40 px from the page's left edge and the body 43 px, and their right edges agree. Each part is set 0 px in, so the title's left edge lies 3 px off the paragraph's and the list's, under a brand whose smallest step of space is 4 px.
- **The matching parts of a row.** Two cards stand in a row, their tops on one line. The first sets its title 16 px down and the second 18 px. The cards are siblings and line up. Their titles are not siblings.
- **One spacing.** Two lists of like items on one page set their items 12 px and 14 px apart. Each list's own gaps are even.

A group draws nothing of its own. Its box is the room its component was given, which may reach past what is drawn in it: a header's room may start at the margin while its title is set 10 px in.

The words it uses:
- **A part** is an element a scene draws, a group among them. A group **holds** only the parts it holds directly, as in DEC-063. A part lies **below** a group that holds it, and below each group that group lies below. **Siblings** are the parts one group holds.
- **A drawn part** is a part the check compares among siblings that is no group, such as a text, a box, a picture or a sign: no line, no element of no area and no page (DEC-046 point 3).
- **Two drawn parts of different groups** are two drawn parts that different groups hold.
- **A page** is what parts are drawn on (DEC-046 point 3). A part lies **on** a page where it lies below it.
- **A box** is the rectangle a drawn part takes on the page (DEC-046, Context). A part lies **inside** another's box where its own box lies within the other's, within a pixel of rounding on each side.
- **The lines of an axis** are, across, the left edges, the centres and the right edges, and, down, the tops, the middles and the bottoms. A part's **insets** in a box it lies inside are its distances from that box's edges on the two sides of an axis.
- **The sides** of two parts are the two parts that the nearest group both lie below holds, and that the two are or lie below. They are siblings. The *first* side is the one that group holds first, in drawing order. A title in a header and a paragraph in a body have the header and the body as their sides. A title the page holds and a box in its visual group have the title and the visual group.
- **The offset** of a near miss is, on a line, the value of the pair's part at the second side less that of its part at the first. Of insets, it is the inner part's inset at the start of the axis, its left or its top, less its inset at the end.
- **A spacing** of a group is, on one axis, the gaps between its siblings that lie within a pixel of each other, each gap taken from a sibling to the nearest one after it that stands beside it, as the check takes them among siblings (the capability's list of checks).
- **A list of like items** is a group whose compared siblings are each a group, and all drawn by one component, such as the items a list's field repeats.
- **Nearly agree:** two values differ by more than a pixel of rounding and by less than the brand's smallest step of space at the scene's key (DEC-046 point 3). Two parts whose values on a line nearly agree show a **near miss** there.
- **Measured**, **a statement**, **a reader of the checks** and **a label**, the name a finding carries, are DEC-063's.

## Decision

**The check of edges that nearly line up compares every two drawn parts of different groups on one page, and the spacings of every two lists of like items there, by the tests it makes of siblings. The near misses of one offset between the same two sides are one finding, which names each pair of drawn parts that shows it, under a label of its own. Where the check reports those two sides by that offset among siblings, their finding names the pairs instead. Where the check did not measure two parts of different groups, the reviewer judges them on the image first.**

### 1. Two drawn parts of different groups on one page are compared as two siblings are

- **Which pairs.** Every two drawn parts of different groups that lie on one page.
- **Drawn parts alone.** A group is compared with no part of another group. Its box is room, which draws nothing, so an edge of it is no edge a reader sees beside a part of another group. What a group's parts show, the check compares part by part. Among siblings, DEC-046 point 3 still compares a group with its siblings.
- **Where on the page.** Each box is taken where it lies on the page: its own place, with the places of the groups it lies below added.
- **What it compares.** What DEC-046 point 3 compares of two siblings: on each axis, their three lines. Where one lies inside the other's box, it compares the inner part's insets on the two sides of each axis in place of those lines.
- **It never fails.** Each finding asks the reviewer for attention, as among siblings (DEC-046 point 3).
- **This check alone.** The check of boxes that overlap compares siblings alone, as DEC-046 point 3 decides.

### 2. A near miss across groups is named by the tests made of siblings

The check names a near miss across groups by the tests DEC-046 point 3 makes of two siblings, as that point stands and as later records refine it, and adds no test of its own. It judges nothing: whether a place it names is meant is the reviewer's.
- **The bound.** Two values nearly agree only where they differ by more than a pixel and by less than the brand's smallest step. A difference that small is no spacing the brand offers, whichever groups hold the two parts. A difference of a step or more may be one, and the check names none.
- **Weaker evidence across groups.** A part's place on the page is the sum of every placement above it, and some are worked out from what they hold, as a component centres a label in a band. So across groups a small difference is weaker evidence than among siblings that no one chose it. The check names it all the same, and the reviewer judges it on the image ("What it costs" counts them).
- **The line chosen.** A pair that lines up within a pixel on one line of an axis is not reported on another line of it. Texts of different widths on one margin differ at their right edges, and that near miss follows from the line chosen. A part inside another's box, within a pixel of one of that box's edges on an axis, lies on that edge, so its insets on that axis are not compared. Whether a part centred on another's edge has chosen a line is left to a later record. The check reads it across groups as DEC-046 point 3 reads it among siblings.
- **No test of distance, kind or depth.** A margin runs the length of a page, so a heading at its top and a note at its foot lie on one line for a reader, whatever lies between them. A text and a picture share a margin. The matching parts of two cards lie as deep below their page as each other, and a header's title and the paragraph of a body section do not.
- **What a box does not show, down the page.** A text's box holds the room of its line spacing, so its top may differ from a picture's while the letters and the picture line up. That holds among siblings too, where DEC-046 point 3 compares a text with an icon all the same. What a box does not show is the reviewer's to judge on the image.
- **The rest is the reviewer's.** A pair that passes these tests and is no fault, such as two parts of unrelated groups that lie a few pixels apart by chance, is named for the reviewer. It judges the place on the image, as it judges each place the check names (DEC-046 point 3).

### 3. One finding for each offset between two sides, naming every drawn pair that shows it

- **One finding.** The near misses of one page are one finding where they share an axis, two sides, a way of comparing, lines or insets, and an offset within a pixel. Offsets lie within a pixel where each lies within a pixel of the least of them, as gaps do that make one spacing.
- **What it names.** The two sides, between which the mend lies, and each pair of drawn parts. For each pair it names each line or inset on which the pair nearly agrees, the difference there, and the step. The title 3 px off the paragraph and the list below it is one finding. It names the header and the body, the title with the paragraph, and the title with the list.
- **No near miss goes unnamed.** Every pair of drawn parts of different groups that nearly agree is named in a finding, in one of its own or in a finding among siblings. A finding names a pair once, with the others of its offset, and never in place of them.
- **Named in the finding of two siblings.** Where the check reports the two sides on that axis, by lines or by insets as the pairs are compared, with an offset within a pixel of one of the pairs', the near miss is theirs. That finding then names the pairs of drawn parts too, and they take no finding of their own. A card set 2 px lower than the card beside it is one finding, of the two cards, naming each pair of their drawn parts that shows the 2 px.
- **A finding of two siblings that no drawn pair shows.** Where one of two siblings the check reports is a group, or both are, and no pair of drawn parts below them shows the near miss, the finding says so. The near miss then lies only between the room a group was given and a sibling. A header whose room starts 3 px left of the body's, while its title is set 10 px in, is named so.
- **A label of its own.** A finding of its own across groups carries the label `near-alignment-across`, under the check's id, which stays. A finding among siblings keeps `near-alignment`, whether or not it names pairs across groups.

### 4. The spacings of two lists of like items on one page are compared

- **Two spacings that nearly agree.** Two lists of like items on one page, neither below the other, are reported where a spacing of one and a spacing of the other, on one axis, nearly agree. The finding names the two lists and the gaps of each spacing, and carries the label of point 3.
- **Each list's spacings** are those the check takes among its siblings.
- **Like items alone.** One spacing used across a page is a line of its own where the lists are alike: a reader sees two lists of like cards set 12 px and 14 px apart as one spacing missed. Groups of unlike parts, such as a row of tags and a column of paragraphs, set their gaps for what they hold, and a reader looks for no one spacing between them.
- **Neither below the other.** Two sibling lists are compared, as are two lists in different groups. A list and a list below it are not. Their spacings set one level of grouping apart from another, which `RS-GRAPHIC-002` judges ("what is separate is set apart"), and they show no spacing missed.
- **Once.** Where one of the two lists also has a spacing within a pixel of the other's, the near miss lies among that list's own spacings. The check reports it among its siblings, and not again here.
- **Not the gap between two parts of different groups.** Such a gap is no spacing a component gave: it adds the two parts' places in their groups to the gap between those groups. The check compares those places where it compares the parts, and that gap where it compares the groups among siblings.

### 5. What it leaves out

- **Two parts that no one page holds,** on different pages, or one of them on no page. A page is drawn beside no other part (DEC-046 point 3).
- **A group, with any part but its siblings:** with a part of another group, or with a part below it (point 1). A drawn edge round a part, such as a card's box behind its title, is a drawn part, which the check compares with the title, among siblings or across groups.
- **The spacings of other groups:** a group that is no list of like items, two lists of unlike items, and a list with a list below it (point 4).
- **The gap between two parts of different groups,** and a list's spacing near another's where one of the two lists has both (point 4).
- **What DEC-046 point 3 leaves out among siblings,** for the same reasons: a line, a group that draws nothing but lines, an element of no area, a page, and the lines and insets the line chosen passes over (point 2).

### 6. Where the check did not measure, the reviewer judges parts of different groups on the image first

- **Where it measured, no sums.** At two parts of different groups that the check measured, as DEC-063 point 3 reads its silence, the reviewer does no sums over the frames for what the check measures, as among siblings (DEC-046 point 3).
- **Where it did not, the image first.** Two parts of different groups that the check did not measure, such as at a key whose brand gives no step of space, the reviewer judges on the image first. It reads the frames there only where the image shows a near miss. Siblings the check did not measure, it reads from the frames (DEC-063 point 4).
- **The statement covers every place the check measures at its key:** each pair and group of siblings, each two drawn parts of different groups on one page, and each two lists of like items there (DEC-063 point 2).
- **Reading the findings.** The reviewer judges each pair a finding names on the image, as it judges each place the check names (DEC-046 point 3). For two drawn parts, it judges whether a reader sees them on one line. For two lists, it judges whether a reader takes them for one spacing. For a finding of two siblings that says no drawn pair shows its near miss, it judges whether a reader sees any edge there.

## Rationale

**Why every two drawn parts of one page.** A reader sees the lines of a page, not its groups. A margin, a column or the top of a row runs across groups, so a part a few pixels off it is as plain beside a part of another group as beside a sibling. Groups are how components place their parts, and a reader never sees them. A narrower set, such as the parts of two sibling groups alone, would leave out pairs a reader lines up as surely: the deeper parts of two cards, or a title and a paragraph that lie at different depths. Its edge would follow the components' structure, which no reader sees.

**Why drawn parts alone.** A group draws nothing. Its box is room, which may reach past what is drawn in it, and a reader sees no edge of it. Compared with a part of another group, a group would be named for a near miss nothing drawn shows: a header's room at 40 px beside a paragraph at 43 px, while the header's title is set at 50 px. Where its parts did show a near miss, a finding at the group would name an edge no reader sees in place of those parts. A reviewer that learned to pass room edges over would then pass that one over too. Compared part by part, each finding names what a reader sees. Among siblings, a group is still compared, as DEC-046 point 3 decides, and point 3 has its finding name the drawn parts that show its near miss, or say that none does.

**Why one finding for each offset between two sides.** A title 3 px off the paragraph and the list below it is one defect. A card's content set 2 px lower than its neighbour's sets each of its parts 2 px off its match. Reported pair by pair, one offset would be a finding at each pair, each asking the reviewer to judge the same defect again. The two sides are the siblings whose places differ where the mend lies: the header's and the body's, or the two cards'. So one finding for an offset between them names the defect once, says where to look, and names every pair that shows it.

**Why the finding of two siblings names the pairs.** Where the check reports two siblings by an offset, and their drawn parts show it, a finding of its own for the parts would ask the reviewer to judge one defect twice. Where no drawn part shows it, the near miss lies at a room's edge. The finding says so, so the reviewer can pass it over without passing over one a drawn part shows.

**Why a label of its own.** The check names more places across groups than among siblings, many of them chance, and how many a reviewer finds meant is not known before a calibration. A label of its own lets the reviewer tell them from findings among siblings, and lets a calibration count them, which the trigger to revisit needs (Implications).

**Why the tests made of siblings.** Each rests on a reason that does not depend on which group holds a part. The bound rests on the brand's steps, which place parts through every group: a difference under the smallest step is no spacing the brand offers, whichever groups hold the two. Across groups that is weaker evidence that no one chose the difference, since a place there adds many placements. The check still names the place, the reviewer judges it, and "What it costs" counts it. The line chosen holds alike: two texts on one margin differ at their right edges whether or not one group holds both. Tests of their own across groups would name a near miss by something other than what DEC-046 point 3 gives its reasons for. As later records refine the tests among siblings, the check follows them across groups too, so a pair is read alike whichever groups hold it.

**Why no test of distance, kind or depth.** Each would leave out a near miss a reader sees: a margin a heading and a far note share, a text and a picture on one margin, matching parts at different depths. A test of kind down the page alone, for a text's line spacing, would leave out a text and a picture that miss each other across. What a box does not show is the same among siblings, where DEC-046 point 3 leaves it to the reviewer. What these tests would leave out that is meant, the reviewer judges on the image, at the cost of a look, as it judges every place the check names.

**Why the spacings of two lists of like items alone.** One spacing used across a page is a line of its own where the lists are alike: a reader sees two lists of like items set 12 px and 14 px apart as one spacing missed. Items one component draws look alike, so that component tells the check the lists are alike. Groups of unlike parts set their gaps for what they hold, and gaps fall in a narrow band of lengths, so two that nearly agree would mostly do so by chance. A list and a list below it set one level of grouping apart from another, which is `RS-GRAPHIC-002`'s to judge, not one spacing missed. The gap between two parts of different groups is no spacing a component gave: it adds the gap between their groups to the parts' places in them, which the check compares on their own.

**Why the image first where the check did not measure.** DEC-063 point 4 has the reviewer read from the frames what a check of boxes measures wherever it did not measure a place of a kind its reach measures. Read so once the check measures across groups, a key whose brand gives no step of space would send the reviewer to sum most pairs of a page. DEC-063 rejects that for any reader ("The reviewer sums the frames at every kind of place the checks of boxes leave out"). So two parts of different groups that the check did not measure are judged as DEC-063 point 4 judges a kind of place a reach leaves out. Widening the check takes sums from the reviewer, and asks it none.

**Why this overturns nothing.** DEC-063 point 4 sends the reviewer to the frames wherever a check of boxes did not measure a place of a kind its reach measures. Its Rationale gives the reason: siblings are the places the checks sum, and the rest are most pairs of a page, which no reader sums. So its line divides siblings from the rest, and its words "a kind its reach measures" named siblings, the only kind either check measured. This record keeps that line, now that one check measures pairs across groups too: siblings from the frames, the rest on the image first, whether or not a check measured them. Each edit below rewords DEC-046 and DEC-063 to keep their line and their reason, and no decision of theirs is undone.

They name, among others, the centre of a paragraph's box with a box's centre, a text's right edge with a paragraph's, the tops and bottoms of cards in two columns joined by ties, and a tie's sign beside a card's part. On sockets, four of the eight findings name a tie's sign. Read from the frames, most look like chance, and the reviewer judges each on the image.
- **A row of like parts repeats its finding.** Each two sides take a finding of their own. So a card whose content misses its matches in a row of n cards gives n − 1 findings, one with each other card. A row of n cards, each with a dot centred on its top edge, gives n(n − 1) across groups, two for each two cards. That comes on top of the n among siblings that DEC-046 point 3 gives, a dot's top with its card's: in a planted row of four cards, twelve and four.
- **Sums over many pairs of a page,** which the check makes alike each time. The work keeps them to the pairs whose values on a line lie within a step of each other.
- **What stays unsummed:** a near miss between parts of different groups too small to see, on an image the check did not measure, or at a key whose brand gives no step of space. So does a near miss with a group's room, which no drawn part shows, and the spacings of groups that are not two lists of like items.

### Alternatives considered

- **The parts of two sibling groups alone.** Rejected, for the reason above: it leaves out the deeper parts of two cards, and parts at different depths.
- **Only pairs whose groups line up on the line compared.** Rejected: a heading in a sidebar and one in the main column, whose groups start at different heights, would be left out, though the headings nearly share a top.
- **Only parts that stand beside each other, with nothing between.** Rejected, for the reason above: a margin runs the length of a page.
- **Parts of one kind alone,** such as texts with texts. Rejected: a text and a picture share a margin.
- **Groups compared with the parts of other groups, each near miss reported at the outermost pair that shows it.** Rejected. A group's box is room, so it names near misses no drawn part shows, and real ones at the room's edge in place of the parts that show them. Nor does it report once. A group with a part and a part with a group are not outer pairs of each other, so each can report the same miss. At the margin, the title would be reported with the body, and the header with the paragraph and with the list, while no finding named the title with the paragraph.
- **Lines of the page.** Each edge that two or more drawn parts share within a pixel is a line, and each part a pixel to a step off one is named, once for each line. Not taken. It names no near miss between two parts whose edge no third part shares, such as a lone title and paragraph. Nor does the inset of a part inside another's box lie on a line of the page. As a way to group the near misses this record names, one finding for each two lines of the page that nearly agree, it gives more findings. A part off its match at its top, middle and bottom is three lines. On the pages measured, it gives 6 findings on showcase in English, 4 in Czech and 11 on sockets in each language, where this record gives 6, 3 and 8.
- **Lines of the page and two sides joined:** near misses joined into one finding where they share two sides and an offset, or two lines of the page. Not taken. It gives the fewest findings: 5 on showcase in English, 3 in Czech and 8 on sockets in each language, where this record gives 6, 3 and 8. So on the pages measured it saves one finding of the 25 this record gives. A row of like parts that misses one line takes one finding: the dots on four cards are one, where this record gives twelve. But it joins near misses through chains of shared lines, so one finding may hold those of many sides and offsets, and names no two sides between which the mend lies. Its rule has two parts and a chain, which each audit of the check's paths reads again (DEC-063 point 1). The trigger to revisit takes in a row that repeats its finding (Implications).
- **Every pair reported, with no grouping.** Rejected: one offset would be a finding at each pair of drawn parts. On the pages measured that is 11 on showcase in English, 6 in Czech and 14 on sockets in each language, where this record gives 6, 3 and 8.
- **The finding of two siblings left as it is, the pairs in a finding of their own.** Rejected: one defect would be two findings, and the finding of two groups would still name only edges no reader sees.
- **The spacings of every two groups on one page.** Rejected, for the reason above: groups of unlike parts set their gaps for what they hold. On the pages measured, every two groups neither below the other gives no finding, nor does it with a group and one below it added. So the narrowing costs nothing measured there.
- **The spacings of two groups one component draws.** Rejected. A list's field holds its items in a group that names no component, so this compares no two lists. Only the inner gaps of two cards would be compared, and the check compares their parts' places part by part.
- **Spacings across groups left to a later record.** Not taken. Two lists of like items set 12 px and 14 px apart are a near miss a reader sees. The lines across groups catch it only where the two lists start on one line, and only at the items that have drifted less than a step: in a planted case, one item of four. Left out, it would go to the reviewer's eye, in the craft the first calibration found weak. Compared only between lists of like items, the cost is bounded, and on the pages measured it is none.
- **A check of its own across groups,** with an id and a reach of its own. Not taken. Its tests would be the same, and one measurement would take two reaches, each changed with the other. A label of its own lets the reviewer tell its findings apart and a calibration count them.
- **The parts a tie draws, such as its sign, left out across groups.** Not taken. A tie's sign lies where the tie's route puts it, and a near miss of it with a part beside it is seen like any other. The reviewer judges each such place on the image, as it judges every place the check names.
- **The reviewer reads two parts of different groups from the frames wherever the check did not measure them.** Rejected, for the reason above.
- **Widening the check of boxes that overlap in the same change.** Not taken. This record answers a near miss too small to see, which DEC-063 found unsummed across groups. An overlap is seen on the image. Where the viewing step reads what covers a text, it names a text that a part of another group covers ([visual-communication:DEC-037-text-the-page-hides] point 1). Its trigger to revisit: a calibration finds the review missing overlaps across groups.

## Implications

- **This record changes nothing a project could observe.** The work that builds it changes a check's findings and adds a label, and declares that in its changeset, in the same change. It owes no migration: no file a project keeps changes its shape, and a label added to a check asks nothing of a project's files.
- **It lands before the calibration,** as DEC-063's Implications place this widening, for economy and by no wait this record decides ([visual-communication:DEC-042-grades-bar-and-loop] point 9).
- **When to revisit.** A calibration that finds most findings labelled `near-alignment-across` judged meant reopens which pairs across groups the check compares, and how it groups them. So does a piece in which a row of like parts repeats a finding across groups, one for each two of its parts.
- **Each edit below refines its record in place, and this record overturns none** ("Why this overturns nothing"). Each is made in this change once this record is accepted, and cites it (COR-066 point 5). Accepting this record accepts them.
  - **DEC-046 point 3,** the bullet "Edges that nearly line up". After its first sentence, "A check compares the boxes of siblings: their edges, their centres and the gaps between them.", it comes to add: "It compares every two drawn parts of different groups on one page too, and the spacings of every two lists of like items there, by the same tests. A finding of two siblings, one of them a group or both, names the drawn parts below them that show its near miss, or says that none does (refinement per [visual-communication:DEC-067-edges-across-groups] points 1 to 5)."
  - **DEC-046 point 3,** the bullet "The reviewer judges each place the checks name". Its sentence "Where it did not measure a place of a kind its reach measures, the reviewer reads from the frames what that check measures." comes to read: "Where it did not measure a place among siblings of a kind its reach measures, the reviewer reads from the frames what that check measures.
