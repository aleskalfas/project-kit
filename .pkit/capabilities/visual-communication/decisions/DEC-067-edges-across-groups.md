**In plain terms:** the check of edges that nearly line up compares only siblings, the parts one group holds directly. So it never compares a title in a header with the paragraph in the body below it, nor the titles of two cards in one row, though a reader sees whether those line up. This record widens the check to every two *leaves* of different groups on one page, the parts that hold no other part, such as texts, boxes, pictures and signs, and to the spacings of two lists there whose items one component draws. It compares no group across groups, since a group's box is only the room its component was given, which no reader sees. For the same reason it changes what the check compares among siblings: a text's room, its box on the side its lines do not stand on, is no edge. So a text is compared across only on the edge or the centre it stands its lines on, unless it has one line that fills its width to within a pixel. Across groups the check names a near miss by the tests it makes of siblings: two values that differ by more than a pixel and by less than the brand's smallest step of space. The near misses of one offset between the same two parts of a group, such as two cards of a row, are one finding, under a label of its own, which names each pair of leaves that shows it. Where the check already reports those two parts by that offset, their finding names the pairs instead.
- **The reviewer** does no sums where the check measured. Where it did not measure two leaves of different groups or two such lists, such as at a key whose brand gives no step of space, it judges them on the image first, since no reader sums over most pairs of a page.
- **The check of boxes that overlap** is not widened, and reads a text's whole box.

## Context

[visual-communication:DEC-046-faults-lower-grades] point 3 adds two checks of boxes, which compare the siblings of each scene: one reports edges that nearly line up, the other boxes that overlap. Each states its *reach*, the kinds of place it measures and those it leaves out, and a reader reads its silence as measured only at a kind its reach measures ([visual-communication:DEC-063-where-a-check-measures] points 1 and 3). Both compare siblings alone, so the reach of both leaves out two parts of different groups. There the reviewer judges on the image first, and reads the frames only where the image shows a near miss or an overlap (DEC-063 point 4). DEC-063 names the cost: a near miss too small to see goes unsummed there, in the craft the first calibration found the review weak on. It names this widening as the follow-up that makes the commonest of those places the check's (DEC-063, Implications). DEC-046 point 3 decides what the check compares among siblings, so which pairs across groups it compares, and how it names a near miss among them, is this record's. So is one refinement of what it compares among siblings, a text's room (point 3), without which the margin below is passed over.

Three places a reader lines up across groups:
- **A margin.** A header group holds a title, and a body group holds a paragraph and a list. The header starts 40 px from the page's left edge and the body 43 px, and their right edges agree. Each text is set 0 px in and takes the rest of its group's width, as the starter library's components give a text the width left to it. So the title's left edge lies 3 px off the paragraph's and the list's, under a brand whose smallest step of space is 4 px. Each text's lines stop short of that width, and the three boxes' right edges agree where no text draws.
- **The matching parts of a row.** Two cards stand in a row, their tops on one line. The first sets its title 16 px down and the second 18 px. The cards are siblings and line up. Their titles are not siblings.
- **One spacing.** Two lists of like cards on one page set their items 12 px and 14 px apart. Each list's own gaps are even.

A group draws nothing of its own. Its box is the room its component was given, which may reach past what is drawn in it: a header's room may start at the margin while its title is set 10 px in. A text's box may reach past what it draws too. A text breaks its lines to the width its component gives it, and stands each line at the start, in the middle or at the end of that width (the capability's components page, "Primitives"). The engine fills each line with as much as fits in that width less 2 % of the font size (the components page, "Breaking lines"). Each line is as long as its words, so a text given a column's width reaches past its lines on the side they do not stand on, and the lines of a text of several lines end raggedly on that side.

**The pages measured.** The figures this record gives come from a script that follows its words, run on two pieces of the repository the capability is authored in: its showcase deck, of four pages, and its `sockets` figure, of one. Each was measured at each scene it draws: at its one key, under a brand whose smallest step of space is 4 px, in English and in Czech.

The words it uses:
- **A part** is an element a scene draws, a group among them. A group **holds** only the parts it holds directly, as in DEC-063. A part lies **below** a group that holds it, and below each group that group lies below. **Siblings** are the parts one group holds, and a part's siblings are the others that the group holding it holds. The parts a group holds are never called its siblings.
- **A leaf** is a part that holds no part, of those the check compares among siblings, such as a text, a box, a picture or a sign: no group, no line, no element of no area and no page (DEC-046 point 3). **Two leaves of different groups** are two leaves that different groups hold.
- **A tie** is drawn by a component that takes ends, between the two ends its pattern gives it (the capability's components page, "Ties and connectors"). Its **connector** is the line the connector tool draws for it, which the scene marks by the elements its ends meet, `attached` (the components page, "What a scene holds"). **A tie's parts** are the leaves below the group that holds its connector, such as the sign the starter library's connector stands halfway along its line.
- **A page** is what parts are drawn on (DEC-046 point 3). A part lies **on** a page where it lies below it.
- **A box** is the rectangle a drawn part takes on the page (DEC-046, Context). A group takes one too, its frame in the scene, which is the room its component was given (the components page, "What a scene holds"). A part lies **inside** another's box where its own box lies within the other's, within a pixel of rounding on each side.
- **A text's anchor** says where the text stands each of its lines in its width: at the start, in the middle or at the end (the components page, "Primitives"). A text **fills** its width where it has one line, and that line's width lies within a pixel of the text's, neither more than a pixel short of it nor more than a pixel past it. So a text of more lines than one never fills its width, whatever its widest line, and nor does a text whose line overflows it (the components page, "Breaking lines"). Where a text does not fill its width, its box on the side or sides its lines do not stand on is its **room**: there its lines end raggedly, where their words do, or its letters run past its edge.
- **The lines of an axis** are, across, the left edges, the centres and the right edges, and, down, the tops, the middles and the bottoms. A part's **insets** in a box it lies inside are its distances from that box's edges on the two sides of an axis.
- **The sides** of two parts are the two parts that the nearest group both lie below holds, and that the two are or lie below. They are siblings. The *first* side is the one that group holds first, in drawing order. A title in a header and a paragraph in a body have the header and the body as their sides. A title the page holds and a box in its visual group have the title and the visual group.
- **The offset** of a pair on a line is the value of its part at the second side less that of its part at the first, and of two siblings, the second's value less the first's. Its offset on an axis is the least in size of its offsets on the lines of that axis on which it nearly agrees, the first of them in the order above where two are as small. Of insets, it is the inner part's inset at the start of the axis, its left or its top, less its inset at the end.
- **A spacing** of a group is, on one axis, the gaps between the parts it holds that lie within a pixel of each other, each gap taken from a part to the nearest one after it that stands beside it, as the check takes them among siblings (the capability's list of checks).
- **A list** is a group whose compared parts, its *items*, are each a group, all drawn by one component, such as the items a list's field repeats. **Two lists whose items one component draws** are two lists whose items are drawn by the same component.
- **A place among siblings** is two siblings, or the spacings of the parts one group holds: the places DEC-046 point 3 compares.
- **Nearly agree:** two values differ by more than a pixel of rounding and by less than the brand's smallest step of space at the scene's key (DEC-046 point 3). Two parts whose values on a line nearly agree show a **near miss** there.
- **Measured**, **a statement**, **a reader of the checks** and **a label**, the name a finding carries, are DEC-063's.

## Decision

**The check of edges that nearly line up compares every two leaves of different groups on one page, and the spacings of every two lists there whose items one component draws, by the tests it makes of siblings. The room of a text is no edge it compares, among siblings or across groups. The near misses of one offset between the same two sides are one finding, which names each pair of leaves that shows it, under a label of its own. Where the check reports those two sides by that offset among siblings, their finding names the pairs instead. Where the check did not measure two leaves of different groups or two such lists, the reviewer judges them on the image first.**

### 1. Two leaves of different groups on one page are compared as two siblings are

- **Which pairs.** Every two leaves of different groups that lie on one page, where neither is a tie's part.
- **Leaves alone.** A group is compared with no part of another group. Its box is room, which draws nothing, so an edge of it is no edge a reader sees beside a part of another group. What a group's parts show, the check compares leaf by leaf. Among siblings, DEC-046 point 3 still compares a group with its siblings.
- **A tie's parts.** A tie's part is compared with no part of another group. Its component places it from the tie's line, so it lies where the tie's two ends put it, and no one sets it on a line of the page. A near miss of it with a part beside it follows from where those ends lie. So it is left out as lines and arrows are (DEC-046 point 3), whose ends "meet or cross what they join" (the capability's list of checks). Where that near miss is a fault, it lies in the parts the tie joins, which the check compares leaf by leaf. Among siblings, DEC-046 point 3 still compares a tie's parts with each other.
- **A tie, told by its connector.** The scene does not say which group a component that takes ends draws, so the check takes the group that holds a line the scene marks with `attached` for the tie's, and the leaves below that group for the tie's parts. Where a component holds its connector in a group of its own, below the group the component draws, the check takes that inner group for the tie's, and compares the tie's other parts, such as its sign, as leaves, with nothing to tell it so: it names their near misses as if someone had placed them, and the reviewer judges each on the image. A record that has the scene mark the group a component that takes ends draws would remove the guess (Implications). Where the scene holds no connector for a tie, as where its ends cannot be computed, the tie is reported (the components page, "Ties and connectors"), and any part drawn of it is compared as a leaf is.
- **Where on the page.** Each box is taken where it lies on the page: its own place, with the places of the groups it lies below added.
- **What it compares.** What DEC-046 point 3 compares of two siblings: on each axis, their three lines, less those of a text's room (point 3). Where one lies inside the other's box, it compares the inner part's insets on the two sides of each axis in place of those lines.
- **It never fails.** Each finding asks the reviewer for attention, as among siblings (DEC-046 point 3).
- **This check alone.** The check of boxes that overlap compares siblings alone, as DEC-046 point 3 decides.

### 2. A near miss across groups is named by the tests made of siblings

The check names a near miss across groups by the tests DEC-046 point 3 makes of two siblings, as point 3 below refines them, and adds no test of its own. It judges nothing: whether a place it names is meant is the reviewer's. A later record that refines a test among siblings says what its refinement does across groups, since a reason it gives may rest on the two parts sharing a group.
- **The bound.** Two values nearly agree only where they differ by more than a pixel and by less than the brand's smallest step. A difference that small is no spacing the brand offers, whichever groups hold the two parts. A difference of a step or more may be one, and the check names none.
- **Weaker evidence across groups.** A part's place on the page is the sum of every placement above it, and some are worked out from what they hold, as a component centres a label in a band. So across groups a small difference is weaker evidence than among siblings that no one chose it. The check names it all the same, and the reviewer judges it on the image ("What it costs" counts them).
- **The line chosen.** A pair that lines up within a pixel on one line of an axis is not reported on another line of it. Two cards of different widths on one margin differ at their right edges, and that near miss follows from the line chosen. A part inside another's box, within a pixel of one of that box's edges on an axis, lies on that edge, so its insets on that axis are not compared. Whether a part centred on another's edge has chosen a line is left to a later record, which a follow-up asks for (Implications). The check reads it across groups as DEC-046 point 3 reads it among siblings.
- **No test of distance, kind or depth.** A margin runs the length of a page, so a heading at its top and a note at its foot lie on one line for a reader, whatever lies between them. A text and a picture share a margin. The matching parts of two cards lie as deep below their page as each other, and a header's title and the paragraph of a body section do not.
- **What a box does not show, down the page.** A text's box holds the room of its line spacing, so its top may differ from a picture's while the letters and the picture line up. That holds among siblings too, where DEC-046 point 3 compares a text with an icon all the same. What a box does not show is the reviewer's to judge on the image.
- **The rest is the reviewer's.** A pair that passes these tests and is no fault, such as two parts of unrelated groups that lie a few pixels apart by chance, is named for the reviewer. It judges the place on the image, as it judges each place the check names (DEC-046 point 3).

### 3. A text's room is no edge, among siblings and across groups

- **On the line its anchor sets.** A text that does not fill its width is compared across on one line alone: its left edge where it stands its lines at the start, its centre where it stands them in the middle, and its right edge where it stands them at the end. Its other lines across lie in its room, or halfway into it, and no reader sees them.
- **Two parts, on the lines both are compared on.** Across, two parts are compared on each line both are compared on, and on none where they share none. So two texts that do not fill their widths, one standing its lines at the start and one in the middle, are not compared across.
- **The line chosen reads those lines alone.** A pair lines up on one line of an axis, for the test of point 2, only on a line both are compared on. The margin of the Context is so named: its three texts' boxes agree at their right edges, in their room, and pass nothing over, and the title's left edge is named 3 px off the paragraph's and the list's.
- **Its insets.** Where a text that does not fill its width lies inside another part's box, or another part lies inside its box, the insets across are compared only where the text stands its lines in the middle. Its room then lies alike on its two sides, so the two insets its box takes differ by as much as those its lines take. At the start or the end, one of its insets reaches into its room, and the two are not compared.
- **Its gaps.** A gap across that starts or ends at a text's room is no gap a reader sees, and the check takes it into no spacing.
- **A text that fills its width** draws to both its edges, so it is compared as any part is, on every line. A label as wide as its words, round which a tag's box is drawn, is one: its insets in the tag's box are compared. A text given no width is as wide as its widest line (the components page, "Primitives"), so one of one line fills it.
- **Only a text of one line fills its width.** The engine fills each line of a text to its width less 2 % of the font size, and stands it in the whole width (the components page, "Breaking lines"). So each line of a text of more lines than one ends where its words do, its far edge is ragged, and no reader sees an edge there, however near it its widest line happens to end. Such a text is compared across on the line its anchor sets alone. So is a text whose letters run more than a pixel past its width, as an overflowing text's do: they run past its far edge, so that edge bounds nothing drawn.
- **Down,** a text's box is as tall as its lines (the capability's components page, "Primitives"), and it is compared on every line. What its line spacing does not show is the reviewer's (point 2).
- **Among siblings too.** This refines the tests DEC-046 point 3 makes of siblings, for the reason point 1 compares no group's room: room is no edge a reader sees. A text is read alike among siblings and across groups.
- **From the scene.** The scene gives each text its anchor, its lines and the width of each (the capability's components page, "What a scene holds"), so the check reads a text's room where it reads its box. The frames give boxes alone, so where the reviewer reads a text from the frames, it tells its room from the image (point 7), less finely than the check ("What it costs").
- **This check alone.** The check of boxes that overlap reads a text's whole box, as DEC-046 point 3 decides.

### 4. One finding for each offset between two sides, naming every pair of leaves that shows it

- **One finding.** The near misses of one page are one finding where they share an axis, two sides, a way of comparing, lines or insets, and an offset within a pixel. Offsets lie within a pixel where each lies within a pixel of the least of them, as gaps do that make one spacing. A pair takes one offset on an axis, so it lies in one finding there.
- **What it names.** The two sides, since the mend lies at or below one of them, and each pair of leaves. For each pair it names each line or inset on which the pair nearly agrees, the difference there, and the step. The title 3 px off the paragraph and the list below it is one finding. It names the header and the body, the title with the paragraph, and the title with the list.
- **No near miss goes unnamed.** Every pair of leaves of different groups that nearly agree is named in a finding, in one of its own or in a finding among siblings. A finding names a pair once, with the others of its offset, and never in place of them.
- **Named in the finding of two siblings.** Where the check reports the two sides on that axis, by lines or by insets as the pairs are compared, and the difference on one of the lines or insets that finding names lies within a pixel of one of the pairs' offsets, the near miss is theirs. That finding then names those pairs of leaves too, no tie's part among them (point 1), and they take no finding of their own. A card set 2 px lower than the card beside it is one finding, of the two cards, naming its box with the other's and its title with the other's. A header 40 px from the page's left edge and 720 px wide, beside a body 43.5 px from it and 718 px wide, nearly agree across at their left edges by 3.5 px, their centres by 2.5 px and their right edges by 1.5 px. A title in the header and a paragraph in the body, each standing its lines at the start, show the 3.5 px at their left edges, so the finding of the header and the body names them.
- **A finding of two siblings that no pair of leaves shows.** Where one of two siblings the check reports is a group, or both are, and no pair of leaves that are or lie below the two, neither of them a tie's part, shows the near miss, the finding says so. The near miss then lies only between the room a group was given and a sibling. A header whose room starts 3 px left of the body's, while its title is set 10 px in, is named so.
- **A label of its own.** A finding of its own across groups carries the label `near-alignment-across`, under the check's id, which stays. A finding among siblings keeps `near-alignment`, whether or not it names pairs across groups. The label lets a reader of the checks tell the findings apart and count them. The reviewer judges each place a finding names on the image, whatever its label (point 7).

### 5. The spacings of two lists whose items one component draws are compared

- **Two spacings that nearly agree.** Two lists on one page whose items one component draws, neither below the other, are reported where a spacing of one and a spacing of the other, on one axis, nearly agree. The finding names the two lists and the gaps of each spacing, and carries the label of point 4.
- **Each list's spacings** are those the check takes among its items, less the gaps point 3 takes into none.
- **Lists of like items alone.** One spacing used across a page is a line of its own where the items are alike: a reader sees two lists of like cards set 12 px and 14 px apart as one spacing missed. Items one component draws look alike, so that component tells the check the lists are alike. Groups of unlike parts, such as a row of tags and a column of paragraphs, set their gaps for what they hold, and a reader looks for no one spacing between them. Two lists whose items different components draw, such as a list of cards and a list of tags, are unlike too.
- **Neither below the other.** Two sibling lists are compared, as are two lists in different groups. A list and a list below it are not. Their spacings set one level of grouping apart from another, which `RS-GRAPHIC-002` judges ("what is separate is set apart"), and they show no spacing missed.
- **Once.** Where one of the two lists also has a spacing within a pixel of the other's, the near miss lies among that list's own spacings. The check reports it among that list's items, and not again here.
- **Not the gap between two parts of different groups.** Such a gap is no spacing a component gave: it adds the two parts' places in their groups to the gap between those groups. The check compares those places where it compares the parts, and that gap where it compares the groups among siblings.

### 6. What it leaves out

- **Two parts that no one page holds,** on different pages, or one of them on no page. A page is drawn beside no other part (DEC-046 point 3).
- **A group, with any part that is not its sibling:** with a part of another group, or with a part below it (point 1). A drawn edge round a part, such as a card's box behind its title, is a leaf, which the check compares with the title, among siblings or across groups.
- **A tie's part, with a part of another group** (point 1).
- **A text's room,** among siblings and across groups: the centre and the far edge across of a text that does not fill its width, a text of more lines than one among them, its insets across and those of a part inside its box unless it stands its lines in the middle, and a gap across that starts or ends at its room (point 3).
- **The spacings of other groups:** a group that is no list, two lists whose items different components draw, and a list with a list below it (point 5).
- **The gap between two parts of different groups,** and a list's spacing near another's where one of the two lists has both (point 5).
- **What DEC-046 point 3 leaves out among siblings,** for the same reasons: a line, a group that draws nothing but lines, an element of no area, a page, and the lines and insets the line chosen passes over (point 2).

### 7. Where the check did not measure, the reviewer judges leaves of different groups and two lists on the image first

- **Where it measured, no sums.** At two leaves of different groups, or two lists whose items one component draws, that the check measured, as DEC-063 point 3 reads its silence, the reviewer does no sums over the frames for what the check measures, as among siblings (DEC-046 point 3).
- **Where it did not, the image first.** Two leaves of different groups, and two lists whose items one component draws, siblings or not, that the check did not measure, such as at a key whose brand gives no step of space, the reviewer judges on the image first. It reads the frames there only where the image shows a near miss. A place among siblings that the check did not measure, it reads from the frames (DEC-063 point 4).
- **A text's room, on the image.** Where the reviewer reads a text from the frames, it tells from the image where the text stands its lines, and how many lines it has. A text of more lines than one it compares across on that line alone, as the check does (point 3). A text of one line it compares on every line where the image shows its letters reaching both sides of its frame, and else on that line alone.
- **The statement covers every place the check measures at its key:** each place among siblings, each two leaves of different groups on one page, and each two lists there whose items one component draws (DEC-063 point 2).
- **Reading the findings.** The reviewer judges each pair a finding names on the image, whatever the finding's label, as it judges each place the check names (DEC-046 point 3). For two leaves, it judges whether a reader sees them on one line. For two lists, it judges whether a reader takes them for one spacing. For a finding of two siblings that says no pair of leaves shows its near miss, it judges whether a reader sees any edge there.

## Rationale

**Why every two leaves of one page.** A reader sees the lines of a page, not its groups. A margin, a column or the top of a row runs across groups, so a part a few pixels off it is as plain beside a part of another group as beside a sibling. Groups are how components place their parts, and a reader never sees them. A narrower set, such as the parts of two sibling groups alone, would leave out pairs a reader lines up as surely: the deeper parts of two cards, or a title and a paragraph that lie at different depths. Its edge would follow the components' structure, which no reader sees.

**Why leaves alone.** A group draws nothing. Its box is room, which may reach past what is drawn in it, and a reader sees no edge of it. Compared with a part of another group, a group would be named for a near miss nothing drawn shows: a header's room at 40 px beside a paragraph at 43 px, while the header's title is set at 50 px. Where its parts did show a near miss, a finding at the group would name an edge no reader sees in place of those parts. A reviewer that learned to pass room edges over would then pass that one over too. Compared leaf by leaf, each finding names what a reader sees. Among siblings, a group is still compared, as DEC-046 point 3 decides, and point 4 has its finding name the leaves that show its near miss, or say that none does.

**Why a tie's parts are compared with no part of another group.** A tie's component places its parts from its line, as the starter library's connector stands its sign halfway along it, so a tie's sign lies where the tie's two ends put it. Its near miss with a card's middle is where those ends happen to fall: no one set the sign on that line, and a finding at it would point the reviewer at a part no one placed. Where the miss is a fault, it lies in the parts the tie joins, which the check compares leaf by leaf, so leaving the sign out loses no near miss of what was placed. A tie's line is left out for the same reason, since its ends follow what it joins. On the pages measured, comparing a tie's parts would add four findings on the `sockets` figure in each language, each naming a tie's sign. The check tells a tie by its connector, since the scene marks that line, and does not say which component takes ends. That is a guess at the scene's structure, and where it misreads a tie, it compares the tie's other parts and passes nothing over, so its error costs the reviewer looks on the image, never a near miss unnamed. A tie the scene draws no connector for is reported already, so a part drawn of it is compared as a leaf, and a finding at it costs the reviewer a look on the image.

**Why a text's room is no edge.** A text's room, like a group's, is room no reader sees: the width a text was given, past where its lines stop. Compared as an edge, it errs both ways:
- **It passes over a near miss a reader sees.** The margin's three texts take the rest of their groups' widths, so their boxes agree at their right edges, and the line chosen would pass the title's 3 px at the left over. Among siblings the same room hides the same miss: a title set 3 px in beside a paragraph, both taking the rest of their group's width, agree at their boxes' right edges.
- **It names near misses at edges no one sees,** such as a text's right edge with a paragraph's.

A text's anchor says on which line it stands its lines, so every line of it reaches that line, and a reader sees it across. Its far edge, or both its edges where it stands its lines in the middle, a reader sees as an edge only where its lines end there, as the one line of a text that fills its width does. A wrapped text's lines end where their words do, each short of its width by 2 % of the font size at least, so its far edge is ragged, and above a font size of 50 px no line comes within a pixel of it. Whether its widest line happens to stop within a pixel of its width says nothing a reader sees: read so, a paragraph would be compared on one line or on three as an edit or a translation moves its words. An overflowing text's letters run past its far edge, so that edge bounds nothing drawn. The scene gives each text's anchor, its lines and their widths, so the check needs nothing more to read the room.

A text of one line given a width may fill it by chance, where its words end within a pixel of it. On the pages measured one does, the lead of one of the showcase deck's pages in English, 0.96 px short of its width, and it is in no finding. Its box's edges are then drawn, and a reader sees the end of its line there, so it is compared on every line, as any part whose edge happens to lie near another's is (point 2). The check still compares no end of a line that stops short of its box ("A text compared by the box of its lines", Alternatives).

This narrows DEC-046 point 3, and refines it in place. Its decision stands: a check compares the boxes of parts, asks for attention and never fails. Point 3 narrows which lines of a text's box count, and to tell them, the check reads what a text draws, how many lines it has and how wide each is. A line chosen is then a line a reader sees as well as one a component chose. At the margin, the components chose the three boxes' right edges, as the width left to each text, but those edges lie in the texts' room, so the line chosen does not rest on them, and the title's left edge is named. DEC-046's words that what a box does not show of what is drawn in it is the reviewer's body's narrow in effect, and take a pointer to point 3 (Implications). Where the check measured, it leaves a text's room across out, so the body need not tell the reviewer of that room there. The body tells of it where the reviewer reads a text from the frames (point 7), beside what a box does not show down the page, or of a picture or a drawing. It is decided here, for siblings and across groups alike, since across groups the room would pass over the margin this record widens the check for, and a text read one way beside a sibling and another beside a part of another group would make its findings disagree.

On the pages measured, every text stood its lines at the start. Read by its whole box, a text's room lines up with another part and passes the axis across over for 7 pairs of siblings and 98 pairs across groups on the showcase deck in English, 7 and 101 in Czech, and 2 and 11 on the `sockets` figure in each language. Compared at their left edges, none of them nearly agrees. - **The margin as the Context words it** gives one finding, naming the title with the paragraph and the title with the list. So it does with the paragraph and the list wrapped, the widest line of each within a pixel of its width.
- **Texts of one line.** With the paragraph of one line filling its width, the margin gives one finding too. With both texts of one line filling their widths, their right edges agreeing, it gives none, since those edges are drawn.
- **Texts whose widest line reaches the width.** With both texts wrapped, their widest lines within a pixel of their widths and their right edges agreeing, the margin gives one finding. - **Insets.** A title in a card, its box set 16 px in on the left and 18 px on the right, gives no finding of insets. A label that fills its tag, 8 px in on the left and 10 px on the right, still gives one, and a label that overflows its tag gives none.

**Why one finding for each offset between two sides.** A title 3 px off the paragraph and the list below it is one defect. A card's content set 2 px lower than its neighbour's sets each of its parts 2 px off its match. Reported pair by pair, one offset would be a finding at each pair, each asking the reviewer to judge the same defect again. The two sides are the siblings at or below one of which the mend lies: the header or the body, one card or the other. So a finding for an offset between them names the defect once for those two sides, says where to look, and names every pair there that shows it. A pair takes the offset of least size among its lines, so it lies in one finding, which names each of its near lines. Where more siblings share the line, a near miss is named once with each of them ("What it costs").

**Why the finding of two siblings names the pairs.** Where the check reports two siblings by an offset, and their leaves show it, a finding of its own for the leaves would ask the reviewer to judge one defect twice. Where no leaf shows it, the near miss lies at a room's edge. The finding says so, so the reviewer can pass it over without passing over one a leaf shows. The pairs are matched with each line or inset the siblings' finding names, not with the siblings' offset on the axis alone. Two siblings of different widths nearly agree on each line by a different amount, and their leaves show the amount of the line they are compared on. The header and the body differ by 1.5 px at their right edges, and their texts show the 3.5 px at their left. Matched with the siblings' offset, the texts would take a finding of their own for the 3.5 px the siblings' finding already names. In planted cases, the header and the body give one finding, and the card set 2 px lower gives one, naming its box and its title with the other card's.

**Why a label of its own.** The check names more places across groups than among siblings, and how many a reviewer judges no fault is not known before a calibration. A label of its own lets a calibration count them, which the trigger to revisit needs (Implications), and lets any reader of the checks tell them apart. It is no weight for the reviewer. A reviewer that learned to pass a label over would pass its real near misses over too, as one that learned to pass room edges over would ("Why leaves alone"). So it judges each pair on the image, whatever the label.

**Why the check names them though most look like chance.** Read from the frames, most of the near misses the pages measured look like chance. Each costs the reviewer a look on the image. Left out, a near miss across groups goes unsummed, in the craft the first calibration found the review weak on, and the reviewer meets it only by eye, where a miss too small to see is passed. A look at a chance near miss costs less than a missed one. A calibration counts what the reviewer judged of them, and reopens the rule where most are no fault (Implications).

**Why the tests made of siblings.** Each rests on a reason that does not depend on which group holds a part. The bound rests on the brand's steps, which place parts through every group: a difference under the smallest step is no spacing the brand offers, whichever groups hold the two. Across groups that is weaker evidence that no one chose the difference, since a place there adds many placements. The check still names the place, the reviewer judges it, and "What it costs" counts it. The line chosen holds alike: two cards on one margin differ at their right edges whether or not one group holds both. A text's room is no edge whichever group holds it (point 3). Tests of their own across groups would name a near miss by something other than what DEC-046 point 3 gives its reasons for. A later record that refines a test among siblings may give a reason that rests on the two parts sharing a group. So it says what its refinement does across groups, and the check does not follow it there unread.

**Why no test of distance, kind or depth.** Each would leave out a near miss a reader sees: a margin a heading and a far note share, a text and a picture on one margin, matching parts at different depths. A test of kind down the page alone, for a text's line spacing, would leave out a text and a picture that miss each other across. What a box does not show down the page is the same among siblings, where DEC-046 point 3 leaves it to the reviewer. What these tests would leave out that is meant, the reviewer judges on the image, at the cost of a look, as it judges every place the check names.

**Why the spacings of two lists whose items one component draws alone.** One spacing used across a page is a line of its own where the items are alike: a reader sees two lists of like items set 12 px and 14 px apart as one spacing missed. Items one component draws look alike, so that component tells the check the lists are alike. Groups of unlike parts set their gaps for what they hold, and gaps fall in a narrow band of lengths, so two that nearly agree would mostly do so by chance. A list and a list below it set one level of grouping apart from another, which is `RS-GRAPHIC-002`'s to judge, not one spacing missed. The gap between two parts of different groups is no spacing a component gave: it adds the gap between their groups to the parts' places in them, which the check compares on their own.

**Why the image first where the check did not measure.** DEC-063 point 4 has the reviewer read from the frames what a check of boxes measures wherever it did not measure a place of a kind its reach measures. Read so once the check measures across groups, a key whose brand gives no step of space would send the reviewer to sum most pairs of a page. DEC-063 rejects that for any reader ("The reviewer sums the frames at every kind of place the checks of boxes leave out"). So two leaves of different groups that the check did not measure are judged as DEC-063 point 4 judges a kind of place a reach leaves out. So are two lists whose items one component draws, siblings or not: comparing two lists' spacings is no place among siblings, so the reviewer sums no spacing across two lists that it did not sum before. A place among siblings stays the frames', as DEC-063 point 4 has it. This reverses what DEC-063's Rationale expected of this widening: that the places it widens to would become the check's, with the frames, by point 4's words, as the fallback where the check did not measure them. They are the check's where it measures, and the image's first where it does not, by DEC-063's own reason that they are most pairs of a page. Widening the check across groups takes sums from the reviewer, and asks it none.

**Why this overturns nothing.** DEC-063 point 4 sends the reviewer to the frames wherever a check of boxes did not measure a place of a kind its reach measures. Its Rationale gives two reasons: siblings are the places the checks sum, and the rest are most pairs of a page, which no reader sums. So its line divides places among siblings from the rest, and its words "a kind its reach measures" named places among siblings, the only kind either check measured. This record keeps that line, as one check measures more than siblings: where a check of boxes did not measure a place, the reviewer reads a place among siblings from the frames and judges the rest on the image first, and where it measured, the reviewer does no sums. The line rests on DEC-063's second reason. The first, read once the check sums across groups, would send the reviewer to the frames there too, so the edits reword it to say that siblings are places both checks sum, few enough for a reader to sum (Implications). Point 3 narrows which lines of a text's box the check compares, among siblings too, and which lines pass the others over for the line chosen, and it reads what a text draws to tell them. DEC-046 point 3's decision stands: a check compares the boxes of parts, asks for attention and never fails ("Why a text's room is no edge"). Each edit below rewords DEC-046 and DEC-063 to keep their line, and no decision of theirs is undone. One rewords a reason DEC-063 gives for its line, and two reverse what DEC-063 expected of this widening, as the Implications say of each.

### What it costs

- **More places for the reviewer to judge,** each on the image. On the pages measured (Context), the check among siblings gives one finding, a spacing on one page of the showcase deck, in each language, and point 3 changes no finding among siblings there. Across groups it gives 3 findings of their own on the showcase deck in each language, naming 4 pairs of leaves, and 4 on the `sockets` figure in each language, naming 10. No finding among siblings names a pair across groups, and no two lists' spacings nearly agree. Each of these findings compares lines, and none compares insets. They name, among others, the centre of a command's text, one line as wide as its words, with a box's centre, the tops of names and signs in two columns, the tops and bottoms of cards in two columns joined by ties, and the centre of a tag's label with a card's. Read from the frames, most look like chance, and the reviewer judges each on the image.
- **One placement repeats its finding with each side that shares its line.** Each two sides take a finding of their own. So where n siblings share a line and the leaves below one of them miss it, one placement gives n − 1 findings across groups, one with each other side. A title 3 px off a margin that a header, three sections and a footer share is four. A row of n cards, each with a dot centred on its top edge, gives n(n − 1) across groups, two for each two cards, though the dots are meant. That comes on top of the n among siblings that DEC-046 point 3 gives, a dot's top with its card's: in a planted row of four cards, twelve and four. Of n lists whose items one component draws, one set a little apart from the rest gives n − 1 findings of spacings.
- **Sums over many pairs of a page,** which the check makes alike each time. The work keeps them to the pairs whose values on a line lie within a step of each other.
- **What stays unsummed:** a near miss between parts of different groups too small to see, on an image the check did not measure, or at a key whose brand gives no step of space. So does a near miss with a group's room, which no leaf shows, a near miss of a tie's part with a part of another group, and the spacings of groups that are not two lists whose items one component draws.
- **A spacing across of texts that do not fill their widths goes unsummed too,** since their gaps start or end in their room (point 3). In a row of texts given equal widths, such as a table's row whose cells are texts, gaps uneven by less than a step are passed over, and no line catches them, since the texts' left edges lie a text's width and a gap apart. In a planted row of three texts 200 px wide, set 16 px and 19 px apart, a text compared by its whole box gives one finding ("A text compared by its whole box", Alternatives), and this record none. On the pages measured, point 3 takes 7 gaps across out of the spacings of 6 groups on the showcase deck in each language, and 3 out of 3 groups on the `sockets` figure, such as the gap between a slot's name and its text. None of them was in a finding. What point 3 leaves out of a text's room, among siblings as across groups, the reviewer judges on the image first, as a kind of place the reach leaves out (DEC-063 point 4).
- **The reviewer reads a text less finely from the frames than the check does from the scene.** The frames give boxes alone (DEC-015 point 5), so where the reviewer reads a text from them, it tells from the image whether a text of one line reaches both sides of its frame (point 7), and cannot tell it within a pixel, as the check does. So one text may be compared on one line where the check measured, and on three where the reviewer reads the frames. The reviewer falls back so only at a place among siblings the check did not measure, such as at a key whose brand gives no step of space, or on an image whose report carries no reach for the check. A follow-up has the frames carry each text's drawn extent across (Implications).

### Alternatives considered

- **The parts of two sibling groups alone.** Rejected, for the reason above: it leaves out the deeper parts of two cards, and parts at different depths.
- **Only pairs whose groups line up on the line compared.** Rejected: a heading in a sidebar and one in the main column, whose groups start at different heights, would be left out, though the headings nearly share a top.
- **Only parts that stand beside each other, with nothing between.** Rejected, for the reason above: a margin runs the length of a page.
- **Parts of one kind alone,** such as texts with texts. Rejected: a text and a picture share a margin.
- **Groups compared with the parts of other groups, each near miss reported at the outermost pair that shows it.** Rejected. A group's box is room, so it names near misses no leaf shows, and real ones at the room's edge in place of the leaves that show them. Nor does it report once. A group with a part and a part with a group are not outer pairs of each other, so each can report the same miss. At the margin, the title would be reported with the body, and the header with the paragraph and with the list, while no finding named the title with the paragraph.
- **A text compared by its whole box,** its room among its edges. Rejected, for the reason above: it passes the margin over, and names edges no reader sees.
- **A text compared by the box of its lines,** from the line its anchor sets to the far end of its widest line. Not taken. That far end lies where the text's words end, which no one sets, so it names near misses no one chose. In a text of more lines than one, the other lines end short of it, so it names a ragged edge no reader sees. Where two texts' widest lines happen to end together, it passes over a near miss on the line they stand on.
- **A text fills its width where its widest line stops within a pixel of it,** whatever its number of lines. Rejected. A text of more lines would then fill where its widest line happens to end within a pixel of its width, which no wrapped text does above a font size of 50 px. A paragraph would flip between one line compared and three as an edit or a translation moves its words. Two wrapped texts that filled so and agreed at their right edges would pass over a 3 px miss at their left, as would an overflowing text, whose widest line is wider than its width. On the pages measured it gives 6 findings on the showcase deck in English, where this record gives 3, and the 3 more each name the centre or the right edge of one text of three lines. In Czech, and on the `sockets` figure, it gives as many as this record.
- **A text's room left to a later record,** the margin counted as a cost here. Not taken: across groups it passes over the near miss this record widens the check for, and the scene already gives what the check needs to read the room.
- **A text's room left out across groups alone.** Rejected: a title set 3 px in beside a paragraph in one group, both taking the rest of its width, would still be passed over among siblings, and one text would be read two ways.
- **A gap that starts or ends at a text's room measured from the line the text stands its lines on,** so that a row of texts is measured from start to start, rather than taken into no spacing. Not taken. The check takes a spacing as the gaps between facing edges (DEC-046 point 3), and this would measure a text by another measure, the distance between two lines of the parts, as a distance between centres is. One group's spacings would then mix gaps and distances from start to start, which no one bound compares as one spacing. On the pages measured, none of the gaps point 3 takes into no spacing was in a finding, so the measure would add a reading for a case met only where planted. The row of texts given equal widths stays a cost ("What it costs").
- **Lines of the page.** Each edge that two or more leaves share within a pixel is a line, and each part a pixel to a step off one is named, once for each line. Not taken. It names no near miss between two parts whose edge no third part shares, such as a lone title and paragraph. Nor does the inset of a part inside another's box lie on a line of the page. As a way to group the near misses this record names, one finding for each two lines of the page that nearly agree, it gives more findings. A part off its match at its top, middle and bottom is three lines. On the pages measured, it gives 4 findings on the showcase deck in each language and 7 on the `sockets` figure in each language, where this record gives 3 and 4.
- **Lines of the page and two sides joined:** near misses joined into one finding where they share two sides and an offset, or two lines of the page. Not taken. It gives the fewest findings, though on the pages measured no fewer than this record: 3 on the showcase deck in each language and 4 on the `sockets` figure in each language. A placement that repeats with each side that shares its line takes one finding: the dots on four cards are one, where this record gives twelve. But it joins near misses through chains of shared lines, so one finding may hold those of many sides and offsets, and names no two sides at or below one of which the mend lies. Its rule has two parts and a chain, which each audit of the check's paths reads again (DEC-063 point 1). The trigger to revisit takes in a placement that repeats its finding (Implications).
- **Each line of a pair grouped by its own offset.** Not taken: a pair whose lines differ by different amounts, as two boxes of different widths do, would lie in two findings.
- **Every pair reported, with no grouping.** Rejected: one offset would be a finding at each pair of leaves. On the pages measured that is 4 on the showcase deck in each language and 10 on the `sockets` figure in each language, where this record gives 3 and 4.
- **The finding of two siblings left as it is, the pairs in a finding of their own.** Rejected: one defect would be two findings, and the finding of two groups would still name only edges no reader sees.
- **The pairs matched with the siblings' offset on the axis,** the least of the differences their finding names. Rejected: where two siblings differ in width, the pairs show the difference of the line they are compared on, which need not be the least. The planted header and body would give two findings, the two groups' and their texts', for one defect. On the pages measured no finding of two siblings names pairs, so the two readings agree there.
- **The spacings of every two groups on one page.** Rejected, for the reason above: groups of unlike parts set their gaps for what they hold. On the pages measured, every two groups neither below the other gives no finding, nor does it with a group and one below it added. So the narrowing costs nothing measured there.
- **The spacings of two groups one component draws.** Rejected. A list's field holds its items in a group that names no component, so this compares no two lists. Only the inner gaps of two cards would be compared, and the check compares their parts' places leaf by leaf.
- **Spacings across groups left to a later record.** Not taken. Two lists of like cards set 12 px and 14 px apart are a near miss a reader sees. The lines across groups catch it only where the two lists start on one line, and only at the items that have drifted less than a step: in a planted case, one item of four. Left out, it would go to the reviewer's eye, in the craft the first calibration found weak. Compared only between lists whose items one component draws, the cost is bounded, and on the pages measured it is none.
- **A check of its own across groups,** with an id and a reach of its own. Not taken. Its tests would be the same, and one measurement would take two reaches, each changed with the other. A label of its own lets a reader of the checks tell its findings apart and a calibration count them.
- **A tie's parts compared across groups,** each finding pointing at the tie's sign. Not taken, for the reason above: the sign lies where the tie's ends put it, so a finding at it points the reviewer at a part no one placed. On the pages measured it adds four findings on the `sockets` figure in each language.
- **The reviewer reads two parts of different groups from the frames wherever the check did not measure them.** Rejected, for the reason above.
- **Widening the check of boxes that overlap in the same change.** Not taken. This record answers a near miss too small to see, which DEC-063 found unsummed across groups. An overlap is seen on the image. Where the viewing step reads what covers a text, it names a text that a part of another group covers ([visual-communication:DEC-037-text-the-page-hides] point 1). Its trigger to revisit: a calibration finds the review missing overlaps across groups.
