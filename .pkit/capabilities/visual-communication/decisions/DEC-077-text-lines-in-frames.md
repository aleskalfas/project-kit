**In plain terms:** the frames the viewing step writes beside each image give each element's box alone. To tell whether a text fills its width, and so on which lines across to compare it, the check of edges that nearly line up reads three facts from the scene: where the text stands its lines, how many it has, and whether its component gave it its width. A reviewer reading a text from the frames tells the first two from the image, less finely, and cannot tell the third. So it compares a text of one line given a width, whose line happens to reach it, on three lines where the check compares it on one. This record has each text's frame name those three facts beside its box, in the scene's own terms, read from the one place in the scene's code that the checks read. The reviewer then reads them from the frames, as the check reads them from the scene, and applies the same rule. Where a frame does not name them, as no frame an earlier release wrote does, it tells the text's room from the image, and says in its report that it did.

## Context

- **The frames.** Beside each image lies each element's frame, in the image's pixels, with its trace id ([visual-communication:DEC-015-viewing-step-and-reviewer] point 5). The frames are the boxes of the scenes the step computes ([visual-communication:DEC-062-checks-beside-the-frames], Context), so a box is where the scene places an element, not what the tool drew.
- **A text's room.** A text breaks its lines to the width its component gives it, and stands each line at the start, in the middle or at the end of that width, by its *anchor*. Only a text of one line given no width *fills* its width, since the engine makes it as wide as that line. Any other text has *room* past its lines, on the side or sides they do not stand on, which no reader sees. So the check of edges that nearly line up compares such a text across on the line its anchor sets alone. It reads the room from what the scene gives each text: its anchor, its lines, and whether its component gave it its width ([visual-communication:DEC-067-near-alignment-between-groups] point 3). Where a text's lines stand across is worked out in one place, in the code that defines the scene, which that check and the check of contrast both read (DEC-067, Implications).
- **Where the reviewer reads a text from the frames.** At a place among siblings that a check of boxes did not measure, and at two leaves of different groups it did not measure where the image shows a near miss (DEC-067 point 7). There it tells from the image where the text stands its lines and how many it has. It compares a text of one line on every line where the image shows its letters reaching both sides of its frame, since the frames do not say whether its component gave it its width. So a text of one line given a width, whose line happens to reach it, is compared on one line where the check measured, and on three where the reviewer reads the frames (DEC-067, "What it costs").
- **Where that matters.** Wherever the reviewer reads a text from the frames, and most at two kinds of place. One is an image the checks' code stopped on, which the step still draws, and which its report says no check measured (DEC-062 point 1): there the reviewer reads every place among siblings from the frames ([visual-communication:DEC-046-faults-lower-grades] point 3). The other is a row of siblings of different sizes, whose distances from centre to centre no check measures, and which the reviewer judges (DEC-046 point 3): a text's centre lies in its room unless it fills its width or stands its lines in the middle (DEC-067 point 3). Frames an earlier release of the capability wrote give boxes alone, and this record leaves them so: there the reviewer reads a text's room from the image, and says so (point 3). At a key whose brand gives no step of space it changes little, since nothing can be near there, whatever a text's room ([visual-communication:DEC-063-where-a-check-measures] point 2).
- **The follow-up.** DEC-067 names frames that carry each text's drawn extent across, and whether its component gave it its width, read from that one place rather than derived again, as the work that refines DEC-015 point 5 (DEC-067, Implications). [project-kit#3404](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3404) is that work, and asks that each text's frame name where its lines stand across.

This record uses *anchor*, *fills*, *room* and *across* as DEC-067 defines them.

## Decision

**Each text's frame names, beside its box, where the text stands its lines across, how many lines it has, and whether its component gave it its width. The viewing step reads all three from the place in the scene's code that the checks read, and works out none of them itself. Where a text's frame names them, the reviewer reads from it what the check reads from the scene, and applies DEC-067 point 3's rule. Where it does not, the reviewer tells the text's room from the image, as DEC-067 point 7 says, and says in its report that it did.**

### 1. A text's frame names where it stands its lines, how many it has, and whether its component gave it its width

- **Beside its box.** A text's frame keeps what every element's frame gives, and adds:
  - **its anchor,** `text-anchor`, `start`, `middle` or `end`: where it stands each of its lines in its width. The key keeps the scene's own term (the capability's components page, "What a scene holds"), not a side of the image, since right-to-left text, whose start would lie at its right, is not reached ([visual-communication:DEC-020-html-page-drawing], Context);
  - **how many lines it has,** `line-count`, the number of lines the scene holds for it, zero for a text of no lines;
  - **whether its component gave it its width,** `given-width`, false where the engine made the text as wide as its widest line (the components page, "Sources").
- **The scene's, not the tool's.** Each is what the scene holds, as a box is where the scene places an element. The tool may draw a run otherwise, such as in another family (DEC-015 point 2), or drop a text, so the lines the image shows may differ from the count its frame names (DEC-015 point 3). The frames promise nothing about pixels, as the images do not (DEC-015 point 6).
- **No line's place.** A text's frame names no place or width of a line, across or down. Neither the check's reading of a text's room nor the reviewer's reads one (Alternatives).
- **Texts alone.** Every other element's frame stays its box. A text is the one part whose box holds room that the check leaves out while it compares the rest of the box (DEC-067 point 3). It compares every other box whole, or, as a group's with a part of another group, not at all (DEC-067 point 1).
- **In every frames file the step writes:** through either renderer, under a forced palette, and in each band that shows the text, in part or whole, alike in each.

### 2. They are read from the scene's one place, and worked out nowhere else

- **From the place the checks read.** The step takes each of the three from the place in the code that defines the scene where a text's lines are stood across, which the checks read (DEC-067, Implications): the anchor as that place reads it, the start where the scene holds none; the number of lines the scene holds for the text; and whether the scene names the engine as the source of its width (the components page, "Sources"). The check of edges that nearly line up reads there too whether a text fills its width, from its count of lines and the source of its width.
- **No second reading.** The step works none of them out itself. So the frames and the checks never disagree on where a text stands its lines, on how many it has, or on whether it was given its width, and a change to that place changes both at once.

### 3. The reviewer reads a text's room from the frames that name it

- **As the check does.** Where a text's frame names all three, the reviewer reads from it what DEC-067 point 3 has the check read from the scene, and applies that point's rule to them. So it reads a text alike from the frames and where the check measured. A count of zero is a text of no lines, which the reviewer reads as the check reads it, and not as a frame that names none.
- **Frames that lack them.** Where a text's frame lacks any of the three keys, as every frame an earlier release of the capability wrote does, the reviewer tells that text's room from the image, as DEC-067 point 7 says. Such frames reach it, for one, in a pipeline's drawings of a commit whose capability wrote none, which keep the frames with the images (the capability's page on outputs, "A pipeline's drawings"). The reviewer says in its report that it read that text's room from the image, since its frame named none (DEC-015 point 7).
- **Where, unchanged.** Where the reviewer reads a text from the frames stays DEC-067 point 7's and DEC-063 point 4's. This record decides how it reads a text there.

## Rationale

**Why the frames, and not the image.** The image shows where a text's letters end, not the width its component gave it, so no look at it tells whether a text of one line was given its width. Read from the image, a text whose line happens to reach its width is compared on every line, and the same text where the check measured on one. That reads one text two ways, which DEC-067 refuses for the check itself, among siblings and between groups (its Alternatives, "A text's room left out between groups alone"). The scene holds the answer, and the frames are where the reviewer reads the scene's numbers.

**Why facts, and not the rule's verdict.** The frames describe the scene, in the terms the capability's components page gives it: a text's anchor is the scene's `text-anchor`, its lines are those the scene holds, and whether its width was given is the source the scene names for that width ("What a scene holds", "Sources"). `given-width` says where the width came from, a fact in those terms, and not DEC-067's term *fills*. A key that said whether a text fills its width, or on which lines the check compares it, would carry a rule's verdict into the frames. What the checks find lies in the report, not in each image's frames (DEC-062, "Why in the report, and not in each image's frames"), and a verdict of a check's rule in the frames would make them a second channel for what the checks conclude. So the frames name facts of the scene, true whatever DEC-067 point 3 comes to say, and the reviewer applies the rule from its own body, as it applies the check's other tests ("What it costs").

**Why these three, and no more.** DEC-067 point 3's rule reads three facts of a text across: the line its anchor sets, how many lines it has, and whether its component gave it its width, the last two telling whether it fills its width. The image tells none of them surely. It shows no width a component gave. It shows no anchor where a text's one line reaches its width, the line's left edge, centre and right edge lying on its box's: that is the text DEC-067 point 3 compares on one line alone, and from the image the reviewer would guess which. And it shows the lines as drawn, which a tool that draws a run otherwise, or drops it, may show otherwise than the scene holds them. Each fact is one key, and the rule reads nothing more.

**Why the fallback says so.** A text read from the image is read less finely than one read from its frame, and nothing unseen reads as clean: the reviewer's report says what it read (DEC-015 point 7). So a reader of the review learns where a text's room was guessed from the image, and why. The fallback fires on a frame that lacks the keys, not on a count of zero. A text of no lines has a count, as the scene holds it, and read as a frame that names none, it would send a text the frames describe to the image.

**Why from the scene's one place.** DEC-067 put where a text's lines stand in one place, so that two checks could not read it two ways. A third reading, in the viewing step, could drift from theirs: a change to how the scene's code reads an anchor, or the source of a width, would move the checks' comparisons and leave the frames where they were, and the reviewer would compare a text otherwise than the check, with nothing to show it. Read from that place, the frames follow the checks by construction.

### What it costs

- **Three keys on each text's frame,** in each frames file that shows the text. Only a person or an agent reads the frames (DEC-015 point 1), and nothing commits them (DEC-015 point 4), so the cost is a reader's.
- **The reviewer's body restates the rule's test.** The frames name facts, not a verdict, so the body says how DEC-067 point 3's rule reads them. A refinement of that point then edits the body too, or the reviewer reads a text otherwise than the check.
- **Frames that give boxes alone remain** wherever an earlier release of the capability wrote them, as in a pipeline's drawings of an earlier commit. There the reviewer reads a text's room from the image, less finely, as DEC-067 names, and says so.
- **The facts are the scene's, not the tool's.** The image may show a text's lines otherwise than its frame counts them (point 1). The reviewer reads the text's room from its frame, as the check reads it, and judges a shared line on the image, by what is drawn, as it judges a box.

### Alternatives considered

- **A key that says whether the text fills its width,** or on which lines the check compares it, in place of the facts. Rejected, for the reason above: it carries a rule's verdict into the frames, which describe the scene.
- **Each line's place and width across,** beside the count or in its place. Rejected. Nothing reads them: the check compares a text on the line its anchor sets, an edge or the centre of its box, and tells whether it fills its width from its count of lines and the source of its width, and the reviewer reads the same. They invite comparing where a text's lines end, its ragged ends, which DEC-067 rejects as an edge (its Alternatives, "A text compared by the box of its lines"). And they cost an entry for each line in every frames file that shows the text. An overflowing text needs them no more: the scene marks it, and a check names it (the components page, "What a scene holds"; the capability's list of checks). A line's place down, its top or its baseline, nothing reads either.
- **One extent of the text's lines,** from where they start to the far end of the widest, as DEC-067's follow-up names it. Not taken. It gives no count of lines, which the rule reads, and is the box of the text's lines, which DEC-067 does not compare (its Alternatives, "A text compared by the box of its lines").
- **The count and the source of the width without the anchor.** Rejected, for the reason above: a text of one line given a width, whose line reaches it, shows no anchor in the image.
- **The step reads the three from the scene's text itself.** Rejected, for the reason above: a second reading, which could drift from the checks'.
- **The scene's texts handed to the reviewer beside the frames.** Rejected. The reviewer would match each text of a file in px of the page to its frame by its trace id, and read a file that holds far more than it reads, beside the frames that already name each element it reads.
