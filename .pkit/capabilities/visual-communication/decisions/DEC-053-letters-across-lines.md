**In plain terms:** where a text's lines lie so close that a letter of one line touches a letter of the next, such as the tail of a g and the accent of the á below it, the check names the text and asks for attention from the owner of the brand version that sets the line step, since a wider step there mends it. Letters touch where their shapes meet, as the engine sets them, with no distance added. The check finds them from the shapes of the letters the engine sets, never from the font's line height, which would name texts whose letters lie well apart. It reads no screen, so it names the case the engine settles: where letters meet or part by less than about one to two device pixels, a browser may draw them otherwise, and across that band the check is silent on what a screen shows. A brand may mark a type role as set tight on purpose. Where a released version of the brand both says so and sets the role's line height, validation states the letters that meet as a fact, which asks no one to act. The review is not told of the mark, and takes none it reads in a brand as a ruling on whether the text can be read. Where the review asks the designer's round to mend such a text, a step that a released brand sets becomes a question to the person, and the designer may widen one that the project's own brand, not yet released, sets, telling the person that every piece under that version changes. The engine keeps the line step the brand gives. For example, a Czech heading set at a step of 1.0 em, a g over an á of the line below, is named, and the same heading at the 1.2 em the neutral brand gave it, as probed, is not.

## Context

[project-kit#3232](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3232) asks what holds where a text's line step lets the letters of one line touch those of the next. The record of forced colours names this a known gap ([visual-communication:DEC-039-forced-colours-grounds-and-edges] point 3). The letters touch in the piece's own colours. The step that sets the lines is the brand's spacing, so no renderer mends them, and a wider line step does. The look report names letters where a part drawn after them, the next line among them, hides more than a sliver of their strong ink, or takes an island of their ink, and letters that only touch lose little or none of it to the next line (refinement per [visual-communication:DEC-054-html-reads-islands] point 1). Three answers were open: a check that reports such a text, an engine that sets no step below the letters' height, or the gap left unnamed, with its reason.

The answer meets three things that already hold:
- **Attention or a fact.** A finding that never fails asks for attention when someone is expected to act, and names who, and states a fact when no act is expected. A reading of taste asks for attention wherever the judgement it calls for is still owed. Only a record lets a file give that judgement, and it says whose file may and in which reading of the finding, as a released version's word that an overlap is meant gives it in validation alone ([visual-communication:DEC-002-files-and-validation] point 7, [visual-communication:DEC-066-meant-overlaps] point 2).
- **The designer's one round.** The designer mends each failure of a check before any review. In its one round it mends what the review reports among the kinds [visual-communication:DEC-070-ship-call-and-one-round] point 3 names, and the piece then goes to the person, with the review's report and what the designer changed and why (DEC-070 point 2). It writes a version of the project's own library not yet released, and never a released version nor a copy of a library the capability ships. It asks the person where a fix needs a write it may not make ([visual-communication:DEC-030-design-agents] point 5, DEC-070 point 4).
- **A check's reach.** A scene check that states a reach names the kinds of place it measures and those it leaves out, and states as a fact each place of a kind it measures where it could measure nothing ([visual-communication:DEC-063-where-a-check-measures] points 1 and 2).

Terms:
- A text's *line step* is how far apart its lines' baselines lie. The engine gives a text one step, the greatest line height among the typography roles its runs take, each a ratio the brand gives of its font size ([visual-communication:DEC-012-text-runs] point 4). The brand gives the ratio, and the engine takes any number. The step *comes from* the role whose line height it is.
- A role's *token* is the one that gives the role its value at a key, as the engine resolves the brand: once the brand's sources are merged over its base's tokens and the roles' defaults, the token that gives the role a value, or, where none does, the token the role's default leads to ([visual-communication:DEC-004-brands-and-notations] points 6 and 7).
- The file and the field that *give* a step are the file that holds the number the role's line height takes, reached from the role's token through each alias its value or its `lineHeight` takes, a base's tokens included, and that number's place there: the `lineHeight` of a typography token, or the value of a number token. The version whose file it is gives the step too.
- Two lines' *reach* is the step below which their letters overlap: at the one point along the line where it is greatest, how far the upper line's letters fall below its baseline, added to how far the lower line's rise above its own. Each letter is read at its own run's size, and the reach is given in the em of the size of the role the step comes from.
- A face's *ascent and descent* are as the engine reads them, from the font's horizontal header. Its *typographic* ascent and descent are those its OS/2 table gives.

## Decision

**The check names a text where the shape of a letter of one line meets the shape of a letter of the next, as the engine sets them, and asks for attention from the owner of the brand version that gives the step. Where that version is released and marks the role the step comes from as set tight on purpose, validation states it as a fact. The engine keeps the line step the brand gives.**

### 1. Letters touch where their shapes meet across a text's lines

- **Which lines.** Each line of a text and the next one, as the engine sets them, across its paragraphs. Lines of two texts are not compared: the layout sets their distance, not the step.
- **What a line draws.** Its letters: the glyphs the engine shapes for each run's text, each from the face that shaped it and at its run's size, where the engine places it along the line and on the line's baseline. A run's sign counts with them, where the scene places it ([visual-communication:DEC-036-concept-signs] point 3).
- **Characters drawn to join.** The characters a font draws to join the next line's do not count: Unicode's Box Drawing, U+2500 to U+257F, its Block Elements, U+2580 to U+259F, and the pieces of a tall bracket or integral, U+2320, U+2321 and U+239B to U+23B3. The capability's page of checks lists the set. It leaves out Unicode's Symbols for Legacy Computing, U+1FB00 on, which holds mosaics drawn to join beside characters that are not, such as its segmented digits. Every other character counts as a letter, theirs among them.
- **When they touch.** Two lines' letters touch where the outline of a letter of one line and the outline of a letter of the next share a point, the lower line set the text's step below the upper. So two lines' letters touch at any step at or below their reach, and at none above it.
- **No distance.** Letters that come near without meeting do not touch. That no distance is added is a choice, which no measure sets (Rationale).
- **To a tolerance.** The check reads whether the outlines share a point with each curve flattened into straight segments, none further than 0.001 em of its letter's size from the curve. So letters that meet or part by less than about 0.002 em may be read either way.
- **Measured from the shapes.** The check reads each letter's outline from the face that shaped it: the family of its run's role that measures the character, a later family where the first lacks it ([visual-communication:DEC-004-brands-and-notations] point 9). It scales each letter to its own run's size. It reads a sign's shape from its drawing, its icon or its shape fitted to its square (DEC-036 point 1). Neither the face's ascent and descent nor a letter's box stands for the shape.

### 2. The finding asks the brand's owner for attention

- **Its level.** It asks for attention and never fails. Letters that meet may be what a brand means, such as a display type set tight on purpose, so no record makes their parting an invariant ([visual-communication:DEC-002-files-and-validation] point 7). Where the version that gives the step is released and says it means them, validation states them as a fact instead (point 4).
- **Who acts.** It names the owner of the version that gives the step (Context), since the step is that version's: the project, for a version of its own not yet released; a released version's owner; and, for a copy of a brand the capability ships, whoever maintains the capability ([visual-communication:DEC-003-change-over-time] point 9). In a brand built on another, that is its base's owner where the base's file gives the number.
- **Where it lies.** At the piece, by the text's trace id, naming no library version, since the step comes from the brand under the piece's lock and the letters from the piece's content. The brand's version that the finding carries for point 4 settles only how validation reads it, never where it lies.
- **What it says.** The two lines, the letters that meet and the step, with the typography role it comes from, the file and the field that give it, and their reach, the step above which they part. Its fix: a wider line step for the role, which that field gives. Where the brand means the setting, its owner may say so instead (point 4).
- **When it is found.** With the scene's other findings, in each language and at each key over the size axes, and kept in the findings record ([visual-communication:DEC-005-engine-and-plugins] point 13). Its label is `letters-touch`, and `letters-touch-meant` for point 4's fact.
- **In the designer's round.** It fails nothing, so the designer does not mend it before the review ([visual-communication:DEC-070-ship-call-and-one-round] point 2). It reaches the review and the person in the viewing step's report, as every scene check's finding does ([visual-communication:DEC-062-checks-beside-the-frames] point 1). The round mends it only where the review reports it as one of the kinds DEC-070 point 3 names, such as a thing a reader cannot make out. Its fix lies in the version that gives the step, so the round mends it by who may write that version (DEC-070 point 4, DEC-030 points 4 and 5):
  - **A version the designer may not write,** a released version among them: the finding becomes a question to the person, naming the version's owner and the file and the field that give the step, as a fix that needs a write the designer may not make does.
  - **A version of the project's own brand not yet released:** the designer may widen the role's step there, but only by telling the person, with what it changed, that every piece whose lock names that version changes with it, since a brand's step reaches each of them and not the piece at hand alone.

### 3. The engine keeps the brand's line step

The engine widens no line step for the letters it sets, and breaks no line elsewhere to part them. A step at which letters touch stays as the brand gives it, and the check names the text.

### 4. A brand may mark a role as set tight on purpose

- **The word.** A brand marks a typography role whose lines it sets tight on purpose with `tight-lines: true` in the capability's extension key on the role's token, `"$extensions": {"pkit.visual-communication": {"tight-lines": true}}`. It says that letters that meet across the lines of a text whose step comes from that role are meant. This record calls it the role's *word*.
- **On the role's token.** The word lies on the token of the role the step comes from (Context), as the engine resolves the brand at the scene's key. A brand built on another writes it only on a role its base opens to it, as it gives any value (DEC-004 point 6).
- **In the version that gives the step.** The word counts only where the role's line height comes from the version whose file holds the word: where the file that gives the step (Context) lies in that version. That holds however the role's token reaches the number, through a chain of aliases or through a role a base opens. So a word on a role whose `lineHeight` aliases a token another version gives, such as a base's, counts for nothing, and nor does a base's word on a role whose number a brand built on it gives, through a token the base opens. Where more than one role gives a text its step, each must carry the word, and their line heights come from the one version that holds it. Where the word does not count, it gives the check nothing, as where no word lies.
- **A value given drops the base's word.** A brand that gives a role its base opens a value writes its own token for the role, which takes the base's place whole, its `$extensions` with it, as the engine merges every token (DEC-004 point 6). So the base's word no longer lies on the role's token, and the word lies there only where the brand writes it on its own token.
- **Released, and still as it was released.** The word gives the fact only where the brand's version whose file holds it is released and still as it was released, as an overlap's word does ([visual-communication:DEC-066-meant-overlaps] point 2). For a copy of a brand the capability ships, the list's hash must also be the one the capability records for that version ([visual-communication:DEC-013-shipped-library-releases] point 5). Anywhere else, as in a brand of the project's own not yet released, the word gives the check nothing: the finding asks for attention exactly as where no word lies.
- **Read in validation alone.** Where the word counts, the finding carries the version that holds it, and no mark of release, in the field of a finding that DEC-066's work adds to a piece's findings record for the version that holds an overlap's word (DEC-066, Implications). Validation reads it each time it reads the finding, as it reads an overlap's (DEC-066 point 2): `letters-touch-meant`, a fact, where that version is released and still as it was released, and `letters-touch` elsewhere. The fact's message says what `letters-touch` says, and that the role's token carries `tight-lines`.
- **Measured, with a finding.** The check measured the two lines, so `letters-touch-meant` is a finding of what it measured, and no statement that it could measure nothing ([visual-communication:DEC-063-where-a-check-measures] point 2). Its reach gains no clause, and the two lines read as measured, with a finding, in validation and in the report alike (DEC-063 point 3).
- **The report gives `letters-touch`.** The viewing step's report gives every such finding as `letters-touch`, with nothing of the word in its message. So the review reads each text whose letters meet as where no word lies, and judges on the image whether a reader can make it out (DEC-070 point 3).
- **The reviewer takes no word as a ruling.** It judges on the image alone whether a reader can make out a text whose letters a `letters-touch` names, and a `tight-lines` it reads on any brand's token, released or not, is no ruling on that. It reads the brand the piece names ([visual-communication:DEC-015-viewing-step-and-reviewer] point 7), and its tools can read the repository (DEC-070 point 5), so it may meet the word there. Its body holds this line (Implications), as it holds the one DEC-066 point 2 gives it for a part's word, and this record rests on the reviewer keeping it (What it costs).
- **The scene holds nothing of it.** The check reads the word from the brand as the engine resolves it, beside the scene, where it reads a role's value, since the scene keeps values as roles (DEC-005 point 5). So no renderer and no plugin meets it, and the seam's version stays as it is (DEC-005 point 8).
- **A fact, since it asks no act.** The finding asks the owner of the version that gives the step to choose the role's step. That version's word, once released, makes that choice, fixed for that version, so in validation no one is expected to act (DEC-002 point 7).
- **What it adds to format 1.** A key in the capability's extension on a typography token, where format 1 refuses that extension on any token but a `fontFamily`. On a typography token, the extension holds `tight-lines: true` alone: any other key or value there, and `tight-lines` on a token of another type, is refused as `tokens-shape`, as format 1 refuses each of them already. The key is an addition, so every file format 1 accepted reads as before, and the format number stays 1 (DEC-003 points 1 and 3). The findings record gains no field (above). A brand's version that writes the word is released only on a capability that reads it, since its release reads it in full first and names that capability's version as the release it needs ([visual-communication:DEC-014-own-library-needed-release] point 1).
- **Not on a group.** The word speaks for one role's step, so `tight-lines` in a group's `$extensions` is refused: it says nothing, and the check asks for attention from the owner of the version whose file holds it, as `tight-lines-refused`, naming the role's token as where the word lies. It fails nothing, since format 1 fails nothing in a group's `$extensions` but a picture role's, and no value it accepts comes to fail while its number stays (DEC-003 point 3). On a picture role's group, format 1 refuses it already, as it refuses any key there but the file's ([visual-communication:DEC-025-picture-files] point 4).

### 5. Its reach, and what it leaves unnamed

The check states its reach beside its id, `letters-across-lines`, in its entry on the capability's page of checks and, in the same words, in its code (DEC-063 point 1).
- **It measures:** each two lines that follow each other in a text a page draws, across its paragraphs, in each language and at each key over the size axes, for letters and runs' signs that meet at the text's step, each read as point 1 reads it.
- **It leaves out:** two lines of two texts, whose distance the layout sets (point 1); the characters drawn to join (point 1); and the lines of a renderer that wraps text itself, as a medium may by a declared choice (DEC-005 point 4), since the check reads the lines the engine sets.
- **Where it can measure nothing, a fact.** Where the engine has no outline for a letter, since a font profile measures it from a table of widths or by an estimate (DEC-004 point 9), or its face draws it from a bitmap or an SVG of its own, the check states, at the text, that it measured nothing at each pair of its lines that holds such a letter, and why (DEC-063 point 2).
- **No screen.** It reads no renderer's pixels, so across a band of about one to two device pixels, where a browser may draw letters otherwise than the engine sets them, it is silent on what a screen shows (Rationale, "Where a renderer draws the letters").
- **What nothing names.** Nothing names where the letters of two texts set close touch, nor letters the engine sets apart and a browser draws together, nor a letter that meets a character drawn to join the next line's.

## Rationale

**Why a check, not the engine.** The step is the brand's typography, and a brand gives each role its value ([visual-communication:DEC-004-brands-and-notations]). An engine that widened a step below the face's ascent and descent would have widened the neutral brand's 1.1667 and 1.2 em as probed, where no Latin letter, Czech's among them, came within 2.4 px of the next line. One that widened a step below the reach of the letters each text sets would make the step follow each text's words, so two texts of one role would lie unevenly, and a type set tight on purpose would lose its step. Either way every scene of a brand set tight would change, where a finding changes none and lets a person choose.

**Why not leave the gap unnamed.** On these faces no Latin letter with Czech's reached further than 1.104 em, so the neutral brand as probed parted every such letter, and on them only a step below 1.104 em lets such letters touch. The engine takes such a step, and nothing names what it does: at 1.0 em the Czech heading's letters touched in 2.2 % of its pairs of lines, an acute under a g. A check costs one measure of each pair of a text's lines, and the outlines need reading only where the letters' boxes meet. At the neutral brand's steps as probed none did.

**Why from the letters' shapes.** The face's measures miss both ways. On these faces, the ascent and descent lie 0.15 em above any Latin letter's reach, so a check by them names each title, and each heading at a desktop, that the neutral brand at the version probed set on more than one line. The typographic ascent and descent lie below the reach of Czech letters, so a check by them misses letters that touch between 1.0 and 1.104 em. A letter's box over-reaches across the line: it named ten times the pairs that touch. The shapes are the letters themselves. A letter that a later family shapes is drawn from that family's face, and a run of another size at that size, so each is read so.

**Why letters touch where their shapes meet, with no distance.** Meeting is a relation of the shapes alone, so it holds at every size. A distance in px of the page names other letters on each screen. An HTML page shrinks with a narrow window ([visual-communication:DEC-020-html-page-drawing] point 4), so a px of the page often takes less than one device pixel, and at the device scales the capability's records probed, from 0.625 to 2, one device pixel is 1.6 to 0.5 px of the page. A fixed px is also a larger share of a small text's em than of a large one's. A distance in em would hold at every scale, but nothing measured sets its size. Letters less than a device pixel apart share a smoothed pixel at some places of the pixel grid and not at others, and the fainter of the two covers at most 29 % of it. No one judged whether such letters read as one shape (Context). So the check names letters that meet as the engine sets them, and adds no margin: the zero is a choice, made since no evidence sets a margin. At the neutral brand's steps as probed, no Latin letter with Czech's met the next line's: the nearest lay 2.4 px apart.

**Why a tolerance of 0.001 em.** The check compares straight segments, so it flattens each curve, and the tolerance bounds how far that moves a shape. At 0.001 em it moves a shape by less than one unit of a face drawn on 1 000 units to the em, and by less than 0.05 px at 48 px, the largest size probed: far inside the band of about one to two device pixels across which the check is silent on a screen (below).

**Where a renderer draws the letters.** The check measures each letter where the engine places it, and reads no renderer's pixels. Each renderer that draws the engine's lines draws each line at the engine's baseline, its letters at or near the engine's places along it. The PDF renderer places each glyph at the advances the engine shaped. A browser held each line's baseline within 0.024 px of the engine's by its layout, and each line's width within 0.41 px. It drew each line's ink within 0.49 device pixels of that baseline at a device scale of 1, within 1.37 at 1.225, and within 1.44 in a 375 px window (DEC-020, Context). It places each line alone (DEC-020 point 2), so one line's letters may come nearer the next line's, or move further from them, by about one to two device pixels, and by more where two lines move opposite ways, which no probe measured. Letters less than a device pixel apart may also share a smoothed pixel (Context). So the check names the case the engine settles, letters that meet as it sets them, and across that band it is silent on what a screen shows: a browser may draw together letters the engine sets apart by less, which the check leaves unnamed, and draw apart letters that meet by less, which it names all the same. At the neutral brand's steps as probed, the nearest Latin letters with Czech's, a g over a Ů 2.4 px of the page apart in a 24 px heading, lay 1.5 device pixels apart at a scale of 0.625, within that band, and 4.8 at 2. The check needs no browser for the case it names. A renderer that wraps text itself draws lines of its own, which the check does not read, so its reach leaves them out (point 5).

**Why characters drawn to join are left out.** A font draws them to meet the next line's, so a tree of folders meets at any step below 1.4 em in Source Code Pro, as it is meant to. The set is the blocks and pieces Unicode gives for shapes built across lines, so it names the same characters in every font. The probe measured box drawing alone. Symbols for Legacy Computing hold such mosaics too, but beside segmented digits and other signs drawn within their own line, so the block whole would leave those unmeasured. Read as letters, its mosaics that meet across lines are named, and the finding fails nothing.

**Why a run's sign counts.** It is drawn on the line in the run's paint, and a letter it meets reads as badly as one another letter meets.

**Why it asks for attention, and names the owner of the version that gives the step.** A tight display type with letters that meet may be what a brand means, and a released brand's step is no file the project corrects. So the finding never fails. The step is the brand's, given by one version's file, so the act the finding asks, a wider step or the word of point 4, is that version's owner's, and the finding names that owner. Only that owner can take it: a brand built on a base cannot change a number its base keeps, and a base's owner cannot change one a brand built on it gives. The file and the field the finding names are where the act lies. The designer mends only failures before the review, and in its round only what the review reports among the kinds DEC-070 point 3 names, so the finding waits for the review and the person.

**Why the round asks where the designer may not write, and tells where it may.** The fix lies in the version that gives the step, and the designer writes only a version of the project's own library not yet released (DEC-030 point 5). A fix that needs a write it may not make is a question to the person (DEC-070 point 4), so a step a released version gives goes to the person, who may ask its owner for a version with a wider step. Where the designer may write the version, a wider step is a mend at the place the finding's fix belongs (DEC-070 point 3), but it reaches every piece whose lock names that version, beyond the piece the round mends. The person makes the ship call on each of them (DEC-070 point 1), so the person learns of it from what the designer changed.

**Why a brand may mark the setting meant.** Without a word, a brand that sets a role tight on purpose is asked for attention in every piece that sets such a text, for ever, a question its choice has answered.
