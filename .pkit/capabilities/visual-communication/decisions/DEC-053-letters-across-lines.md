**In plain terms:** where a text's lines lie so close that a letter of one line touches a letter of the next, such as the tail of a g and the accent of the á below it, the check names the text and asks the brand's owner for attention, since a wider line step in the brand mends it. Letters touch where their shapes meet, as the engine sets them, with no distance added. The check finds them from the shapes of the letters the engine sets, never from the font's line height, which would name texts whose letters lie well apart. It reads no screen, so it names the case the engine settles: where letters meet or part by less than about one to two device pixels, a browser may draw them otherwise, and across that band the check is silent on what a screen shows. A brand may mark a type role as set tight on purpose, and where a released version of the brand says so, validation states the letters that meet as a fact, which asks no one to act. The engine keeps the line step the brand gives. For example, a Czech heading set at a step of 1.0 em, a g over an á of the line below, is named, and the same heading at the 1.2 em the neutral brand gave it, as probed, is not.

## Context

[project-kit#3232](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3232) asks what holds where a text's line step lets the letters of one line touch those of the next. The record of forced colours names this a known gap ([visual-communication:DEC-039-forced-colours-grounds-and-edges] point 3). The letters touch in the piece's own colours. The step that sets the lines is the brand's spacing, so no renderer mends them, and a wider line step does. The look report names letters only where they lose strong ink to a part drawn after them, which letters of one colour on one ground do not. Three answers were open: a check that reports such a text, an engine that sets no step below the letters' height, or the gap left unnamed, with its reason.

The answer meets three things that already hold:
- **Attention or a fact.** A finding that never fails asks for attention when someone is expected to act, and names who, and states a fact when no act is expected. A reading of taste asks for attention wherever the judgement it calls for is still owed. Only a record lets a file give that judgement, and it says whose file may and in which reading of the finding, as a released version's word that an overlap is meant gives it in validation alone ([visual-communication:DEC-002-files-and-validation] point 7, [visual-communication:DEC-066-meant-overlaps] point 2).
- **The designer's one round.** The designer mends each failure of a check before any review. In its one round it mends what the review reports among the kinds [visual-communication:DEC-070-ship-call-and-one-round] point 3 names, and the piece then goes to the person, with the review's report (DEC-070 point 2).
- **A check's reach.** A scene check that states a reach names the kinds of place it measures and those it leaves out, and states as a fact each place of a kind it measures where it could measure nothing ([visual-communication:DEC-063-where-a-check-measures] points 1 and 2).

Terms:
- A text's *line step* is how far apart its lines' baselines lie. The engine gives a text one step, the greatest line height among the typography roles its runs take, each a ratio the brand gives of its font size ([visual-communication:DEC-012-text-runs] point 4). The brand gives the ratio, and the engine takes any number. The step *comes from* the role whose line height it is.
- Two lines' *reach* is the step below which their letters overlap: at the one point along the line where it is greatest, how far the upper line's letters fall below its baseline, added to how far the lower line's rise above its own. Each letter is read at its own run's size, and the reach is given in the em of the size of the role the step comes from.
- A face's *ascent and descent* are as the engine reads them, from the font's horizontal header. Its *typographic* ascent and descent are those its OS/2 table gives.

### What the probe showed

The probe computed the scenes of the showcase deck of the repository the capability is authored in, through the engine's own code, in its two languages, English and Czech, at the projector size the deck names and at the desktop size. It drew each line of each text alone, from the face its run names, shaped as the engine shapes it and placed where the engine places it, at 240 pixels to the em, a pixel counting as ink where it is half covered. It measured each pair of lines' reach: 78 pairs of lines that follow each other in a text, and 6 168 pairs of any two lines that one face sets at one font size, in one language and one scene, as if one text held them one above the other. The deck's brand, the neutral brand at the version probed, set three faces, Source Sans Pro in a regular and a semibold file and Source Code Pro, at steps from 1.1667 em, the deck's title on a projector, and 1.2 em, a heading at a desktop, to 1.5 em, code at a desktop. The probe's code is thrown away.

- **The deck's own pairs.** At each text's own step no letters touched. The closest pair lay 0.331 em apart, 7.3 px, in code at 22 px. The greatest reach was 0.967 em, a g over a č. Below the brand's steps, 2 of the 78 pairs touched at 0.95 em and 10 at 0.9 em, where the most two lines' letters overlapped was 0.0038 em².
- **Any two lines.** The greatest reach was 0.95 em in English, a g over an f, and 1.017 em in Czech, a g over an á in the semibold. At 1.05 em no pair touched. At 1.0 em 7 did, 0.1 %, all Czech, each an acute under a g, a j or a y: 2.2 % of a 24 px heading's pairs at a desktop. At 0.95 em 2.9 % did, and at 0.9 em 15.6 %. At its role's own step, the closest pair lay 4.7 px apart, a g over an í in a 24 px heading at 1.2 em.
- **Any Latin letter with Czech's.** Set alone, a g over a Ů reached 1.099 em in both Source Sans Pro files and 1.104 em in Source Code Pro. At the neutral brand's tightest steps as probed, such letters lay 2.4 px apart in a 24 px heading at 1.2 em, and 3.3 px in a 48 px title at 1.1667 em. In the two Source Sans Pro files, the g and the ring overlapped by 0.005 to 0.006 em² at 1.05 em, and by 0.005 to 0.010 em² at 1.0 em.
- **The faces' measures.** In each of the three faces, the ascent and descent add up to 1.257 em, and the typographic ascent and descent to 1.0 em. The first lies 0.15 em above the greatest reach of any Latin letter with Czech's on these faces, so steps between them, the neutral brand's 1.1667 and 1.2 em as probed among them, part every such letter. The second lies 0.1 em below it, so such letters touch at steps above it.
- **The letters' boxes.** Measured by each letter's box, the bounds of its outline, the pairs whose boxes meet across lines at 1.0 em were ten times those whose letters touch: 1.1 % of any two lines against 0.1 %. Measured by each line's deepest foot and highest top, wherever they lie along it, they were 10 %. At each pair's own step no boxes met, the nearest 4.3 px apart.
- **Within a pixel.** A second probe took the furthest-reaching pair, the g over the á in the semibold, and read their outlines exactly: their shapes met at 1.018 em. It set the two lines at exact distances apart, each drawn alone, and moved the device's pixels under them by eighths of a pixel down and quarters across, 32 places, at device scales of 0.625, 1, 1.225 and 2. Letters less than one device pixel apart shared a smoothed pixel at some places and not at others: at 0.1 device px apart at 24 to 28 of the 32, at 0.5 at 10 to 14, and at 0.75 at 1 to 5. From one device pixel apart they shared none. Where they shared one, the fainter of the two letters covered at most 29 % of it at 0.1 device px apart, 10 % at 0.5 and 2 % at 0.75. One device pixel is 1.6 px of the page at a scale of 0.625, and 0.5 px at 2. No one judged whether letters that share such a pixel read as one shape.
- **Characters drawn to join.** Source Code Pro's box drawing reaches 1.4 em, so the lines of the deck's tree of folders meet at their own step of 1.2727 em on a projector, as the font draws them to: 10 of the 78 pairs. Their letters lay apart.
- **A run's sign.** A sign came no nearer a letter of the other line than letters did: its greatest reach was 0.967 em, a sign under a y.

**Not reached:** another brand's faces; a language whose marks rise higher, such as Vietnamese, whose marks stack; another script, such as Greek or Cyrillic; characters other than letters and digits, such as punctuation, brackets and arrows; a browser's drawing of letters that meet or nearly meet across lines; a device scale below 0.625 or above 2; whether letters that share a smoothed pixel without meeting read as one shape; a reader's own text spacing or minimum font size; the lines of two texts set close.

## Decision

**The check names a text where the shape of a letter of one line meets the shape of a letter of the next, as the engine sets them, and asks the brand's owner for attention. Where a released version of the brand marks the role the step comes from as set tight on purpose, validation states it as a fact. The engine keeps the line step the brand gives.**

### 1. Letters touch where their shapes meet across a text's lines

- **Which lines.** Each line of a text and the next one, as the engine sets them, across its paragraphs. Lines of two texts are not compared: the layout sets their distance, not the step.
- **What a line draws.** Its letters: the glyphs the engine shapes for each run's text, each from the face that shaped it and at its run's size, where the engine places it along the line and on the line's baseline. A run's sign counts with them, where the scene places it ([visual-communication:DEC-036-concept-signs] point 3).
- **Characters drawn to join.** The characters a font draws to join the next line's do not count: Unicode's Box Drawing, U+2500 to U+257F, its Block Elements, U+2580 to U+259F, and the pieces of a tall bracket or integral, U+2320, U+2321 and U+239B to U+23B3. The capability's page of checks lists the set. Every other character counts as a letter.
- **When they touch.** Two lines' letters touch where the outline of a letter of one line and the outline of a letter of the next share a point, the lower line set the text's step below the upper. So two lines' letters touch at any step at or below their reach, and at none above it.
- **No distance.** Letters that come near without meeting do not touch. That no distance is added is a choice, which no measure sets (Rationale).
- **To a tolerance.** The check reads whether the outlines share a point with each curve flattened into straight segments, none further than 0.001 em of its letter's size from the curve. So letters that meet or part by less than about 0.002 em may be read either way.
- **Measured from the shapes.** The check reads each letter's outline from the face that shaped it: the family of its run's role that measures the character, a later family where the first lacks it ([visual-communication:DEC-004-brands-and-notations] point 9). It scales each letter to its own run's size. It reads a sign's shape from its drawing, its icon or its shape fitted to its square (DEC-036 point 1). Neither the face's ascent and descent nor a letter's box stands for the shape.

### 2. The finding asks the brand's owner for attention

- **Its level.** It asks for attention and never fails. Letters that meet may be what a brand means, such as a display type set tight on purpose, so no record makes their parting an invariant ([visual-communication:DEC-002-files-and-validation] point 7). Where a released version of the brand says it means them, validation states them as a fact instead (point 4).
- **Who acts.** It names the brand's owner, since the step is the brand's: the project, for a brand of its own not yet released; a released version's owner; and, for a copy of a brand the capability ships, whoever maintains the capability ([visual-communication:DEC-003-change-over-time] point 9).
- **Where it lies.** At the piece, by the text's trace id, naming no library version, since the step comes from the brand under the piece's lock and the letters from the piece's content. The brand's version that the finding carries for point 4 settles only how validation reads it, never where it lies.
- **What it says.** The two lines, the letters that meet and the step, with the typography role it comes from and their reach, the step above which they part. Its fix: a wider line step for the role, in the brand. Where the brand means the setting, its owner may say so instead (point 4).
- **When it is found.** With the scene's other findings, in each language and at each key over the size axes, and kept in the findings record ([visual-communication:DEC-005-engine-and-plugins] point 13). Its label is `letters-touch`, and `letters-touch-meant` for point 4's fact.
- **In the designer's round.** It fails nothing, so the designer does not mend it before the review ([visual-communication:DEC-070-ship-call-and-one-round] point 2). It reaches the review and the person in the viewing step's report, as every scene check's finding does ([visual-communication:DEC-062-checks-beside-the-frames] point 1). The round mends it only where the review reports it as one of the kinds DEC-070 point 3 names.

### 3. The engine keeps the brand's line step

The engine widens no line step for the letters it sets, and breaks no line elsewhere to part them. A step at which letters touch stays as the brand gives it, and the check names the text.

### 4. A brand may mark a role as set tight on purpose

- **The word.** A brand marks a typography role whose lines it sets tight on purpose with `tight-lines: true` in the capability's extension key on the role's token, `"$extensions": {"pkit.visual-communication": {"tight-lines": true}}`. It says that letters that meet across the lines of a text whose step comes from that role are meant. This record calls it the role's *word*.
- **On the role's token.** The word counts on the token the brand gives the role the step comes from, or, where it gives that role none, on the token the role's default leads to (DEC-004 point 7), as the engine resolves the brand at the scene's key. A brand built on another writes it only on a role its base opens to it, as it gives any value (DEC-004 point 6). Where more than one role gives a text its step, each must carry the word.
- **Released, and still as it was released.** The word gives the fact only where the brand's version whose file holds it is released and still as it was released, as an overlap's word does ([visual-communication:DEC-066-meant-overlaps] point 2). For a copy of a brand the capability ships, the list's hash must also be the one the capability records for that version ([visual-communication:DEC-013-shipped-library-releases] point 5). Anywhere else, as in a brand of the project's own not yet released, the word gives the check nothing: the finding asks for attention exactly as where no word lies.
- **Read in validation alone.** The finding carries the brand's version whose file holds the word, and no mark of release. Validation reads it each time it reads the finding, as it reads an overlap's (DEC-066 point 2): `letters-touch-meant`, a fact, where that version is released and still as it was released, and `letters-touch` elsewhere. The fact's message says what `letters-touch` says, and that the role's token carries `tight-lines`.
- **The report gives `letters-touch`.** The viewing step's report gives every such finding as `letters-touch`, with nothing of the word in its message. So the review reads each text whose letters meet as where no word lies, and judges on the image whether a reader can make it out (DEC-070 point 3).
- **The scene holds nothing of it.** The check reads the word from the brand as the engine resolves it, beside the scene, where it reads a role's value, since the scene keeps values as roles (DEC-005 point 5). So no renderer and no plugin meets it, and the seam's version stays as it is (DEC-005 point 8).
- **A fact, since it asks no act.** The finding asks the brand's owner to choose the role's step. A released version's word makes that choice, fixed for that version, so in validation no one is expected to act (DEC-002 point 7).
- **What it adds to format 1.** A key in the capability's extension on a typography token, where format 1 refuses that extension on any token but a `fontFamily`, and a field of a finding in a piece's findings record, which names the brand's version that holds the word. Each is an addition, so every file format 1 accepted reads as before, and the format number stays 1 (DEC-003 points 1 and 3). On a typography token, the extension holds `tight-lines: true` alone: any other key or value there, and `tight-lines` on a token of another type, is refused as `tokens-shape`. A brand's version that writes the word is released only on a capability that reads it, since its release reads it in full first and names that capability's version as the release it needs ([visual-communication:DEC-014-own-library-needed-release] point 1).

### 5. Its reach, and what it leaves unnamed

The check states its reach beside its id, `letters-across-lines`, in its entry on the capability's page of checks and, in the same words, in its code (DEC-063 point 1).
- **It measures:** each two lines that follow each other in a text a page draws, across its paragraphs, in each language and at each key over the size axes, for letters and runs' signs that meet at the text's step, each read as point 1 reads it.
- **It leaves out:** two lines of two texts, whose distance the layout sets (point 1); the characters drawn to join (point 1); and the lines of a renderer that wraps text itself, as a medium may by a declared choice (DEC-005 point 4), since the check reads the lines the engine sets.
- **Where it can measure nothing, a fact.** Where the engine has no outline for a letter, since a font profile measures it from a table of widths or by an estimate (DEC-004 point 9), or its face draws it from a bitmap or an SVG of its own, the check states, at the text, that it measured nothing at each pair of its lines that holds such a letter, and why (DEC-063 point 2).
- **No screen.** It reads no renderer's pixels, so across a band of about one to two device pixels, where a browser may draw letters otherwise than the engine sets them, it is silent on what a screen shows (Rationale, "Where a renderer draws the letters").
- **What nothing names.** Nothing names where the letters of two texts set close touch, nor letters the engine sets apart and a browser draws together, nor a letter that meets a character drawn to join the next line's.

## Rationale

**Why a check, not the engine.** The step is the brand's typography, and a brand gives each role its value ([visual-communication:DEC-004-brands-and-notations]). An engine that widened a step below the face's ascent and descent would have widened the neutral brand's 1.1667 and 1.2 em as probed, where no Latin letter, Czech's among them, came within 2.4 px of the next line. One that widened a step below the reach of the letters each text sets would make the step follow each text's words, so two texts of one role would lie unevenly, and a type set tight on purpose would lose its step. Either way every scene of a brand set tight would change, where a finding changes none and lets a person choose.

**Why not leave the gap unnamed.** On these faces no Latin letter with Czech's reached further than 1.104 em, so the neutral brand as probed parted every such letter, and on them only a step below 1.104 em lets such letters touch. The engine takes such a step, and nothing names what it does: at 1.0 em the Czech heading's letters touched in 2.2 % of its pairs of lines, an acute under a g. A check costs one measure of each pair of a text's lines, and the outlines need reading only where the letters' boxes meet. At the neutral brand's steps as probed none did.

**Why from the letters' shapes.** The face's measures miss both ways. On these faces, the ascent and descent lie 0.15 em above any Latin letter's reach, so a check by them names each title, and each heading at a desktop, that the neutral brand at the version probed set on more than one line. The typographic ascent and descent lie below the reach of Czech letters, so a check by them misses letters that touch between 1.0 and 1.104 em. A letter's box over-reaches across the line: it named ten times the pairs that touch. The shapes are the letters themselves. A letter that a later family shapes is drawn from that family's face, and a run of another size at that size, so each is read so.

**Why letters touch where their shapes meet, with no distance.** Meeting is a relation of the shapes alone, so it holds at every size. A distance in px of the page names other letters on each screen. An HTML page shrinks with a narrow window ([visual-communication:DEC-020-html-page-drawing] point 4), so a px of the page often takes less than one device pixel, and at the device scales the capability's records probed, from 0.625 to 2, one device pixel is 1.6 to 0.5 px of the page. A fixed px is also a larger share of a small text's em than of a large one's. A distance in em would hold at every scale, but nothing measured sets its size. Letters less than a device pixel apart share a smoothed pixel at some places of the pixel grid and not at others, and the fainter of the two covers at most 29 % of it. No one judged whether such letters read as one shape (Context). So the check names letters that meet as the engine sets them, and adds no margin: the zero is a choice, made since no evidence sets a margin. At the neutral brand's steps as probed, no Latin letter with Czech's met the next line's: the nearest lay 2.4 px apart.

**Why a tolerance of 0.001 em.** The check compares straight segments, so it flattens each curve, and the tolerance bounds how far that moves a shape. At 0.001 em it moves a shape by less than one unit of a face drawn on 1 000 units to the em, and by less than 0.05 px at 48 px, the largest size probed: far inside the band of about one to two device pixels across which the check is silent on a screen (below).

**Where a renderer draws the letters.** The check measures each letter where the engine places it, and reads no renderer's pixels. Each renderer that draws the engine's lines draws each line at the engine's baseline, its letters at or near the engine's places along it. The PDF renderer places each glyph at the advances the engine shaped. A browser held each line's baseline within 0.024 px of the engine's by its layout, and each line's width within 0.41 px. It drew each line's ink within 0.49 device pixels of that baseline at a device scale of 1, within 1.37 at 1.225, and within 1.44 in a 375 px window (DEC-020, Context). It places each line alone (DEC-020 point 2), so one line's letters may come nearer the next line's, or move further from them, by about one to two device pixels, and by more where two lines move opposite ways, which no probe measured. Letters less than a device pixel apart may also share a smoothed pixel (Context). So the check names the case the engine settles, letters that meet as it sets them, and across that band it is silent on what a screen shows: a browser may draw together letters the engine sets apart by less, which the check leaves unnamed, and draw apart letters that meet by less, which it names all the same. At the neutral brand's steps as probed, the nearest Latin letters with Czech's, a g over a Ů 2.4 px of the page apart in a 24 px heading, lay 1.5 device pixels apart at a scale of 0.625, within that band, and 4.8 at 2. The check needs no browser for the case it names. A renderer that wraps text itself draws lines of its own, which the check does not read, so its reach leaves them out (point 5).

**Why characters drawn to join are left out.** A font draws them to meet the next line's, so a tree of folders meets at any step below 1.4 em in Source Code Pro, as it is meant to. The set is the blocks and pieces Unicode gives for shapes built across lines, so it names the same characters in every font. The probe measured box drawing alone.

**Why a run's sign counts.** It is drawn on the line in the run's paint, and a letter it meets reads as badly as one another letter meets.

**Why it asks for attention, and names the brand's owner.** A tight display type with letters that meet may be what a brand means, and a released brand's step is no file the project corrects. So the finding never fails. The step is the brand's, so the act the finding asks, a wider step or the word of point 4, is the brand's owner's, and the finding names that owner. The designer mends only failures before the review, and in its round only what the review reports among the kinds DEC-070 point 3 names, so the finding waits for the review and the person.

**Why a brand may mark the setting meant.** Without a word, a brand that sets a role tight on purpose is asked for attention in every piece that sets such a text, for ever, a question its choice has answered. The step is the role's, so the brand says it once, on the role, and every piece under the brand is answered. A piece's word would answer one piece for a choice its brand made, and a word on each pair of letters would follow the words of each text, which the brand does not write.

**Why on the pattern of the meant overlaps.** Only a released version's word gives the fact, since a released version never changes, and a brand the project can still correct fixes no word: the line DEC-002 point 7 draws, which DEC-066 follows for an overlap (DEC-066, Rationale, "Why only a released version's word gives the fact"). The word stays out of the review, so no brand's word tells the reviewer that a text it sees can be read, and the review judges each such text on the image. Whether a version is released is read where the finding is read, as its level is, so neither the scene nor the findings record holds it (DEC-066 point 2).

### Alternatives considered

- **The engine widens a step below the face's ascent and descent:** it would widen the neutral brand's steps as probed, where no letters touched.
- **The engine widens a step below the reach of the letters a text sets:** two texts of one role lie unevenly, and a brand loses a step it chose.
- **The engine or the piece picks another line break:** it hangs on the words, so it holds in one language and at one key and fails in another, and it clears one text and leaves the role's step as tight for the next.
- **A check by the face's ascent and descent:** it names each title, and each heading at a desktop, that the neutral brand at the version probed set on more than one line, its letters 2.4 px apart or more.
- **A check by the typographic ascent and descent:** it misses letters that touch between 1.0 and 1.104 em.
- **A check by the letters' boxes:** it names ten times the pairs whose letters touch.
- **A finding at the brand, for each role, by the reach of every letter of its face in the languages the project sets:** it lies where the fix is and shapes no text, but it names a role set at 1.05 em in a project that sets Czech, where none of the deck's lines touched, for letters such as a g over a Ů that its texts may never set.
- **Letters within a distance in px of the page, such as 1 px:** it names other letters on each screen and at each text size, and means nothing in a PDF.
- **Letters within a distance in em:** nothing measured sets its size.
- **A finding that fails:** a tight setting may be meant.
- **No word, the finding asking for attention for ever:** it asks, piece by piece, a question a released brand's choice has answered (DEC-002 point 7).
- **The fact wherever the word lies,** in a brand not yet released too: there the word lies in a file the project can still correct, as for an overlap (DEC-066, Alternatives).
- **A word on the piece:** one piece's answer to its brand's choice, said again in each piece.
- **A released word gives the review the fact:** not taken, since a brand's word would settle for the review whether a text it sees can be read, as DEC-066 keeps an overlap's word from it. Its trigger to revisit: a test run on the review compares its judgement of letters that meet across lines with a person's, as DEC-070 point 9 reads such a trigger.
- **The look report:** it names strong ink lost to a part drawn after a text, which letters of one colour lose none of, and it needs a browser where this measure needs the faces alone.
- **The gap left unnamed:** Czech letters touch at steps the engine takes, and nothing names them.
