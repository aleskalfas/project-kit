**In plain terms:** a *run* is a span of a text set in a style of its own, such as the prompt, the command and the comment of a terminal's line, or one phrase set apart in a sentence. A component lets content give runs in a field of text it chooses, and declares for that field the words of its styles, such as `prompt` and `command`. Content then gives the field a list of runs: each run is a text, or a mapping of its `text`, its `style` and perhaps its `concept`. A run that names no style takes the field's first one, so a field given as text reads as before. The component gives each style its font and its colour, as it gives an option's word its effect. A run that names a concept is read with its notation's name, as an item's concept is, and colours the run wherever the component paints it from a colour family, unless a state of the instance that draws it gives another. The engine measures each run in its own style, and breaks lines across runs. The format fixes a run's three keys, since a run places no component, so no key is reserved anew and no field a component declares is refused. Every file format 1 accepted reads and draws as before.

## Context

The founding record makes content what a piece shows: its text, its pictures, and which component shows each part. Content holds no literal font or colour, and chooses only among what its libraries declare. What content places may name a concept, read with its notation, and its component decides how that looks. Colour says what a thing is ([visual-communication:DEC-001-visual-communication-discipline] points 3 and 6).

The record of the engine has the scene's text hold paragraphs of runs, each with a style role and perhaps a link. The engine shapes each run from a committed font file, and breaks lines itself, by Unicode's rules and each language's own. A component chooses by an option, or by whether a field is filled ([visual-communication:DEC-005-engine-and-plugins] points 1, 4 and 5). The record of brands and notations has a notation map each concept to a colour family, and perhaps to an icon or a shape. It measures each drawn text against the fill under it ([visual-communication:DEC-004-brands-and-notations] points 8 and 10).

Every mapping that places a component reserves `id`, `use`, `concept`, `state` and `override`, and no component declares a field of these names ([visual-communication:DEC-002-files-and-validation] point 4). So an item's concept needs no record of its own: the reserved key reaches it, and the record of brands and notations reads it ([project-kit#2509](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2509) builds it). A run is no mapping that places a component, so the reserved `concept` does not reach it.

Format 1 builds part of that:
- **A text part,** the part of a component that draws a text, shows one field of its component. It sets the field in the *typography role* its `style` names, the role that gives a text its font, size, weight and line height. It colours it with its *paint*, the colour role its `fill` names. Either may switch on an option.
- **Colour.** An instance's colour family is its concept's, else its place's, else its component's, else that of the component it sits in, and a state may change it (DEC-010 point 6). A state its component gives a family replaces the instance's family, its own concept's included. A paint that reads a member of the family, `:colour.<member>`, takes it from that family. A paint that names a role takes that role, whatever the family. The foundations promise a family's member for text only on that family's own fill. The only other pair they declare is body text on the background (DEC-004 point 8).
- **A concept's other signs.** Format 1 draws no concept's icon or shape, and checks no bound on how many concepts one piece tells apart by colour (DEC-004 point 10).
- **The field holds text.** The check reads it against the field's schema. The engine draws a number there as text, and refuses any other value, such as a list, as content of the wrong shape. A text is split into paragraphs at its line feeds.
- **Fonts.** A brand gives each font family one file, which measures the family whatever its weight. A weight takes a file of its own as a family of its own, as the test brand's heading role names a semibold family.
- **A component's fields** are a fixed subset of JSON Schema, in which the component may mark a field that holds prose. No other tool reads content against them: the schema of a content file names no field's value.
- **The scene's text** holds one typography role, one paint, its fonts and its lines. It holds no run.

The capability's next slice draws texts in several styles and weights, such as a terminal's line whose prompt, command and comment take different colours ([project-kit#2501](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2501), T6). Until validation accepts files at an older format number, the format grows only by additions that every file written before still meets ([visual-communication:DEC-003-change-over-time] point 3). A new reserved key would refuse a field of its name that format 1 accepts (DEC-002 point 4). The records of a list's fields and of ties met that bound on the like questions. They chose fields a pattern declares, a word given on each item, a key the format fixes on a mapping that places no component, and the shape format 1 gives an option that names an item ([visual-communication:DEC-010-list-item-fields] points 1, 3 and 5, [visual-communication:DEC-011-tie-ends] point 2).

## Decision

**A component marks which of its fields of text take runs, and declares for each the words of its run styles, the first the default. Content gives such a field a text, as in format 1, or a list of runs: each a text, or a mapping of `text`, `style` and `concept`, keys the format fixes on a run. A run's style is a word of its field, and the component's text part chooses the run's typography role and paint by it, as by an option's word. A run's concept is read with its notation's name, and gives the run its colour family, which every paint that reads a family takes, unless a state of its instance gives another. Each run is measured in its own style, and lines break across runs. A field of runs is drawn where it is declared.**

### 1. A component marks the fields of text that take runs

- **A run** is a span of a text set in one style, perhaps naming a concept. A text is one or more runs, joined in order. A field that may hold runs is a *field of runs*.
- **The component's choice, field by field.** A component declares which of its fields take runs, by a mark on the field among its fields, or on a slot among those a layout declares, as it marks a field that holds prose. Whatever its spelling, the mark is a key that format 1's schema of a component does not know. A field it does not mark takes what its schema says, as in format 1.
- **Only a field of text.** The mark lies only on one of the component's fields itself, or on a slot a layout declares, never on a schema within one, such as a list's items or an object's property, nor on the schema of all its fields. No `$ref` leads to a marked schema, so no other field, list or property takes runs through one. The field's schema takes text alone: its `type` is text and no other, and it holds no `enum`, `const` or `$ref`. The schema still says what the field's text is. The mark says that the field takes a list of runs as well, and the check reads a list there by this record, never against the schema's type. A field that takes a number, a list or one of several words besides takes no runs, so no value it held changes its meaning. So a field's shape is its schema widened by its mark, and whatever offers a component's fields to an author offers that shape: the capability's reference pages, its agents, or a schema an editor reads.
- **Text, or a list of runs.** A field of runs takes text, read as one run in the field's first style (point 2), or a list of runs. So a component whose field of text comes to take runs asks nothing of content already written, and content writes a list only where a run differs.
- **A run** is text, set in its field's first style, or a mapping whose `text` holds text, and which may hold `style` (point 2) and `concept` (point 3). It holds no other key.
- **Joined as written.** The runs' texts are joined in order, with nothing added between them. A line feed in a run's text ends a paragraph, as in format 1, and the next paragraph goes on in the same run.
- **Filled where it holds text.** A list of runs fills its field, as a part's condition reads it, where one of its runs holds text, as text fills it where it is not empty.
- **Keys the format fixes.** A run places no component, so no component declares a key of it, and the reserved keys do not bind it (DEC-002 point 4). The format fixes `text`, `style` and `concept` on a run alone, as it fixes `layout` on a page drawn in a layout, which places no component either (DEC-010 point 5). So they refuse no field format 1 accepts, and the runs of every component read alike, for people, agents and translators.
- **No id.** A run carries no id, since nothing names it: no option, end or reference reaches a run. A list of runs holds runs, not items, so the rules that give an item its id, unique in its list and the same in every language, do not reach it (DEC-001 point 3, DEC-002 point 4). An edit taken back from a renderer finds a run by its place among its field's runs, only while they stay as they were at the render (DEC-001 point 3).
- **A field's value in each language.** Each language's content holds the same fields and ids (DEC-001 point 3). A field's runs are its value, which differs between languages as its text does, so their number, order and styles may differ too.
- **Where a field of runs lies.** It is one of a component's own fields: a field of a top, of a component content places, an item included, or a slot of a layout, which the top declares with the slot (DEC-010 point 5). An arrangement field, an end field and an option take words or keys, never runs (DEC-010 point 3, DEC-011 point 2). A list's component field gives no field (DEC-010 point 1).

### 2. A run names a style by a word its field declares

- **Run styles.** For each field of runs, the component declares the words of its *run styles*, such as `prompt`, `command` and `comment` for a terminal's line, or `plain` and `strong` for a sentence. Each word takes the shape of a name, and appears once. They are the field's own, so another field of the component may declare other words.
- **The first word is the default.** A run that names no style, and a field given as text, take the first word, as an option of words takes its first where content gives none, and as an arrangement field does (DEC-010 point 3).
- **One word on each run,** given as `style: <word>`. The word is no role, no font and no value: content chooses among what its component declares (DEC-001 point 3).
- **The text part chooses by it, as by an option.** The text part that shows a field of runs gives each run the typography role and the paint its style chooses, as it gives an option's word its effect. So the choice is declared, never computed (DEC-005 point 1). The part may give two words one role and differ in paint alone. Where the field declares more than one word, at least one text part of the component that shows the field chooses its role, its paint or both by them. Another may show the field in one look, as a summary might, and draws every run in it. A brand's drawing need not choose by them (below).
- **Only how the run is set.** A run's word chooses only that run's typography role and paint. It chooses no place or size, no other part, none of the component's named values and no option of a placed component. A text part chooses by the words of the field it shows, and by no other field's.
- **Drawn where it is declared.** A field of runs is drawn by a text part of the component that declares it, or, for a top, of a drawing of its layout. Only a text part shows it, and a part's condition reads whether it is filled. No other part reads it, such as an image that names a field. No part of the component passes it on to a component it places, for now (Rationale). A part may still pass a field of text on to a placed field of runs, which reads it as one run in its first style.
- **Written against the component, under every drawing.** The words are what content gives the component, as its options' words are. So a brand's drawing of a layout takes them as words the top declares, and gives each its own look (DEC-009 point 5). A drawing *takes* a field's words where a text part of it that shows the field chooses by them. One that chooses by none takes only the first. One that passes the field on to a component it places takes no run, so it fits only an instance that gives the field text. A drawing that does not take what an instance's runs give does not fit that instance, and the top's own drawing draws it (DEC-009 point 6). What an instance's runs give, for a drawing's fit, is the words they name: a run's concept counts for no drawing's fit, as an item's concept does not, since it gives a family that every paint reading a family takes. So no change of a top that point 6 counts as not breaking fails a brand's drawing (point 8).

### 3. A run names a concept with the key `concept`

- **The key `concept`.** A run may name a concept as `concept: <notation>/<concept>`. It takes the shape format 1 gives an item's concept, read with its notation's name, from the notations the piece names (DEC-004 point 10). It means on a run what it means wherever content places a component: what the run stands for, such as the name of a service in a sentence about it.
- **No new reserved key.** The reserved `concept` binds the mappings that place a component (DEC-002 point 4), and a run places none. The format fixes the key on a run, with the reserved key's name, shape and meaning, so `concept` means one thing wherever content writes it.
- **A run's family.** A run that names a concept takes that concept's colour family, before its instance's own, as an item's family is its own before that of the component it sits in (DEC-010 point 6). A run that names none takes the family of the instance whose text part draws it, as in format 1.
- **A state reaches every run.** A state its component gives a family replaces the instance's family, its own concept's included, as in format 1. It replaces the family of each run of the instance too, a run's concept included. So a dropped step greys every run of its text, as it greys the rest of its look, since its component gives each state its look (DEC-001 point 6). Content keeps no run's concept under such a state, as it keeps no item's.
- **A run's paint.** A text part paints each run with the paint its style chooses, or with the role an `override` points the part at, as format 1 paints a text (DEC-001 point 6). A paint that reads a member of the family, `:colour.<member>`, takes that member of the run's family. A paint that names a role takes that role, whatever the run's concept, as it does for every component content places.
- **The component decides how a concept shows.** So a run's concept colours the run where the component paints its style from a family, and the component chooses which member it reads. A run that names a concept in a style the component paints with a role, or on a part an `override` points at a role, shows its concept in no colour. The check names it (point 8).
- **Guarded as drawn.** Each run is measured against the fill under it, at its own size and weight and in its own paint, as every drawn text is (DEC-004 point 8). The foundations promise a family's member for text only on that family's own fill, and the fill under a run's text may be another family's or a plain role. So the measure as drawn alone guards the colour a run's concept gives it, and a piece may pass under one brand and fail under another, though both meet every pair they are given.
- **Signs beside colour, later.** Format 1 draws no concept's icon or shape, for an item or for anything else, and checks no bound on how many concepts one piece tells apart by colour (DEC-004 point 10). A run's concept counts toward that bound as an item's does. How a run shows a sign beside its colour, such as its concept's icon beside the run or a chip on its family's fill, is left to the change that builds icons and a legend, for runs and items alike. So this record narrows no rule of that point.

### 4. Each run is measured in its own style, and lines break across runs

- **Its own role.** The engine measures each run in the typography role its style chooses, from the files the brand gives that role's families, as it measures every text (DEC-005 point 4, DEC-004 point 9). A role it cannot read, or a character no family of a run's role measures, is reported or refuses the render as for any text.
- **Breaks across runs.** The engine finds where a paragraph may break in its whole text, across its runs, by Unicode's rules and the language's own (DEC-005 point 4). Each piece of a line is measured in its own run's role. So a word whose letters lie in two runs stays one word, and in Czech a one-letter word still moves to the next line with the word after it, whichever runs hold them.
- **The margin.** The engine breaks against the planned width less a margin taken at the greatest font size among the styles the text's runs take, so a renderer's metrics move no word of any run (DEC-005 point 4).
- **One line height for the text.** Each line of a text takes the greatest line height among the styles the text's runs take. The text's measured height is its lines times that height, as in format 1. So the lines of one text lie evenly, and the scene's text keeps one line height (DEC-005 point 5). A text whose runs all take one style is measured as format 1 measures it.
- **One baseline on each line.** The runs of one line stand on one baseline. A line whose runs all take one style places it as format 1 does, splitting the leading evenly. A line of several styles places it from its runs' metrics (Implications). A run's letters may reach into the leading, as a style's letters may in format 1, where its line height is less than its font's ascent and descent.
- **A weight's own file.** Each run is measured and drawn from the files its role's families name. A brand that gives a weight a family of its own, as the test brand's heading does, gives each weight its own file with no change of format.

### 5. How each choice is given, beside the records of a list's fields and of ties

- **Holding runs: a field the component declares.** The component marks one of its own fields of text as taking runs, as a pattern declares a field of its own in which content names a list's component (DEC-010 point 1). Unlike that field, a field of runs is a kind format 1 already has, a field of text, whose value may now be a list of runs. Unlike arrangement fields and end fields, it is no field a pattern declares for the items of a list, so it needs no declaration apart from the component's fields (DEC-010 point 2, DEC-011 point 1).
- **A run's style: a word its field declares, given on each run.** It is an option of words given on each run, as an arrangement field is an option given on each item (DEC-010 point 3). The component chooses by it as by an option's word.
- **A run's keys and its concept: keys the format fixes on a mapping that places no component,** as `layout` on a page drawn in a layout (DEC-010 point 5). No component declares a key of a run, so fixing them refuses nothing. `concept` keeps format 1's shape and meaning there, as an end field keeps the shape of format 1's option that names an item (DEC-011 point 2).
- **No new reserved key,** for the reason both records gave: it would refuse a field of its name that format 1 accepts (DEC-002 point 4, DEC-003 point 3). The example component of the capability's own schema declares a field named `text`.
- **No clash of two libraries.** Both records meet a key that an item's component and its list both declare, which neither reads (DEC-010 point 2, DEC-011 point 1). A run's keys are the format's alone. Its style's words are its field's, and only the component that declares the field draws it, so no other library's words meet them.
- **No new rule of fit.** The records of a list's fields and of ties each needed a rule for versions that do not fit together, since keys of two libraries meet on one item (DEC-010 point 2, DEC-011 point 4). A field of runs, its words and the text parts of its component that draw it lie in one version of one library, and no part passes it on, so no versions cease to fit together over runs. A brand's drawing, which another library holds, draws a top's field of runs only where it takes what an instance's runs give, else the top's own drawing draws the instance (DEC-009 point 6, point 2). That is the record of layouts' fit of a drawing to an instance, which needs no new rule either.

### 6. What breaks

The record of change over time already counts removing or renaming a name a piece uses, or changing what it means, as a breaking change of its library ([visual-communication:DEC-003-change-over-time] point 6). A run style's word is such a name. Beyond it:

- **Breaking:** a field that ceases to take runs, since content may hold runs there. So is a change of the field's first word, a new word put first included: every run that names no style, and every text the field holds, would then be a run of another word, and a word says what a run is (point 2).
- **Not breaking:** a field of text that comes to take runs, since its text still reads as one run in its first style. Nor is a new word after the first. Nor is a change of the role or paint a word chooses, which changes how pieces look. A piece meets it only when its lock moves (DEC-003 point 8).

### 7. What this adds to format 1, and what still holds

- **Added, each optional:**
  - a component's mark that a field of text takes runs, with the words of its run styles
  - a list of runs as the value of such a field, with the keys `text`, `style` and `concept` on a run
  - a text part's typography role and paint chosen by each run's style
  - a run's colour family from its concept
  - a text's line height from the styles its runs take, and the scene's runs, each with its role, its paint and its fonts
- **The schema of a content file keeps its keys.** It names no field's value, so runs add none there.
- **How content is read.** The check reads a field of runs as text, against its schema, or as a list of runs, each run against its field's words and the piece's notations. It reads every other field as in format 1.
- **What still holds.** The mark is a key format 1's schemas refuse, so no component they accepted declares a field of runs. Every field format 1 shows as text takes text alone, or a number drawn as text, and the engine refuses a list there, so no file format 1 accepted gives runs. Each such text is one run, in the role and the paint its part gives it, measured, broken and drawn as before. Every file format 1 accepted is read and drawn as before, and no finding arises from one that format 1 did not already raise.

### 8. What the checks report, and how severely

Each finding below fails validation where it lies in a file the project can correct, as the record of files and validation decides ([visual-communication:DEC-002-files-and-validation] point 7). Elsewhere, as in a released library version, it asks for attention and names who can correct it. A final piece's drawing is not judged, as that record decides.

- **Where each lies.** A finding on what a component declares, or on what one of its parts gives, lies in the component's version. A finding on a brand's drawing lies in the brand's version, judged when the brand is checked alone (DEC-009 point 9). A finding on a run lies in the content file, at the run, named by its field and its place among the field's runs. A finding on how a run is drawn lies at the piece, as for any drawn text.
- **Fails validation:**
  - in a component: a mark anywhere but directly on one of its fields or on a slot a layout declares, a `$ref` that leads to a marked schema, or a mark on a field whose schema takes more than text, or holds `enum`, `const` or `$ref`; a field of runs with no word; or a word that does not take the shape of a name, or appears twice
  - in a component, a top's own drawing of a layout included: a text part that shows a field of runs the component declares, whose role or paint chooses by words the field does not declare, or whose switch by the field's words leaves one of them out, as a switch on an option names each of its words
  - in a component: a field of runs of more than one word that no text part of the component, a top's own drawing included, chooses by
  - in a component: a part that passes a field of runs the component declares on to a component it places, or a part other than a text part that reads one, beyond a condition on whether it is filled
  - in a component or a brand's drawing: a choice by a run's style anywhere but the typography role and paint of a text part that shows its field
  - in a brand's drawing: a text part whose role or paint chooses by a word the top's field does not declare, as a drawing that takes a word the top does not declare (DEC-009 point 9)
  - in content: a list in a field of text that takes no runs, as format 1 already reports a value of the wrong shape
  - in content: a run that is neither text nor a mapping, or a mapping with no `text`, whose `text` is no text, or with another key than `text`, `style` and `concept`
  - in content: a style its field does not declare
  - in content: a concept of another shape, from a notation the piece does not name, or that its notation does not hold, as for an item's concept (DEC-004 point 11)
  - at a piece: a run below the contrast it must reach against the fill under it, as for any drawn text (DEC-004 points 8 and 11), or whose family has no member its paint reads, as for any part
- **Asks for attention, never failing:**
  - in a brand's drawing: a text part that shows a field of runs and leaves one of its words out, or chooses by none of them where the field declares more than one, or a part that passes a field of runs on to a component it places. It names the brand's owner. An instance whose runs the drawing does not take is drawn by the top's own drawing (point 2), and the piece states it, as for any drawing that does not fit (DEC-009 point 9).
  - at a piece: a run that names a concept but is painted with a role, by its style or by an `override` on its part, so that its concept shows in no colour. It names the run, and its style or the override. Its content's author may choose another style or drop the override, or the owner of what paints the style, the component's or, where a brand's drawing draws it, the brand's, paint the style from a family.

## Rationale

**Why a list of runs, not marks inside the text.** Marks in a text, such as Markdown's `**` around a strong phrase, would change what text format 1 accepted means: a `*` or a `_` written as a character would turn into a mark. Every such character would then need escaping, in every language. A translator or an agent would edit marks inside the words, and an edit taken back from a renderer would have to place them. A list holds each run apart, so the check reads each run's style and concept, and a text whose runs do not differ stays plain text.

**Why the component marks which fields take runs.** The component decides how its texts look (DEC-001 point 6), and which of them may hold spans of another look is part of that. A tag or a page's number drawn in one style for a reason would otherwise take a strong phrase from any content. A field also needs its words before a run can name one. Marked field by field, runs reach only the components whose owners chose them, so every component format 1 accepted stays as it was.

**Why a mark that widens a field of text, and only such a field.** The field's schema keeps checking its text as format 1 checks it, its prose mark included, and the mark adds one more value it takes, a list of runs. No tool of format 1 reads content against a component's fields but the check, since the schema of a content file names no field's value, so the two never disagree in use: the check reads text by the schema and a list by this record. A tool that offers fields to an author later reads the mark with the schema (point 1), as the check reads arrangement fields by the record of a list's fields, not by JSON Schema (DEC-010 point 2). What it costs is a schema that says text where the field may also hold a list, which the mark beside it corrects. A field that took a number, a list or one of a few words would change what some value it held means, or refuse it, once it took runs. Kept to fields of text alone, a field that comes to take runs refuses nothing it held.

**Why words, not roles.** Content chooses only among what its libraries declare (DEC-001 point 3). A run that named a typography role and a colour role would set a look in content, which content does only by an `override`, part by part, and never for one span. A word says what the run is, such as a prompt or a strong phrase, and the component says how it looks. So switching the brand restyles every run with no content changed (DEC-001 point 5), and a brand's drawing of a layout gives the same words its own look (DEC-009 point 5).

**Why the words lie with the field, not with the part that draws it.** Content writes the words, so they are what the component declares for content, as its fields and its options' words are. A brand's drawing takes what the top declares, never what a drawing declares (DEC-009 point 5). Words that a text part declared would be the drawing's own, so each drawing would declare others, and two parts that show one field could declare two sets.

**Why each field declares its own words.** A terminal's line, a sentence and a title set apart different things. One set of words for all of a component's fields would let a title's run name `prompt`, which the title draws as it draws the rest. With words for each field, the check holds each run to its field's words, and a text part that shows a field of several words to choosing by them.

**Why the first word is the default.** It is how an option of words takes its default, and how an arrangement field does (DEC-010 point 3). A field given as text then reads as one run of the first word. So a component whose field comes to take runs asks nothing of its content, and a run writes a word only where it differs. Since a word says what a run is, the first word is what every unstyled run is, so changing it breaks the field's library, unlike an arrangement field's first word, which says nothing of what an item is (point 6, DEC-010 point 3).

**Why the key is `style`.** A run is a span in a style of its own, and `style` is the word an author reaches for. In content, `style` can only name a word, since content names no role there. In a component and in the scene, `style` names a typography role, and it is a text part's `style` that chooses by the run's word. So one key holds a word in content and a role in a component and the scene, and each file uses it in one sense. The overlap is a cost: a reader who meets `style` must know which file it is in.

**Why the format fixes a run's keys.** A run places no component, so no component declares a key of it, and fixing its keys refuses no field, as the record of a list's fields found for `layout` (DEC-010 point 5). Keys each component named for its runs would read differently in each component, and an agent or a translator would learn them component by component. The keys are the format's alone, and a run holds no other, so a later key, such as a run's link (DEC-005 point 5) or the methodology's container's key, is an addition that refuses nothing a run held before. So no key need be kept free on a run now, though the record of files and validation keeps the container's key free where content places a component. That record says so in its point 4 (Implications).

**Why `concept` keeps its name and shape on a run.** Content writes one key wherever it says what something stands for, as `layout` and the reserved keys read alike in every piece. The reserved key binds only a mapping that places a component (DEC-002 point 4), so the format fixes it on a run as well, with the same shape and meaning.

**Why a run's concept reaches only the paints that read a family.** A concept says what a thing is, and its component decides how that looks (DEC-001 point 6). On every component content places, a concept reaches the paints that read the family, and a paint that names a role stays that role. On a run it does the same, so the key means one thing everywhere, and the component, not the format, decides how a run looks. Neither rule makes a run's colour safer: no member a component can read is promised where a run's text lies, so the measure as drawn guards it either way (point 3). What the rule costs is a concept that shows in no colour, where a component paints a style with a role.

**Why the check asks for attention when a run's concept shows in no colour.** Format 1 raises nothing for an item whose component paints nothing from its family, since the item's look is its component's alone. A run's look is the style its content chose, and another word of the same field may paint from the family. An `override` on its part is content's choice too. So the content's author can often mend it, and the check names the run and its style or the override. Where every word of the field paints with a role, the mend is the owner's of what paints it: the component's, or a brand's where its drawing draws the run.

**Why a state reaches a run that names a concept.** A state says something of its instance, such as a step dropped from a flow, and its component gives the state its look (DEC-001 point 6). In format 1 it replaces the instance's family, its own concept's included. A run lies within one of the instance's parts, so the state reaches it as it reaches the rest of the part. A component switches on options, never on a state, and chooses a run's paint only by its style. Were a run's concept to win, a dropped step's component could not grey a run that names one, and the format, not the component, would decide how the run looks in that state. An item inside another keeps its own concept whatever the outer item's state (DEC-010 point 6), but that item is an instance of its own, with states of its own. A run has none.

**Why one line height for the text.** Lines as tall as their own runs, as CSS's line boxes are, would lie unevenly within one paragraph, and the scene's text holds one line height (DEC-005 point 5). Lines at the first style's height would be shorter than a larger run's own line height, so its letters would reach into the line above further than its own style lets them. The greatest height among the styles a text takes gives each run at least its own style's line height and keeps the lines even, and a text in one style keeps format 1's height and baseline. A height taken from every word the field declares would set every text of the field wide, even one that uses no larger style. A field that came to take runs would then change the height of text already written.

**Why a field of runs is drawn where it is declared, for now.** Passing a field of runs on would have the part map its field's words onto the placed component's, as a part gives a placed component's option a word, or a switch on an option of its own (the capability's components page, "Placing a component"). Content would still write only its own component's words. No slice needs it yet: a terminal's line and a sentence with a strong phrase are drawn by the component that declares them. The map needs a spelling of its own, and lifting the refusal later refuses nothing. What the refusal costs is reuse. A card whose title takes runs draws the title with a text part of its own, and cannot hand it to a heading block, though small parts are what a project reuses (DEC-001, Context, DEC-007 point 1). The starter's blocks are planned to compose ([project-kit#2501](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2501), T12). If they need to pass a field of runs on, the change that builds them decides the map, spelled as an option's switch.

**What runs cost.** A text with one span set apart is three runs where it was one text. A run's text is joined to its neighbours with nothing added, and YAML drops the spaces at the end of a plain text. So a space at a run's edge is written inside quotes, and so is a run whose text YAML would read as a number, a yes or no, or nothing, such as `null` or `true`. The founding record measures what content costs for each page (DEC-001, Implications), and the slice that draws runs measures it again ([project-kit#2501](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2501)).

### Alternatives considered

- **Marks inside the text, such as Markdown's or tags.** Rejected. Text format 1 accepted would change its meaning, and every such character would need escaping.
- **A run that names its typography role and its colour role.** Rejected. Content would set a look, span by span, and a brand's drawing could not restyle it.
- **Every field shown as text takes runs, with no mark.** Rejected. Content could set apart a span in a text its component draws in one style for a reason, and no field would declare the words a run may name.
- **A field whose schema says it takes a list of runs, as `type` and `items` do.** Rejected. Each component would write the run's shape again, and could write it differently.
- **Fields of runs declared apart from `fields`, keyed by field name, as a pattern declares arrangement fields (DEC-010 point 2).** Rejected. Arrangement fields lie apart because they are no field of the item's component. A field of runs is the component's own field of text, so its schema would still say text, and a reader would look in two places for one field. Kept to the component's own fields and its layouts' slots, the mark lies at no other depth either (point 1).
- **Runs in any field, whatever its schema takes.** Rejected. A field that held a list of texts, a number or one of a few words would change what such a value means, or refuse it.
- **Run styles declared by the text part that draws the field.** Rejected. Content would give what a drawing declares, which the record of layouts refuses (DEC-009 point 5).
- **One set of words for all of a component's fields.** Rejected. A run could name a word its field draws no differently.
- **A set of words the format or the foundations fix for every field, such as `strong` and `emphasis`.** Rejected. What sets a span apart differs from one kind of text to another, as a terminal's prompt does from a strong phrase, so each field would still need words of its own. The words an option takes are its component's too.
- **A run as an item that places a component.** Rejected. A run would be an instance with a frame of its own. Lines could not break across runs, and the engine would need an operator that flows instances along lines.
- **New reserved keys, such as `text` and `style`.** Rejected. They would refuse fields format 1 accepts, such as the field `text` of the capability's own example component (DEC-002 point 4, DEC-003 point 3).
- **Keys each component names for its runs.** Rejected. Runs would read differently in each component.
- **A run as a mapping from its word to its text, such as `strong: the exact version`.** Rejected. A run's key would be a word of its field, so runs would read differently in each field, and a word could take the name of a key the format fixes.
- **Paragraphs as a list of lists of runs.** Rejected. Every text of runs would nest one level deeper, and a line feed in a run already ends a paragraph.
- **An id on a run.** Rejected. Nothing names a run, and ids would ask each language for the same runs, though word order differs.
- **A run's concept that paints it in its family's member for text, whatever the style's paint.** Rejected. The format, not the component, would decide how the run looks.
- **A run's concept that wins over its instance's state.** Rejected. The component gives each state its look, and switches on no state, so a dropped step could not grey a run that names a concept (DEC-001 point 6).
- **A state's declaration that says whether it reaches runs that name a concept.** Rejected for now. No slice needs it, and an optional key on a state with format 1's order as its default is an addition later.
- **A finding where a run's concept shares its family with another concept the piece names.** Rejected for now. Format 1 checks the bound on concepts told apart by colour for no item, and the change that builds icons and a legend decides what tells concepts apart beyond it, for runs and items alike.
- **A run's concept shown as a chip, on its family's fill in its family's text.** Left to later. It reads the one pair the foundations promise for a family, and its shape is a sign beside colour. But it needs a run's own background in the scene, which a run style's role and paint do not give, so it comes with the change that builds icons and a legend.
- **Lines each as tall as their own runs.** Rejected. One paragraph's lines would lie unevenly.
- **Every line at the first style's line height.** Rejected. A larger run's letters would reach into the line above further than its own style lets them.
- **A line height taken from every word the field declares.** Rejected. Every text of the field would be set wide, and a field that came to take runs would change the height of text already written.
- **A field of runs passed on, its words mapped to the placed component's.** Rejected for now. No slice needs it yet, and the map needs a spelling of its own. Lifting the refusal later is an addition (Implications).

## Implications

- **The change that builds runs** ([project-kit#2501](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2501), T6) builds fields of runs, run styles, a run's concept and its paint, measuring and breaking across runs, and the scene's runs. It sets the spelling: how a component marks a field of runs and names its words, how a text part chooses its role and paint by a run's style, and from which run's metrics a line's baseline is placed. The check that reads ids in every list of content skips a list of runs (point 1). The capability's pages describe each with that change.
- **Typography roles for run styles.** A run style chooses roles as any part does: the shared roles of the foundations, or a library's own roles, which may carry a default, the starter's each a default that ends at a shared role ([visual-communication:DEC-008-library-role-defaults] points 1 and 2). The change that builds runs, or the starter's, adds the roles its styles need, such as a monospace role for a terminal and a strong role at the body's size.
- **The scene and the seam.** The scene's text holds paragraphs of runs, each with its typography role, its paint, its fonts and its source, and one line height. A renderer reads each run's paint and fonts, which it did not read before, so the seam's version rises with that change (DEC-005 point 8). A renderer that cannot draw a run's style reports the loss (DEC-005 point 9).
- **A family's colour on the shared surfaces.** The foundations may declare, for each family, a member promised on the shared surfaces, such as `surface` and `background`, so that a component that paints a run's concept on them reads a promised pair. A new pair breaks nothing, but every brand that names the foundations is checked against it with their new version, and some brand may not meet it (DEC-008 point 7). The change that builds runs ([project-kit#2501](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2501), T6) chooses for the starter's run styles that read a family: to add such a pair, or to paint them from a family only where they lie on its fill. Until one holds, a run's concept colour rests on the measure as drawn (point 3).
- **A weight's own file.** Whether a brand may give one family a file for each weight is the brand's declaration of its fonts, which the record of brands and notations frames (DEC-004 point 9). The change that builds runs may add it, as an optional addition. This record decides nothing of it, and a weight given as a family of its own still holds. Until a brand gives each weight its own file, a role that keeps a family and changes its weight is measured from that family's one file and drawn in its weight, as format 1 measures it, with no finding. Runs make that common, so the change that builds runs either adds a finding for it, or each weight T6 relies on is given as a family of its own.
- **Sources and traces.** A run's source names its field and its place among the field's runs, as a content source names an item with no id by its place (DEC-005 point 5). A text's trace id stays its part's.
- **Translation.** The rule that each language holds the same ids asks nothing of runs, which carry none. Whether the check holds anything of a field's runs equal across languages, such as the concepts they name, is left to translation (DEC-001 point 3, and Implications).
- **Taking edits back** from a renderer finds a run by its place, and changes its text. How an edit that splits or joins runs comes back, and how a medium that returns plain text keeps them, is left to the record of taking edits back (DEC-001, Implications, DEC-005 point 4).
- **Left to later changes:**
  - a run's link (DEC-005 point 5), a key the format adds on a run, which refuses nothing
  - a sign beside a run's colour, such as its concept's icon or a chip, and what tells a run's concept apart beyond the bound, with the change that builds icons and a legend (point 3)
  - a field of runs that a part passes on to a component it places, with its words mapped to that component's. Lifting the refusal of point 8 is an addition. The starter's blocks may need it when they compose ([project-kit#2501](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2501), T12).
  - the structure a tagged document or a web page gives a run, such as emphasis or code. A run's word is its field's own and says nothing of structure to a renderer, so how a run's structure is declared is decided apart (DEC-005 point 5).
- **The starter's README** names each field of runs with its words, as it names options. A run style chooses a run's look, not a pattern's arrangement, so its words count toward no guide of how many options a pattern holds ([visual-communication:DEC-007-starter-library] point 2). The starter's reference renders draw each run word of each field once, as they draw each value of each option once (DEC-007 point 3).
- **The release's comparison** of a library's stable components with its previous version (DEC-003 point 7) reads fields of runs and their words, under point 6.
- **Six accepted records take an edit once this one is accepted,** each citing this record. A person accepts each edit (the decisions README, "Refining an accepted record"). They sit beside the edits that state the record of ties ([project-kit#2546](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2546)).
  - The founding record's point 3: content chooses among the words of a field's run styles too, and a list of runs holds runs, not items, with no ids. A second language's content has the same shape in its fields and ids, and a field's runs may differ in number, order and style between languages, as its text does. Its point 6: a run of a text may name a concept, which gives that run its colour family before its instance's own, and a state of the instance replaces it, as it replaces the instance's.
  - The record of files and validation's point 3: the strict reading also takes a run's keys, and a list of runs in a field marked to take them. Its point 4: a list of runs holds runs, not items, so no id is unique in it or the same in every language. The format fixes `text`, `style` and `concept` on a run, which places no component, `concept` with the reserved key's shape and meaning. The methodology's container keeps no key free on a run, since a run's keys are closed and a later key is an addition. A run style's word is among the names that hold no path marks.
  - The record of brands and notations' point 8: the engine measures each run of a drawn text against the fill under it, at the run's own size and weight.
  - The record of the starter library's point 3: a component's reference renders also draw each run word of each field once, as they draw each value of each option.
  - The record of the engine's point 1: a text part may also choose by a run's style, which sets only that run's typography role and paint. Its point 4: a run of this record is a span in one style, and the engine shapes it in pieces of one font each, split where the role's next family measures a character the first lacks (DEC-004 point 9). The engine breaks a paragraph across its runs, each measured in its own role. Its point 5: each run of a text holds its paint and its fonts too, the text's line height is the greatest among the styles its runs take, and each run says how it was measured.
  - The record of layouts: its point 5, a drawing takes the words of the top's fields of runs, as it takes its options' words. Its point 6: a drawing takes a field's words where its text part that shows the field chooses by them, and fits only an instance whose runs it takes. Its point 9: a drawing's text part that chooses by a word the top's field does not declare fails, and one that leaves a word out, chooses by none of several, or passes a field of runs on asks for attention and names the brand's owner.
