**In plain terms:** a pattern, such as a row of cards, may let content name one component for every item of a list. It does so in a field the pattern declares for that list, which content fills as it places a component: the component, and words for its options. A pattern also declares, for each list, the fields its items may carry for how it arranges them, such as how wide an item is. Those that take a word, its arrangement fields, take one the pattern declares, never a number. A weight is such a field, and the pattern turns its word into a share of the row. A list of ties, such as arrows from one card to another, also carries two end fields, each naming an item (refinement per [visual-communication:DEC-011-tie-ends]). Where an item's component and its pattern both claim one key, neither reads it: a draft does not move to versions that clash so, and the check names the clash. In a top whose layouts are its pages' forms, such as a deck with a cover and a slide, each page names its layout with the key `layout`, never by the component that draws it. A colour by place keys on where an item stands in its list, never on a word the item carries, since what an item is, its concept says. Every file format 1 accepted reads and draws as before.

## Context

The founding record has a pattern arrange one or more lists of items, and an item component draw each item. For each list, the pattern names the component that draws its items by default. Content may name another for the whole list, and an item may name its own. An item's fields are its item component's, with any the pattern declares for its arrangement, each checked by the schema that declares it. A component's option takes a word from a list it declares. A top's layouts are the forms its pages take, and each layout has slots that content fills. Content holds no literal size, and chooses only among what its libraries declare ([visual-communication:DEC-001-visual-communication-discipline] points 3, 4 and 6).

Format 1 builds part of that:
- **A list's component.** The part of a pattern that repeats over a list names the list's default component, and an item names its own by `use`. Nothing names a component for a whole list.
- **An item's keys** are its component's fields and options, and the reserved keys. The capability's check reads a repeated list's items against the components that draw them. It never reads the list field's own `items` schema in the pattern's fields.
- **Sizes.** The items of a list each take the size they ask for, or share the length of their row or column equally.
- **Colour.** The part that repeats over a list gives every item of it one default colour family.
- **Layouts.** A top declares one layout, whose slots are fields of the top. A slot marked as holding pages makes each item of its list a page, drawn by its item component ([visual-communication:DEC-005-engine-and-plugins] point 5).

The record of layouts gives each of a top's layouts the top's own drawing, which a brand may replace. A component that content names draws its item under every brand. So a page whose form a brand may draw names its layout, never a component, and content never names a brand's drawing. That record leaves to this one how a page names its layout among a top's several ([visual-communication:DEC-009-layout-defaults] points 1, 2 and 5, and Implications).

The capability's design work drew the talk's slides with a prototype ([project-kit#2424](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2424)):
- Each slide named its layout in a key of its own, such as `layout: slide`.
- A list of reasons named one card for all its items, with an option and a concept given once for every item.
- A row of panels took its widths from an option of the pattern, such as wide beside narrow.
- A pyramid shaded its levels by their place, from a ramp of colours.

The capability's next slice rests on this choice. Its deck's slides each take a layout, and the items of a row share its size by weights ([project-kit#2501](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2501)). Until validation accepts files at an older format number, the format grows only by additions that every file written before still meets ([visual-communication:DEC-003-change-over-time] point 3). A new reserved key would refuse a field of its name that format 1 accepts, since no component declares a field with a reserved key's name ([visual-communication:DEC-002-files-and-validation] point 4).

## Decision

**A pattern declares, for each list it repeats, a field in which content may name the list's component, and the arrangement fields its items may carry. An arrangement field takes a word the pattern declares. A weight is an arrangement field, whose word the pattern turns into a share. A list of ties also carries two end fields, each naming an item (refinement per [visual-communication:DEC-011-tie-ends]). A key that both an item's component and its list declare is read by neither. A top whose layouts are its pages' forms marks the field that holds its pages, and each of its layouts' slots is a field of a page. Each such page names its layout with the key `layout`, or names its component. A place's colour keys on the item's position, never on what the item carries.**

### 1. Content names a list's component in a field its pattern declares

- **A field of the pattern's own.** A pattern lets content name one component for every item of a list by declaring a field for it: the list's *component field*. The pattern still names the list's default component (DEC-001 point 6). A pattern with several lists declares one for each list it lets content choose for.
- **Spelled as content places a component.** Content gives the field a mapping that names a component by `use`, read with its library and never with a version (DEC-002 point 4). It may give words for that component's options, and `concept`, `state` and `override`, which mean there what they mean wherever content places a component. The methodology's container keeps its key free there too.
- **It places nothing.** No component is drawn where the field lies, and no part of the pattern places it or passes it on. So the check reads the field as this point says, and never as content placing a component: it asks none of the component's required fields of the field, and asks them of each item that takes it. The field gives no id and no field of the component, since an id names one thing, and a field holds what one item shows.
- **Given to each item that names no component.** Such an item is drawn by the component the field names. It takes each key the field gives that it does not give itself, and a key it gives replaces the field's whole: an item that gives its own `override` takes none of the field's.
- **An item that names its own component takes nothing from the field,** its concept included. Such an item may be another kind of thing than its list's, such as a note that ends a row of cards. In a list of ties every item is a tie, so an item that names its own component there is a tie too (refinement per [visual-communication:DEC-011-tie-ends]).
- **The item's choice first, then the list's, then the pattern's.** What draws an item is the component the item names, else the one its list's component field names, else the pattern's default.
- **A `use` like any other.** The piece locks the component's library (DEC-001 point 7). Content may not name there a component that a brand declares as a drawing, as it may name one nowhere (DEC-009 point 5). Every value an item takes from the field traces back to the field (DEC-001 point 3).
- **Where a pattern declares none,** content names a component item by item, as in format 1. This narrows the founding record's point 6, by which content may name another component for a whole list, to the lists whose pattern declares a component field. The capability's own patterns declare one for each list they repeat (Implications).

### 2. A pattern declares, for each list, the fields its items carry for its arrangement

- **Arrangement fields, and end fields** (refinement per [visual-communication:DEC-011-tie-ends]). For a list it repeats, a pattern declares the fields an item of that list may carry for the pattern's arrangement (DEC-001 point 6). Those that take a word are its *arrangement fields* (point 3). For a list of ties, each of which it draws as a connector between two other items, it also declares two *end fields*, each naming an item, beside the arrangement fields. Both lie on the item, beside the fields and options of whichever component draws it. So an item's keys come from two declarations: its component's, and its list's pattern's. The bullets below hold of both.
- **A declaration of their own.** The pattern declares them for each list, apart from its own fields and options. They do not lie in the list field's `items` schema, which format 1 never reads for a repeated list's items. A component format 1 accepted may hold such a schema, which would then bind items it never bound.
- **The pattern reads them, and the item component never does.** The pattern reads an arrangement field where it sizes or sets the item, such as the share of a row it takes (point 4). Nothing draws one as text, since what an item shows is its component's (DEC-001 point 6).
- **Names.** An arrangement field takes the shape of a name, and no reserved key's name, as every field does (DEC-002 point 4).
- **A key both claim is read by neither.** Where two libraries released apart each declare keys of one mapping, neither reads a key that both declare. On an item, that is a field or an option of its component that has the name of an arrangement field or an end field of its list. The component's field stays unfilled there. The arrangement field takes its default, and an end field gives no end (refinement per [visual-communication:DEC-011-tie-ends]). Either may be what the item's author meant, and a later release of either library may make the clash, so no reading of the key is safe.
- **Versions that clash so do not fit together.** A draft's render moves its lock only to versions that fit together (DEC-003 point 8). Versions under which an item of the piece carries a key that both its component and its list declare do not fit, for that piece. So the render holds one of the two libraries back, and states it (point 9). Where a person moves a piece's lock to such versions, or content gives such a key, the item is a finding.

### 3. An arrangement field takes a word the pattern declares

- **An option given on each item.** An arrangement field takes one word from a list the pattern declares, as an option of words takes one (DEC-001 point 6). The first word is the default, which an item that leaves the field out takes. So no arrangement field is required, and adding one asks nothing of content.
- **Never a number, a size, a raw value or text.** Content chooses only among what its libraries declare, and holds no literal size (DEC-001 point 3). What a word stands for, such as a share, lies in the pattern's data, where its formulas lie and a number with no unit may stand (DEC-005 point 1).
- **A size or a position in space, never a look.** A word sets how the pattern sizes its item and where it sets it, such as its share of a row or its side of a line. It never sets a colour, a role or a state. What an item is, its concept and its state say, and how it looks inside, its component draws (DEC-001 point 6, and point 6 below).
- **The pattern chooses by it as by an option.** The pattern gives each word its effect, read for each item, as it gives an option's word its effect, so the choice is declared, never computed. The record of the engine names two ways a component chooses, by an option or by whether a field is filled, and lets the content's order and the declared options decide how items are arranged (DEC-005 points 1 and 2). This record widens the option there to one given on each item, and changes nothing else in how a component chooses (Implications).
- **It never chooses, filters or sorts.** The items keep the content's order, and none is left out for what it holds (DEC-005 point 2).
- **No word names an item** (refinement per [visual-communication:DEC-011-tie-ends]). An option may name an item by its id (DEC-001 point 6), and an arrangement field names none. A list of ties names its ends in end fields (point 2).

### 4. A weight is an arrangement field, whose word the pattern turns into a share

- **Where it lies, and what it holds.** The founding record's point 6 lets a pattern declare fields for its items' arrangement, and an item's share of its row is the item's own. So a weight is an arrangement field. The founding record's point 3 says what it holds: a word the pattern declares, never a number, since content holds no literal size.
- **A share for each word.** The pattern gives each word of a weight a *share*, a number with no unit, in its data. The part that repeats over the list shares its length among the items in proportion to their shares, less the gaps between them. The lengths still come from the size the part gets, so a brand's sizes still set them.
- **Equal sizes are its plainest case.** Format 1's part shares its length equally, which is the case where every item takes the same share. So a weight widens how the layout operator that gives equal sizes shares a length, and adds no operator (DEC-005 point 2). CSS's flexible layout shares a row the same way among items whose base size is zero, by each item's grow factor.
- **An option where the shares are the pattern's.** A pattern whose shares follow from its arrangement alone, such as two panels, a wide one beside a narrow one, gives them by an option of its own (DEC-001 point 6). A weight serves a list of any length, where some items take more of the row than others.

### 5. A page names its layout with the key `layout`

- **Where it applies.** In a top whose layouts are the forms its pages take, such as a deck's cover and slide, each page is drawn in a layout (DEC-009 point 1). Format 1's tops each hold one layout, so none is such a top. The change that gives a top several layouts brings them (DEC-009 point 8).
- **The field that holds the pages.** Such a top marks one of its fields as holding its pages: a list, each item a page. The field lies in no layout, since each page is drawn in a layout of its own. So in such a top a field the top marks declares its pages, where a top with one layout declares them by a slot (DEC-005 point 5).
- **A layout's slots are a page's fields.** Each layout's slots are what a page in that layout fills, which the top declares for that layout. They are no fields of the top, as format 1's slots are. A drawing of the layout takes them, with the top's other fields, its options and its states (DEC-009 point 5).
- **The key `layout`.** Each page of such a top names its layout by the key `layout`, whose value is the name of one of the top's layouts. The format fixes the key, so the pages of every such top read alike, for people, agents and translators. The key binds only a page drawn in a layout, which places no component. So it refuses no field format 1 accepts, and such a top declares no slot named `layout` for its pages.
- **Every page names one.** A page names its layout, or the component that draws it, and no page takes a layout by default. So a layout a top adds, or a new order of its layouts, changes no page.
- **Never by its drawing.** A page whose form a brand may draw names its layout, never the component that draws that layout (DEC-009 point 5). So switching the brand changes how the page looks, and leaves its content as it is (DEC-001 point 5).
- **A page's content is its layout's.** A page carries `layout`, the reserved keys and the slots its layout declares, and nothing else (DEC-009, Implications). The methodology's container keeps its key free there too. The check judges each page against the layout it names. The layout chooses which drawing draws the page, so it is no content a drawing takes, and counts for no drawing's fit (DEC-009 point 6).
- **Drawn by its layout's drawing.** That is the brand's, where the piece's brand draws the layout and its drawing fits the page, else the top's own (DEC-009 points 1, 2 and 6). A page in a layout has no item component.
- **A page that names its own component** by `use` is drawn by that component under every brand (DEC-009 point 2). Its keys are that component's, so it takes no layout. A `layout` key on it is that component's field, where the component declares one, and otherwise a key the check refuses. The list of such a top's pages has no component field and no arrangement field, since a page's content is its layout's or its component's.
- **A top with one layout for the whole top,** such as format 1's deck: its parts are that layout's drawing, and they place each page as an item, drawn by its item component (DEC-009 point 1). Its pages name no layout.

### 6. A place's colour keys on the item's position, never on what it carries

- **An item's place** is its position in the order of the items its list draws. A weight sets the item's share, never its place, since no operator sorts items by what they hold (DEC-005 point 2).
- **A family for each place.** A pattern may give each place of a list a colour family of its own, in its data, and may switch those families on an option (DEC-001 point 6). A pyramid with no notation shades its levels so. Format 1's one family for a list is the case where every place takes the same.
- **A place past those the pattern names** takes no family from the pattern, and the order goes on to its item component's. The pattern's families do not start again from the first, since two places would then take one family with nothing to say they are alike.
- **Never on an arrangement field.** A word an item carries to choose its colour would say what the item is. An item says that by naming a concept, which a notation maps to a family ([visual-communication:DEC-004-brands-and-notations] point 10). A condition of the item, such as marked, is a state its component declares, which may change the family (DEC-001 point 6).
- **Items that belong together** name one concept. Or they are the items of an inner list that one item of an outer list holds: that outer item takes its family by its own concept or place, and passes it to what sits in it, where nothing nearer gives one (DEC-001 point 6).
- **The order stands.** An item's family is its concept's, else its place's, else its item component's, else that of the component it sits in, and a state may change it (DEC-001 point 6).

### 7. What breaks

A component field, an arrangement field and each of its words, the field that holds a top's pages, and a layout a page names are names a piece uses. So the record of change over time already counts removing or renaming any of them, or changing what it means, as a breaking change of its library ([visual-communication:DEC-003-change-over-time] point 6). Beyond it:

- **Breaking, for a pattern:** marking a field it holds as a list's component field, since what content gave that field changes its meaning.
- **Breaking, for a top:** making its layouts its pages' forms, or ceasing to, since every page's content changes with it (point 5).
- **Not breaking:** a new component field, a new arrangement field, a new word or a new layout. Nor is a change of a word's share, of an arrangement field's default or of the family a place takes, which changes how pieces look. None changes what an item already says (DEC-003 point 6), and a piece meets each only when its lock moves (DEC-003 point 8).
- **A clash two releases make.** A new arrangement field, or a new field or option of an item component, may take a name the other already declares for the same item. Neither library can see it when it releases, so neither release breaks. A draft's render does not move into it, and a person who moves a lock into it meets it as a finding (point 2).

### 8. What this adds to format 1, and what still holds

- **Added, each optional:**
  - a pattern's component field for a list
  - a pattern's arrangement fields for a list, each with its words
  - a share for each word of a weight, by which a part shares its length
  - a colour family for each place of a list
  - a top whose layouts are its pages' forms, with the field that holds its pages, its layouts' slots as a page's fields, and the key `layout` on each page, which come with the change that gives a top several layouts (DEC-009 point 8)
- **The schema of a content file keeps its keys.** It names no key of an item or a page, so the additions add none there. A component field is spelled as content places a component, and read as point 1 says.
- **How content is read.** The check reads an item's keys against its component's fields and options, the reserved keys, and the arrangement fields and end fields of its list (refinement per [visual-communication:DEC-011-tie-ends]). It reads a page in a layout against that layout. A repeated list's `items` schema stays unread, as in format 1.
- **What still holds.** Format 1's schemas refuse a key they do not know, so no component they accepted declares a component field, an arrangement field, a share or a family for a place. No top they accepted has layouts that are its pages' forms. Every item format 1 accepted carries only its component's fields and options and the reserved keys, and they keep their meaning. Every file format 1 accepted is read and drawn as before, and none of point 9's findings arises from one.

### 9. What the checks report, and how severely

Each finding below fails validation where it lies in a file the project can correct, as the record of files and validation decides ([visual-communication:DEC-002-files-and-validation] point 7). Elsewhere, as in a released library version, it asks for attention and names who can correct it.

- **Where each lies.** A finding on what a component declares lies in that component's version. A clash with a pattern's default component is judged when the pattern's library is checked alone, against the newest version the project holds of each major it names, as a brand is checked ([visual-communication:DEC-008-library-role-defaults] point 1). A finding on what content gives lies in the content file, at the item, the page or the field. A clash that only the versions a piece locks show lies at the piece's lock, which combines them.
- **Fails validation:**
  - in a pattern: a component field or an arrangement field declared for a list the pattern does not repeat, a component field that is no field of the pattern, or one that a part of the pattern places or passes on
  - in a pattern: an arrangement field with no word or with one word twice, or a word whose effect sets a colour, a role or a state
  - in a pattern: an arrangement field that the list's default component also declares as a field or an option, since the pattern chose both
  - in a top whose layouts are its pages' forms: no field marked as holding its pages, or two; a slot named `layout`; or a component field or an arrangement field for its list of pages
  - in content: a word an arrangement field does not take, or a key on an item that is no field or option of its component, no arrangement field or end field of its list and no reserved key (refinement per [visual-communication:DEC-011-tie-ends])
  - in content: a component field that names no component, or gives an id or a field of its component
  - in content: a page that names neither a layout nor a component, a layout its top does not declare, or a key that is no slot of its layout and no reserved key
  - at a piece's lock: an item that carries a key that both its component and its list declare (point 2)
- **Stated as a fact:**
  - at a piece: a library that its draft's render held back for such a clash, with the item and the key
  - at a piece's lock: a key that both an item's component and its list declare, where no item carries it, so its author learns of the clash before writing one

## Rationale

**Why a field the pattern declares, and not a reserved key.** A reserved key means the same in every mapping, so no component may declare a field of its name (DEC-002 point 4). A new one would refuse each component that declares such a field, which format 1 accepts, and the format grows only by additions until validation accepts older files (DEC-003 point 3). A pattern with two lists also needs a name for each, which one reserved key cannot give. A field the pattern declares refuses nothing, and the pattern names it as it names its other fields.

**Why the founding record's promise narrows to the patterns that declare one.** A key the format gave every list, with no pattern declaring it, would need a name derived from the list's. Joined to the list's name by a mark no name holds, it would put a path mark into a key. Spelled outside the shape of a name, it reads as the format's mark rather than a word of the pattern (Alternatives considered). A pattern that declares no component field still draws what content wants: each item names its component, as in format 1. The capability's own patterns each declare one, so the promise holds wherever its starter is used.

**Why it is spelled as content places a component, yet places nothing.** Content's author writes one shape wherever content names a component, with `use`, option words and a concept. The prototype named a list's component so, under a key the pattern chose. What the field holds differs from a slot's content only in what is drawn there: nothing, since it gives each item its component. The pattern's README names the field, and its name, chosen by the pattern, says what it holds.

**Why it gives no id and no field.** An id names one placed thing, and the field gives one component to many items. A field holds what one item shows, and every drawn part traces back to the field it shows, so an edit taken back from a renderer changes one item's words (DEC-001 point 3). Given once for a list, one field would show in every item, and an edit of one item would change them all. An option's word, a concept, a state and an override are no words an item shows, so each serves every item at once.

**Why arrangement fields lie apart from the list's schema.** The list field's `items` schema is where JSON Schema says what a list holds. Format 1 never reads it for a repeated list, whose items their components judge. So a component format 1 accepted may declare one there that requires a field no item gives. Read as arrangement fields, it would refuse items format 1 accepted. A declaration of its own appears in no file format 1 accepted, and keeps what the pattern reads apart from what an item's component takes.

**Why neither reads a key both claim.** An item's keys come from two libraries, released apart: the pattern's and the item component's. A pattern's owner cannot know every component content will name for its items, and a component's owner cannot know every pattern that will draw it. Each can add a name in a release that breaks nothing on its own side (point 7). With the component's names first, an item component that adds a field would take the key from the pattern, and a weight's word would become text the card shows. With the arrangement field first, a pattern that adds one would take the key from the component, and words the card showed would be read as a weight. Either way, an item's meaning would change with no one told. Read by neither, the key changes no meaning, and the check names it. The pattern's own default component is the pattern's choice, so a clash with it is the pattern's to mend.

**Why a draft does not move into a clash.** The capability's records keep a library's addition from failing a piece: a library's own roles take defaults below every brand (DEC-008 point 1), and a top's own drawing draws what a brand's drawing cannot (DEC-009 point 6). Were a clash only a finding, an addition in either library would fail a draft at its next render, since the render moves a draft's lock to the newest versions (DEC-003 point 8). Counted as versions that do not fit, the clash holds one library back instead, and a fact names the item and the key. The render already reads which libraries the piece's content uses, so it reads which keys its items carry with no new source. A piece fails only where a person moved its lock into the clash, or its content gave the key, and both are mended in the piece's own files.

**Why a word, never a number.** A free number would let content set each item's size, which the founding record keeps out of content (DEC-001 point 3). No set of renders could show every value, while a word from a declared list is checked by the schema, and drawn once for each word (DEC-001, Rationale). What an item shows is its component's, so an arrangement field holds no text either. The first word as the default keeps every arrangement field optional, so a pattern adds one without asking anything of content.

**Why an arrangement field is an option given on each item.** It takes a word from a declared list, and the pattern switches on that word, as on an option's. So it adds no third way for a component to choose, and the record of the engine's rule that a choice is declared holds of it (DEC-005 point 1). What it adds is where the word is given: on each item, since the choice is the item's own. A word that set a look would give content a second way to say what an item is, beside its concept and its state, so the word sets a size or a position in space alone.

**Why such a top marks the field that holds its pages.** In a top with one layout, the slot of pages lies in that layout, whose drawing places each page as an item (DEC-005 point 5). Where each page takes a layout of its own, the list of pages lies in none of them: inside a page's layout it would be a page inside a page, which the record of the engine counts an error. So the top marks the field, and a layout's slots are what a page fills. That is what the record of layouts has a drawing take: a page's content in its layout (DEC-009 point 5, and Implications).

**Why a page names its layout with one key the format fixes.** A component that content names draws under every brand, so it would fix the page's form whatever the brand, which the record of layouts refuses (DEC-009 point 5). A page in a layout places no component, so no component's field can take the key there, and fixing it costs no field format 1 accepts. One key for every such top lets every deck's pages read alike, as the prototype's slides named their layouts. A key each top named for itself would read differently in each deck, and each top would still need to keep its pages' slots off that name.

**Why every page names its layout.** A default would be the top's first layout, or one the top marks. A top whose release added a layout before its first, or marked another, would move every page that named none into a layout with other slots. Each such page would then fail its new layout, after a change that the record of change over time counts as an addition (DEC-003 point 6). Named on every page, the layout changes only when the content changes it. A deck's author writes one word on each page, as the prototype's slides did.

**Why a weight is a word.** The two rules the question names hold together: the founding record's point 6 places the weight on the item, and its point 3 keeps the number out of content. The prototype gave a row of panels its widths by an option of the pattern, which serves a fixed number of panels. The next slice asks for weights on a row's items ([project-kit#2501](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2501), T9). The item names a word, and the pattern holds the share, so the number stays in the library. Shares widen the equal sizes format 1 gives, so they need no operator of their own.

**Why colour keys on place, never on a word an item carries.** Colour says what a thing is (DEC-001 point 6). A word an item carries to pick its colour would give content a second way to say what the item is, outside the notation, in one pattern's own words. A component library would then carry a subject's categories, with none of a concept's guarantees: one meaning in every piece and under every brand, and an icon or a shape that keeps colour from being its only sign (DEC-004 point 10). A place gives content no such way. The pattern alone colours by place, as the founding record's pyramid with no notation shades its levels (DEC-001, Rationale).

### Alternatives considered

- **A new reserved key beside a list, which names its component.** Rejected. It refuses a field of its name that format 1 accepts (DEC-002 point 4, DEC-003 point 3).
- **A key for each list, joined from the list's name and `use` by a mark.** Rejected. It puts a path mark into a key, which DEC-002 point 4 keeps out of every name, so that a later reading can name a part of a file by the keys that lead to it.
- **Keys spelled outside the shape of a name, such as with `_`, for a list's component and for arrangement fields.** No field can take such a key, so it refuses nothing format 1 accepted, as the keys that bind a file do (DEC-002 points 3 and 4), and no clash could ever arise. Rejected. Content's authors write arrangement fields on the items of every list, and a key spelled so reads as a mark of the format, not a word of the pattern. The format would also fix how every pattern spells its own names. The clash it removes needs two libraries to choose one name for one item, which point 2 keeps from changing any meaning.
- **A list written as a mapping that holds its component and its items.** Rejected. A list field would take two shapes, and every path to an item would change.
- **A component field that also gives each item a field.** Rejected. One field would show in every item, and an edit taken back would change them all.
- **Arrangement fields in the list field's `items` schema.** Rejected. It would refuse items format 1 accepted, where such a schema requires a field.
- **Arrangement fields under one key of each item.** Rejected. Spelled as a name, that key would be a new reserved key. Spelled outside the shape of a name, it is the alternative above.
- **The component's names first, or the arrangement field's first, where both claim a key.** Rejected. Each changes what items say, with no one told, when the other library adds the name: the first when an item component adds a field, the second when a pattern adds an arrangement field.
- **A clash that fails a draft at its next render.** Rejected. A release that breaks nothing on its own side would fail a piece the project did not change, which no other addition does.
- **Arrangement fields that hold numbers or text.** Rejected, for the reasons above.
- **A layout field each top names for itself.** Rejected. The pages of every deck would name their layout by another key, and each top would still keep its pages' slots off that name.
- **A page that takes its top's first layout by default.** Rejected. A layout added before the first would move every page that named none into a layout with other slots, after a change counted as an addition.
- **A page that names its layout as the component that draws it,** such as a brand's drawing read with the brand's name. Rejected. Content never names a brand's drawing, and a component content names draws under every brand (DEC-009 points 2 and 5).
- **The list of pages as a slot of every layout.** Rejected. Each page would hold the list of pages again, a page inside a page.
- **A weight as a number with no unit.** Rejected. Content would hold a size in all but name.
- **Shares only by an option of the pattern.** Rejected. An option serves a fixed shape, not a list of any length.
- **A family keyed on an arrangement field, such as a group the item names.** Rejected. It would be a concept that one pattern defines, with none of a notation's guarantees.
- **Families that start again past the last place a pattern names.** Rejected. Two places would take one family with nothing to say they are alike.
- **Roles named by a place, such as a ramp from a first level's role to the last's.** Rejected. A role's name would be computed from the place, where every role a component reads is written in its file. A family for each place is written in the pattern.

## Implications

- **The capability's pages** describe a list's component field and arrangement fields, with their findings, when the change that shares a row by weights builds them ([project-kit#2501](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2501), T9). They describe a top whose layouts are its pages' forms, with the key `layout`, with the change that gives the deck its layouts (T3), and a family for each place with the first pattern that colours its places apart.
- **The spelling is the reference's.** The change that builds each addition sets how a pattern declares a component field and its arrangement fields with their words, how it gives a share for each word and a family for each place, and how a top marks the field that holds its pages and declares its layouts' slots.
- **The starter's patterns** each declare a component field for each list they repeat, so that content may name another component for a whole list, as the founding record intends (DEC-001 point 6). The starter's README names each pattern's arrangement fields with their words, as it names its options. They count toward the guide of about four options a pattern holds ([visual-communication:DEC-007-starter-library] point 2).
- **The change that gives a top several layouts** (DEC-009 point 8) builds the top of point 5. It also decides:
  - what such a top draws around its pages, and how it draws each page by its layout's drawing
  - whether a slot that holds one page, rather than a list, names a layout the same way
  - how a top that is a page alone, such as a figure, would choose among several forms of its own. There the page is the content file's top, which places a component, and a format 1 top may declare a field named `layout`, so the key of point 5 does not carry over as it stands.
- **The record of ties** ([visual-communication:DEC-011-tie-ends]) builds on how content gives a list. No arrangement field names an item: a tie names its ends in end fields its pattern declares, and point 2's rule for a key both claim applies to them.
- **Translation.** A page's layout, an item's component, and an arrangement field's or option's word are written in each language's content. Whether the check holds each equal in every language, and how it matches an item or a page of one language to another's, such as by id and else by place, is left to translation (DEC-001 point 3, and Implications).
- **The release's comparison** of a library's stable components with its previous version (DEC-003 point 7) reads component fields, arrangement fields and their words, the field that holds a top's pages, and whether a top's layouts are its pages' forms, under point 7.
- **Trace ids stay as they are.** An item's trace id still comes from its list's part and the item's id or place (DEC-005 point 5). A value an item takes from its list's component field names that field as its source.
- **Five accepted records take an edit once this one is accepted,** each citing this record. A person accepts each edit (the decisions README, "Refining an accepted record").
  - The founding record's point 6: content names another component for a whole list where its pattern declares a field for it.
  - The record of files and validation's point 3: the strict reading also takes the keys an item's list's pattern declares for it, and the slots a page's layout declares. Its point 4: the reserved keys, and the methodology's container's key, bind a component field's mapping and a page in a layout too, and the format fixes `layout` on a page in a layout alone.
  - The record of change over time's point 8: versions under which an item of a piece carries a key that both its component and its list declare do not fit together for that piece.
  - The record of the engine's points 1 and 2: an option by which a component chooses may be one a pattern declares for each item. Its point 5: in a top whose layouts are its pages' forms, the field the top marks declares its pages.
  - The record of layouts' point 5 and its Implications cite this record, in place of [project-kit#2498](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/2498), for how a page names its layout. Its Implications also say what this record decides of how a page relates to its item component: a page in a layout has none, the list of pages has no component field, and a page that names its own component is drawn by it. They leave to the change that gives a top several layouts how the top draws each page by its layout's drawing.
