> **Partially superseded by [visual-communication:DEC-070-ship-call-and-one-round].** Overturned: what this record says of the review's grades, the loop's end by a verdict, the hold or a calibration. Among it: in the Context's bullet "The designer writes what is not yet released", its sentences on the verdict that ends the designer's loop and on what lapses the hold; in the Rationale's "Why the word never reaches the review", the words "and no calibration has measured how the review reads a meant overlap" and "it moves no grade and ends no loop"; in the alternative "A released word gives the review the fact", the words "and nothing calibrated the review's reading of a meant overlap", and its trigger to revisit, which reads as DEC-070 point 9 gives it; in the alternative "The word in the message of `box-overlap`", what it says of grades; and, in the Implications, the items read against DEC-042 points 6 and 9 and DEC-065 point 1. What keeps a bad design out stays the review's reading of the drawn result, which no word reaches. The rest stands, and those passages stay as they stood.

**In plain terms:** a component may place one of its parts on another on purpose, such as a badge on a card's corner. The check of boxes that overlap cannot tell that from a fault, so it asks the reviewer to judge it in every piece. This record lets a part name, under `lies-over`, the parts of its own component that it lies over on purpose. Where a released version of a library, still as it was released, names a pair, validation states their overlap as a fact, which asks no one to act. Anywhere else, as in a piece's own component or in a version not yet released, naming the pair gives the check nothing. The word never reaches the review, save a refusal's message, which names, beside its part, only a name refused: the reviewer is given every overlap as before, and judges each on the image. So a word settles no overlap for the review and moves no grade. The check of edges that nearly line up compares the pair as before. A meant overlap is still reported in every piece, which overturns the aim of [visual-communication:DEC-046-faults-lower-grades] point 3 that it not be reported for ever.

## Context

DEC-046 point 3 has two checks do the arithmetic of craft among the *siblings* of each scene, the elements one group holds directly. One reports edges that nearly line up. The other reports two siblings whose boxes overlap, where neither holds the other. Both ask the reviewer for attention and never fail, since a component may place one part on another on purpose, such as a badge on a corner. The reviewer judges each place they name on the image, and says whether it is a fault or is meant. The point leaves it to the work that builds the checks to settle how a component says an overlap is meant, "so that it is not reported for ever". Where that adds a field, the work drafts the record first (COR-065 point 3).

[project-kit#3327](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3327) is that work. It asks three things: how a component says it; whether saying it also leaves the pair out of the check of edges that nearly line up; and how a library the capability ships says it, in a new version ([visual-communication:DEC-013-shipped-library-releases]).

The answer meets six things that already hold:
- **Parts are drawn in the order written,** and the scene keeps that order among each group's elements. So of two siblings, the later is drawn over the earlier ([visual-communication:DEC-005-engine-and-plugins] point 5). A part's name ends the trace id of what it draws. Parts are written in a component's `parts`, in a top's drawing of a layout, and in a brand's drawing ([visual-communication:DEC-009-layout-defaults] point 1, [visual-communication:DEC-034-brand-drawings] point 1).
- **The checks of boxes read scenes,** each element's box and trace id, and no component's file (DEC-046 point 3, DEC-005 point 13).
- **The frames, the report and validation carry a component's word only as a check's finding.** The viewing step's frames hold each element's box and trace id, and its report what the checks found on each image and each check's reach ([visual-communication:DEC-062-checks-beside-the-frames] points 1 and 2). Validation shows what the checks found. The reviewer reads what a check found from the report alone, never from a piece's findings record ([visual-communication:DEC-015-viewing-step-and-reviewer] point 7), and judges an overlap on the image and from the frames (DEC-046 point 3, [visual-communication:DEC-063-where-a-check-measures] point 4). It reads components where its records send it: a piece's own, with the piece (DEC-015 point 7), and those that draw a part where no check measured whether a size hides it (DEC-062 point 3, DEC-063 point 4). A check's silence at a place of a kind its reach measures reads as measured and clean (DEC-063 point 3). So DEC-062 gives every overlap, since a pair left out "would read as measured and clean by the check of boxes that overlap, while the page shows the overlap" (DEC-062, "Why no overlap is left out").
- **A finding that never fails asks for attention or states a fact.** It asks for attention when someone is expected to act, and names who, and states a fact when no act is expected. A finding read from a heuristic or a threshold that no record makes an invariant asks for attention ([visual-communication:DEC-002-files-and-validation] point 7). The check of overlaps is such a reading, since whether an overlap it finds is a defect needs a judgement (DEC-046 point 3), and it names the reviewer. Two kinds of finding state a fact, though a reader still reads the places they name: a check's statement that it could measure nothing at a place, since a reader that relies on the check "judges the places it covers itself, as part of its own reading, which no finding asks of it" (DEC-063 point 2); and what a component hides at a size: "So a reviewer sees what a reader at that size does not get" ([visual-communication:DEC-021-size-and-shape] point 5).
- **A released version never changes.** A change goes into the version being built ([visual-communication:DEC-003-change-over-time] point 6, DEC-013 points 3 and 4). The location's list marks which versions are released, with a hash of each, so the check reads from the files whether a version is released and still holds what was released (DEC-003 point 4). A copy of a library the capability ships is listed released with the hash the capability records for that version (DEC-013 point 5). The level of a finding that lies in a version follows from that (DEC-002 point 7, DEC-005 point 14), worked out each time the check reads the finding and never stored (the list of checks, "A library version the project does not correct"). So neither the scene nor the key of a piece's findings record, a hash of every file its scenes were computed from, holds whether a version is released (DEC-005 points 5 and 13). Whoever maintains the capability releases a version of a library it ships (DEC-013 point 1).
- **The designer writes what is not yet released.** It writes a piece's own components and a version of the project's own library not yet released, and, where the capability is authored, a shipped library's version being built. It writes no released version. What commands write, a release among them, stays theirs, and the designer runs those commands where its body says ([visual-communication:DEC-030-design-agents] points 4 and 5). Where the verdict ends its loop at a project's bar, under the *hold* that an accepted calibration switches on, it mends each grade that fails the bar (DEC-030 point 2, [visual-communication:DEC-042-grades-bar-and-loop] points 6 and 9). A check's value that the piece's files fix, or that a version of a library its lock names holds, lapses no hold, and a record that has a check take a value from elsewhere decides how a change to it is seen ([visual-communication:DEC-065-checks-lapse-the-hold] point 2).

## Decision

**A part names, under `lies-over`, the parts before it in its `parts` that it lies over on purpose. Where a released version, still as it was released, holds the word, validation states the overlap as a fact; anywhere else the word gives the check nothing. The review never reads the word, save a refusal's message, which names, beside its part, only a name refused: it is given every overlap as before, judges each on the image, and takes no word it reads in a component as a ruling.**

### 1. A part names the parts it lies over on purpose

- **`lies-over`** is a key any part may take: a list of the names of parts written before it in the same `parts`, and so drawn before it. It says that the part lies over each of them on purpose, and this record calls it the part's *word* on each pair it names. A badge drawn over a card's box writes `lies-over: [box]`.
- **Wherever parts are written:** in a library's component or a piece's own, in a top's drawing of a layout, and in a brand's drawing. Where the word gives the fact, and where it gives the check nothing, is point 2's.
- **Only the parts the check compares with it.** It names parts of that same `parts` alone, since the check of overlaps compares siblings alone (DEC-046 point 3), and the parts of one `parts` are drawn as siblings. So it names no part inside a component a part places, and no item of a list. A part that places a component, holds a slot or repeats over a list is named whole, by what it draws, as the check compares it. A change that widens the check of overlaps beyond siblings decides whether `lies-over` may name more.
- **Of boxes, as the check is.** `lies-over` speaks of the parts' boxes, which the check compares, not of what is drawn in them. A component may name a pair whose boxes overlap where their drawing does not, such as a text whose line spacing reaches into the box of the part above it. That use is meant: the overlap of the boxes is the component's design, as any other is. Whether what is drawn touches stays the reviewer's to see on the image, since "a box may differ from what is drawn in it" (DEC-046 point 3).
- **Always, or not at all.** It takes no switch. Wherever the two parts overlap, under any option, at any size and in any language, the component says that the overlap is meant. Where they do not overlap, or where the check does not compare them, as where one is a line or holds the other, it changes nothing.
- **Refused when the component is read:** a name that is no part of the same `parts`, the part's own name, the name of a part written after it, and a name's second writing in the list. The order of drawing decides which part lies over which, so a part that named one drawn after it would say the opposite of what is drawn. A name refused says nothing: at a pair no other writing names, the check asks for attention, as at any pair not named. A name's first writing names its part.
- **Each refusal is a finding** that names the part and the name refused, at the level DEC-002 point 7 gives by who can correct the file that holds it. It fails where the project can, as in a piece's own component or a version of the project's own library not yet released. Elsewhere it asks for attention and never fails, naming who can correct it: a released version's owner; and inside a copy of a library the capability ships, released or not, whoever maintains the capability (DEC-003 point 9).

### 2. The scene carries it, and validation states each overlap a released version names as a fact

- **In the scene.** The element that a part with `lies-over` draws holds `lies-over`: the trace ids of the elements the parts it names draw in that scene, and the library's version that holds the part, where a library's version holds it. A piece's own component lies in no library's version, so its word names none. The scene holds no mark of whether that version is released: it states what the files say, and release status is applied where the finding is read (below). A part it names that draws nothing there, such as a slot left empty or a part whose `when` does not hold, adds none.
- **Released, and still as it was released.** The word's version reads so where the location's list marks it released and its folder still holds what was released, as the list's hash shows (DEC-003 point 4). For a copy of a library the capability ships, the hash the list keeps must also be the one the capability records for that version (DEC-013 point 5): a mark that no release of the capability's gave never reads so. A version from another repository reads so as one of the project's own library does, since it is fetched whole at its release and listed so, with its hash (DEC-003 point 4). Wherever this record speaks of a released version's word, it means a word in a version that reads so.
- **The mark lies in the list.** Which versions are released, and the hash of each, the location's list holds (DEC-003 point 4), which is neither the piece's files nor a version its lock names, and commands rewrite it (DEC-002 point 5). This record adds no rule of who writes it, and decides how the hold sees a change to it (Implications, on DEC-065 point 2).
- **No property the element draws.** It may stand on an element of any type, and no renderer draws it, so it counts among what no element draws, beside the element's type, its trace id, its sources and its children. So it names no source, no renderer's declaration lists it, and no loss names it (DEC-005 points 5 and 9). The seam's version stays as it is, since no plugin must read anything differently (DEC-005 point 8).
- **In validation, a released version's word gives a fact.** The check of boxes that overlap compares every two siblings as before. Where two overlap, and the later names the earlier under `lies-over`, its finding carries the version that holds the word, where one does, and its label and message are settled where the finding is read. Where that version is released and still as it was released, validation states the overlap as a fact, `box-overlap-meant`, in place of the call for attention the list of checks labels `box-overlap`. Its message names the pair, how far they overlap and which lies over which, as the message of an overlap does, and says that the part on top names the part beneath under `lies-over`. It points where `box-overlap` points.
- **Any other word gives the check nothing.** Where the later names the earlier under `lies-over` anywhere else, as in a piece's own component or in a version not yet released, validation reads the finding as `box-overlap`, a call for attention to the reviewer, exactly as at a pair no one named, and its message says nothing of the word. The reading of the component still refuses what point 1 refuses, and a word in a version not yet released gives the fact once that version is released.
- **Read in validation alone.** Validation reads the finding so each time it reads it from the piece's findings record, which holds it with the word's version and no mark of release, as it works out a finding's level (the list of checks, "A library version the project does not correct"). So a release that leaves the version's folder byte for byte as it was turns the finding into the fact in validation at once, and changes no scene and no findings record's key (DEC-005 points 5 and 13). The viewing step reads each finding's level through the same reading, and gives every overlap's finding the plain label (Implications).
- **The review never reads the word,** save the message of a refusal, which names, beside its part, only a name refused (point 1). The viewing step's report gives every overlap as `box-overlap`, whatever names the pair, with nothing of the word in its message, and the reviewer reads what a check found from that report alone (DEC-015 point 7). It judges each such place on the image, a fault or meant, as it does at any overlap (DEC-046 point 3). So a word never settles an overlap for the review, and never ends a loop or moves a grade (Rationale, "Why the word never reaches the review").
- **Every overlap the part does not name** asks for attention as before: a badge named `lies-over: [box]`, which a long title pushes over the card's head, is reported at the head with `box-overlap`.
- **A fact, since it asks no act.** In validation, `box-overlap` asks the reviewer to rule on its place, a fault or meant (DEC-046 point 3, DEC-062 point 3). At a pair a released version names, its word gives that ruling in validation, a word that never changes (below). So validation's finding expects no one to act: not the piece's author, whom the check never names; not the version's owner, whose version says it; and not the reviewer, whose ruling the report asks of it, as at every overlap (above).
- **Who may give the ruling.** A reading of taste asks for attention wherever the judgement it calls for is still owed, and only a record lets a file give that judgement, and says whose file may and in which reading of the finding. This record refines DEC-002 point 7 to say so (Implications). For an overlap of two parts, it lets only a released version's word give it, and only in validation: a word fixed in a version that never changes, which DEC-002 point 7 sets apart from the files the project can still correct, such as a piece's own component and a version not yet released (Rationale, "Why only a released version's word gives the fact").
- **The reviewer takes no word as a ruling.** It judges on the image alone whether an overlap a `box-overlap` names is meant, and a `lies-over` it reads in any component's file, released or not, is no ruling. Its records send it to read components (Context), and its tools can read the repository ([visual-communication:DEC-070-ship-call-and-one-round] point 5), so it may meet the word there. Its body holds this line (Implications), which follows DEC-046 point 3's judging on the image, and this record rests on the reviewer keeping it (What it costs).
- **Measured, with a finding.** The check measured the pair, so the fact is a finding of what it measured, and no statement that it could measure nothing (DEC-063 point 2). Its reach gains no clause, and the pair reads as measured, with a finding, in validation and in the report alike (DEC-063 point 3).
- **Every other reading of the pair stays.** The viewing step still names a text that a part drawn after it covers, where it reads what covers a text ([visual-communication:DEC-037-text-the-page-hides] point 1). The check of contrast still measures a text against the fill under it ([visual-communication:DEC-004-brands-and-notations] point 8).

### 3. The check of edges that nearly line up compares the pair as before

- **`lies-over` speaks of an overlap, not of a line.** The check of edges that nearly line up compares the pair as it compares any two siblings (DEC-046 point 3).
- **A meant overlap still has edges meant to land somewhere.** A tab meant to lie over its panel's top edge is meant to lie flush with the panel's left edge too. It may lie off that edge by more than a pixel and by less than the brand's smallest step of space, and the check measures that edge whether or not the two parts overlap.
- **What it costs.** A part centred on another's edge, such as a dot on a card's corner, has its own edge on that side half its size away from the other's. Where the part is shorter on each axis than twice the brand's smallest step and two pixels, as such a dot is, the check does not compare those two edges, and compares the pair on its other lines on that axis (refinement per [visual-communication:DEC-076-centred-on-an-edge] point 1). The near miss of a longer part laid across an edge, such as a tab's indicator on a panel's border, and any other near miss a component means, such as optical overshoot, a round icon it sets a little past a margin so that it looks in line, are reported in every piece that draws the component. Each asks for attention and fails nothing, as every near miss does.
- **Left open:** whether a component may say that a near miss is meant. This record leaves it for a later record, which weighs one carrier for an owner's words on a part before it adds a key beside `lies-over` (Implications, "Left open"). The near misses a centre set on the other's edge gives are decided for every pair, named under `lies-over` or not, from the two parts' boxes: where the part so centred is shorter on each axis than twice the brand's smallest step and two pixels, the check does not compare the two at that edge (refinement per [visual-communication:DEC-076-centred-on-an-edge] point 1).

### 4. A library the capability ships says it in the version it builds

- **In the version being built.** A component of a library the capability ships, whose parts overlap on purpose, comes to say so in the library's version being built. A released version never changes, so it never says so (DEC-003 point 6, DEC-013 point 4). Whoever maintains the capability judges which overlaps are meant, as the owner of any library does, and releases the version (DEC-013 point 1).
- **A fact once released.** Until that version is released, a piece that draws it, where the capability is authored or from a copy not yet released, is asked for attention at the pair exactly as at a pair no one named (point 2). Its release makes the word give the fact, in validation, in each piece whose lock names the released version.
- **A patch at least.** Saying it, or ceasing to, changes no look and nothing that a piece or a library built on the library names, so it is no break (DEC-003 point 6). Nor is it an addition to the library, which would take a minor: the comparison of two versions names a library's additions kind by kind, and a part's own keys are of none of those kinds (DEC-003 point 1; the capability's page on files, "What a release compares"). So the version being built takes a patch at least, as for any change to it (DEC-013 point 3).
- **The release it needs reads `lies-over`.** A shipped version's release reads it in full, refusing any key the capability that releases it does not know, and names that capability's current version as the release the version needs (DEC-013 point 3, [visual-communication:DEC-014-own-library-needed-release] point 1). An installed capability older than that release cannot read the version. So the change that teaches the engine `lies-over` lands before, or with, the change that first writes `lies-over` into a shipped library's version being built.
- **A piece under an earlier released version,** which does not say it, is asked for attention at that overlap, until its lock moves to a released version that says it is meant. Who moves the lock is what the piece's state says (DEC-003 point 8). The attention fails nothing meanwhile.

## Rationale

**Why the component says it, not each piece.** The overlap is the component's design. Every piece that uses the component draws it, and none of their authors chose it. Said once, in a released version, it reaches every piece whose lock names that version, and holds as long as the parts it names do. A piece that accepted the overlap, as it accepts a renderer's loss, would have to say it again in every piece: the repetition this record removes. A piece's own component reaches one piece, so there the word would be that piece's answer to its own overlap, and it gives the check nothing (point 2).

**Why only a released version's word gives the fact.** A word in a released version is fixed: a released version never changes (DEC-003 point 6), and taking the word back takes a new version. A piece's own component and a version not yet released are files the project can still correct, where no word is fixed. The line is the one DEC-002 point 7 draws between such a file and a released version only its owner corrects, and the one point 4 reads a piece under a released version by.

**Why the word never reaches the review.** Given to the review, a word would settle there what the reviewer judges, whether an overlap is a fault or is meant (DEC-046 point 3), and no calibration has measured how the review reads a meant overlap (Alternatives, "A released word gives the review the fact"). Kept to validation, it moves no grade and ends no loop, so whoever writes or releases it, the designer among them, passes no design by it: what keeps a bad design out is the review's reading of the drawn result, which no word reaches.

**Why a fact, and not silence.** A check's silence at a place of a kind its reach measures reads as measured and clean (DEC-063 point 3). A validation that left the pair out would so tell its reader that the parts stand clear while the page shows them overlap, which DEC-062 rejected for the report (Context). A fact keeps the pair measured, with a finding, and says how far the parts overlap, which a silence cannot. A loss a piece accepts falls silent instead (DEC-005 point 9), since the loss report has no reach, so its silence claims no place measured.

**Why a fact, and not a call for attention.** A call for attention expects someone to act, and names who (DEC-002 point 7). `box-overlap` names the reviewer, and the act it expects is a ruling: whether the overlap is a fault or is meant, judged on the image and said in the review's report (DEC-046 point 3). At a pair a released version names, the version's word says it is meant, fixed for that version, and this record lets that word answer validation's finding (point 2). A call there would ask, piece by piece, a question that word has answered. The report still asks it of the reviewer, whose own judgement of the page no word gives (point 2). DEC-063 point 2 states a fact where a check could measure nothing (Context), and the check of content a size hides states a component's choice as a fact (DEC-021 point 5). The check of overlaps is a reading of taste, which DEC-002 point 7 has ask for attention, so this record refines that bullet: such a reading asks for attention wherever the judgement it calls for is still owed (point 2, and "Why DEC-002 point 7 is refined, and not overturned" below).

**What the fact gains, and what it does not.** Validation prints a call for attention as a warning and a fact as information, and prints both (COR-055 point 5; the CLI's page, `validate`). So it still prints a line for each overlap a released version means, in each piece that draws it, and none of those lines is a warning. No entry goes away: the overlap is still reported in every piece that draws it, for ever, in validation and in the report, which is the departure from DEC-046 point 3 (below).

**Why DEC-046 point 3 is overturned in part, and not refined.** The point's bullet "An overlap that is meant" gives the work its aim: a way for a component to say an overlap is meant, "so that it is not reported for ever". This record has each such overlap reported in every piece that draws it, for ever: in validation, as a fact where a released version says it and as a call for attention elsewhere, and in the report as a call for attention. Another record then decides otherwise than the point, so that clause is overturned, as DEC-062 overturned point 5 of the same record (DEC-062, "Why DEC-046 point 5 is overturned in part, and not refined"). What the rest of the bullet decides stands, and this record carries it out: the work settles how a component says an overlap is meant, and drafts the record first. The edit to the point's bullet on what the two checks ask refines it, since it decides what it decided at every place but, in validation, an overlap a released version names. The reason it gives, that whether an overlap is a defect needs a judgement since a component may place one part on another on purpose, is what the released word answers for validation's finding at the pairs it names.

**Why DEC-002 point 7 is refined, and not overturned.** The point's bullet "A reading of taste never fails" has a finding read from a heuristic or a threshold that no record makes an invariant ask for attention. The same point says when a finding that never fails asks for attention: when someone is expected to act ("Attention or a fact"). A reading of taste asks for it since whether its result is a defect needs a judgement, which is no check's ([visual-communication:DEC-028-principles-of-visual-communication] point 1), so someone is expected to give it. The edit narrows the bullet to what that reason reaches: a reading of taste asks for attention wherever the judgement it calls for is still owed. Where that judgement has been given, the point's own test, "Attention or a fact", decides the finding. The bullet's decision stands, a reading of taste never fails, and no record decides otherwise than the point, so the edit refines it in place (the decisions README, "Refining an accepted record"). Of who may give a judgement, the edit settles only that no file gives one unless a record lets it, and that such a record says whose file may and in which reading of the finding. At an overlap, this record lets only a released version's word give it, in validation alone (point 2). Worded so, the bullet names no check's label: a later record that lets a file answer another reading, such as a near miss that is meant (point 3), decides itself whose word that is.

**Why a part names parts.**
- **Not a flag on the part.** `lies-over: true` would make every overlap of that part meant. A badge meant to lie over the card's box, but pushed over its head by a long title, would then ask no attention at the head. Naming each part keeps the check's attention on every pair the component did not mean.
- **Not a list of pairs on the component.** It would say the same thing, away from the part it explains. On the part, the key lies beside what it speaks of, as `when` lies beside the part it draws or not.

**Why only parts drawn before it.** What the component means is that one part lies on top. The order of drawing decides which does (DEC-005 point 5), so the key reads in that order. A badge moved before the box would be hidden under it, which is a fault. The reading then refuses the name, and the check asks for attention at the pair again, rather than state as meant a badge no one can see.

**Why the scene carries it.** The check reads scenes, and compares siblings by their trace ids. A check that read each component's file would have to find again, for each group, the version and the drawing that drew it, which the engine has already done. A scene says which component drew each group for the same reason (DEC-005 point 5). The version that holds the part is carried with the names on the element, not beside the version its group names, since a part need not lie in that version, as a brand's drawing's parts lie in the brand's version while they draw a top's layout (DEC-034 point 1). Whether that version is released the scene does not carry: it states what the files say, and release status is applied where findings are read, as their levels are (Context). So a release that leaves the version's folder byte for byte as it was changes no scene, as two scenes with one root differ only in the text of an export's facts (DEC-005 point 5), and the findings record stays a fact about the files its key covers (DEC-005 point 13).

**Why it is no property the element draws.** It may stand on an element of any type, and says nothing a renderer could draw. Listed in a renderer's declaration, it would stand under every type there, and a renderer added later would have to list it under each type, or report a loss of it in every piece that names a meant overlap. Counted beside an element's type, its trace id, its sources and its children, it is one entry, in the code that finds the losses. A group's `component`, `version`, `options` and `page` no renderer draws either, yet when this record was drafted each renderer's declaration listed them under `group`. This record does not move them, so a follow-up picks one rule for both (Implications, "Left open").

**Why no switch.** The fact never hides an overlap: wherever a pair named so overlaps, under any option and at any size, every reader is told, with how far. So an overlap under an option the component did not mean it for is still shown, though it asks no act. A switch would cost the key a second form, with branches and a refusal in each, for a need no component of the libraries the capability ships had when this record was drafted (Implications). Without one, a component that means an overlap under some options or sizes alone leaves the pair unnamed, or draws the part as two parts, each with its `when`, whose trace ids then differ by option.

**Why the check of edges that nearly line up still compares the pair.** A word a component says reaches only what its words name. That an overlap is meant says where two parts share room, not where their edges lie, and a meant overlap still has edges meant to land somewhere (point 3). Leaving the pair out of both checks would let a meant overlap hide a part set a little off the edge it was meant to lie on. Whether such a near miss is meant is a question of near alignment. This record leaves it open, rather than settle it by a word that speaks of overlaps.

### What it costs

- **A line for each meant overlap a released version names,** in validation, in each piece where the parts overlap, for ever, beside the call for attention the report gives at every overlap. It states a fact, as a piece that uses an experimental component is stated (DEC-003 point 9), and asks no act. The aim of DEC-046 point 3, that a meant overlap not be reported for ever, is given up.
- **No call in validation, however far the parts overlap,** where a released version names them. A caption band meant to lie over a picture's foot, which a long caption wraps to three lines at one size or in one language, covers the picture's subject and asks validation's reader no attention. The fact names how far the band overlaps the picture, and the review still judges the place on the image (point 2).
- **A call for attention at a pair a version not yet released names,** in validation, exactly as at a pair no one named, until the version is released. No reader learns from the check that the pair is named. At a pair a piece's own component names, the call stays for as long as the component does.
- **The review still judges every overlap a component means,** in every round, as it judges any overlap: no word spares it one (point 2).
- **A mark written by hand gives the fact too.** A mark that a person or an agent writes by hand in the list, with the version's hash, gives the fact in validation, and no check sees it, since the list holds the mark and the folder matches the hash. That holds for a version of the project's own library, and for one from another repository whose folder and mark are written by hand where no command fetches it. The designer's grant covers the location, and only its body keeps it from writing either (DEC-030 point 5). Like a release, the mark reaches no review (point 2). A copy of a library the capability ships cannot be marked so, since its word reads as released only where its hash is the one the capability records (point 2).
- **The reviewer's line is an instruction.** This record keeps every finding the reviewer is handed free of the word, save the message of a refusal, which names, beside its part, only a name refused (points 1 and 2). That the reviewer takes no `lies-over` it reads in a component as a ruling rests on its keeping the line its body holds (point 2), as [visual-communication:DEC-070-ship-call-and-one-round] point 5's order of reading rests on its restraint. A name written twice is the one case where a refusal's message repeats a name the word gives, and there too only that line keeps it from the ruling. No check sees a breach.
- **No switch.** A component that means an overlap under some options or sizes alone asks for attention where the overlap is meant, or splits the part in two (Rationale).
- **Near misses a component means,** in every piece that draws it, such as those of a long part laid across an edge, and optical overshoot. A part shorter on each axis than twice the brand's smallest step and two pixels, centred on another's edge, gives none at that edge, which the check does not compare (point 3; refinement per [visual-communication:DEC-076-centred-on-an-edge] point 1).
- **A piece under an earlier released version** keeps the call for attention in validation until its lock moves (point 4).

### Alternatives considered

- **The check infers what is meant,** such as a small part on a corner. Rejected: whether an overlap is a fault needs a judgement, and only what needs none is a check's (DEC-028 point 1).
- **The check leaves out each pair named so,** its reach naming that kind of place among those it leaves out. Rejected, for the reason above: no reader could tell such a pair from one measured and clean, and DEC-062 gives every overlap for that reason.
- **The fact wherever the word lies,** in a piece's own component and in a version not yet released too, perhaps with the designer bound to ask the person before it writes such a word. Rejected: there the word lies in a file the project can still correct, which no release fixes, and which DEC-002 point 7 sets apart from a released version; asking the person first would fix no word, which the loop could still change in its next round (Rationale, "Why only a released version's word gives the fact").
- **A released word gives the review the fact,** the report giving `box-overlap-meant` and the reviewer giving the place no ruling. Not taken: it would let a word settle what the review judges, and nothing calibrated the review's reading of a meant overlap. Its trigger to revisit: a calibration that measures the review's judgement of overlaps.
- **The word in the message of `box-overlap`, wherever the word lies.** Rejected: validation's warnings would still count every meant overlap, and the report would carry the word to the review, which this record keeps from it (point 2). A word the loop wrote would be an account of its work, which DEC-042 point 4 has the review read none of as it grades; read only once the grades are written, as that point orders the brief's reasons, it would move none of them.
- **`lies-over` refused in a piece's own component,** as a finding that fails, since the project can correct it. There the word gives the check nothing anyway, and the refusal would close the main way the reviewer meets it, in the components it reads with the piece. Not taken: it would fail ordinary design work, and the word would no longer travel with a component promoted into a library. The reviewer would still meet words in a library's components (DEC-063 point 4), so the line of point 2 would be needed all the same.
- **The fact stated once, where the component's version is checked alone,** and in no piece, as a check states once for a brand that one of its picture roles has no value ([visual-communication:DEC-025-picture-files] point 9). Rejected. A version checked alone draws no piece, so it could state only that a part names others, never that they overlap in a piece, nor how far, which changes with the content, the language and the size. Each piece's findings would then give nothing at the pair, which brings back the leave-out above.
- **The mark in the scene,** whether the word's version is released carried with the names, and the list's entry for each locked version added to the findings record's key, so that a release stales the record. Not taken: release status is applied where a finding is read, as its level is (Context), so the mark would read one fact in two places. And two scenes with one root could then differ in that mark, against DEC-005 point 5, unless the root named it too.
- **`lies-over` with a switch,** on an option of words or on a size, one level deep, as a `repeat` part's `colour` takes one. Not taken, for the reasons above. Its trigger to revisit: a component that overlaps its parts on purpose under some options or sizes, and stands clear under others.
- **A piece that accepts a meant overlap too,** for a component of a library the project does not own, whose owner has not released a version that says it. Not taken: the call fails nothing meanwhile, and a piece's word would be one piece's answer to its own overlap, unshared and fixed by no release (Rationale, "Why the component says it, not each piece"), saying again piece by piece what the component's owner says once.
- **Two items of one list may be named.** When this record was drafted, no pattern of the libraries the capability ships laid two of its items over each other on purpose: each laid them out one after another, save its ties, which run between the items they join (Implications). Two ties that each draw their concept's sign may overlap as boxes while their lines stand apart, and that overlap asks for attention (DEC-046 point 3). Patterns elsewhere do lay items over each other, such as overlapping avatars, a fanned stack of cards or a Venn diagram's circles. A pattern lays out its items with an operator, which `lies-over` on a part cannot name, so such an overlap asks for attention as before. Its trigger to revisit: a component that lays its items over each other on purpose.
- **The name `over`,** as short as a part's other keys. Not taken. When this record was drafted, the starter's card, aside, level and code, the components likeliest to name a meant overlap, named a value `over` that their formulas read, so one file would hold one word in two senses. `over` may also read as an instruction to draw the part on top, where `lies-over` states how the parts lie. Nor `drawn-over`, which leaves unclear which part lies on top.
- **A word for a near miss that is meant,** beside `lies-over`. Not this record's: point 3 leaves it open.
