**In plain terms:** a component may place one of its parts on another on purpose, such as a badge on a card's corner. The check of boxes that overlap cannot tell that from a fault. Unless the component says so, the check asks for attention at that place in every piece that uses the component, for ever. So a part may name the parts of its own component that it is drawn over on purpose. The engine carries the names into the scene, which is what the check reads. The check then states each overlap of a pair named so as a fact, which asks no one to act, in place of asking for attention. It still names the pair, so every reader of the checks sees the overlap, and the reviewer still judges it on the image.
- **Only the check of overlaps.** The check of edges that nearly line up still compares the pair. That an overlap is meant says nothing of where the two parts' edges lie.
- **A library the capability ships** says it as it makes any other change: in the version it is building, which takes a patch at least.

## Context

[visual-communication:DEC-046-faults-lower-grades] point 3 has two checks do the arithmetic of craft among the *siblings* of each scene, the elements one group holds directly. One reports edges that nearly line up. The other reports two siblings whose boxes overlap, where neither holds the other. Both ask the reviewer for attention and never fail, since a component may place one part on another on purpose, such as a badge on a corner. The point leaves it to the work that builds the checks to settle how a component says an overlap is meant, so that it is not reported for ever. Where that adds a field, the work drafts the record first (COR-065 point 3).

[project-kit#3327](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3327) is that work. It asks three things: how a component says it; whether saying it also leaves the pair out of the check of edges that nearly line up; and how a library the capability ships says it, in a new version ([visual-communication:DEC-013-shipped-library-releases]).

The answer meets five things that already hold:
- **Parts are drawn in the order written,** and the scene keeps that order among each group's elements. So of two siblings, the later is drawn over the earlier ([visual-communication:DEC-005-engine-and-plugins] point 5). A part's name ends the trace id of what it draws. Parts are written in a component's `parts`, in a top's drawing of a layout, and in a brand's drawing ([visual-communication:DEC-009-layout-defaults] point 1, [visual-communication:DEC-034-brand-drawings] point 1).
- **The checks of boxes read the scene:** each element's box and trace id. They read no component's file.
- **A reader learns of a component only what the checks say.** The viewing step's frames hold each element's box and trace id. Its report holds what the checks found on each image and each check's reach, and nothing of what a component says ([visual-communication:DEC-062-checks-beside-the-frames] points 1 and 2). A check's silence at a place of a kind its reach measures reads as measured and clean ([visual-communication:DEC-063-where-a-check-measures] point 3). So DEC-062 gives every overlap, since a pair left out "would read as measured and clean by the check of boxes that overlap, while the page shows the overlap" (DEC-062, "Why no overlap is left out").
- **A finding that never fails asks for attention or states a fact.** It asks for attention when someone is expected to act, and states a fact when no act is expected ([visual-communication:DEC-002-files-and-validation] point 7). A fact still reaches every reader. The check of content a size hides states as a fact what a component hides at a size, "so a reviewer sees what a reader at that size does not get" ([visual-communication:DEC-021-size-and-shape] point 5).
- **A released version never changes.** A change goes into the version being built, and raises its number as far as the change needs ([visual-communication:DEC-003-change-over-time] point 6, DEC-013 points 3 and 4).

## Decision

**A part names, under `over`, the parts written before it in the same `parts` that it is drawn over on purpose. The engine carries those names into the scene. The check of boxes that overlap states each overlap of a pair named so as a fact, in place of asking for attention. No other check reads them.**

### 1. A part names the parts it is drawn over on purpose

- **`over`** is a key any part may take: a list of the names of parts written before it in the same `parts`, and so drawn before it. It says that the part lies over each of them on purpose. A badge drawn over a card's box writes `over: [box]`.
- **Wherever parts are written:** in a library's component or a piece's own, in a top's drawing of a layout, and in a brand's drawing.
- **Only the parts the check compares with it.** It names parts of that same `parts` alone, since the check of overlaps compares siblings alone (DEC-046 point 3), and the parts of one `parts` are drawn as siblings. So it names no part inside a component a part places, and no item of a list. A part that places a component, holds a slot or repeats over a list is named whole, by what it draws, as the check compares it. A change that widens the check of overlaps beyond siblings decides whether `over` may name more.
- **Of boxes, as the check is.** `over` speaks of the parts' boxes, which the check compares, not of what is drawn in them. A component may name a pair whose boxes overlap where their drawing does not, such as a text whose line spacing reaches into the box of the part above it. That use is meant: the overlap of the boxes is the component's design, as any other is. Whether what is drawn touches stays the reviewer's to see on the image, since "a box may differ from what is drawn in it" (DEC-046 point 3).
- **Always, or not at all.** It takes no switch. Wherever the two parts overlap, under any option, at any size and in any language, the component says that the overlap is meant. Where they do not overlap, or where the check does not compare them, as where one is a line or holds the other, it changes nothing.
- **Refused when the component is read:** a name that is no part of the same `parts`, the part's own name, the name of a part written after it, and a name written twice. The order of drawing decides which part lies over which, so a part that named one drawn after it would say the opposite of what is drawn. A name refused says nothing, so the check asks for attention at that pair as before, and a name written twice counts once. Each refusal is a finding that names the part and the name refused. It fails where the project can correct the file that holds it: a piece's own component, or a version of the project's own library not yet released. In a released version it asks that version's owner for attention, and never fails (DEC-002 point 7).

### 2. The scene carries it, and the check of overlaps states each such overlap as a fact

- **In the scene.** The element that a part with `over` draws holds `over`: the trace ids of the elements the parts it names draw in that scene. A part it names that draws nothing there, such as a slot left empty or a part whose `when` does not hold, adds none.
- **No property the element draws,** as its type, its trace id, its sources and its children are none. No renderer draws it, and nothing is lost where none does. So it names no source, no renderer's declaration lists it, and no loss names it (DEC-005 points 5 and 9). The seam's version stays as it is, since no plugin must read anything differently (DEC-005 point 8).
- **The check of boxes that overlap** compares every two siblings as before. Where two overlap and the later names the earlier under `over`, it states the overlap as a fact, `box-overlap-meant`, in place of asking for attention with `box-overlap`. Its message names the pair, how far they overlap and which is drawn over which, as the message of an overlap does, and says that the part drawn over names the other under `over`. It points where `box-overlap` points. Every overlap the part does not name asks for attention as before: a badge named `over: [box]`, which a long title pushes over the card's head, is still reported at the head.
- **Measured, with a finding.** The check measured the pair, so the fact is a finding of what it measured, and no statement that it could measure nothing (DEC-063 point 2). Its reach gains no clause, and the pair reads as measured, with a finding (DEC-063 point 3). So no reader takes the pair for measured and clean. The viewing step's report gives the fact on each image the pair shows, as it gives each finding of a check that runs on a scene (DEC-062 point 1), and validation shows it as it shows any fact.
- **The reviewer judges it on the image,** as it judges each place the checks name (DEC-046 point 3). A defect it sees there is a fault of overlaps, and never a drawing that disagrees with a passing check, since the check gave a finding (DEC-063 point 4). The component's word says why the check asks no one to act. It never says that the drawing reads well.
- **Every other reading of the pair stays.** The viewing step still names a text that a part drawn after it covers, where it reads what covers a text ([visual-communication:DEC-037-text-the-page-hides] point 1), and the reviewer reads that text as the covered entry's fault alone, as it reads every overlap of a covered text (DEC-062 point 3). The check of contrast still measures a text against the fill under it ([visual-communication:DEC-004-brands-and-notations] point 8).
- **Until the next render.** A piece's findings record that a render wrote before the change that builds this record keeps a call for attention at a pair named so, and validation shows it until the piece's next render, as DEC-063 point 2 says of a statement. The viewing step computes its findings from the files as they stand, so its report gives the fact at once (DEC-062 point 1).

### 3. The check of edges that nearly line up compares the pair as before

- **`over` speaks of an overlap, not of a line.** The check of edges that nearly line up compares the pair as it compares any two siblings (DEC-046 point 3).
- **A meant overlap still has edges meant to land somewhere.** A tab meant to lie over its panel's top edge is meant to lie flush with the panel's left edge too. It may lie off that edge by more than a pixel and by less than the brand's smallest step of space, and the check measures that edge whether or not the two parts overlap.
- **What it costs.** A part centred on another's edge, such as a dot on a card's corner, has its own edge on that side half its size away from the other's. Where that half is more than a pixel and less than the brand's smallest step, as for a dot more than 2 and less than 8 px across under the neutral brand's step of 4 px, the check reports a near miss on each axis on which the part is so centred, in every piece that draws the component. It follows from a line the check does not compare: a centre set on an edge. Each asks for attention and fails nothing, as every near miss does.
- **Left open:** whether a component may say that a near miss is meant; and whether, for a pair named under `over`, a centre set on the other's edge counts as a line chosen, so that the near misses it gives are not reported, which would remove this point's cost by arithmetic. This record decides neither. Each is for the work of [project-kit#3343](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3343), which takes up the check of edges that nearly line up, or for a later record.

### 4. A library the capability ships says it in the version it builds

- **In the version being built.** A component of a library the capability ships, whose parts overlap on purpose, comes to say so in the library's version being built. A released version never changes, so it never says so (DEC-003 point 6, DEC-013 point 4). Whoever maintains the capability judges which overlaps are meant, as the owner of any library does.
- **A patch at least.** Saying it, or ceasing to, changes no look and nothing that a piece or a library built on the library names, so it is no break (DEC-003 point 6). Nor is it an addition: the comparison of two versions names its additions kind by kind, and a part's own keys are of none of those kinds (DEC-003 point 1; the capability's page on files, "What a release compares"). So the version being built takes a patch at least, as for any change to it (DEC-013 point 3).
- **The release it needs reads `over`.** A shipped version's release reads it in full, refusing any key the capability that releases it does not know, and names that capability's current version as the release the version needs (DEC-013 point 3, [visual-communication:DEC-014-own-library-needed-release] point 1). An installed capability older than that release cannot read the version. So the change that teaches the engine `over` lands before, or with, the change that first writes `over` into a shipped library's version being built. This record's work does both in one change.
- **A piece under a released version** is still asked for attention at that overlap, until its lock moves to a version that says it is meant. Who moves the lock is what the piece's state says (DEC-003 point 8). The attention fails nothing meanwhile.

## Rationale

**Why the component says it, not each piece.** The overlap is the component's design. Every piece that uses the component draws it, and none of their authors chose it. Said once, where the parts are written, it reaches every piece, and holds as long as the parts it names do. A piece that accepted the overlap, as it accepts a renderer's loss, would have to say it again in every piece: the repetition this record removes.

**Why a fact, and not silence.** A check that left the pair out would bring back what DEC-062 rejected. The frames and the report carry nothing of what a component says, so no reader could tell a pair left out by `over` from two siblings the check measured and found clean (Context). A reviewer that saw the overlap and judged it a defect would then blame a check that did as it was told, as a drawing that disagrees with a passing check. A fact keeps the pair measured, with a finding, and reaches every reader through the findings each already reads, so neither the frames nor the report changes form. What a meant overlap must not do is ask for attention in every piece, for ever, which is what DEC-046 point 3 asks the work to end (Context): that asks someone to act, at a place no author of a piece chose. A fact asks no act (DEC-002 point 7). The capability already passes on a component's choice so: the check of content a size hides states as a fact what a component hides at a size (DEC-021 point 5). A fact also says how far the parts overlap, which a silence cannot. So an overlap grown past what the component meant is still put before the reviewer.

**Why a part names parts.**
- **Not a flag on the part.** `over: true` would make every overlap of that part meant. A badge meant to lie over the card's box, but pushed over its head by a long title, would then ask no attention at the head. Naming each part keeps the check's attention on every pair the component did not mean.
- **Not a list of pairs on the component.** It would say the same thing, away from the part it explains. On the part, the key lies beside what it speaks of, as `when` lies beside the part it draws or not, and a reader of the badge sees there that it lies over the box on purpose.

**Why only parts drawn before it.** What the component means is that one part lies on top. The order of drawing decides which does (DEC-005 point 5), so the key reads in that order. A badge moved before the box would be hidden under it, which is a fault. The reading then refuses the name, and the check asks for attention at the pair again, rather than state as meant a badge no one can see.

**Why the scene carries it.** The check reads scenes, and compares siblings by their trace ids. A check that read each component's file would have to find again, for each group, the version and the drawing that drew it, which the engine has already done. A scene says which component drew each group for the same reason.

**Why it is no property the element draws.** It may stand on an element of any kind, and says nothing a renderer could draw. Listed in each renderer's declaration, under each kind of element, it would have every renderer claim to draw what none draws, and a renderer added later would report a loss of it in every piece until its declaration listed it.

**Why no switch.** The fact never hides an overlap: wherever a pair named so overlaps, under any option and at any size, every reader is told, with how far. So an overlap under an option the component did not mean it for is still shown, though it asks no act. A switch would cost the key a second form, with branches, a refusal in each branch, and a rule for which branch an instance's scene names, for a need no component of the libraries the capability ships is known to have. Without one, a component that means an overlap under some options or sizes alone has two ways, each with its cost: it leaves the pair unnamed, and asks for attention where the overlap is meant; or it draws the part as two parts, each with its `when`, whose trace ids then differ by option.

**Why the check of edges that nearly line up still compares the pair.** A word a component says reaches only what its words name. That an overlap is meant says where two parts share room, not where their edges lie, and a meant overlap still has edges meant to land somewhere (point 3). Leaving the pair out of both checks would let a meant overlap hide a part set a little off the edge it was meant to lie on. Point 3 names what keeping the check costs: near misses in every piece for a small part centred on an edge. Whether such a near miss is meant is a question of near alignment. This record leaves it open, rather than settle it by a word that speaks of overlaps.

### What it costs

- **A fact for each meant overlap,** in validation and in the viewing step's report, in each piece where the parts overlap, as a piece that uses an experimental component is stated (DEC-003 point 9). It asks no act.
- **No call, however far the parts overlap.** A caption band meant to lie over a picture's foot, which a long caption wraps to three lines at one size or in one language, covers the picture's subject and asks no attention. The fact names how far the band overlaps the picture, and the reviewer judges it on the image.
- **No switch.** A component that means an overlap under some options or sizes alone asks for attention where the overlap is meant, or splits the part in two (Rationale).
- **Near misses of a centre set on an edge,** in every piece that draws a small part so (point 3).
- **A piece under a released version** keeps the call for attention until its lock moves (point 4).

### Alternatives considered

- **The check infers what is meant,** such as a small part on a corner. Rejected: whether an overlap is a fault needs a judgement, and only what needs none is a check's ([visual-communication:DEC-028-principles-of-visual-communication] point 1).
- **The check leaves out each pair named so,** its reach naming that kind of place among those it leaves out. Rejected, for the reason above: no reader could tell such a pair from one measured and clean, and DEC-062 gives every overlap for that reason.
- **`over` in the frames, or a list in the report of each image's pairs left out by `over`,** read by the reviewer. Rejected: it changes the form of the frames or of the report (DEC-015 point 5, DEC-062 point 2), and the reviewer's reading of them, to tell the reviewer what a finding tells every reader, and validation would still show nothing at the pair.
- **The reviewer reads each component's `over`.** Rejected: the reviewer would find again, for each group, the version and the drawing that drew it, as a check that read component files would (Rationale), and would judge from files no other reading of the checks needs.
- **`over` with a switch,** on an option of words or on a size, one level deep, as a `repeat` part's `colour` takes one. Not taken, for the reasons above. Its trigger to revisit: a component that overlaps its parts on purpose under some options or sizes, and stands clear under others.
- **A piece that accepts a meant overlap too,** for a component of a library the project does not own, whose owner has not released a version that says it. Not taken: the call fails nothing meanwhile, and a piece's word would say again, piece by piece, what the component's owner says once.
- **Two items of one list may be named.** No component of the libraries the capability ships is known to lay two of its items over each other. Patterns elsewhere do, such as a row of avatars that overlap, a fanned stack of cards, or the circles of a Venn diagram drawn as a list's items. A pattern lays out its items with an operator, and `over` on a part cannot name them, so an overlap of two items asks for attention as before. Its trigger to revisit: a component, of a library the capability ships or of a project's own, that lays its items over each other on purpose.
- **Another name, such as `lies-over`,** which reads as a statement to the check, where `over` may read as an instruction to draw the part on top. Not taken: `over` is as short as a part's other keys, and that misreading shows at once. A part written before the one it means to cover names a part written after it, which is refused with a finding.
- **A word for a near miss that is meant,** beside `over`. Not this record's: point 3 leaves it open.
