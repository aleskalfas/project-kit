**In plain terms:** a component may place one of its parts on another on purpose, such as a badge on a card's corner. The check of boxes that overlap cannot tell that from a fault. So at that place, in every piece that uses the component, it asks the reviewer whether the overlap is a fault or is meant. This record lets a part name, under `lies-over`, the parts of its own component that it lies over on purpose. The engine carries the names into the scene, which is what the check reads. The check then states each overlap of a pair named so as a fact, which asks no one to act, since the component has answered what the check would ask. The fact still names the pair and how far they overlap, so every reader of the checks sees the overlap, and the reviewer reads the image there as it reads the rest of the page. So the overlap is still reported in every piece, as a fact in place of a call for attention. The record on faults gave this work the aim that a meant overlap not be reported for ever ([visual-communication:DEC-046-faults-lower-grades] point 3), and this record overturns that aim.
- **Only the check of overlaps.** The check of edges that nearly line up still compares the pair. That an overlap is meant says nothing of where the two parts' edges lie.
- **A library the capability ships** says it as it makes any other change: in the version it is building, which takes a patch at least. None needs to yet, since no reference piece of the starter shows an overlap of a component's parts.

## Context

DEC-046 point 3 has two checks do the arithmetic of craft among the *siblings* of each scene, the elements one group holds directly. One reports edges that nearly line up. The other reports two siblings whose boxes overlap, where neither holds the other. Both ask the reviewer for attention and never fail, since a component may place one part on another on purpose, such as a badge on a corner. The reviewer judges each place they name on the image, and says whether it is a fault or is meant. The point leaves it to the work that builds the checks to settle how a component says an overlap is meant, "so that it is not reported for ever". Where that adds a field, the work drafts the record first (COR-065 point 3).

[project-kit#3327](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3327) is that work. It asks three things: how a component says it; whether saying it also leaves the pair out of the check of edges that nearly line up; and how a library the capability ships says it, in a new version ([visual-communication:DEC-013-shipped-library-releases]).

The answer meets five things that already hold:
- **Parts are drawn in the order written,** and the scene keeps that order among each group's elements. So of two siblings, the later is drawn over the earlier ([visual-communication:DEC-005-engine-and-plugins] point 5). A part's name ends the trace id of what it draws. Parts are written in a component's `parts`, in a top's drawing of a layout, and in a brand's drawing ([visual-communication:DEC-009-layout-defaults] point 1, [visual-communication:DEC-034-brand-drawings] point 1).
- **The checks of boxes read the scene:** each element's box and trace id. They read no component's file.
- **A reader learns of a component only what the checks say.** The viewing step's frames hold each element's box and trace id. Its report holds what the checks found on each image and each check's reach, and nothing of what a component says ([visual-communication:DEC-062-checks-beside-the-frames] points 1 and 2). A check's silence at a place of a kind its reach measures reads as measured and clean ([visual-communication:DEC-063-where-a-check-measures] point 3). So DEC-062 gives every overlap, since a pair left out "would read as measured and clean by the check of boxes that overlap, while the page shows the overlap" (DEC-062, "Why no overlap is left out").
- **A finding that never fails asks for attention or states a fact.** It asks for attention when someone is expected to act, and names who, and states a fact when no act is expected. A finding read from a heuristic or a threshold that no record makes an invariant asks for attention ([visual-communication:DEC-002-files-and-validation] point 7). The check of overlaps reads such a threshold, more than a pixel of rounding across and down, and names the reviewer. Two kinds of finding state a fact, though a reader still reads the places they name. A check's statement that it could measure nothing at a place is a fact, since a reader that relies on the check "judges the places it covers itself, as part of its own reading, which no finding asks of it" (DEC-063 point 2). And the check of content a size hides states as a fact what a component hides at a size, "so a reviewer sees what a reader at that size does not get" ([visual-communication:DEC-021-size-and-shape] point 5).
- **A released version never changes.** A change goes into the version being built, and raises its number as far as the change needs ([visual-communication:DEC-003-change-over-time] point 6, DEC-013 points 3 and 4).

## Decision

**A part names, under `lies-over`, the parts written before it in the same `parts` that it lies over on purpose. The engine carries those names into the scene. The check of boxes that overlap states each overlap of a pair named so as a fact, since the component has answered what the check would ask the reviewer: whether the overlap is a fault or is meant. No other check reads the names.**

### 1. A part names the parts it lies over on purpose

- **`lies-over`** is a key any part may take: a list of the names of parts written before it in the same `parts`, and so drawn before it. It says that the part lies over each of them on purpose. A badge drawn over a card's box writes `lies-over: [box]`.
- **Wherever parts are written:** in a library's component or a piece's own, in a top's drawing of a layout, and in a brand's drawing.
- **Only the parts the check compares with it.** It names parts of that same `parts` alone, since the check of overlaps compares siblings alone (DEC-046 point 3), and the parts of one `parts` are drawn as siblings. So it names no part inside a component a part places, and no item of a list. A part that places a component, holds a slot or repeats over a list is named whole, by what it draws, as the check compares it. A change that widens the check of overlaps beyond siblings decides whether `lies-over` may name more.
- **Of boxes, as the check is.** `lies-over` speaks of the parts' boxes, which the check compares, not of what is drawn in them. A component may name a pair whose boxes overlap where their drawing does not, such as a text whose line spacing reaches into the box of the part above it. That use is meant: the overlap of the boxes is the component's design, as any other is. Whether what is drawn touches stays the reviewer's to see on the image, since "a box may differ from what is drawn in it" (DEC-046 point 3).
- **Always, or not at all.** It takes no switch. Wherever the two parts overlap, under any option, at any size and in any language, the component says that the overlap is meant. Where they do not overlap, or where the check does not compare them, as where one is a line or holds the other, it changes nothing.
- **Refused when the component is read:** a name that is no part of the same `parts`, the part's own name, the name of a part written after it, and a name written twice. The order of drawing decides which part lies over which, so a part that named one drawn after it would say the opposite of what is drawn. A name refused says nothing, so the check asks for attention at that pair as before, and a name written twice counts once. Each refusal is a finding that names the part and the name refused. It fails where the project can correct the file that holds it: a piece's own component, or a version of the project's own library not yet released. In a released version it asks that version's owner for attention, and never fails (DEC-002 point 7).

### 2. The scene carries it, and the check of overlaps states each such overlap as a fact

- **In the scene.** The element that a part with `lies-over` draws holds `lies-over`: the trace ids of the elements the parts it names draw in that scene. A part it names that draws nothing there, such as a slot left empty or a part whose `when` does not hold, adds none.
- **No property the element draws.** It may stand on an element of any type, and no renderer draws it, so it counts among what no element draws, beside the element's type, its trace id, its sources and its children. So it names no source, no renderer's declaration lists it, and no loss names it (DEC-005 points 5 and 9). The seam's version stays as it is, since no plugin must read anything differently (DEC-005 point 8). A group's `component`, `version`, `options` and `page` no renderer draws either, yet each renderer's declaration lists them under `group`. Those stand on one type of element, and `lies-over` on every type (Rationale). This record leaves those entries as they stand.
- **The check of boxes that overlap** compares every two siblings as before. Where two overlap and the later names the earlier under `lies-over`, it states the overlap as a fact, `box-overlap-meant`, in place of asking the reviewer for attention with `box-overlap`. Its message names the pair, how far they overlap and which lies over which, as the message of an overlap does, and says that the part on top names the part beneath under `lies-over`. It points where `box-overlap` points, at `piece.yaml`, and names no library version. Every overlap the part does not name asks for attention as before: a badge named `lies-over: [box]`, which a long title pushes over the card's head, is still reported at the head.
- **A fact, since it asks no act.** `box-overlap` asks the reviewer to rule on its place, a fault or meant (DEC-046 point 3). At a pair named so, the component has given that ruling, once for every piece. So no one is expected to act: not the reviewer; not the piece's author, whom the check never names; and not the component's owner, who said it (Rationale).
- **Measured, with a finding.** The check measured the pair, so the fact is a finding of what it measured, and no statement that it could measure nothing (DEC-063 point 2). Its reach gains no clause, and the pair reads as measured, with a finding (DEC-063 point 3). So no reader takes the pair for measured and clean. The viewing step's report gives the fact on each image the pair shows, as it gives each finding of a check that runs on a scene (DEC-062 point 1), and validation shows it as it shows any fact.
- **The reviewer gives the place no ruling.** It does not judge whether the overlap is a fault or is meant, and its report does not list the place among those the checks ask it to judge. It does no sums there for the overlap, which the check measured (DEC-063 point 3). It reads the image there as it reads the rest of the page (DEC-015 point 7). The component's word says that the two parts need not stand clear of each other, and never what is drawn where they meet. So the overlap itself is no fault, and a defect the image shows where they meet, such as the letters of a text lost or a picture's subject hidden, takes the fault it takes where `box-overlap` names the place. None is a drawing that disagrees with a passing check, since the check gave a finding (DEC-063 point 4).
- **Every other reading of the pair stays.** The viewing step still names a text that a part drawn after it covers, where it reads what covers a text ([visual-communication:DEC-037-text-the-page-hides] point 1), and the reviewer reads that text as the covered entry's fault alone, as it reads every overlap of a covered text (DEC-062 point 3). The check of contrast still measures a text against the fill under it ([visual-communication:DEC-004-brands-and-notations] point 8).

### 3. The check of edges that nearly line up compares the pair as before

- **`lies-over` speaks of an overlap, not of a line.** The check of edges that nearly line up compares the pair as it compares any two siblings (DEC-046 point 3).
- **A meant overlap still has edges meant to land somewhere.** A tab meant to lie over its panel's top edge is meant to lie flush with the panel's left edge too. It may lie off that edge by more than a pixel and by less than the brand's smallest step of space, and the check measures that edge whether or not the two parts overlap.
- **What it costs.** A part centred on another's edge, such as a dot on a card's corner, has its own edge on that side half its size away from the other's. Where that half is more than a pixel and less than the brand's smallest step, as for a dot more than 2 and less than 8 px across under the neutral brand's step of 4 px, the check reports a near miss on each axis on which the part is so centred, in every piece that draws the component. It follows from a line the check does not compare: a centre set on an edge. Each asks for attention and fails nothing, as every near miss does.
- **Left open:** whether a component may say that a near miss is meant; and whether, for a pair named under `lies-over`, a centre set on the other's edge counts as a line chosen, so that the near misses it gives against that part are not reported. That would remove this point's cost between the two parts named alone: `lies-over` names siblings only (point 1), so a near miss the dot gives against any other part would stay, such as one of another group, were the check to compare parts of different groups. This record decides neither, and leaves each for a later record.

### 4. A library the capability ships says it in the version it builds

- **In the version being built.** A component of a library the capability ships, whose parts overlap on purpose, comes to say so in the library's version being built. A released version never changes, so it never says so (DEC-003 point 6, DEC-013 point 4). Whoever maintains the capability judges which overlaps are meant, as the owner of any library does.
- **A patch at least.** Saying it, or ceasing to, changes no look and nothing that a piece or a library built on the library names, so it is no break (DEC-003 point 6). Nor is it an addition: the comparison of two versions names its additions kind by kind, and a part's own keys are of none of those kinds (DEC-003 point 1; the capability's page on files, "What a release compares"). So the version being built takes a patch at least, as for any change to it (DEC-013 point 3).
- **The release it needs reads `lies-over`.** A shipped version's release reads it in full, refusing any key the capability that releases it does not know, and names that capability's current version as the release the version needs (DEC-013 point 3, [visual-communication:DEC-014-own-library-needed-release] point 1). An installed capability older than that release cannot read the version. So the change that teaches the engine `lies-over` lands before, or with, the change that first writes `lies-over` into a shipped library's version being built.
- **A piece under a released version** is still asked for attention at that overlap, until its lock moves to a version that says it is meant. Who moves the lock is what the piece's state says (DEC-003 point 8). The attention fails nothing meanwhile.

## Rationale

**Why the component says it, not each piece.** The overlap is the component's design. Every piece that uses the component draws it, and none of their authors chose it. Said once, where the parts are written, it reaches every piece, and holds as long as the parts it names do. A piece that accepted the overlap, as it accepts a renderer's loss, would have to say it again in every piece: the repetition this record removes.

**Why a fact, and not silence.** A check that left the pair out would bring back what DEC-062 rejected. The frames and the report carry nothing of what a component says, so no reader could tell a pair left out by `lies-over` from two siblings the check measured and found clean (Context). A reviewer that saw the overlap and judged it a defect would then blame a check that did as it was told, as a drawing that disagrees with a passing check. A fact keeps the pair measured, with a finding, and reaches every reader through the findings each already reads, so neither the frames nor the report changes form. A fact also says how far the parts overlap, which a silence cannot. So an overlap grown past what the component meant is still put before every reader.

**Why a fact, and not a call for attention.** A call for attention expects someone to act, and names who (DEC-002 point 7). `box-overlap` names the reviewer, and the act it expects is a ruling: whether the overlap is a fault or is meant, judged on the image and said in the review's report (DEC-046 point 3). At a pair the component names, the component's owner has given that ruling once, where the parts are written, for every piece. A call there would ask the reviewer, image by image, a question already answered, and its answer would add nothing the component did not say. What is left at the place is the reviewer's own reading of what is drawn where the parts meet. It makes that reading at every place of the page, named by a check or not (DEC-015 point 7), and no finding asks it of the reviewer. DEC-063 point 2 states a fact for the same reason, where a check could measure nothing: a reader that relies on the check "judges the places it covers itself, as part of its own reading, which no finding asks of it". The capability passes on a component's choice so already. The check of content a size hides states as a fact what a component hides at a size (DEC-021 point 5). There too the component's word answers what a reader would otherwise ask, whether a part is missing, and whether the page reads well without it stays the reader's own judgement. The check reads the overlap against a threshold that no record makes an invariant, and DEC-002 point 7 has such a reading ask for attention. So this record edits that bullet to name a meant overlap as a fact (Implications), as DEC-063 edited the same point to admit its statements.

**What the fact gains, and what it does not.**
- **Validation's warnings stop counting meant overlaps.** Validation prints a call for attention as a warning and a fact as information, and prints both (COR-055 point 5; the CLI's page, `validate`). So it prints a line for each meant overlap in each piece that draws it, as before, and none of those lines is a warning.
- **The review gives those places no ruling,** and no line of its report, and reads the image there as it reads the rest of the page.
- **Every reader still sees each overlap,** with how far.
- **No entry goes away.** The overlap is still reported in every piece that draws it, for ever. That is the departure from DEC-046 point 3 (below).

**Why DEC-046 point 3 is overturned in part, and not refined.** The point's bullet "An overlap that is meant" gives the work its aim: a way for a component to say an overlap is meant, "so that it is not reported for ever". This record has each such overlap reported in every piece that draws it, for ever, as a fact. Another record then decides otherwise than the point, so that clause is overturned, as DEC-062 overturned point 5 of the same record (DEC-062, "Why DEC-046 point 5 is overturned in part, and not refined"). The bullet's sentence stays as it stood, under the line that names this record. What the rest of the bullet decides stands, and this record carries it out: the work settles how a component says an overlap is meant, and drafts the record first. The edits to the point's bullets "Both ask for attention and never fail" and "The reviewer judges each place the checks name" refine them. Each still decides what it decided at every place but an overlap a component names, and the reason the first gives, that whether an overlap is a defect needs a judgement since a component may place one part on another on purpose, is what the component's word answers for the pairs it names.

**Why a part names parts.**
- **Not a flag on the part.** `lies-over: true` would make every overlap of that part meant. A badge meant to lie over the card's box, but pushed over its head by a long title, would then ask no attention at the head. Naming each part keeps the check's attention on every pair the component did not mean.
- **Not a list of pairs on the component.** It would say the same thing, away from the part it explains. On the part, the key lies beside what it speaks of, as `when` lies beside the part it draws or not, and a reader of the badge sees there that it lies over the box on purpose.

**Why only parts drawn before it.** What the component means is that one part lies on top. The order of drawing decides which does (DEC-005 point 5), so the key reads in that order. A badge moved before the box would be hidden under it, which is a fault. The reading then refuses the name, and the check asks for attention at the pair again, rather than state as meant a badge no one can see.

**Why the scene carries it.** The check reads scenes, and compares siblings by their trace ids. A check that read each component's file would have to find again, for each group, the version and the drawing that drew it, which the engine has already done. A scene says which component drew each group for the same reason.

**Why it is no property the element draws.** It may stand on an element of any type, and says nothing a renderer could draw. Listed in a renderer's declaration, it would stand under every type there, in each renderer the capability ships. A renderer added later would have to list it under each type, or report a loss of it in every piece that names a meant overlap. Counted beside an element's type, its trace id, its sources and its children, which stand on every type too, it is one entry, in the code that finds the losses, and no declaration names it. A group's `component`, `version`, `options` and `page` are listed under `group` though no renderer draws them, and would cost a later renderer the same, but only on that one type. This record does not move them.

**Why no switch.** The fact never hides an overlap: wherever a pair named so overlaps, under any option and at any size, every reader is told, with how far. So an overlap under an option the component did not mean it for is still shown, though it asks no act. A switch would cost the key a second form, with branches, a refusal in each branch, and a rule for which branch an instance's scene names, for a need no component of the libraries the capability ships has: none overlaps its parts in the starter's reference pieces (Implications). Without one, a component that means an overlap under some options or sizes alone has two ways, each with its cost: it leaves the pair unnamed, and asks for attention where the overlap is meant; or it draws the part as two parts, each with its `when`, whose trace ids then differ by option.

**Why the check of edges that nearly line up still compares the pair.** A word a component says reaches only what its words name. That an overlap is meant says where two parts share room, not where their edges lie, and a meant overlap still has edges meant to land somewhere (point 3). Leaving the pair out of both checks would let a meant overlap hide a part set a little off the edge it was meant to lie on. Point 3 names what keeping the check costs: near misses in every piece for a small part centred on an edge. Whether such a near miss is meant is a question of near alignment. This record leaves it open, rather than settle it by a word that speaks of overlaps.

### What it costs

- **A line for each meant overlap,** in validation and in the viewing step's report, in each piece where the parts overlap, for ever. It states a fact, as a piece that uses an experimental component is stated (DEC-003 point 9), and asks no act. The aim of DEC-046 point 3, that a meant overlap not be reported for ever, is given up.
- **No call, however far the parts overlap.** A caption band meant to lie over a picture's foot, which a long caption wraps to three lines at one size or in one language, covers the picture's subject and asks no attention. The fact names how far the band overlaps the picture, and what the band hides is the reviewer's to see, in its reading of the image.
- **No switch.** A component that means an overlap under some options or sizes alone asks for attention where the overlap is meant, or splits the part in two (Rationale).
- **Near misses of a centre set on an edge,** in every piece that draws a small part so (point 3).
- **A piece under a released version** keeps the call for attention until its lock moves (point 4).

### Alternatives considered

- **The check infers what is meant,** such as a small part on a corner. Rejected: whether an overlap is a fault needs a judgement, and only what needs none is a check's ([visual-communication:DEC-028-principles-of-visual-communication] point 1).
- **The check leaves out each pair named so,** its reach naming that kind of place among those it leaves out. Rejected, for the reason above: no reader could tell such a pair from one measured and clean, and DEC-062 gives every overlap for that reason.
- **A call for attention, with the component's word in its message.** Rejected: it would ask the reviewer to rule, in every piece, on a place whose ruling the component has given, and validation's warnings would still count every meant overlap. The record would gain a sentence in a message.
- **The fact stated once, where the component's version is checked alone,** and in no piece, as the check states a brand's picture role with no value (the list of checks, `picture-role-unvalued`). Rejected. A version checked alone draws no piece, so it could state only that a part names others, never that they overlap in a piece, nor how far, which changes with the content, the language and the size. Each piece's findings would then give nothing at the pair, so the viewing step's report, which gives every finding of a check that runs on a scene with no class (DEC-062 point 1), would show the pair as measured and clean, and validation, which shows a piece's findings from its findings record (DEC-005 point 13), would show nothing there either. It would bring back the leave-out above, priced at a line per version rather than a line per piece.
- **`lies-over` in the frames, or a list in the report of each image's pairs left out by `lies-over`,** read by the reviewer. Rejected: it changes the form of the frames or of the report ([visual-communication:DEC-015-viewing-step-and-reviewer] point 5, DEC-062 point 2), and the reviewer's reading of them, to tell the reviewer what a finding tells every reader, and validation would still show nothing at the pair.
- **The reviewer reads each component's `lies-over`.** Rejected: the reviewer would find again, for each group, the version and the drawing that drew it, as a check that read component files would (Rationale), and would judge from files no other reading of the checks needs.
- **`lies-over` with a switch,** on an option of words or on a size, one level deep, as a `repeat` part's `colour` takes one. Not taken, for the reasons above. Its trigger to revisit: a component that overlaps its parts on purpose under some options or sizes, and stands clear under others.
- **A piece that accepts a meant overlap too,** for a component of a library the project does not own, whose owner has not released a version that says it. Not taken: the call fails nothing meanwhile, and a piece's word would say again, piece by piece, what the component's owner says once.
- **Two items of one list may be named.** No component of the libraries the capability ships lays two of its items over each other: the starter's reference pieces show no overlap at all (Implications). Patterns elsewhere do, such as a row of avatars that overlap, a fanned stack of cards, or the circles of a Venn diagram drawn as a list's items. A pattern lays out its items with an operator, and `lies-over` on a part cannot name them, so an overlap of two items asks for attention as before. Its trigger to revisit: a component, of a library the capability ships or of a project's own, that lays its items over each other on purpose.
- **The name `over`,** as short as a part's other keys. Not taken. The starter's card, aside, level and code already name a value `over`, how far the sign reaches below the head, which their formulas read. Those are the components with a sign at the end of the head's row, the likeliest to name a meant overlap, so one file would hold one word in two senses. A part's key and a named value never meet in a formula, so nothing would break, but a reader of the file would meet both. `over` may also read as an instruction to draw the part on top, where `lies-over` reads as what it is, a statement of how the parts lie. Nor `drawn-over`, which leaves unclear whether the part it stands on is the one on top or the one beneath.
- **A word for a near miss that is meant,** beside `lies-over`. Not this record's: point 3 leaves it open.
