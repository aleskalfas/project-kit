**In plain terms:** when the viewing step draws a page of the SVG renderer through resvg, its report lists in `dropped` each text the page hides or cuts by more than a sliver, as a browser's report does (DEC-037). A text the page's edge cuts, and a text a part drawn after it covers, each take one entry with the cause and the share of the text's ink the cause loses. A cause is named where it loses at least a *threshold*, a number of pixels set for resvg's drawing, or where it *takes* an *island* of the text's ink, such as a dot, a háček or a full stop that stands apart from its letter, however few its pixels: it hides some of the island wholly, and leaves none of it showing at its strength. In the probes, a box that only touches the foot of a text was named at most draws below an em of 25, and at seven in ten or more from 25 to 40. Four kinds of loss can still pass unnamed, and the record lists them: among them a small dot that a box's edge fades to a faint line without hiding any of it wholly, and a mark joined to its letter, such as an ogonek. The tool's own drops take a cause too, so every entry of `dropped` names one in `why`. The report's notice that cover and cut were not read is taken out. For example, where a later box hides the decimal point of "3.5", the report lists that text as covered, and says that the cause took an island of its ink.

## Context

[visual-communication:DEC-037-text-the-page-hides] has a browser's report name the texts a page hides, and leaves the SVG renderer's report to say that cover and cut were not read until [project-kit#3111](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3111) builds the read. Through resvg a cut text is gone for every reader, since the page's frame clips it, so a reader of the report has less to find it by than through a browser, where it spills past the page.

This record decides for resvg what DEC-037 decides for a browser, and DEC-037 stays the browser's record. These terms are DEC-037's:
- A *span* is a line of a text, or a run of a line, that the renderer writes as one element.
- A span's *ink* is what it draws alone in its own paint, as DEC-037 takes it from [visual-communication:DEC-020-html-page-drawing] point 6. A span that draws none is `no ink`.
- A *sliver* is a loss too small to name.

These are this record's own:
- The *counting draw* is a draw of its own: a span's letters alone, opaque black on white, on a swath. A pixel's *darkness* is its darkest sample there, from 0 for black to 255 for white. A span's *strong ink* is the pixels of that draw nearer black than white, 127 or darker, as DEC-037 takes strong ink between two opaque colours. Its *faint ink* is its lighter pixels, down to the lightest level the reads take (point 1), and its *counted ink* is the two together.
- *Cut* and *covered* are DEC-037's two causes, judged here on counted ink: counted ink past the page's edge is cut, and counted ink on the page that a part drawn after it hides is covered. DEC-037 judges them on strong ink alone. Here a loss's size is judged on strong ink and its shape on islands of counted ink, so a faint dot with no strong ink can be covered here.
- The *threshold* is the number of pixels of strong ink, set by the span's em, from which a loss is named by its size (point 1). A smaller loss that takes no island is a sliver.
- An *island* of a span's counted ink is a set of its pixels at a level of darkness or darker, each joined to another of the set and to no other such pixel (point 1). An island is what the measure finds. A *mark* is what a reader needs to tell a word or a number. A letter's stem is an island and no mark, and a dot is both where it stands apart from its letter.
- A pixel *shows at a level* where the page drawn whole still shows so much of its ink that, drawn black on white, the pixel would be that dark or darker. A cause *takes* an island where it hides some pixel of the island wholly and leaves none of it showing at the island's level (point 1).
- A text's *em* is its font size in the image's pixels. The *scale* is how many image pixels the image gives each px of the page.
- A *háček* is the small mark over a letter such as ž. Lost, it turns one word into another. A full stop, a decimal point and the dot of ż are smaller marks. Lost, they run two sentences together, or turn 3.5 into 35.
- A *graze* is a later box's edge across the foot of a text's descenders. The *least graze* hides only the last row of the foot's ink.
- A *plant* is a text a probe sets on a page with a known loss, as DEC-037's first read plants its losses.
- A *swath* is the strip of image pixels on which the reads draw a span alone (point 1). It is not [visual-communication:DEC-022-web-site-and-parts]'s band, a strip of a page the viewing step draws as one image.

This record takes by name DEC-037 point 2's entries (point 2), point 4's part for the reviewer, and point 1's "No such loss is accepted" (point 3). It decides apart where resvg differs from a browser: what is too small to name, a threshold and a measure of islands, since the area DEC-037 leaves to the outputs page was set from a browser's drawing, and how the reads draw (point 1). It gives the tool's own drops a `why`, which DEC-037 point 2 asks of every entry, and takes `dropped-not-read` out of the SVG renderer's report (point 2).

### What the probes showed

Probes drew a page of the SVG renderer through the module's own reads, with resvg 0.48.1 in `resvg-py` 0.5.0, on one Mac. They drew it in the three faces the showcase deck of the repository the capability is authored in sets: Source Sans Pro Regular, which the first probes drew alone, Source Sans Pro Semibold and Source Code Pro Regular. They set each plant at every tenth of an em from 10 to 40, and drew it at five scales: 0.5, 1, 1.225 and 2, which are DEC-037's, and 0.8167, at which a 1920 by 1080 page is drawn 1568 pixels wide (DEC-015 point 5). That is 1505 draws of each plant in each face. Past 40 they drew ems of 48, 64, 80 and 96. A maintainer's tool of that repository draws them again, and tests of the look in that repository hold what this record rests on (Implications).

**The figures are those of the values the probes used:** the threshold at 0.01 of the em squared, in whole pixels and at least one pixel, and islands at three levels of darkness, 127, 159 and 191 of 255, their pixels joined by a side or a corner. The outputs page states these values (point 1), and a change there leaves the figures as they were found. Where a figure was measured at the threshold 0.0125, which the probes weighed first, or with an island taken otherwise than point 1 takes it, it says so.

A *step* is one place of that edge. A dot is *gone* at a step where no pixel of it shows at its own level: strong ink where it holds any, and else the darkest of the lighter levels it reaches.

## Decision

**Through resvg, the tool's module names each span that another part covers or the page's edge cuts, where the cause loses at least the threshold of its strong ink or takes an island of its counted ink. It gives one entry for each cause, with the share of the span's strong ink lost, or of its counted ink where it holds no strong ink, found in what resvg drew.**

### 1. How it is measured

- **Named:** a cover or a cut that loses at least the threshold, 4 pixels of strong ink at an em of 20 at the share the probes used, or that takes an island of the span's counted ink, such as a dot, a háček or a full stop that stands apart from its letter, however few its pixels.
- **Left unnamed:** a loss under the threshold that takes no island, such as part of a stroke, or a dot of which a pixel still shows at its level.
- **Known gaps of the read:** four losses of a mark can pass unnamed.
  - **A fused dot.** The dot of ż in Regular at ems of 10.1 and 10.2, which the face draws fused to the z (Rationale).
  - **A mark joined to its letter, below the baseline.** A mark below the baseline lies where a graze lies, and no rule here tells them apart. In the probes, a box that hid the ogonek whole from below was left unnamed in Source Code Pro, over that of ą at 4 draws, at ems of 10 to 10.3, and over that of ę at 3, at 10.2 to 13. In Regular and Semibold, and past 40 in every face, the read named it at every draw.
  - **Two causes at once.** A mark partly past the page's edge and partly under a part is left unnamed where neither cause takes an island of it or loses the threshold, since each cause is judged alone. It needs a part laid at the page's edge over a cut text.
  - **A small dot faded without a pixel of it hidden wholly.** Where a box's edge falls across a small dot so that it fades the dot's ink at its own level and hides none of it wholly, as where that ink lies one row or one column deep across the edge, the cause takes no island at that level and loses no strong pixel to the threshold. At the lighter levels it hides none of the dot wholly, or leaves some of it showing. The dot shows as a faint line where its row was, as it does where the box hides one pixel of it wholly, which names it: that one pixel is all that parts the two. In the probes this held only below an em of 20. From an em of 10 to 40 it held at 5.7 in a hundred of the steps at which a box's edge left a dot gone in Regular, 2.2 in Semibold and 1.3 in Source Code Pro, and at 1.7, 0.5 and 0.3 in a hundred of all its steps. It falls most on a dot with no strong ink, which of the dots the probe set apart only Regular drew: there it held at 825 of the 2,838 steps at which such a dot was gone, 29 in a hundred, at 68 of its 76 draws (Rationale, "Why an island is taken where some of it is hidden and none of it shows").
- **The cost:** a box that touches a text's foot is named at most draws, and whether it is cannot be told from the text's size. The cure is the layout's: the box moved clear of the letters.

How each is found:
- **Counting, in a draw of its own.** The reads count a span's letters in the counting draw, with no other span of its text. So a letter's count is its shape's, whatever the alpha of its paint. Whether a span left any ink at all is read in a draw of its own paint (point 2).
- **Room past the page.** A span's counted ink is taken as far as a page's width past each of the page's sides, and two of the span's largest font sizes past its top and foot. Ink further out is taken as far as the swath holds, and the span is cut all the same. resvg draws nothing past the page's frame, so a cut text is gone from the image for every reader, and only this read finds it. The swath is first drawn with room for two font sizes past each side, and drawn again at a page's width where it shows no ink, or the ink comes within a font size of its side, since the draw's cost grows with its width.
- **Cut and covered.** Counted ink past the page's edge is cut. Counted ink on the page that the page drawn whole shows alike with the span's letters black and with them white, all else as it stands, is covered, and a pixel the two show apart shows as much of its ink as they differ. Every span with counted ink on the page is read for cover, however little it holds; a span with none, whose counting draw holds no pixel at the lightest level, is read for neither cause. A span may be cut, covered, both or neither. A span whose counted ink lies wholly past an edge is off the page (point 2).
- **The threshold, in whole pixels of strong ink.** A cause names an entry where it loses at least the whole pixels of strong ink that a share of the span's em squared holds, and at least one pixel. The em and the pixels are the image's: the em is the span's font size times the image's width in pixels over the page's width in px. The share, and how the product is rounded to whole pixels, are the outputs page's, as DEC-037 point 1 left the browser's area to it, and change there with no new record. The Rationale argues the share it holds.
- **An island, taken.** A cause also names an entry where it takes an island of the span's counted ink, whatever the threshold holds: it hides some pixel of the island wholly and leaves none of it showing at the island's level. Past the page's edge a pixel is cut or shows, so there a cause takes an island only by cutting all of it. Islands are found at more than one level, from strong ink to a lighter one. So a mark the face draws faint is an island at the lighter level, and a mark that meets its letter there is one at a darker level. The levels and how pixels join are the outputs page's, and change there with no new record. The Rationale argues the ones it holds.
- **Each cause alone.** Each cause is judged alone, by the threshold and by the islands: the pixels the page's edge cuts and those a part covers are never added together, and an island is taken only by one cause.
- **What it names and leaves unnamed.** In the probes, in the three faces at every tenth of an em from 10 to 40 and at 48, 64, 80 and 96, at the values the probes used (Context):
  - Every háček and dot a box hid whole was named, and every such mark past the page's edge, but the fused dot of ż.
  - A graze is named where its last row holds at least the threshold's pixels, and left unnamed where it holds fewer, since it takes part of a stroke and no island. The row's pixels turn on where the foot's edge falls among them, so whether a graze is named cannot be told from the text's size. In Regular the least graze was named at 699 of 750 draws below an em of 25 and at 529 of 755 from 25 to 40, and at more of each in the two other faces. At an em of 40 it was named at no scale in Regular, at 0.8167 and 1.225 in Semibold, and at 0.5, 1 and 2 in Source Code Pro, where the foot's last row held as many pixels as the threshold or more.
  - On the graze plant, across the foot of "Typography gypsy", the measure of islands named no step that the threshold leaves unnamed. Under a translucent box alone it named no island.
- **What stands round a text.** The reads draw a span alone at its place, so they follow a group's move and nothing else. A group may carry its move and the attributes by which the renderer names it, which change nothing drawn. A page whose group carries anything else fails, naming the group, since a clip, a mask, an opacity or another transform would change what is drawn, and the reads would read ink it hides as a cover.
- **What stands round the page.** The swaths carry the page's own style, and the tool fails a page whose root carries an attribute a text would inherit. So a span drawn alone is drawn as the page draws it.
- **Neither a pre-filter nor a batch is built** (the cost, below). SVG paints in document order, so the parts drawn after a span are known exactly, which a pre-filter for the cover read would use. But one draw of the page with all text removed shows the earlier parts as well, a ground or a panel the span lies on, so no strip of it is blank; and a layer of only the later parts is a draw for each span, the cost it was to save. A box for every later part needs the whole of the path data the renderer writes, arcs and relative moves among it. The cover draw is cropped to the span's counted ink.

### 2. Entries and report

- **An entry of `dropped`,** as DEC-037 point 2 sets it: the element, the characters, `why`, and for `cut` and `covered` `share`. Every entry names one cause in `why`.
- **`share`** is the share of the span's strong ink the cause loses, from 0 to 1, as DEC-037 point 2 gives it. Through resvg, a span that holds no strong ink takes the share of its counted ink, as off the page is judged below, so a faint full stop hidden whole reads 1. The HTML renderer's report is unchanged.
- **`island`** is true on a `cut` or `covered` entry whose cause takes an island of the span's counted ink, whatever its share. Another entry holds no such key. So a reader tells a dot lost from a graze where both lose under one percent, and the command's line says so.
- **The tool's own drops take a cause.** A text or run resvg drew nothing of is `off the page` where its ink lies wholly past an edge, and `no ink` otherwise. Its ink there is its strong ink where it holds any, and else its counted ink. The entry keeps `whole`, true for a whole text and false for a run lost from a line, so a reader of it is unchanged.
  - **One fault takes one cause,** by how much of the span lies on the page. A span with any of that ink on the page is cut, so a text 100% past the edge is off the page, and one 99% past is cut with a share of 0.99.
  - **A text whose line lies wholly past a side is `off the page`, even beyond the swath's reach,** where the swath finds no ink of it. The engine's own geometry of its line, which the look holds, tells it from a text with no ink: its start, its measured width and its anchor. A span further past a side than the swath reaches, on a line that starts on the page, reads `no ink`, since the line does not lie wholly past the side. Such a line is longer than the page is wide.
- **A text whose runs left for two causes** gives an entry for each run, with `whole` false.
- **`dropped-not-read` is taken out,** with the command's line that said it. A reader of the key breaks. The key came with #3088's change, so only a release that holds that change and not this one's carries it.

### 3. A loss is mended where its cause lies, and no finding at validation names a cut text

- **No such loss is accepted,** as DEC-037 point 1 has it through a browser. A cut or covered entry through resvg is mended where its cause lies: in the piece's layout or content, or in the spacing a component or a brand sets between lines and between texts.
- **What the reviewer reads** is DEC-037 point 4's.
- **A finding at validation, from the engine's own measure, for a cut text,** is not taken. It reads no cover, and the engine's measure of a line and resvg's shaping differ (DEC-015), so its frame would call a text cut that resvg draws inside the page, and miss one resvg draws past it. In its strongest form, a finding at validation first and the read at the look as its backstop, it would stay a second place a cut text is named, to be kept in step with the first, for a measure that is wrong where resvg is the judge. The read at the look is the one place a text is named.

## Rationale

**Why the same two draws.** DEC-037's reasons for a draw alone and a change between two colours hold for resvg: the scene knows no cover, and changing one span's letters shows where the page lets them through, whatever their own colour. Others do not: resvg has no backplates, no forced palette and no z-index, so the document's order tells the painting order whole, and what hides a span is a later part's fill, its opacity, or a picture's alpha.

**Why room in the document the tool is given.** The page's frame clips a cut text away, so a read of the page as drawn finds a cut text whole or none of it. A swath that reaches past the page holds the ink to count. The draw that reads a span's own paint, and tells a span with no ink, has no room past the page. So a text wholly off it left no ink there, and the read that has room tells it from a text with no ink.

**Why one `why` for every entry.** DEC-037 point 2 has each entry give one cause in `why` because its readers parse that as one string. An entry without one would break that for the SVG renderer's report alone. Every entry names one, so a reader sorts them by cause as through a browser, and `whole` stays for a reader that wants the text or run.

**Why a threshold for size, and an island for shape.** A browser smooths a box's edge over the pixels it touches, and DEC-037 set its area between that graze and a háček's loss. Here a pixel is hidden only where a box covers all of it, so a graze hides whole rows, and a row is a larger part of a small letter than of a large one. At the text floor the least graze hid 8 to 13 pixels, where the háček lost 2 or 3, so no number names every háček there and leaves every graze unnamed. Nor does any number tell a dot from a graze. In Regular at an em of 40, the dot of ż held 14 or 15 pixels and the least graze 6 to 14, and at 20 the dots held 3 to 6 against a threshold of 4. Shape tells them apart: a graze takes part of a stroke, and a box over a dot takes all of an island. So the threshold judges a loss by its size against the em, and names a loss as large as a háček's whatever hid it. The measure judges its shape, and names a mark taken however small. A graze the threshold names is the cost. It is a box that touches the letters, which the layout mends by moving the box clear, where a mark lost unnamed costs a reader the word or the number.

**Why an island is taken where some of it is hidden and none of it shows.** Seven ways of taking an island were weighed at the threshold 0.01, on every step of a box's edge across each of the five dots, on every graze step and under the translucent boxes, in the three faces:
- **(A)**, where all of the island is hidden;
- **all of it but one pixel**;
- **(A2)**, where all of it at strong ink is hidden, and most of it at the lighter levels;
- **point 1's**, where the cause hides some of it wholly and none of it still shows at its level;
- **(A2) or point 1's**, an island either takes;
- **(B)**, where none of it still shows at its level;
- **(B) narrowed**, where none of it still shows at its level and some pixel of the span's ink is hidden wholly.

Two tests sort them. A rule must name no graze step that the threshold leaves unnamed, in any face, and no island under a translucent box; (B) and (B) narrowed fail both (Alternatives). Of the rules that pass, point 1's leaves the fewest steps unnamed at which a dot is gone, in every face, and (A2) or point 1's leaves the same steps as point 1's alone, with two tests where point 1's has one:
- **No graze.** Of the 19,565 graze steps the probes drew across the foot of "Typography gypsy" in each face from an em of 10 to 40, and the 260 past 40, it named none that the threshold leaves unnamed. A graze hides the foot's last row and fades the row above it, so the stroke still shows at its level. Below an em of about 14.1, where the threshold is one pixel, the test has no force on strong ink, since a cause that hides a strong pixel wholly is named there by its size. Its evidence there is the lighter levels, and it holds at every level from an em of 14.2 on.
- **Covered means hidden.** It asks the cause to hide some pixel wholly, which a translucent part never does. Under a box of opacity 0.8, 0.5 or 0.2 over each of the five dots, at ems of 20 and 40, it named none in any face, so under a translucent part alone covered keeps the meaning DEC-037 gives it through a browser. With an opaque part that hides a pixel of the dot too, it names the dot (Context, "Not probed").
- **The fewest dots left.** It left a dot gone and unnamed at 15,809 steps in Regular, 5,916 in Semibold and 3,498 in Source Code Pro, from an em of 10 to 40, where (A2) left 33,894, 10,090 and 6,343, and (A) 81,634, 12,570 and 8,928. Its shares of the steps at which a dot was gone, 0.057, 0.022 and 0.013, sum to 0.09 over the three faces, against (A2)'s 0.18 and (A)'s 0.37. It left none from an em of 20 on, nor past 40.

What point 1's rule leaves has one cause, stated in point 1's fourth gap: a box that fades a dot's ink at its own level without hiding any of it wholly. That is how it comes about, not how often. **Why a dot that keeps a row at its level is not named.** Where a box hides all of a dot but one row or column, and that row still shows at the dot's level, the dot is not gone: a reader sees a row of it at its level. The cause hides part of an island and leaves part of it showing at its level, as a graze does a stroke, so the read cannot tell that row from what a graze leaves, and the threshold judges both by their size. In the sample, a box hid some of a dot's strong ink and left it showing at its level in one row or column alone at 16,665 of 95,880 steps in Regular, 19,635 of 114,220 in Semibold and 20,323 of 121,276 in Source Code Pro.
