# Type, grid and space

How text is set, how a page is divided, and how the space between parts keeps a rhythm. The faces, the sizes, the scale and the unit of space a piece draws with are its brand's values; this topic covers how they are chosen and used.

## What the field teaches

- **Type serves the text.** Robert Bringhurst: typography exists to honour content. A face is chosen for the kind of reading it serves, long reading or a glance at a heading, and its first job is to be read. *The Elements of Typographic Style*.
- **Size, line spacing and line length are set together.** Bringhurst chooses the measure, the length of a line, for comfortable reading, and the leading, the space between lines, by the face, the text and the measure. A longer line needs more space between lines, so the eye finds the start of the next. Tim Brown: on a screen these are not fixed values but relations that respond to the reading conditions, and the typesetter decides how they change together as the screen changes. *Flexible Typesetting*.
- **Sizes come from a scale.** Bringhurst likens the sizes a typographer uses to the notes of a musical scale: chosen in a deliberate relation, not one at a time. Brown's modular scale derives a page's sizes from one ratio, so that they belong to one family of proportions. "More Meaningful Typography".
- **Space keeps a rhythm.** Bringhurst adds and removes vertical space in measured intervals, so text keeps its rhythm down the page and lines up across columns. Josef Müller-Brockmann sizes a grid's fields from the lines of text, so text and pictures share one measure.
- **The grid orders a page and a series of pages.** Müller-Brockmann divides a page into columns, rows, fields and the gutters between them. Text and pictures placed on that grid give each page an order, and give a series of pages one structure, so a reader learns where to look. The grid is an aid to deciding, not a guarantee of a good page. *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*.
- **Space is a tool, not what is left over.** Adam Wathan and Steve Schoger: start with too much space, and take some away, rather than add space only where a page looks cramped. Draw the space from a small set of steps, so it is chosen rather than nudged. *Refactoring UI*.

## Designing and judging by it

- Do texts of one kind share face, size, weight and spacing, and do texts of different kinds differ clearly?
- Do the page's sizes and spaces come from its brand's scale, or were some nudged by hand?
- Which grid does the page sit on? Where does a part leave it, and why?
- Can a line of body text be read without losing its place, at the size it is shown?
