## Summary

**In plain terms:** at some gates, the process engine checks that an authorisation came from another authority than the actor who makes the move. Where its caller names no actor, the engine's move asks the hosting service which account acts, and takes a made-up actor, `operator`, where the ask fails. Its status view and its check whether a move may be made take `operator` without asking. `operator` matches nobody, so the check passes whoever gave the authorisation, the actor included.

This record takes the engine out of the asking:

- **The engine asks nobody.** It takes the actor its caller names, and none by default.
- **No placeholder.** Where an authorisation names who gave it and no actor is named, a gate that must confirm another authority is not judged. The move through it is refused, and the refusal says to name the actor. The status view marks the gate as one it cannot judge, a field of its structured status says why, and the gate makes no wait.
- **Every other gate is judged as for any actor.** A move none of whose gates compares the actor goes on, and its journal entry names no actor.
- **Like with like.** The engine compares the actor with whoever gave the authorisation without regard to case or surrounding whitespace. A binding gives both in the same terms, and a binding of accounts on the hosting service gives them in one spelling of accounts, for project-kit's capabilities the one spelling of [ADR-094](ADR-094-acting-account-reading.md), accepted with this record.
- **A break, with a migration that warns.** A call that names no actor gets no default. A backbone migration warns where a process definition whose callers the release does not bring holds such a gate.
- **The cost.** project-management's moves name no actor wherever the identity they hand the engine is unresolved, and each such move to Done is then left out of the journal, with a warning. That ends only where the caller names the acting account in the binding's terms, as project-management's records decide ([project-kit#3164](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3164)). This record and ADR-094 alone relieve none of it.

## Context

**The terms.**

- **The actor** is the authority a move is made by, in the terms of the binding whose gate judges it ([COR-033](../../../.pkit/decisions/core/COR-033-process-substrate.md) P4).
- **A producer** is the authority an authorisation names as having given it, as the binding's predicate reports it.
- **A cross-authority gate** is a gate that must confirm that its authorisation came from another authority than the actor, as a gate of kind `authorisation-artifact` does ([ADR-036](ADR-036-open-region-engine-strategy.md) point 4).
- **A binding of accounts** is a binding whose actors and producers are accounts on the hosting service.

**What happens today.**

- **The engine's default actor.** Where its caller names no actor, the engine's move asks the hosting service for the login, through `gh`, and takes the fixed value `operator` where the ask fails. Its status view and its check whether a move may be made take `operator` without asking. The engine's page warns a caller to name one actor on each call, or a sign-off gate can give two verdicts.
- **What the placeholder does at the cross-authority gate.** The gate passes where the authorisation's producer is not the actor, compared as two strings, case and all. `operator` equals no login, so a gate judged under it passes whoever produced the authorisation, the actor included.
- **What project-management's move hands the engine.** It writes the move to the tracker first, then hands it to the engine to journal, and only warns where the engine refuses. So the engine's verdict decides what the journal records, and the move to Done is guarded by the capability's own approval check ([project-management:DEC-026](../../../.pkit/capabilities/project-management/decisions/DEC-026-work-ownership-lifecycle.md)). As the actor, it hands the engine its membership identity: the login of the user's account the service answers the credential acts as, wherever it answers one, and otherwise `PM_INVOKER_LOGIN` wherever that is set, which may name the person an agent works for. Where that identity is unresolved, it hands none, and the placeholder decides. Its gate on Done confirms the pull request's merge, whose producer the capability's gate command reports ([project-management:DEC-033](../../../.pkit/capabilities/project-management/decisions/DEC-033-rebind-issue-lifecycle-onto-process-substrate.md) D2 and D4).

**What the records already say.**

- **A gate's authorisation must come from another authority than the actor it gates** (COR-033 P4). The engine confirms neither the actor nor the producer, so the gate holds only where the caller names the actor truthfully ([the process area's reference, "Gate-checkability"](../../../.pkit/process/README.md#gate-checkability-the-load-bearing-rule)).
- **The engine's cross-authority check** passes where the authorisation exists, names its producer, and the producer differs from the actor (ADR-036 point 4). Written so, it passes with no actor named, a case it never met while the engine always had a default. The same record holds an indeterminate predicate to be the only case in which an open region's exit fails closed (point 3).
- **The engine is content-free,** shipped in the backbone's binary and interpreting the definitions capabilities own ([ADR-020](ADR-020-process-engine-code-home.md)).
- **A wait** is derived from the gates' verdicts: a subject with no legal move, or one whose only way on is a person's move, with no autonomous move open ([COR-034](../../../.pkit/decisions/core/COR-034-human-pause-gate.md)).
- **The journal's entries** record who moved the subject, one for each move the engine executes (COR-033 P7). Where project-management keeps a journal, it is the capability's canonical audit trail. A move that fails to journal is surfaced rather than dropped, and where labels carry the state, the drift check reads a state change with no entry as an out-of-band change or a governed move that failed to journal ([project-management:DEC-049](../../../.pkit/capabilities/project-management/decisions/DEC-049-audit-journal-model.md) points 1 and 3).
- **A command's machine-readable output is a contract** of its own keys, what each means and the values of its sets, never its prose. A key or a value added misleads no caller, one that goes is a declared breaking change of its component, and none comes to mean anything else. A caller reads what it does not know as could not tell, and an undocumented key means what it gives ([ADR-097](ADR-097-machine-output-contract.md) points 2 to 5). Neither the engine's page nor the CLI reference states the engine's structured status's `legal_moves[].indeterminate`. The engine gives it true where it can give a gate no verdict, such as for a predicate that gave no answer, a gate with no predicate, a gate kind it does not know, a keyed inner process with no subject, and every move from an indeterminate position, where it runs no gate.
- **A breaking change of a command's signature ships a migration,** such as a flag whose meaning inverts ([COR-010](../../../.pkit/decisions/core/COR-010-resource-lifecycle.md), "Migrations are mandatory on adopter-breaking surface changes").

## Decision

**The process engine takes the actor its caller names and no other, and never a placeholder. Where a cross-authority gate's verdict turns on an actor nobody named, it refuses the move.**

### 1. The actor is the caller's

- **From the caller alone.** The engine takes the actor from its caller and from nowhere else: it asks no service, and takes no default. Which authority the actor is, the binding's records decide (COR-033 P4). An actor named blank names none.
- **One rule in every command.** Every engine command that judges a gate, such as the status view, the check whether a move may be made and the move, judges it for the actor named, or for none. So a precheck and its move judge one actor.
- **No placeholder.** No fixed value stands for an actor no caller named.
- **By hand.** A person who names the actor by hand names it as the binding's reference says. The engine takes their word, as it takes every caller's, so a misspelt actor is a false one (the process area's reference, "Gate-checkability").

### 2. The engine compares like with like

- **What it sets aside.** The engine compares the actor with the producer the binding's predicate reports, setting aside case, as Unicode's case folding sets it aside, and surrounding whitespace, and confirms neither. To the engine, under any binding, two names that differ only in those are one authority, which can only refuse more.
- **Every letter's case.** A binding's names may hold any letter, such as names in a file, so the engine folds the case of every letter.
- **What a binding owes the comparison.** A binding names its actor, and its predicates report each producer, in the same terms, so that the engine compares like with like. A binding of accounts gives both in one spelling of accounts. For the bindings project-kit's capabilities ship, that is the one spelling, given with the backbone's function ([ADR-094](ADR-094-acting-account-reading.md) point 5). A binding whose authorities are no accounts, such as a sign-off by a name in a file, names them in its own terms.

### 3. With no actor named, a cross-authority gate is judged only where the actor cannot change its verdict

- **Judged as for any actor where the actor cannot change its verdict.** Where the authorisation does not exist, or names no producer, the gate is shut. Where its predicate cannot be evaluated, it is indeterminate, with its reason.
- **Not judged where the authorisation exists and names its producer,** since the comparison with the actor is all that is left, and nothing can confirm it. The engine's reason gives what the predicate answered, the producer included, for people. No key of the structured status gives the producer: a caller acts on a gate not judged only by naming the actor and asking again, and one that compared the producer with an actor itself would keep a second copy of the engine's comparison ([ADR-057](ADR-057-backbone-engines-and-command-limits.md) point 2).
- **The move refuses.** The refusal says that no actor was named, and that a caller names the authority the move is made by, in its binding's terms, and never a placeholder.
- **The status view marks it `?`,** the mark it gives a gate whose verdict it cannot give, with the reason that no actor was named.
- **The structured status carries it as indeterminate,** as it carries every gate whose verdict the engine cannot give. That is what the key already means. No contract states it, and a key no contract states means what it gives (ADR-097 point 4, its bullet "A fix changes no meaning"): the engine can give the gate no verdict, the move fails closed, and the reason says why. So one more such gate changes no meaning (ADR-097 point 5).
- **A field of its own tells it apart.** A reader that took ADR-036's contract for on-call, which names only a predicate's causes, for all the key gives reads this gate as a predicate to mend, as it already reads a gate with no predicate, or of a kind the engine does not know. So the field `needs_actor` marks the entry as not judged for want of an actor. It is true on such an entry alone, whose `indeterminate` is true, and false on every other entry, another indeterminate one among them. A document without it reads as false, which falls safe (ADR-097 point 3), since every release before it named a default actor, and so judged every such gate.
- **It makes no wait.** For a wait, the engine reads a gate so marked as one that may be open, so it neither shows nor journals a wait that only the missing actor made (COR-034). A gate judged with no actor named gives a wait its verdict, as with any actor.

### 4. A move none of whose gates compares the actor goes on

Its journal entry records the actor as empty, the form a blank actor takes, which names none, and the status view shows the move as made by no named actor.

### 5. What this binds

- **pkit's engine, and the bindings project-kit's capabilities ship.** Point 2's rule on what a binding owes binds those bindings.
- **A binding another project writes** reads the engine's comparison in the process area's reference, which states it as part of the predicate's contract. Nothing here binds it, since an architecture record reaches no other project ([COR-025](../../../.pkit/decisions/core/COR-025-adr-decision-space.md)). The rule follows from how this engine compares, which is the engine's own behaviour, and an architecture record places the engine's behaviour, as ADR-020 and ADR-036 do. So no core record is proposed for it.

## Rationale

**Why the engine reads no account.** The engine is content-free (COR-033): an actor is whatever authority a binding means, and only the binding knows which. The process area's walkthrough binds a sign-off read from a file, whose actors are names that no account of the service's would match. A default read from the service would tie every binding to one service's accounts. It would also cost callers that need none of it: each status view would ask the service, and a binding whose checks read only local files would wait out the time bound offline, then refuse. And it guards nothing a named actor does not. Any caller may name an actor, and the engine takes its word, so a default helps only an honest caller who left the actor out. So the caller that knows the binding's terms names the actor, and the engine refuses where it is told none.

**Why which authority is the actor stays with the binding.** P4 asks that an authorisation come from another authority than the actor, and which authority makes a move turns on the binding's terms: whether an agent that works for a person is an authority of its own, the person's, or one with whoever holds its credential. Only the binding's records can say. project-management's say it already where the service answers that the credential acts as a user's account: every command names that account, the actor its moves hand the engine among them ([project-management:DEC-021](../../../.pkit/capabilities/project-management/decisions/DEC-021-team-membership-gate.md), Implications, "Every reader of the invoker's identity"). Where the service names no user's account, DEC-021 rests whether the variable or git's email may name the invoker on evidence of its own ([project-kit#3164](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3164)), and a choice of the actor here would make that choice ahead of the evidence. What the engine needs, whatever they say, is an actor and producers in the same terms. So this record decides that much, and leaves the choice to the binding.

**Why the engine sets aside only case and whitespace.** The engine's comparison is the check COR-033's Rationale calls load-bearing. A producer reported in another spelling than the actor's makes an actor's own merge read as another authority's. Respelling in the engine would carry one service's spellings into a content-free engine, so the binding spells both sides. Setting aside case and surrounding whitespace carries no service's spelling: it reads no form, and can only refuse more. Where two must differ, unequal reads as another authority, so a comparison that heeded either would fail open on a name typed in another case, or with a space beside it, and the refusal of a move with no actor sends people to name one. A match here only refuses, so the engine folds every letter's case, where ADR-094's function folds only the ASCII letters a login holds, since its match grants.

**Why no placeholder actor.** A placeholder equals no account, so every cross-authority gate passes under it. Refusing such a gate costs its caller a named actor. Where a caller can name none, it costs more. A caller that makes its move before the engine judges it, as project-management's does (Context), and names no actor, leaves each such move through such a gate out of the journal, with a warning. That is the honest record of a move whose gate nobody judged. The capability surfaces a move that failed to journal rather than drop it, and where labels carry the state, its drift check reports the gap (DEC-049 points 1 and 3). The gap closes only where the caller names the acting account in the binding's terms, as project-management's records decide (#3164). A value in other terms than the producer's, such as an App's bare login, or one that names another authority, such as the person an agent works for, would pass the agent's own merge as another authority's, which reopens the defect this record closes. Reading the acting account names an App's account as a bot's (ADR-094 point 2), and nothing in this record or ADR-094 has the capability hand that account to the engine, so they alone close none of the gap. A move none of whose gates compares the actor loses nothing by an unknown actor, so it goes on, and the journal says so plainly.

**Why a gate goes unjudged only where its authorisation names a producer.** Where nobody gave the authorisation, or it names nobody, the gate is shut whatever the actor, so the missing actor changes nothing. The wait it makes is the one COR-034 exists to show, a sign-off nobody gave, and with no default actor, a status view that names none is the usual one. Only where an authorisation names its producer is the comparison all that is left. Its verdict is then unknown, not a determinate no, so the status view marks it as it marks a gate whose predicate gives no verdict, and its reason says what is missing. Its structured status carries it as indeterminate, so an agent that reads the engine's contract for on-call reads a refusal that names why, not a wait for a sign-off. A field of its own tells it from every other gate the engine cannot judge, so the derivation of waits, and an agent that names the actor, read it from a field, never from the reason's prose.

**Why a gate not judged makes no wait.** A wait tells a person what a subject waits on (COR-034). A gate read as shut for want of an actor would show a wait nobody is in, and a move would journal its start, then its end at the next move that names an actor. Reading the gate as one that may be open, for a wait alone, loosens no guard, since a move through it refuses. The predicate runs, so a person who checks a gate without naming an actor sees what it answers. It costs a view or a move that names no actor something only where the actor a caller would name produced the authorisation:

- **The view shows no wait that the actor's own view derives from such a gate.** A wait that reads the subject's want of a legal move is not shown, so the view can show no legal move and no wait at once. Nor is an `awaiting-human` wait that the actor's view shows because such a gate shuts an autonomous move, since this view reads that move as a way out. A wait for a `user` move reads the move's presence, never its gate (COR-034), so both views show it.
- **A move that names no actor journals no wait** that only such a gate makes at the state it lands in. The wait is journaled at the next move or reconcile that names an actor, later than it began, or not at all if it ends first.

Neither guards a move, since only the status views and the journaling of waits read waits. Nor would either alternative avoid them. A placeholder passes every such gate, so a view judged under one would show no wait from it either, save where a producer bears the placeholder's name, and reading the gate as one that may be open gives the status's `blocked` the value a placeholder would. So `blocked` keeps what it means (ADR-097 point 5). A default read from the service would cost the same, but only where that account produced the authorisation. So the costs fall on a view or a move whose caller would have named the actor, and are against that caller's view alone.

**Why a record of its own.** The engine's rule holds for every binding, whatever its authorities are: it reads no account and respells none. The reading of the acting account holds for one service's accounts. So each is argued, and revisited, on its own grounds. They rest on each other, and are accepted together. A binding of accounts compares like with like only once both sides are in one spelling, which ADR-094 decides, so this record's rule on such a binding takes it (point 2), and ADR-094 leaves the engine's asking and comparing to points 1 and 2 here. The engine's cost, moves to Done left out of the journal where project-management names no actor, waits on #3164's records either way, so bundling the two would relieve nothing.

### Alternatives considered

- **The engine's default actor is the reading's account,** where its caller names none. Rejected: it ties a content-free engine to one service's accounts, which a binding of names in a file cannot use. Every status view would ask the service, and a binding of local checks would wait offline. And it guards nothing a named actor does not, since any caller may name one.
- **A flag that has the engine take the reading's account.** Not taken: it puts the hosting service back into the engine, for what a person gets by naming the account the reading command shows.
- **This record names the account a binding of accounts gives as its actor,** the one its credential acts as. Not taken: which authority makes a move turns on the binding's terms. For project-management's binding, DEC-021 decides it where the service names a user's account, and rests the rest on evidence of its own (#3164), which this record would decide ahead of. The engine needs only an actor and producers in the same terms.
- **The engine respells the actor and the producer.** Rejected: it carries one service's spellings into a content-free engine.
- **The engine compares exactly, as written.** Rejected: a name typed in another case, or with a space beside it, reads as another authority, which fails open, and the refusal of a move with no actor sends people to name one.
- **A gate not judged wherever no actor is named,** whether or not its authorisation exists. Rejected: a gate nobody signed off is shut whatever the actor, and a view that names no actor would hide the wait COR-034 exists to show.
- **A gate not judged carried as a determinate no** in the structured status. Rejected: its verdict is unknown, and `indeterminate` false would claim a determinate one, a meaning the key does not have (ADR-097 point 5). An agent that keys on ADR-036's contract for on-call would read a correct wait for a sign-off, where the remedy is to name the actor. Told apart from other indeterminate gates by a code in its reason instead of a field, a reader would parse prose to learn what derives a wait (ADR-097 point 2).
- **A key of its own in place of `indeterminate`** for a gate not judged. Not taken: each entry must still give `indeterminate` a value, and only `true` is honest, since `false` would claim a determinate verdict (ADR-097 point 5).
- **A key of the structured status for the producer of a gate not judged.** Not taken: what a caller does about such a gate is to name the actor and ask again, which the engine judges. A caller that compared the producer with an actor itself would keep a second copy of the engine's comparison (ADR-057 point 2), so the reason gives the producer for people.
- **A move with no actor named is journaled, its gate marked as not judged,** in place of refusing it. Not taken. The engine answers the move check and the move alike (point 1), and a wrapper that checks before it writes, as the process area's reference orders it, reads that answer as leave to write. A "not judged" answer would be a yes there, which takes the caller's word for the gate, as P4 forbids, or a no, which is this record's refusal. And an entry for a move the engine refused is no entry for a move it executes (COR-033 P7). It is weighed again if a credential that never resolves proves the usual one in a project that keeps a journal.
- **The engine refuses every move with no actor named.** Rejected: a move whose gates compare no actor would stop for nothing.
- **No migration, since none can mend the break.** Rejected: COR-010 asks a migration of a change that breaks against a project's installed state, and makes no exception for a break no migration can mend. A warning reaches the person at the upgrade, where the break would reach them first at a refused move, as project-management's DEC-033 D6 found for an override it cannot rewrite.
- **The engine's part inside the record on the reading of the acting account.** Not taken: the engine's rule holds for every binding and the reading for one service's accounts, and bundling them relieves none of the engine's cost ("Why a record of its own").

  - the status document's contract, where it is stated (ADR-097 point 6), and the engine's page say what `legal_moves[].indeterminate` means, which neither states yet (Context), and what `needs_actor` means.

  None of it changes the form of anything a project holds. The journal's empty actor is a string, as its schema asks.
- **What changes for callers.**
  - **The lost default.** A call that names no actor gets none: the move's `--actor` no longer defaults to the `gh` login, nor the status view's and the move check's to `operator`. The flag's meaning changes where it is left out, a breaking change of a command's signature of the kind COR-010 names, a flag whose meaning inverts, and a call that leaves it out can be written into a binding's code, which is installed state the change breaks. So it is a breaking change of the backbone, declared by its changeset, and ships a migration (below).
  - **The comparison that sets aside case and whitespace,** and **the status view's mark, reason and field for a gate not judged.** A producer that differs from the actor only in case or surrounding whitespace now reads as the actor, and a gate once passed under the placeholder shows as `?`. Each breaks existing behaviour, declared by the same changeset. Neither owes a migration: nothing a project holds needs rewriting for either to hold, and each can only refuse more, or say why it refuses.
- **A migration that warns.** The backbone's, in the change that takes the default away, looks for each process definition that holds an `authorisation-artifact` gate and whose callers the release does not bring: one the project declares, or one a capability declares whose origin the project's install registry records as incubated in the repository ([COR-031](../../../.pkit/decisions/core/COR-031-capability-origin.md) D2) or as sourced from another repository ([COR-041](../../../.pkit/decisions/core/COR-041-external-source-distribution.md)). A definition of a capability recorded with neither origin, which reads as one the methodology ships, is the release's. That is its positive sign that a call naming no actor may now be refused. For each, it names the definition and the gate, and warns that a caller of the engine's status view, move check and move must now name the actor, in the binding's terms, for the gate to be judged. Where it finds none, it says so and exits clean. It writes nothing, so running it again changes nothing (COR-010; the lifecycle reference, ["Script contract"](../../../.pkit/lifecycle/migrations.md#script-contract)).
  - **A definition a capability of the release declares** gets no warning, and nor does a project's override of one, such as project-management's override of its lifecycle (DEC-033 D6). Their callers are that capability's own code, which the same release brings, and what the capability's moves do with no actor named is said in the capability's reference, which the work brings up to this record (above). A backbone migration can name no setting of a capability's, since the backbone may not depend on a capability (COR-010, "The dependency direction cannot invert").
  - **A person who runs the engine's move by hand** on any definition is told by the release's notes, which say that the move names its actor or a cross-authority gate refuses.
  - **It departs from the contract's "updates the affected resource".** The resource the break reaches is a binding's code, which names its actor by its own records, and no migration can know which authority a binding's moves are made by (point 1). So it warns and updates nothing, as DEC-033 D6's migration warns of an override it cannot rewrite.
- **What it costs.**
  - **Callers that name no actor.** A direct caller of the engine's status view or move check, a person running a move by hand, and a capability another project writes whose code moves a subject with no actor named, each names an actor or sees cross-authority gates refuse where an authorisation names its producer.
  - **Moves to Done where the caller names no actor,** as project-management's moves name none wherever the identity they hand the engine is unresolved, as it can be under an installation's token. Each such move through the gate on Done is left out of the journal, with a warning. Where labels carry the state, the drift check reports the gap. Where the board or a derivation carries it, the check is skipped, and the warning is the only trace. The gap closes only where the caller names the acting account in the binding's terms, as project-management's records on #3164 decide. This record and ADR-094 alone relieve none of it, since neither has the capability hand the engine a bot's account the reading names, which ADR-094 point 8 leaves to the capability's records. A move whose named actor is the account that merged is refused already (DEC-033 D2 and D4), so the loss that matters is a move to Done after another account merged.
- **Left out, for a record of its own:** which account a capability's commands name as the engine's actor where the service names no user's account, which is project-management's to decide (#3164). Where the service names a user's account, DEC-021 has every command name that account, the actor its moves hand the engine among them.
- **Stands on** COR-010, COR-025, COR-031, COR-033, COR-034, COR-041, COR-046, ADR-020, ADR-036, ADR-057, ADR-097, and project-management's DEC-021, DEC-026, DEC-033 and DEC-049, all accepted; and on ADR-094 point 5, proposed beside it, for the spelling a binding of accounts gives both sides in. The two records are accepted together, in this change, with project-management's DEC-076, DEC-078 and DEC-079, all five at once: ADR-094 rests on points 1 and 2 here, and neither is accepted without the other.
