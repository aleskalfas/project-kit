## Summary

**In plain terms:** pkit sometimes needs to know which account on the hosting service its credential acts as: `pkit report` to say which account a report is posted as, and project-management's commands for the membership gate, to name who makes a move, and more. Each asks on its own today, and they fail in different ways. Under a GitHub App's token the usual question is refused. [ADR-098](ADR-098-engine-takes-named-actor.md), accepted with this record, takes the process engine out of the asking.

This record makes one reading in the backbone, which every reader uses:

- **Only the service names the account.** The reading names an account, a user's or a bot's, only from the hosting service's own answer. Where the service answers that the credential's account is no user's and the reading can name none, because the service refuses the request as an integration's or answers with an account of a kind unknown, the reading says so with a cause of its own. Every other outcome is an unresolved identity, with its cause. Nothing else names an account.
- **One spelling.** Accounts are compared and written in one spelling that carries the kind: a user's by its login, a bot's with the service's `[bot]` suffix. No entry that names a person changes. Case and surrounding whitespace are ignored. Only those two kinds were measured, so every other kind is a kind unknown.
- **A missing kind decides nothing.** Where a kind is missing, a comparison that turns on it cannot tell. It then grants nothing, passes nothing as another authority's and frees no claim.
- **An answer lasts one run.**
- **A binding of accounts spells both sides,** its actor and each producer, so that the engine compares like with like.
- **`pkit report` posts only as an account it can name,** and otherwise keeps the draft.
- **What breaks.** `pkit report` keeps the draft where it posted once the person confirmed. In project-management, an App registered as the remote reviewer by its bare login stops matching its own account. A migration rewrites such an entry where the service gives two definite answers that it names an App's account, and warns otherwise.
- **GitHub only, provisionally,** held in the backbone as the landing of pull requests is.
- **Each reader decides the rest in its own records:** what it does where the reading names no account, and for a capability, whose claims and approvals count and which account its commands name as the engine's actor. `pkit report`'s is in this record.

## Context

**The terms.**

- **The acting account** is the account on the hosting service that a credential acts as: the one the service records as the author of each write made with it.
- **A reader** is code that needs the acting account.
- **The service's answer** is the hosting service's reply, to a request made with the credential, that names the account the credential acts as and the account's kind.
- **An account's kind** is what the service records it as. This record reads two kinds: a user's account, and a bot's account, which the service's REST interface gives by its login with a `[bot]` suffix, as it gives the account an app acts through. A user account kept for an agent, a machine user, is a user's account. Every other account is of a *kind unknown*: an organization's, an imported placeholder account (a *mannequin*), an account the service records as a bot's but gives with no suffix, and any type the reading does not know (point 3).
- **An unnamed account** is the outcome where the service answers that the credential's account is no user's, and the reading can name none: it refuses the request for the account because the credential is an integration's, or it answers with an account of a kind unknown (point 2).
- **An unresolved identity** is the outcome where the service gives no answer about who the credential is, reported with its cause ([project-kit#3162](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3162)).
- **The reading names no account** under an unnamed account and under an unresolved identity alike.
- **A run** is one invocation of one command, from its start to its exit. The state-issue seam defines the term for a capability's commands ([ADR-071](ADR-071-state-issue-seam.md) point 8), and this record takes it for every command, the backbone's among them.
- **The service's host** is the host a request to the hosting service goes to, such as an enterprise's own. It is no *agent host*, the software outside pkit that starts harness sessions for a person ([ADR-091](ADR-091-no-host-component.md)).

**What happens today.** Five readers ask the service which account acts, each on its own: the engine's move, `pkit report`, and three of project-management's. They fail three ways: with no account, with a placeholder, or with the actor left to the engine.

- **The engine's move** asks the service for the login where its caller names no actor, and takes the fixed value `operator` where the ask fails, which passes every cross-authority gate. ADR-098 takes the engine out of the asking.
- **What project-management's move hands the engine** as the actor is its membership identity: the login of the user's account the service answers the credential acts as, wherever it answers one, and otherwise `PM_INVOKER_LOGIN` wherever that is set, which may name the person an agent works for. Where that identity is unresolved, it hands none. Its gate on Done confirms the pull request's merge, whose producer the capability's gate command reports from gh's JSON ([project-management:DEC-033](../../../.pkit/capabilities/project-management/decisions/DEC-033-rebind-issue-lifecycle-onto-process-substrate.md) D2 and D4).
- **`pkit report`** names the login in the confirmation it asks before it posts. Where the ask fails, it names "your gh CLI identity (login unresolved)", and posts once the person confirms.
- **project-management's three readers of its own:** the membership gate, which reports an identity it cannot resolve, with its cause; the review command's check for a pull request of one's own; and workstream sync's check for comments of its own.

**What was measured.** On a trial of an agent working under a GitHub App installation's token ([project-kit#3004](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3004)), the request for the signed-in user was refused with "Resource not accessible by integration (HTTP 403)". Read afterwards under the maintainer's own credential ([project-kit#3164](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/3164)), the App's account has two spellings:

- **the service's REST interface** gives its login as `<name>[bot]`, of type `Bot`;
- **gh's JSON** gives the same account as `app/<name>`, marked as a bot.

gh writes the second and takes the first. project-kit's own workflow that keeps the friction report's tracking issue lists its issues by the author `github-actions[bot]`, since a listing by `app/github-actions` matches nothing, and the issues listed then give their author as `app/github-actions`.

gh's own source says how its JSON writes an account ([cli/cli, `api/queries_issue.go`](https://github.com/cli/cli/blob/trunk/api/queries_issue.go), [`api/queries_pr.go`](https://github.com/cli/cli/blob/trunk/api/queries_pr.go) and [`api/query_builder.go`](https://github.com/cli/cli/blob/trunk/api/query_builder.go)):

- **an author the service records as a user** by its login, with its id, marked as no bot;
- **an author the service does not record as a user** as `app/` and its login, marked as a bot, with no id. gh asks the service for an author's id only where it is a user's, and writes every author without one so, an app's account, a mannequin and an organization alike;
- **a pull request's merging account,** `mergedBy`, as it writes an author, since gh declares it an author and asks for it as it asks for one. So a user's merge comes with its id, and any other with `app/` and the bot flag;
- **a comment's author** by its login alone, with no kind. For an app's account, that is the bare name gh prefixes elsewhere;
- **an issue's assignees** by their logins, read from the field the service's GraphQL interface declares to hold users' accounts.

Whether an installation's token can learn its own account and kind, by any request, is not measured. The trial's two attempts to have its agent ask failed before the agent was reached. Nor is how the service's surfaces give an account of any kind but a user's and an app's, such as a mannequin or a bot that is no app's, or which account the service records as merging a pull request that a merge queue lands.

**What the service documents.**

- **A user's login** holds only letters, digits and dashes, and a managed user's adds an underscore and the enterprise's short code ([GitHub, "Username considerations for external authentication"](https://docs.github.com/en/enterprise-cloud@latest/admin/managing-iam/iam-configuration-reference/username-considerations-for-external-authentication)). So no user's login holds a bracket.
- **An app's name** may not be an existing account's, unless it is its owner's own user or organization name ([GitHub, "Registering a GitHub App"](https://docs.github.com/en/apps/creating-github-apps/registering-a-github-app/registering-a-github-app)). So an app's account may share its owner's name.
- **An installation's token** is refused by some REST endpoints. The service's guide gives a GraphQL query for the signed-in account, its `viewer`, which the interface declares a user, as an example of a request made with one, and does not say what it answers ([GitHub, "Authenticating as a GitHub App installation"](https://docs.github.com/en/apps/creating-github-apps/authenticating-with-a-github-app/authenticating-as-a-github-app-installation)).
- **An account's name is looked up without regard to case,** as the REST reference says of a repository's owner ([GitHub, "REST API endpoints for assignees"](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/issues/assignees)).
- **The service's search** names an app's account as `app/<name>`, as in `author:app/robot` ([GitHub, "Searching issues and pull requests"](https://docs.github.com/en/search-github/searching-on-github/searching-issues-and-pull-requests)).
- **A login given up by a rename** is free for anyone else to claim ([GitHub, "Changing your GitHub username"](https://docs.github.com/en/account-and-profile/setting-up-and-managing-your-personal-account-on-github/managing-personal-account-settings/changing-your-github-username)).

**What the records already say.**

- **Two outcomes where nothing resolves the identity.** A command of project-management's that only reads and reports tells a read that did not answer from the service's answer that the credential names no user's account. The service gives the second where it refuses the request for the account because the credential is an integration's, as it refuses a workflow's own token and an App's, and where it answers with an account that is no user's. The resolution of the identity says which it is, with the cause in the service's words, so that no command reads the service's error text for itself, and the line rests on the service's status and words ([project-management:DEC-073](../../../.pkit/capabilities/project-management/decisions/DEC-073-reads-take-no-gate.md) point 2, and its Rationale, "Why two outcomes where the identity does not resolve").
- **A gate's authorisation must come from another authority than the actor it gates** ([COR-033](../../../.pkit/decisions/core/COR-033-process-substrate.md) P4).
- **A claim a seam cannot judge is never freed.** The ownership seam treats no claim it cannot judge as free ([ADR-041](ADR-041-instance-ownership-substrate-contract.md) point 7), and the state-issue seam never drops a comment for a call that failed, since dropping a claim could free a held state (ADR-071 point 3). Each judges an author by its login.
- **The backbone may not depend on a capability** ([COR-010](../../../.pkit/decisions/core/COR-010-resource-lifecycle.md), "The dependency direction cannot invert").
- **A capability's scripts reach the backbone by command, and each computation keeps one home** ([ADR-057](ADR-057-backbone-engines-and-command-limits.md) points 1 and 2). A query a capability supplies runs with its environment marked offline (point 3).
- **The backbone's landing of pull requests** is GitHub-only, labelled, and placed provisionally, with the core question raised in [project-kit#1222](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/1222). It reads its host from the caller's environment, and its documents are a versioned contract. A base that merges through a queue merges only through it ([ADR-061](ADR-061-hosting-service-acts-in-the-backbone.md) points 3, 4, 5 and 7).
- **A command's machine-readable output is a contract** of its own keys, what each means and the values of its sets, never its prose. A key or a value added misleads no caller, one that goes is a declared breaking change of its component, and none comes to mean anything else. A caller reads what it does not know as could not tell ([ADR-097](ADR-097-machine-output-contract.md) points 2 to 5).
- **`pkit report`'s confirmation names the account it posts as,** so that a split between the command line's account and the browser's never misattributes a post ([project-kit#662](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/662)). With nobody at the terminal, it keeps the draft and posts nothing ([ADR-047](ADR-047-report-cross-repo-write.md) point 2).
- **The membership gate** reads the service's login first, by the request its item 1 names, git's configured email second and an environment variable third. Where the service answers that the credential acts as a user's account, that account's login alone is the invoker, for the gate and for every command that names who runs it, and neither other surface stands beside it or in its place. Where it does not, a match on any surface passes. Its code asks the service for the account's type with its login, and reads the variable, in place of the login, only where the service names no user's account. The record leaves identity resolution's edge cases, the posture on network errors, and caching to its implementation ([project-management:DEC-021](../../../.pkit/capabilities/project-management/decisions/DEC-021-team-membership-gate.md), "Identity resolution" and "Why mechanism details are deferred"). It fixes the spelling of a member's `github_login` only for a user's account, as the login the service spells, and the member list's schema describes it as the value `gh api user --jq .login` returns for the member: the REST interface's answer to the request for the signed-in user, which an installation's token is refused (above). Its `add-member` looks an entry's login up with the service, which answers the account's login and type.
- **A service's account in project-management's lists.** The capability admits a CI service's account as a member, and has a workflow set `PM_INVOKER_LOGIN` from a secret where the service does not answer that its credential acts as a user's account (DEC-021, Implications, "CI invocations"). It registers a remote reviewer agent's account, a bot's, an App's or a service account's, and matches a verdict's author against it ([project-management:DEC-028](../../../.pkit/capabilities/project-management/decisions/DEC-028-agent-as-approver-paths.md), "Path 1: Remote agent"). None fixes how such an account is spelled, and DEC-028's example writes one by a bare login.
- **Where an App's bare entry matches today.** The remote reviewer's match reads a verdict's author from gh's comment JSON, which gives an App's account by its bare name, so a bare entry matches it. The state-issue seam reads a comment's author from the REST interface, which gives `<name>[bot]`, and compares it exactly with the members' entries, so a bare member entry never matches the App's comments there. At the gate, a bare `PM_INVOKER_LOGIN` matches a bare member entry.

**What the record proposed beside this one decides.** ADR-098 has the engine take the actor its caller names and no other, asking no service, and compare it with an authorisation's producer setting aside only case and surrounding whitespace (its points 1 and 2). The two records are accepted together. This one rests on those two points, and ADR-098 rests on point 5 here (Implications, "Stands on").

## Decision

**Which account a credential acts as is read once, in the backbone, and only the hosting service's answer names it and its kind. Every reader compares accounts in one spelling that carries the kind, and where a missing kind leaves a comparison unable to tell, takes the answer that grants nothing and frees nothing.**

### 1. One reading of the acting account, in the backbone

- **One home.** One module of the backbone, the acting-account reading, asks the service which account the credential acts as. Nothing else in pkit asks.
- **Every reader takes it from there:** the account `pkit report` posts as (point 6), and each command of a capability that needs the account its credential acts as (point 8), among them the actor it names to the engine, where its records name that account (point 5). No reader asks the service which account acts a way of its own, a tool's own shorthand for the signed-in account among them.
- **The service's own comparison is no reading.** Where a surface a reader reads gives the service's own answer to whether the credential's account wrote something, such as a comment's `viewerDidAuthor`, the reader may take that answer in place of naming the account and comparing. It names no account, so it is no reading of the acting account, and the service compares by its own record of both accounts, so it is no comparison of pkit's (point 3).
- **How a reader reaches it.** The backbone's code imports it. A capability's scripts reach it through a reading command of the backbone, whose output is a document with a schema version, under the contract ADR-097 sets for a command's machine-readable output, and the CLI reference states.
- **What the document gives:** the account in the one spelling (point 3), its kind, `user` or `bot`, the numeric id the service gives it, and its login as the service answered it.
- **Where the reading names no account,** the document gives the cause, as a value of a listed set, one for each case point 2 names:
  - the integration's refusal, and an account of a kind unknown, the two causes of an unnamed account;
  - another refusal; no answer, given apart as `gh` missing, the offline marker or a timeout where the reading can tell them; and an answer that names no account, the causes of an unresolved identity.

  A reason beside the cause gives the service's words, for people. A reader decides on the cause, never on the reason (ADR-097 point 2).
- **When its keys are null.** The document gives each account by its `account`, the one spelling, its `kind`, its `id` and its `login`:
  - **resolved,** each is given, and the `id` is null only where the service's answer gives none;
  - **where the reading names no account,** each is null, the `login` among them even where the service answered with an account of a kind unknown, and the cause is given;
  - **asked to spell or to compare** (point 3), the `id` is null, since the function asks the service nothing, and the `login` is the one the caller gave. An account of a kind unknown, or whose kind no surface gave, has a null `kind` and a null `account`, and a comparison gives one of its three answers.

  A reader names an account only from `account`, never from `login`.
- **Its sets may grow** (ADR-097 point 4): the kinds, the causes and a comparison's answers.
  - **A kind it does not know** a reader takes as a kind unknown in a comparison, and, for the acting account, as an unresolved identity.
  - **A cause it does not know** a reader takes as an unresolved identity, whose cause it cannot tell, since reading it as the service's answer could report a finding the service never gave (DEC-073 point 2).
  - **An answer it does not know** a reader takes as *cannot tell*, and so as point 3's refusal.
  - **A version of the document it does not know, or no document,** a capability's reader takes as no answer (ADR-097 point 3): from the reading, as an unresolved identity, and from a spelling or a comparison, as a kind unknown and as *cannot tell*.
- **The request is chosen by measurement.** Before the reading's request is fixed, the build asks the requests the service documents for the signed-in account under a user's credential, an App installation's token and a workflow's own token, and records what it finds on the reading's issue. This record decides how the answers are read:
  - **What holds.** A request holds for a credential only where it names the account the service records for it, with that account's kind as point 2 reads it: the user's own account as a user's, and the App's own account as a bot's. So an answer whose login carries the bot suffix names a bot's account, whatever type the request declares. A request that answers an installation's token with another account, or with the App's account read as a user's, does not hold for it.
  - **What is taken.** A request that holds under all three, where one does. Where none holds under the two tokens, an ordered pair: a first request that holds under a user's credential and answers each token with the integration's refusal or with no account, and a second, asked only after the integration's refusal, that holds under the tokens. Where no second holds, the reading asks the first alone, and names no account for such a token.
  - **Where neither is left.** Where every request that holds under a user's credential answers one of the tokens with another account, or with the App's account read as a user's, the build takes none, since each would name an account the credential does not act as, which point 2 never allows. It builds no reading on any of them, and the request waits for a record that decides it.
  - **What is known.** The REST request for the signed-in user is refused under an installation's token, as measured.
  - **Other kinds.** Whatever the build finds of a bot that is no app's and of a mannequin, each stays of a kind unknown (point 3). Where it finds that a source point 3 reads a kind from gives such an account a user's kind, the function reads no user's kind from that source until a record decides how it is read. Where it finds one such bot given two logins that each carry the bot suffix, the function answers *cannot tell* between two bots' accounts whose spellings differ, until a record decides, and the suffix stays a source of the kind.
  - **The lookup by login.** project-management's migration and its `add-member` learn that a login names an App's account by asking the service for the account whose login is that one with the suffix, and read a bot's account where the service gives the type `Bot` (point 3; Implications). Where the lookup is found not to answer an App's account so, neither takes it as a sign of a bot's account, and the migration only warns, until a record decides.
- **Apart from the local readings.** The reading reaches the network, so it stays apart from the reading of the repository, which needs none (ADR-061 point 2). For the same reason no query asks the reading: no resolver, filler or validator. A query may call the command's forms that spell and compare, which ask the service nothing (point 3). Under the offline marker a query's run carries (ADR-057 point 3), it asks nothing, and the identity is unresolved, with that cause. It changes nothing, so it runs no cross-repository guard.
- **The service's host** comes from the caller's environment, never from a configuration of the backbone's (ADR-061 point 4). A capability that keeps its own host passes it on through the environment.
- **Bounded.** An ask is bounded in time, and one that runs out is no answer.

### 2. Only the service's answer names the account and its kind

The reading has three outcomes, and reports each with its cause. DEC-073 point 2 tells two outcomes apart, and the three map onto them: a user's account resolved names a user's account; a bot's account resolved, and an unnamed account, are each the service's answer that the credential names no user's account; and an unresolved identity is a read that did not answer.

- **Resolved** only where the service, asked with the credential, answers with the account the credential acts as and that account's kind, by the first request or, after the integration's refusal, by the second (point 1). A login the service answers with the bot suffix names a bot's account, whatever type the answer gives, since no user's login can carry it. The reading gives the account in the one spelling (point 3), with its kind.
- **An unnamed account** where the service answers that the credential's account is no user's, and the reading can name none:
  - it refuses the request for the account because the credential is an integration's, as DEC-073 point 2 says it refuses a workflow's own token and an App's, and no second request names the account. The reading tells this refusal by its status and the service's stated reason, as DEC-073 point 2 reads it, and by nothing else;
  - it answers with an account of a kind unknown, such as one it types as a bot's on a login with no suffix (point 3).
- **An unresolved identity** in every other outcome:
  - a refusal for any other cause, such as a rate limit, a suspended account, or a credential the service does not accept, which says nothing of who the credential is;
  - no answer, such as with `gh` missing (point 7), no network, a timeout, the offline marker, or something other than the service answering;
  - an answer that names no account.
- **Never from anything else:**
  - an account or a kind from a refusal, which names neither, or anything from a refusal beyond the integration's status and stated reason (above);
  - the credential's own form;
  - the record of who signed in that the tool which issued the credential keeps;
  - git's configured identity, or a variable or setting that names an account;
  - which agent host started the session, which pkit never tests (ADR-091 point 2).
- **No fallback.** The reading gives no account the service did not answer with. What a reader does where the reading names no account is decided in the reader's own record: point 6 for `pkit report`, and a capability's records for its commands (point 8).
- **Installation tokens.** Whether one gets an answer that names its account decides only which credentials resolve. Where none does, such a credential reads as an unnamed account where the service gives the integration's refusal, and as an unresolved identity where it gives no answer, and each reader acts as its record says for that.

### 3. One spelling of an account, its kind included

- **The spelling.** A user's account is spelled by its login. A bot's account is spelled by its login, which carries the service's bot suffix, as `name[bot]`: the login the service's REST interface gives an app's account.
- **The kinds.** The function knows those two kinds, and the document's `kind` gives them as `user` and `bot`. They are the kinds measured: users' accounts, which pkit's readers and member lists read today, and an app's, read on the trial (Context). Every other account is of a kind unknown, and has no spelling:
  - an organization's;
  - a mannequin;
  - an account the service records as a bot's but gives with no suffix, as a bot that is no app's may be;
  - an account of a type the function does not know.

  A kind added later, such as a mannequin's once a measurement tells it apart, takes a value of its own in `kind`, and a spelling that no account of the two kinds can take, which the record that adds it decides. Until then the function gives such an account no spelling, so nothing is stored or printed in one, and the kind added respells nothing (ADR-097 points 4 and 5).
- **It carries whether the account is a bot's.** No user's login can hold the suffix's brackets, and every bot's spelling holds them. So a bot's account never reads as a user's, even where an app took its owner's own name.
- **Where a kind comes from.** The one function beside the reading (below) spells an account only from a kind a surface gives:
  - the bot suffix in the login, which names a bot's account whatever type a surface gives, since no user's login can carry it;
  - a type the service records for the account as a user's, read directly, as the REST interface's type `User`, or as gh renders it, by the account's id and no bot flag;
  - the type the service declares for the field a login is read from, where the field holds accounts of one kind only, such as an issue's assignees, which hold users' accounts (Context);
  - the form of an entry pkit holds, or of a value that stands for an account, each of which is read in the spelling (below).

  Every other source leaves the kind unknown, and the function gives the login no spelling:
  - **gh's other mark.** gh writes every author the service does not record as a user's by its `app/` prefix and its bot flag, an app's account, a mannequin and an organization alike (Context). So the mark tells only that the account is no user's, and gives neither kind.
  - **Another type.** A type other than a user's that the service records on a login with no suffix, such as `Bot`, `Organization` or `Mannequin`, names neither kind.
  - **A bare login and nothing more.** gh's JSON for a comment's author is such a surface, and for an app's account it gives the bare name (Context).

  So a reader that compares an account reads its kind from a surface that gives it, such as the REST interface's record of a comment's author, with its type. For an app's account, that is a surface that gives the suffix, such as the REST interface, since gh's JSON gives such an account no kind.
- **The forms a caller gives.** The command's input names each account's form, and the CLI reference states the forms as part of the command's contract: an entry or a value pkit holds; a login with the type the service records for it; a login with gh's id and bot flag; a login from a field the service declares to hold users' accounts only; and a login with nothing more. The form says where the kind comes from, so a caller never gives one form as another, such as gh's bare comment author as an entry, which would read an app's account as a user's. A backbone given a form it does not know refuses the call, which its caller reads as no document (point 1).
- **What a comparison answers.** The function answers that two accounts are one, that they are two, or that it cannot tell:
  - **one account** where both kinds are known and the spellings are equal;
  - **two accounts** where both kinds are known and the spellings differ, save as point 1 has it for a bot found under two logins, or where one of them is a user's account and the other's login, the suffix and the prefix set aside, is another. A user's account has one login, which every surface gives, so another login names another account;
  - **cannot tell** in every other case, where a kind is unknown and either the logins agree once the suffix and the prefix are set aside, or neither account is known to be a user's. Nothing measured says that every surface gives an account of a kind unknown by one login, so another login tells such an account apart from no account but a user's.

  So an app that took its owner's name is another authority than its owner wherever both kinds are known, as the service records them.
- **One against many.** Asked whether an account is one of several, such as a member's entries or the registered remote reviewers, the function folds its answers: *one account* where it is one with some entry, *two accounts* where it is two with every entry, and *cannot tell* otherwise. Each use reads the fold as it reads one pair's answer (below).
- **Each use reads "cannot tell" as its refusal,** so that a missing kind grants nothing and frees nothing:
  - **To grant,** an account must be one with an entry, as a member or as a registered remote reviewer. Only *one account* grants.
  - **To tell apart,** two accounts must be two for something to pass: a reviewer or an approver and the pull request's author, or the actor and an authorisation's producer, whose comparison is the engine's (ADR-098 point 2), on two sides a binding of accounts spells (point 5). *Cannot tell* reads as one authority.
  - **To count a claim or a hold,** which keeps others out, a claim's author must be one with the account it must come from: the issue's assignee, or, on a state issue behind a closed membership gate, a member's entry. *One account* counts the claim, *two accounts* voids it, and *cannot tell* leaves it unjudged. No reader treats an unjudged claim as free, as for every claim a seam cannot judge (ADR-041 points 5 and 7, ADR-071 point 3).
  - **To free a hold,** a release must come from the account that holds. Only *one account* frees it, and *cannot tell* leaves the hold standing.
  - **To skip an act the service refuses to one's own account,** such as a native review of one's own pull request, the refusal is to skip nothing: only *one account* skips the act, and *cannot tell* attempts it. The service's refusal leaves the reader where it would be without the act, so the attempt grants nothing.
  - **Any other use** reads *cannot tell* as the answer that refuses, and the record of the reader says which answer that is.
- **Case and whitespace.** The service looks an account's name up without regard to case, so every comparison sets aside the case of ASCII letters, and no other folding, and surrounding whitespace with it. A spelling is written down in the case the service answered. The engine sets case aside by a rule of its own (ADR-098 point 2).
- **A string with no spelling.** A login holds only letters, digits, dashes and, for a managed user, an underscore (Context), and the function reads those letters as ASCII's. So it gives no spelling to a string that names no account it can tell:
  - an empty one, or one with a character no login holds once the suffix and the prefix are set aside, such as `@alice`, a space inside, or a letter that is not ASCII;
  - one that holds gh's prefix and the suffix together, as `app/x[bot]`, a form no surface gives, since gh puts its prefix before the bare name;
  - a prefix or a suffix with no name, as `app/` or `[bot]`.

  Each compares as *cannot tell* against every account. So an entry with no spelling grants nothing, and in a list it leaves the fold *cannot tell* for every account no other entry is: where a claim's author must be one with a member's entry, the claim stays unjudged until the entry is mended, and the refusal names it (below).
- **One function** beside the reading puts accounts named by any surface into the one spelling, and compares accounts.
  - **Many in one call:** many pairs, or one account against many. So a reader that compares across many items, such as Tasks' assignees or an issue's markers, starts one command for them all.
  - **No request.** It asks the service nothing: it spells and compares only what it is given, so a call to it costs no request, and works offline and under any credential.
  - **Every comparison of two accounts in pkit goes through it,** save the engine's, whose two sides a binding of accounts spells with it (point 5). The service's own answer that the credential's account wrote something is no comparison of pkit's (point 1).
  - **Every account pkit's code stores, and every account a document first printed under this record gives,** is written in the spelling, save the actor the engine's journal records, which is the actor its caller named, given in the spelling by a binding of accounts (point 5).
  - **A key a command already prints keeps what it gives** (ADR-097 point 5). Where one gives an account in another form, the account in the one spelling comes as a key added beside it. Taking the old key away later is a declared breaking change of its component, which that component's records decide (ADR-097 point 4).
  - **How it is reached.** The backbone's code calls the function. A capability's scripts reach it through the backbone's reading command, asked to spell or to compare, which then asks the service nothing, and they keep no copy of it (ADR-057 point 2).
- **Requests.** A request to the service that names an account names it in the form that request takes, made from the account's login and kind. The service's search, for one, names an app's account `app/<name>`. So a request made from a stored bot's entry takes the entry's login with the suffix set aside where the request names the bare name, and the login whole where it takes the login. Neither is a respelling: the entry stays as it is, and nothing is stored or compared in the form the request takes.
- **Stored and given accounts.** An entry pkit holds is in the spelling, and so is a value a capability's record lets stand for an account, such as a variable that names one. So the kind of each is read from its form: a bare one names a user's account.
  - **Users' entries.** A user's login is already in this spelling, so no entry that names a user changes. The member list's schema describes a member's login as the value the REST request for the signed-in user returns for the member, which for a user is that login. It says nothing of an app's account, for which that request is refused (Context), so this record decides that account's spelling, and the schema comes to describe the one spelling (Implications).
  - **A service's account,** as a member or as a remote reviewer, is written in this spelling, an App's as `name[bot]`.
  - **gh's form.** An entry written `app/name` reads as `name[bot]`, and needs no rewrite. The same string from a surface reads as of a kind unknown, since gh writes a mannequin and an organization so too, and an entry copied from gh could name either. Read as `name[bot]`, it still grants nothing to such an account, since no surface gives one with the suffix: it can match only a bot's account of that name, the account gh's form names wherever gh wrote it for an app.
  - **An entry a command writes** is written in the spelling, through the function, from what the service answers. project-management's `add-member` looks a login up with the service, which answers the account's login and type (Context). Given a bare login, it looks up a user's account, and given `name[bot]` or `app/name`, the login with the suffix (point 1, "The lookup by login"), so it writes an App's account as `name[bot]`. Where the service answers an account of a kind unknown, such as an organization's or one of type `Bot` on a login with no suffix, or answers none, it writes no entry, since no spelling names such an account, and says why.
  - **An App's bare login, already written,** reads as a user's, so it does not match the App's account. As a remote reviewer, which gh's comment JSON matched by the App's bare name (Context), what the App's account writes stops counting there. As a member, it matches the App's account on no reader today, and respelling it `name[bot]` mends the state-issue seam, where a bare entry never matched the App's comments. Where the App took its owner's name, the bare entry names the owner, and a reader that picks people from a list, such as reviewers, takes it for a person's, as it does today. Nothing counts that did not before. A migration rewrites a remote reviewer's bare entry where two definite answers of the service's tell that it names an App's account (Implications).
  - **The refusal names it instead.** A reader that refuses for want of a match names each entry or value that explains the refusal: a bare one whose login agrees with a bot's once the suffix and the prefix are set aside, with the spelling that would name the bot, and one with no spelling, with why. So it names an App's bare entry that a bot's account does not match, and an entry such as `@alice`.

### 4. An answer lasts one run

- **Within a run,** the reading asks at most once and reuses its answer. A capability's command that reaches it by command keeps the answer for its own run.
- **Handed on, only where the command takes its caller's word.** Where a run starts a command whose records take the account as its caller's word, such as the engine's move, which takes the actor its caller names (ADR-098 point 1), it hands the account on as an argument. A command whose records have it read the acting account from the service takes no account handed to it in that reading's place: it reads in its own run. So does a command of project-management's behind the membership gate, whose invoker, where the service answers that the credential acts as a user's account, is that account alone, and no setting beside it (DEC-021, "Identity resolution", item 1).
- **Across runs, nothing is kept.** An answer kept from an earlier run, even for the same credential, is no answer of the service's. So each run that needs the account asks.

### 5. A binding of accounts gives its actor and its producers in the one spelling

- **Both sides spelt.** The engine compares an actor with a producer in the binding's terms, setting aside only case and surrounding whitespace (ADR-098 point 2). Where a binding's authorities are accounts on the hosting service, it gives both in the one spelling (point 3): its wrapper spells the actor, and its predicates spell each producer, with the backbone's function.
- **A producer whose kind it cannot learn** a predicate does not name, so the gate is shut, as for any authorisation that names no producer. gh's JSON gives a user's merge its kind and an app's none (Context), so a predicate that reports who merged reads an app's merge from a surface that gives the suffix, such as the REST interface's record of the pull request, or names no producer.
- **Which account such a binding names as its actor** is decided in the binding's records (point 8). The reading gives the account a credential acts as, for a binding whose records name that account.
- **By hand.** A person names an actor of such a binding in the spelling the reading command prints, as the binding's reference says.
- **What this binds:** the bindings of accounts that project-kit's capabilities ship, as point 8 scopes this record. A binding of accounts another project writes reads the spelling in the reading command's reference and the comparison in the process area's reference, and nothing here binds it (COR-025).

### 6. `pkit report` posts only as an account it can name

- **The confirmation** names the reading's account, and says when it is a bot's.
- **Where the reading names no account,** the command posts nothing through the service. It says it cannot tell which account it would post as, and why, and keeps the payload as a draft, as it does with nobody at the terminal (ADR-047 point 2). The draft is posted later, by a person, once the reading names the account, or through the prefilled form, whose account the browser decides, as ADR-047 already warns.
- **The listing of one's own reports** filters by the reading's account. Where the reading names none, it says it cannot tell whose reports to list.

### 7. GitHub only, through `gh`, and provisional

- **Written for GitHub.** The request the reading makes, the kinds it knows and the spelling are GitHub's. Its reference says so, and its document uses GitHub's vocabulary.
- **Through `gh`.** The reading asks the service through `gh`, the service's command-line tool, under the credential `gh` is given. So where `gh` is missing, the identity is unresolved (point 2).
- **Provisional, on ADR-061 point 3's ground.** The backbone's report command needs the reading, and the backbone cannot depend on a capability (COR-010). Whether core content may read or act on one hosting service under a label is raised in [project-kit#1222](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/1222), and this placement joins that question.
- **The engine stays out of it.** It reads no account and respells none (ADR-098 points 1 and 2), so nothing of GitHub's reaches it.
- **On another service,** `gh` cannot answer, so the identity is unresolved. Each reader then acts as its record says for no answer, and none takes a wrong account.
- **When to revisit:**
  - when [project-kit#1222](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/1222) is decided;
  - when a second hosting service is supported. The reading then becomes one implementation behind its command, as ADR-061 point 3 has the landing's: its document gains keys and values in a vocabulary of their own, and GitHub's leave it only as a declared breaking change that raises its `schema_version` (ADR-097 points 4 and 5). Two services' logins may coincide, so an account on another service is given in a key of its own, and the keys that give a GitHub account keep meaning one. The entries a project holds come to name a service only by a change to their format, which COR-010 decides.

### 8. The backbone reads; each reader decides

- **The backbone** holds the reading and the spelling. It decides here what its own reader does (point 6). It reads no capability's files or settings for the account, and calls no capability to learn it (COR-010).
- **A capability's commands** take the acting account and its spelling from the backbone, by command (ADR-057 point 1). Each capability decides in its own records:
  - what its commands do where the reading names no account, for each of its two outcomes, or names a bot's account;
  - which other values, such as an email or a variable that names a person, may stand beside the reading's account;
  - whose claims and approvals count, of the accounts the function tells apart, such as whether a bot's account may approve;
  - which authority its commands name as the engine's actor, in the terms its binding gives the actor and its producers (point 5).

  The reading gives none of them.
- **project-management's commands** are one reader. The membership gate takes its first surface, the service's login, from the reading, in the one spelling. Its other surfaces are the capability's records' to decide.
- **What this binds:** pkit's own code and the capabilities project-kit ships, their bindings of accounts among them (point 5). project-kit's own repository scripts are neither, such as the one that keeps the friction report's tracking issue, which lists by one fixed author.
- **What it leaves free.** The reading command and its document are part of pkit's public surface, so a capability another project writes may use them. Nothing here binds such a capability to them, since an architecture record reaches no other project ([COR-025](../../../.pkit/decisions/core/COR-025-adr-decision-space.md)). No such capability is known to need the acting account, so no core record is proposed for one.

## Rationale

**Why one reading.** Five readers ask on their own today, and fail three ways: with no account, with the placeholder, or with the actor left to the engine. That is the recurrence that calls for one home ([COR-007](../../../.pkit/decisions/core/COR-007-pattern-extraction.md)). Once ADR-098 takes the engine out of the asking, four remain, in the backbone and in project-management, and they still differ where it costs. `pkit report` names no account where its ask fails, and posts once the person confirms. The membership gate reports a cause. The review command's and workstream sync's checks read gh's JSON, which gives one App's account two spellings (Context). Each copy must be mended alone for an installation's token, and each is one more spelling of the same account. One reading, with one spelling beside it, mends them once.

**Why in the backbone.** `pkit report` is the backbone's, and needs the reading whatever a project installs. The backbone cannot depend on a capability (COR-010), and asking one by command, optionally, as `pkit report` asks project-management for a workstream ([ADR-050](ADR-050-report-context-sourcing.md) point 2), fails wherever the capability is absent. A capability reaches the backbone by command (ADR-057 point 1). So the backbone is the one home every reader can reach, as ADR-061 found for landing a pull request.

**Why only the service's answer.** Any other source is a second reading that can disagree with the service:

- **A refusal** names no account. Of its causes, only the integration's says anything of who the credential is, and only that its account is no user's (below).
- **A credential's form** is the issuer's convention. It can disagree with the service's answer in a case nobody has measured, a person's sign-in through an app, and reading it means reading the secret.
- **The issuing tool's record** of who signed in is overridden by a token passed in the environment.
- **Git's configured identity** names whoever set the machine up.

Without the service's answer, pkit cannot tell an app's credential from a person's. Any fallback would make a person the acting account, under an app's credential, whenever its ask fails.

**Why the integration's refusal has a cause of its own.** DEC-073 point 2 draws its line at whether the service answered for who the credential is, and the service refuses the request for the account to a workflow's own token and to an App's because the credential is an integration's. Read as no answer, every run under such a token would report that it could not tell who acts, where the service said that no user does. And DEC-073 has the resolution of the identity tell the two apart, so that no command reads the service's words for itself. So the reading, which every reader takes the identity from, reads the refusal's status and the service's stated reason for that one cause, and gives it in its listed set. It reads nothing else from a refusal: a refusal names no account, and a rate limit, a suspended account or a credential the service does not accept gives other words, none of which says who the credential is. An account of a kind unknown is the same answer by another route, an account the service records as no user's, so it is an unnamed account too.

**Why the reading offers no fallback, and leaves the response to each reader.** What a reader may do without an answer turns on what it guards. `pkit report` guards a public post, project-management's move names the actor of a cross-authority check, and the membership gate keeps its own sources by its own record. One fallback in the reading would decide all of them at once, and would carry a capability's choice into the backbone.

**Why a binding of accounts spells both sides.** A producer reported in another spelling than the actor's makes an actor's own merge read as another authority's, and the engine sets aside only case and whitespace (ADR-098 point 2). The binding's wrapper and its predicates both reach the backbone's function, so the two arrive in one spelling, and the process area's reference states this as part of the predicate's contract. A person who names the actor by hand is taken at their word, as every caller is, so the reference also says that a binding of accounts takes its actors as the reading command prints them.

**Why the service's REST spelling.** It is the login the service's REST interface gives an app's account, and carries the kind in its form, so no field is added, and the member lists hold users' logins in it already. gh's listing by author took it where gh's own form matched nothing, as project-kit's own friction workflow found (Context). gh's form is what the service's search takes for an app's account, but the service gives no account that login, so a request that searches makes the form from the spelling (point 3). A bare login would drop the kind, and an app may take its owner's name, so the app's account would compare equal to its owner's. The spelling takes the suffix only as a login carries it, or as an entry in gh's form names it: a bot whose login no surface gives with the suffix is of a kind unknown (point 3).

**Why only the two kinds measured.** Any other kind, read on no measurement, could give one account two spellings by surface, and two spellings read as two accounts, which fails open wherever two must differ. A mannequin is one such account: gh writes it as it writes an app's account, and the REST interface gives its bare login, with a type of its own. Nor does anything measured say that a bot that is no app's has one login on every surface. Read as of a kind unknown, such an account grants nothing, and another login tells it apart from no account but a user's, whose one login every surface gives. gh's mark for every other author is read so too, since it writes an app's account, a mannequin and an organization alike. A kind is added once a measurement shows how each surface gives it (point 1), with a spelling of its own, so no spelling given before changes (ADR-097 point 5).

**Why a missing kind decides nothing.** Read as a user's, a bare login turns missing information into an answer:

- **Where two accounts must differ,** it reads an app's comment on its own pull request as another authority's. gh writes the pull request's author with its `app/` prefix, and the comment's author by the app's bare name (Context).
- **Where an account must match,** a bare entry and a bare author agree whichever account each means, an app that took its owner's name among them.

Read as matching nothing, it fails open too, where a match keeps others out. A claim that does not count leaves its Task, or its seat, free for another while its holder works on. So the function says that it cannot tell, and each use reads that as its refusal: a grant grants nothing, a telling-apart tells no two authorities apart, a claim stays unjudged, which no reader of either seam frees (Context), and a release frees nothing. An app and its owner are two authorities wherever both kinds are known. The service records them apart, and the owner stands behind an app whatever it is named, so a rule that joined them only where the names coincide would refuse those owners alone.

**Why three answers, and not a rule for each use.** A rule for each use covers the uses a record lists, and a use it missed takes another's rule: a claim, listed among the grants, would free what it holds. Three answers leave the reading to each use, and the rule that each reads *cannot tell* as its refusal holds for a use nobody listed. The fold over a list has one home in the function for the same reason: a reader that folded the answers itself would keep a copy of the rule (ADR-057 point 2).

**Why a string with no spelling cannot tell.** Such an entry is most likely a login mistyped, as `@alice` for `alice`. Read as naming no account, it would void every claim its account makes, and free a held state, where a claim must come from a member's entry. Read as *cannot tell*, it grants nothing and frees nothing, and the refusal names it, so a person mends it. What that costs is the reads it leaves incomplete until then.

**Why these sources of a kind, and why the suffix governs.** Each source is the service's own record. gh's mark for a user's account renders the service's record that an author is a user's, and a field the service declares to hold users' accounts holds no other. The suffix governs a type it disagrees with, since no user's login can carry it, so the disagreement is the surface's. The service's GraphQL interface declares its signed-in account a user, whatever credential asks, so a request for it under an installation's token could give an app's login with the suffix and a user's type, and the measurement must not discard it for that. The form a caller gives is contracted for the same reason: the kind is read from the source, so a source passed as another gives a kind no surface gave.

**Why comparisons ignore case and surrounding whitespace.** The service looks an account's name up without regard to case, so two spellings that differ only in case name one account, and no two accounts' names differ only in case. So ignoring the case of the ASCII letters a login holds grants nothing to another account. A wider folding would: Unicode's case folding matches `ſ` with `s`, and the Kelvin sign with `k`, so an entry typed with either would match a login it does not hold. Nor does trimming grant, since a space is no part of a user's login (Context), and an account given with one beside it is the account it names. Where two accounts must differ, unequal reads as another authority, so a comparison that heeded either would fail open on a login typed in another case, or with a space beside it.

**Why an answer lasts one run.** An answer kept across runs needs a key for the credential, which means reading the secret or trusting the issuing tool's record (point 2). It can also go stale: a login renamed, and perhaps claimed by another account, or a setup that swaps one credential for another, would act on the earlier account. What it would save is a request for each run that needs the account. That is more than one a command: a command that starts others, as a landing starts a merge and a move, costs one in each that reads the account itself, and only a command that takes its caller's word, such as the engine's move, is spared it by having the account handed on (point 4). An installation's tokens share one limit on requests across every session that holds them. It buys no work offline, since every reader reads or writes the service in the run that asks.

**Why `pkit report` keeps the draft.** The confirmation names the posting account so that a person never posts a public issue as an account they did not expect ([project-kit#662](https://github.com/aleskalfas/project-kit/issues/662)). Where the reading names no account, it cannot name one, so the post waits for a way that can, as it does with nobody at the terminal.

**Why the migration asks the service.** A bare entry in the remote reviewer's list names an agent's account, which may be an App's or a machine user's, and only the service's record tells which. The lifecycle's contract limits what the backbone computes for a migration to content alone, and leaves a script free to ask the service (the lifecycle reference, ["Script contract"](../../../.pkit/lifecycle/migrations.md#script-contract)). Asked on the host the capability's requests go to, the service tells an entry that can name only an App's account by two definite answers: the login with the suffix is a bot's, and no account has the bare login. The rewrite then keeps what the entry matched, which gh's comment JSON gave by the App's bare name. Anything less tells nothing. Where the bare login names a user's account too, the entry may mean either, and a rewrite would choose for the person. A rate limit or a server error says nothing of either account, and an enterprise's own host may answer that it finds no account it hides from the credential, such as a machine user the entry still matches. Another host could hold a same-named App. So the migration rewrites only on the two answers, changes nothing else in the file, and warns in every other case, and an upgrade run offline loses only the rewrite, which its warning names.

**Why GitHub only, provisionally.** One service is all pkit supports. A neutral vocabulary would be GitHub's renamed, and COR-007 extracts a common form only once a second instance shows what is common. The conflict between a GitHub-only backbone and the core records is the one ADR-061 point 3 raised, and the same core decision settles both.

**Why the engine's part is a record of its own.** The engine's rule holds for every binding, whatever its authorities are, and the reading holds for one service's accounts, so each is argued and revisited on its own grounds (ADR-098, "Why a record of its own"). They rest on each other, so they are accepted together: this record leaves the engine's comparison to ADR-098 points 1 and 2, and ADR-098's rule on a binding of accounts takes the spelling here (point 5). Bundling them would relieve none of the engine's cost, which waits on project-management's record on #3164. The reading and the spelling stay in one record: the reading's document gives the kind and the spelling, and the function answers from the kinds, so one set of values would be split between two records, each refining the other.

### Alternatives considered

- **Each reader keeps its own ask.** Rejected: the copies fail in different ways, each must be mended alone for an installation's token, and they spell one account more than one way (Rationale, "Why one reading").
- **Every reader that asks whether something is its own takes the service's own answer to that, by rule.** Not taken: whether the service gives it under an installation's token is unmeasured, so the rule could leave a reader with no answer where the reading has one. The service's answer stays open to such a reader, and the readers that must name the account, such as `pkit report` and the engine's actor, need the reading anyway.
- **The reading in project-management, which the backbone asks by command.** Rejected: `pkit report` would lose the reading wherever the capability is not installed (COR-010).
- **An interim reading in project-management, moved to the backbone later.** Rejected: until the move, two readings of one credential run side by side, the backbone's for `pkit report` and the capability's, and nothing keeps them from naming one credential's account differently.
- **The kind read from the credential's form,** such as the prefix the service gives each kind of token. Rejected: it is a second reading that can disagree with the service, the secret must be read to see it, and an unknown form leaves the identity unresolved anyway.
- **An account or a kind read from a refusal, or a refusal's wording read beyond the integration's status and stated reason.** Rejected: a refusal names no account to act as, and a rate limit, a suspended account or a credential the service does not accept gives other words, none of which says who the credential is.
- **Every refusal one cause, the integration's among them.** Rejected: DEC-073 point 2 tells the integration's refusal apart as the service's answer that the credential names no user's account, and has the resolution of the identity say which outcome it is. One cause would leave each reader to read the service's words for itself, which that point forbids, or would tell a workflow under its own token that every run failed.
- **A fallback in the reading,** to git's identity, a variable or the issuing tool's record. Rejected: without the service's answer, a fallback makes a person the acting account under an app's credential, and it decides for every reader what only each reader's record should.
- **An answer kept across runs, keyed by a digest of the credential.** Not taken: it needs the secret read, it can go stale, and it saves a request for each run that needs the account. It is weighed again if the service's limits on requests are seen to bite.
- **Accounts compared by kind and numeric id,** with logins kept for display and the member lists. Not taken:
  - the member lists and the registered reviewers hold logins, and a predicate names its producer by one string, so both forms would change and every entry would migrate;
  - the kind an id would settle is settled by reading it from a surface that gives it (point 3), and gh's JSON, which most readers read today, gives no id for any account but a user's, since it writes every other as `app/<name>` with none (Context);
  - the risk only an id closes is a login renamed and then claimed by another account. It reaches an entry kept past the rename, which grants to the new holder, and a value that stands for an account kept past it, which no longer names the account it meant.

  The document gives the id, so a record that comes to need it compares by it without a new version of the document (ADR-097 point 4).
- **A spelling with the kind as a field of its own.** Rejected: every member list and every stored account would need a migration, and the service's login already carries the kind.
- **gh's spelling, with its `app/` prefix.** Rejected: the service gives no account that login, gh's own listing by author matched nothing in it (Context), and gh writes a mannequin and an organization so too. The member list's schema names the REST interface's answer as a member's login, the interface whose spelling this is, though it decides nothing for an app's account, which that request refuses. The service's search takes gh's form, so a request that searches makes it from the spelling (point 3).
- **The bare login.** Rejected: an app may take its owner's name, so its account would compare equal to its owner's and pass as the owner.
- **A login with no kind read as a user's.** Rejected: where two accounts must differ, it reads an app's comment on its own pull request as another authority's, and where one must match, it lets a bare entry match whichever account a bare author means (Rationale).
- **A rule for each kind of comparison, with a login of unknown kind matching nothing where a comparison grants.** Rejected: a claim grants its author the work and keeps everyone else out, and read as matching nothing, it frees what it holds (Rationale).
- **Each reader folds a list's answers itself.** Rejected: each would keep a copy of the rule, and a reader that read *cannot tell* from one entry as no match would free a claim (ADR-057 point 2).
- **A string with no spelling read as naming no account,** so that it matches nothing. Rejected: an entry typed `@alice` would void each claim of alice's and free what she holds (Rationale, "Why a string with no spelling cannot tell").
- **Kinds read only from a type the service gives each account,** leaving out gh's mark for a user's account and a field's declared type. Not taken: both are the service's record, rendered by gh or declared by the service, and refusing them would move readers off surfaces that already give the kind, for nothing more known.
