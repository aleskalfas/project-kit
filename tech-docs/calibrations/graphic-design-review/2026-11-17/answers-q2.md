# Calibration question 2: his answers

Designs he fails that a judge passes, one at a time. For each: which covered rule makes it fail for him, or none.

### Pair 1: MCM, page 4, his 3rd, just below his line (WMG his 2nd, just above)

Shown on the comparison page (WMG and MCM toggled in place, the file-name close-up, the two footers, the judges' words, the rules).

His words: "I think that the bold in MCM is fail."

So the difference that fails MCM for him is the bold file name, `brand/platform.json`, set bold where step 1 writes it and where step 2 reads it. Which rule it breaks for him is asked next: the bold is emphasis, which two rules' words reach ("Nothing extra", RS-GRAPHIC-003; "emphasis falls only where the point needs it", in RS-GRAPHIC-007).

Asked which rule the bold breaks ("nothing extra", "form", or both), he answered: "I guess it is mistake beacuse the line above should be bolded too because why this line is bolded? There is nothing extra for this line which would not be valid for the css file too. Unless the platform.json should be somehow emphasized which would come from context."

So the bold singles out `platform.json` over the CSS file's line above it, with nothing in the content that sets it apart. Two rules' words reach that reason: RS-GRAPHIC-007 (every difference a reader takes for meaning is one the content has; emphasis only where the point needs it) and RS-GRAPHIC-004 (the same meaning looks the same). Put to him which, or both.

Answer: **both.** ("both") MCM fails for him on RS-GRAPHIC-007 and on RS-GRAPHIC-004, for the bold `platform.json` alone. The footer's wording was not named.

### Pair 2: TXF, page 3, his 3rd (his line now below ZCG; RTJ, his 2nd, fails on RS-GRAPHIC-004 for its orange last step)

Shown on the comparison page (RTJ, TXF and FXK toggled in place; the three joins; the first box's text on TXF and FXK; the judges' words on TXF).

His words: "The connections between arrows and hand over seems pure in comparison with RTJ" (read as "poor").

So what fails TXF for him is how each hand-over line meets its arrow: RTJ's ends in a small ring under the arrow, TXF's stops below it with no end mark. The rule whose words reach it is put to him: RS-GRAPHIC-002 (what belongs together is grouped and lined up, and steps are joined in the order they are read).

Asked whether that rule is RS-GRAPHIC-002, he answered: "I'm not sure that it is the point. For me it seems like conflict of two layers. The box layer is the core layer and the hand over is some addition layer which seems clear in the RTJ but in other too like it would be one layer. When we would but the text directly around the arrows then it could be part of the layer but not for this. So I'm feeling like we need some new rule for layering the design. It happen quite often that we have one layer like schema or process and another layer over it which give additional explanation of what is in the first layer. It is like have a paper map which is the base and we draw in that our routes and notes which give aditional context to the map layer."

So TXF fails for him on **no covered rule**: on a principle no rule names, the layering of a base (the boxes and arrows of a process or schema) and an annotation layer over it (the hand-overs) that explains it. In RTJ the annotation reads as its own layer, pinned to the base by the ring; in TXF it reads as part of one layer. Text set directly around the arrows would belong to the base layer; the hand-overs do not. He proposes a new rule for layering a design. His map analogy: the paper map is the base, and the routes and notes drawn on it give it context.

Offered A (draft a layering rule now, recommended) or B (leave TXF out of the calibration for now), he answered: "Draft it now". So a layering rule for `GRAPHIC` is drafted now, to land with the reviewer's reading of it before the calibration; TXF then fails on that rule.

### Pair 3: FXK, page 3, his 4th

Shown on the same page, FXK selected, with its two differences from TXF (its hand-over lines run up into the arrows and cross them; its box text sits a few pixels lower) and the judges' words on FXK. Asked "What makes FXK fail for you: the same layering as TXF, something else, or both?", he answered: "yes crossing the arrow is fail".

So what fails FXK for him is the hand-over lines crossing the arrows. The box text sitting lower was not named. Which rule it breaks is asked next.

Asked which rule the crossing breaks ("layering", RS-GRAPHIC-002, or both), he answered: "both". So FXK fails for him on the layering rule being drafted and on RS-GRAPHIC-002, for its hand-over lines crossing the arrows.

## Question 2 in sum

- MCM (page 4): RS-GRAPHIC-007 and RS-GRAPHIC-004, for the bold file name.

## The layering rule and the held-out ladder

The architect and the critic flagged a conflict: a layering rule shaped knowing TXF and FXK fail on it would be a change written from the held-out ladder (DEC-042 point 9: "a held-out ladder, of a piece that no change the calibration rests on was written from"). The coordinator's earlier recommendation of A ("draft it now") had missed it. Offered:
- A: TXF leaves this calibration; FXK stays on RS-GRAPHIC-002; the layering rule is drafted from his map principle and the sources alone, and joins the bar at the next calibration, on a fresh ladder;
- B: count the rule on this ladder;
- C: a fresh ladder now.

He answered: "A".

So:
- TXF is left out of this calibration's reading, since it fails on no covered rule.
- FXK stays, read on RS-GRAPHIC-002, for its hand-over lines crossing the arrows.
- #3342 lands after this calibration and does not hold it.
- No part of #3342 draws on TXF or FXK.
