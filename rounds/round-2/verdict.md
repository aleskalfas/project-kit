# Verdict: FAIL

All four pages fail, and so does the deck. The shared skeleton is professional: the title, lead and evidence footer sit in the same place on every page, margins are 80 px, and the type scale is 48 / 24 / 20 with two weights. Body text is never under 24 px and nothing is under 18 px. No text fails contrast in either mode, and nothing is clipped in Czech. What fails is craft on pages 1 and 3, the form on pages 2 and 4, and a colour system that changes meaning from page to page. Measurements below are in slide pixels (image ÷ 1.5).

## Page 1: How pkit fits together — FAIL
Scores: message 4, form 4, hierarchy 4, grid 4, colour 4, legibility 4, craft 3
- [polish] It fails on craft. The two long diagonal arrows run along the pyramid's flanks and come within about 1 px of the top-left corner of layers 3, 4 and 5. The "Checked first" arrowhead ends about 2 px from the top-right corner of layer 2. A reader sees lines scraping the steps, and they read as the pyramid's outline rather than as two relations. This breaks "arrows meet boxes cleanly". Fix on this page: drop the long diagonals. Use a short vertical arrow set beside each label (down beside "Carried out", up beside "Checked first"), so that each label and its arrow form one unit. If the diagonals stay, offset them at least 16 px outboard of the step corners, parallel to the stair line.
- [polish] The labels sit apart from their lines. "Carried out" is 70–100 px from its arrow, while "Checked first" is 30–60 px from its own, so the two relations are not drawn alike (Gestalt proximity). The fix above solves this too.
- [polish] The "Where ideas start" box wraps its text at about 215 px inside a box about 300 px wide, leaving a 60–75 px dead band on the right. In Czech that band carries over four lines, and "ve" ends a line. Padding is unequal. Fix: size the box to its text measure, or widen the measure to fill the box. Keep Czech prepositions with their nouns (a non-breaking space).
- [polish] Some line breaks split a phrase: "or in / a scratchpad note", "reads the / reasons first". Fix: break by phrase in the narrow note columns.
- [polish] In light mode the text is white on layers 1–4 and switches to dark ink on layer 5. The difference is needed for contrast (5.5:1), but it makes "your code" look like a different kind of thing. Fix in the ramp: lighten layer 5 a little more, or darken layer 4, so that the flip lands where the meaning changes, or nowhere.
- Dark mode is designed, not inverted. The ramp runs from pale to saturated, the text is dark on every layer at 5:1 or better, and the apex still holds the focal point.

## Page 2: Install pkit — FAIL
Scores: message 3, form 2, hierarchy 4, grid 4, colour 3, legibility 4, craft 3
- [block] The file lists sit in the step columns, so "pkit's files" reads as the result of "Once, on your machine" and "Your files" as the result of "In each project". Both lists are what the project holds after step 2. The columns are the strongest grouping cue on the page, and they assign the files to the wrong step. A form that contradicts the meaning (containment as belonging). Fix on this page: stack the steps vertically, each one full width, with step 1 above step 2 so that the order reads as an order. Under step 2, show one project tree in which `decisions/core/` sits beside `decisions/project/` and `rules/core.md` beside `rules/project.md`. Colour pkit's entries in the ownership colour and leave yours in ink, with a one-line key. Seeing pkit's files and yours side by side in the same folders is the evidence for "never yours", and it gives the page its focal point.
- [polish] The install command breaks its URL across three lines, at `git+ssh://\`, with `>` continuation prompts. A reader of the handout who copies it gets the prompts and the breaks, and on the projector it reads as three things. This breaks "terminals cropped to what matters". Fix: at full width the command fits on one line at 24 px mono, so the restructure above solves it.
- [polish] The URL is set in the same grey as the `$` prompt, so the part that must be typed exactly is the part that is played down. Fix in the code notation: grey for the prompt, comments and placeholders (`your-project`) only, and ink or the command colour for everything typed literally.
- [polish] The step 2 panel is kept at the height of step 1 and so carries an empty third line, a 56 px band.
- [polish] The footer writes `core/` and `project/` in the sans, while the lists set the same paths in mono. Use one notation for paths everywhere.
- [polish] The code panels (#F2F4F6 on white, 1.1:1; #212326 on #121416 in dark, 1.2:1) will disappear on most projectors. Fix in the foundations: give the panel fill more weight, or add a 1 px keyline.

## Page 3: Where permissions come from — FAIL
Scores: message 3, form 4, hierarchy 4, grid 4, colour 4, legibility 4, craft 3
- [polish] It fails on message. The page is a 3 × 3 grid of text at equal weight. The squint test finds only the chevron band, and the payoff of the lead ("they hold when an agent forgets them") sits in the last column as plain text, like every other cell. The reader has to read nine cells to get the point. Fix on this page: make the "A call meets" column the evidence. Give each outcome a consistent refused/blocked mark in the accent, with the word kept beside it, and quieten the middle column so that the eye runs from the kind to its outcome. The chevrons can stay as the flow.
- [polish] The code notation is inconsistent within one column: `rm -r` is in mono while `sudo` and `force-push` beside it are in the sans, and so are `gh` and `Read, Glob, Grep, WebFetch`. This breaks Mayer's coherence. Fix in the shared notation: every command, tool name and agent id in mono, or none of them.
- [polish] The header text is white on the first two chevrons and switches to ink on the third. The flip makes the last stage look like a different kind of thing. Fix: one text colour on all three. Retune the ramp's light end so that white text holds at 4.5:1, or use a single fill and let the chevron shape carry the order.
- [polish] Cells break mid-phrase: "project-manager: no / raw gh changes", "no command run, no / file written", "refused, so it / uses…". In Czech, "odmítnuto v každém / režimu" leaves a single last word. In English, "refused, in every mode" runs flush to the table's right edge with 0 px clearance, while the other cells keep 20 px or more. Fix: widen the data columns by taking width from the row-label column (about 290 px, most of it empty), and break by phrase.
- [polish] The row rules (#DFE1E4 on white, 1.3:1; 1.4:1 in dark) will not survive projection. Spacing already groups the rows, so either give the rules enough weight to be seen or drop them deliberately.

## Page 4: How capabilities work together — FAIL
Scores: message 3, form 3, hierarchy 3, grid 3, colour 3, legibility 4, craft 3
- [block] Inside the "pkit::work-tracking" frame, the three slot boxes start at the frame's own border (x = 459), while the project-management bar above them is inset to x = 473. On the right, both are inset by 13 px. The slots' rounded left corners merge into the frame's line, which makes a thickened, notched edge, and the left edges inside one container disagree by 15 px. It is visible at a glance. Fix on this page: inset the slots exactly like the bar, with equal padding on all four sides. If the slots are meant to open on the frame, draw them as openings (sockets cut into the edge), not as boxes laid over it.
- [polish] The form blurs the page's key distinction. Role frames and slot boxes share the same blue 2 px outline. The arrows leave from the role frame's edge, at exit points with no logic (living-docs's second arrow leaves from the frame's lower edge, not from the capability). Slot ids appear nowhere on the diagram, so the terminal's `pkit::work-tracking:fixers` has to be matched by guesswork to "which agent fixes each doc". Fix in the notation: give each of the three concepts one shape. For example, a role is a tag, a capability is a solid block, and a slot is an open socket. Start each arrow at the capability block's centre line, and tag slot 2 with `fixers`.
- [polish] There is no clear focal point. The terminal is a 1120 × 140 px grey block, the largest mass on the page, and it competes with the diagram and with the checklist column. Fix: shrink the terminal to the two `bound` lines plus the slot id, set it under the slot it proves, and highlight the matching slot and lines in the same accent.
- [polish] Hierarchy is flat and noisy. Five treatments share one 24 px size: blue mono, brown bold, black regular, grey regular, and the terminal's colours. That is more than three degrees of emphasis. Fix: one style for role ids, one for capability names, and the slot text in ink.
- [polish] The role labels' descenders sit about 2 px above the grey capability bars in all three frames, so the text nearly touches an element. Fix: give the frame's legend at least 8 px clearance above the inner bar.
- [polish] The checklist ticks (✓) are light grey hairlines that read as "done" rather than as benefits. The mono `pkit validate` sets visibly larger than the sans around it, as `pkit visibility private` does on page 2. In Czech, "ve" ends a line ("za jinou ve / stejné roli"). Fix in the type foundations: scale the inline mono to the sans's x-height.

## Deck
Consistency 3
- The skeleton is consistent and good: the title, lead and evidence-footer positions, the 80 px margins, the 48 / 24 / 20 scale, the 2 px strokes, one corner radius, and the shared right note column at x = 975 (pages 1 and 4).
- [polish] This alone holds consistency below 4: colour does not mean the same thing on every page. Green is the layer order on page 1 and the stage flow on page 3. Purple is "pkit's files" on page 2. Blue is roles and slots, and brown is capabilities, on page 4. "pkit's part" is green, then purple, then blue. That makes four hues across four pages and two accents on page 4. This breaks the 60-30-10 rule (a calm base and one accent) and consistency (Nielsen). Fix at the brand: define the colour roles once, as one ordered ramp for sequence (page 1 layers, page 3 stages), one ownership pair for "pkit's" and "yours" used wherever ownership appears (page 2 files, page 4 capabilities), and neutral for everything else. Then map pages 2 and 4 onto it.
- [polish] There are three diagram vocabularies: filled stepped blocks (page 1), chevrons (page 3), and outlined frames with fieldset legends and grey inner bars (page 4). Page 4 looks as if it came from another deck. Fix in the shared pattern: one container and one connector language, with fill, outline and dash each given a meaning.
- [polish] The point of each page, its lead, is set at 24 px in secondary grey, the same size as the body and quieter than the bold labels, while the titles are topics. The glance test lands on the topic, not the claim. Fix in the foundations: set the lead at about 30–32 px in primary ink, or make the title the assertion (Alley).
- [polish] Code notation varies. Mono is applied in part on page 3, paths are in sans in page 2's footer, and the URL on page 2 is greyed. Define in the shared notation what is set in mono and what is set in grey.

## The three changes with the most impact
1. Brand: one colour system with defined roles: an ordered ramp for sequence, one ownership pair for "pkit's" and "yours", and neutral for everything else. Apply it to all four pages, replacing page 2's purple and page 4's blue and brown, and fixing the white-to-ink header flips on pages 1 and 3.
2. Page 2: stack the two steps full width in order, with each command on one line, and after step 2 show one project tree in which pkit's `core/` entries sit beside your `project/` entries. This removes the false step-to-files grouping and the broken URL, and it gives the page its evidence and its focal point.
3. Page 4: redraw the mapping with one shape each for role, capability and slot, with slots inset evenly in their frame and legends clear of the bars. Tag the slot `fixers`, and cut the terminal down to the proof lines, set beside and highlighted with that slot, so that the diagram becomes the focal point.
