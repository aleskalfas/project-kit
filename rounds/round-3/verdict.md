# Verdict: FAIL

Measurements are in slide pixels (image ÷ 1.5). The type scale I measured on every page is about 48 / 29 / 24 / 20 / 19 px: title, lead, body, secondary, evidence line. All text passes WCAG 1.4.3 in both modes, and every dark mode is designed rather than inverted. The faults below are about form, hierarchy, space and system, not legibility. `[block]` marks anything that keeps the page from passing: a blocking fault, or the cause of a score under 4.

## Page 1: How pkit fits together — FAIL
Scores: message 4, form 4, hierarchy 4, grid 3, colour 4, legibility 4, craft 3
- [block] (holds craft at 3) The two relation arrows float free of the stack. "Carried out" starts in empty space beside the second tier, and its head lands in blank space 47 px left of the base. "Checked first" starts in blank space below the base and stops beside the second tier, short of Decisions. Neither arrow is parallel to the stepped edge: the gap grows from about 23 px at the top to 47 px at the bottom on the left, and from 26 px to 50 px on the right. The reader sees perspective lines, not relations between layers. — Principle: arrows attach to what they relate (Gestalt continuity). — Fix in the notation: run each arrow parallel to the steps at a constant offset, from the Decisions tier to the code tier, with tail and head level with those tiers. On page 1, Decisions → code for "Carried out", and the reverse for "Checked first".
- [block] (holds grid at 3) The left column uses three alignment logics. The ideas box is left-aligned on the 80 px margin. The "Carried out" note is right-aligned to x≈288 with a ragged left edge, so it shares no edge with the box above it. The stack is centred. At a glance the note looks dropped in. — Principle: few alignment edges (Müller-Brockmann). — Fix on this page: left-align the left note to the margin under the ideas box, so the left column has one edge. The right note already hangs on one.
- [polish] "Checked first" does not say what is checked, so the reader depends on the note below it. A label like "Disagree? Check the layer first." carries the brief's relation by itself. The Czech "Kontrola" loses "first" altogether.
- [polish] The stack and its tiers are excellent: one hue, ordered lightness, darkest at the source, and the ideas arrow entering the apex. Keep them exactly.

## Page 3: Where permissions come from — FAIL
Scores: message 4, form 4, hierarchy 3, grid 3, colour 3, legibility 4, craft 4
- [block] (holds hierarchy at 3) The three solid green header bars are the heaviest ink on the page: column labels outweigh the content. Inside each outcome tile, the bold word is "Refused" three times, the one thing that does not differ, while the reason that does differ is set regular. Column 2, "Your AI tool gets", is the mechanism the lead names ("your AI tool applies them"), yet it is the only content column in secondary grey. The table carries six text treatments. — Principle: at most three degrees of emphasis, with labels quieter than content (Refactoring UI). — Fix in the shared table pattern: set headers as bold text on a 2 px accent rule, keeping the flow arrows, or as a light tint; move "refused" into the column head ("A call is refused"); bold the reason in each tile; set column 2 in body ink.
- [block] (holds grid at 3) The Limit line sits 34 px under row 3's text and 23 px under the last tile. That is closer than the rows sit to each other (43 px), so the page-wide caveat reads as part of the last row. Cell text in columns 1–2 hangs on the bar edge, while column 3's text aligns with its header text 20 px inside. That gives two alignment logics in one table. — Principle: space between groups is larger than space inside them (Gestalt proximity), and one alignment per table. — Fix on this page: give the caveat its own band, with at least 48 px above it and a hairline or caveat marker. Align every cell's text to the header-text inset.
- [block] (holds colour at 3) Two accents of equal weight compete: a green header band and a blue tile column. Blue reads as "information" or "link", not "refused", and on page 4 the same blue means "the slot in focus". — Principle: one accent, one meaning (60-30-10; Albers). — Fix at the brand (see Deck): outcome tiles in a neutral tint, with the stop icon and word carrying "refused".
- [polish] The green ramp across the three stages (darkest at the first) matches page 1's meaning, nearer the source. Keep that link, but at label weight, not as solid slabs.

## Page 4: How capabilities work together — FAIL
Scores: message 3, form 3, hierarchy 3, grid 3, colour 3, legibility 4, craft 3
- [block] (blocking fault: default-looking boxes as the main visual language, plus a graphics-contrast failure) The whole diagram is one undifferentiated light grey. The capability boxes, the project-management container and the status panel share the same fill and hairline: 1.15:1 fill and 1.5:1 border against white, about 1.2:1 and 1.5:1 in dark. It reads as a wireframe. On a projector the containers vanish, and with them the containment that says the slots belong to the capability that opens them. The status panel uses the same box as the capabilities, so a terminal and a capability look alike. — Principle: WCAG 1.4.11 asks 3:1 for a boundary that carries meaning, and different kinds need different looks (Bertin). Tufte: no default chrome. — Fix in the shared pattern: a designed container style at 3:1 or better (a 2 px stroke in neutral ink, or the accent tint for the opener), and a terminal style that is distinct from diagram boxes and used the same way as on page 2.
- [block] (holds message and form at 3) The page's point, that a capability names only a role, is not drawn. Arrows run from one capability's box into another capability's box, so the reader sees capabilities wired straight together. The role is an unlabeled grey chip, the quietest element in the drawing. Nothing in the picture says "role" or "slot", the two words the lead turns on. — Principle: the visual is the evidence for the assertion (Alley); the form expresses the structure (Mackinlay). — Fix on this page: make the role the thing the arrows land on. Head the container with the role (`pkit::work-tracking`) and give "played by project-management" second. Name "role" and "slot" once in the drawing, in the lead's words.
- [block] (holds hierarchy at 3) Everything in the diagram is 24 px. The monospace role chips are wider and filled, so they outrank the capability names they sit above. At right, three benefits and the status caption share one grey style, though they are different kinds. — Fix: one level for names and a quieter one for roles. Head the benefits ("What it gives you") and set the caption as a caption.
- [block] (holds grid and craft at 3) The slots run flush to the container's left wall but stop 16 px short of its right wall, so they look shifted rather than open. If the open side is meant as a socket, it does not read as one. Stroke weights mix: about a 0.7 px container hairline beside 2 px slot borders. The status highlight band clears line 1's descenders by about 3 px and line 3's by 4 px, then leaves a 10 px grey strip below it. — Principle: equal padding and one stroke weight. Text needs room inside its band. — Fix: inset the slots equally, or draw the opening as a deliberate notch. Use one stroke weight, and give the band 12–16 px of padding.
- [polish] The blue link between the highlighted `fixers` slot and the highlighted status lines is the best idea on the page. Keep it, in the brand's single emphasis tint.
- [polish] Czech: "které dokumenty změna upraví" ends 19 px from the slot's right border, against 25 px on the left. That is at the limit, so widen the slot.

## Deck
Consistency 3
- The skeleton is a real system, and it is strong. Every page has the same title, lead, content top (y 224) and evidence line (y 643). There is one sans, one mono, one arrow style and one corner radius. Keep all of it.
- Colour meanings drift across pages. There are three accent hues, and none keeps one meaning. Green means layers (p1) and stages (p3). Purple means pkit's files (p2) and appears nowhere else. Blue means "refused" (p3) and "the slot in focus" (p4). "pkit's" is green on p1, purple on p2 and grey on p4. — Fix at the brand: one accent with defined roles, used the same way on every page.
- The container language drifts. Page 1 has solid green tiers. Page 3 has solid green bars and blue-tinted tiles. Page 2 has grey terminal panels, and page 4 reuses them as diagram boxes. — Fix in the foundations: define four looks (tier, container, terminal, emphasis tint) and give each one meaning.
- [polish] The "In project-kit:" evidence line, where the deck proves its claims, is the least legible text on every page: 19 px in grey. It passes the 18 px floor, but in a room 20–22 px would earn its place.

## The three changes with the most impact
1. **Rebuild the colour system at the brand level: one accent with fixed meanings.** For example: green for pkit's own parts and for nearness to the source; neutral ink for yours and for structure; one pale accent tint for "the thing in focus". Outcomes are carried by icon and word, never by a second hue. This fixes page 3's competing accents, ties page 2 into the system, gives page 4 its emphasis, and lifts deck consistency.
2. **Redraw page 4 so it shows the role, in a designed container style.** The arrows land on the role's slot, and "role" and "slot" are named once. Containers get a 3:1 boundary and a style distinct from the terminal. Slots are inset evenly, with one stroke weight. This clears the page's blocking fault and its weakest scores (message, form).
3. **Make page 3's labels quieter than its content.** Set the stage headers as text on an accent rule rather than solid slabs. Bold the reason in each tile, not the repeated "Refused". Set column 2 in body ink. Give the Limit caveat its own band. Page 1 is next: two fixes from passing, namely arrows parallel to the steps and anchored to the first and last tier, and one left edge for its left column.
