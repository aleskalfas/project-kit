# Verdict: FAIL

All sizes are in slide pixels (image ÷ 1.5). Type sizes are measured from cap heights. Title ≈ 47 px, lead ≈ 30, body ≈ 23, notes and footnotes ≈ 20. Nothing is under 18, and every text pair measured passes WCAG 1.4.3. The lowest is white on the bottom pyramid step at 4.57:1. Dark mode is designed, not inverted, on every page. Czech text fits everywhere, with no clipping and no lone last words. The failures are composition, emphasis and system, not legibility.

## Page 1: How pkit fits together — FAIL
Scores: message 4, form 4, hierarchy 4, grid 3, colour 4, legibility 4, craft 4
- [block] The two relation labels, "Carried out" and "If they disagree", sit in the bottom corners beside "Your code and documents", not beside their lines. The left label stands 93 px from its line and flush with the page margin. The right label stands 49 px from its line. So the pair is asymmetric about a centred pyramid, and by proximity both read as captions of the bottom layer rather than of the arrows. It breaks Gestalt proximity and symmetry. Fix on this page: centre each label vertically on its line's run (around the middle step) at the same distance from the line on both sides, for example 24 px. This also fills the empty left-middle and the empty top-right quadrant (about 230 × 290 px), which now leave the diagram's surroundings lopsided.
- [polish] The up arrow's head lands in the bottom-right corner of "Decisions", 8 px above its bottom edge where the corner radius starts, and the down line leaves from the matching left corner. Ends that meet a corner look jammed. Fix (notation): attach relation lines to the middle of a face, or at a fixed offset from the corner, never inside the radius.
- [polish] The relation lines are about 1.3 px. They carry the page's two relations and will thin out on a projector. Fix (shared pattern): a 2–2.5 px stroke for every connector in the deck.
- [polish] The up arrow points toward Decisions while its label says the *layer* is checked first. A reader can take the direction as "go back and change the decision". Fix (this page): word the label as the direction reads, for example "the layer is checked against the decision first".
- [polish] White text on the lightest step is 4.57:1, at the threshold, and projectors lose contrast. Fix (brand ramp): darken the last step one notch.

## Page 3: Where permissions come from — FAIL

## Deck
Consistency 3
- What holds: the title, lead and "In project-kit:" footnote sit at identical positions on every page, the left margin is a constant 80 px, the type scale (47 / 30 / 23 / 20) is shared, and each dark mode is designed (page 1's ramp turns lightest-at-top so the apex keeps its emphasis).
- [block] There is no right margin or column grid. Content ends at 1149, 1131, 1200 and 1181 px on pages 1–4, so the right margin runs from 80 to 149 px. Fix (foundations): a 12-column grid with 80 px margins on both sides, and every panel, column and diagram edge snaps to it.
- [block] Four container languages appear across four pages: filled colour blocks (page 1), flat grey panels (pages 2 and 4), outlined white boxes (page 4) and bare rules (page 3). The pages do not read as one system. Fix (foundations): one container component (filled tint, one radius, one padding), and one terminal component.
- [block] Green means a different thing on each page. On page 1 it is order, and it paints *your* code. On page 2 it is ownership ("pkit's"). On page 3 it is a structural rule plus emphasis. On page 4 it is the focused slot. A reader who learns on page 2 that green means "pkit's" misreads pages 1 and 3. Fix (brand): one stated meaning for the accent. "The evidence for this page's claim" fits pages 1, 2 and 4 as they stand. Page 3 moves its accent to the outcome, and page 2's ownership split is carried by the labels as well as the colour.

## The three changes with the most impact
1. **Put a grid in the foundations.** Use 12 columns with 80 px margins on both sides, and have every panel, column and diagram edge snap to it. This one change fixes page 2's ragged panels and dominant URL bar, page 4's cramped channel and oversized status panel, page 1's asymmetric labels, and the deck's wandering right margin.
2. **Write one emphasis rule into the foundations.** The accent and the single bold level beneath the title go only to the evidence for the title's claim. Applied, page 3's spine and "Refused" outcome lead instead of its qualifiers. Page 2's pkit's-versus-yours split becomes the focal point instead of the git URL. Page 4's diagram stays the focal point over the status output. Green then means one thing on every page.
3. **Rebuild page 4 on one notation.** Draw "a capability in a role" as a single chip used for all three boxes, in one container and stroke style shared with the deck. Give the arrows an 80 px channel, and crop the `pkit status` output to its content. This turns the deck's weakest page from a wireframe into the strongest piece of evidence for its claim.
